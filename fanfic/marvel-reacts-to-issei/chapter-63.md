## Theatre Framing

The theatre's architecture shifted with infernal precision. Where moments before had stood marble and celestial stone, now twisted iron supports rose like diseased trees. Flame—not destructive, but control-demonstrative—licked across the walls in geometric patterns. The stage itself had been redrawn with Hell's demarcation lines, a grid of crimson and shadow that spelled authority and law in every language known to damnation.

Mephisto rose from the centre of the stage like smoke given form, his silk-grey suit pristine despite the hellfire raging around him. His horns caught the infernal light and refracted it into something almost beautiful, and his smile was the smile of a lawyer who had won every case he'd ever tried.

In his hands, he held a document. Not merely paper—this was a contract transcribed in binding light, the words glowing with the weight of demonic bureaucracy. Behind him, Hell's legal representatives materialized: devils in suits sharp enough to cut reality, their eyes holding the cold logic of ancient damnation.

"I believe," Mephisto began, his voice carrying the warmth of a funeral director, "it is time we addressed the elephant in the theatre. Or rather, the dragon."

He gestured, and the document unfurled like a scroll, its edges crackling with barely-contained power.

"Issei Hyoudou. Subject in question. Let us examine the facts of his resurrection with the clarity it deserves."

## Gacha Pull 1: "Mephisto Tries to Claim Issei's Soul"

The screen flared. The theatre dissolved into vision.

Issei Hyoudou stood in a grey space between worlds—neither Heaven nor Earth nor Hell, but the space where contracts are negotiated. Before him manifested Mephisto, fully in his demonic authority, horns ablaze with power that made the very air vibrate.

"I have come to inform you of a legality you may have overlooked," Mephisto said, his tone almost gentle. "You were dead, young dragon. Utterly, irrevocably dead. Your soul had departed this mortal coil. And then—" He gestured, and ghostly images formed around them: Rias, her Evil Pieces gleaming, the moment of resurrection performed at such terrible cost. "—you were resurrected. Not by prayer. Not by natural law. But by demonic magic. By a Devil girl and all eight of her Evil Pieces."

Mephisto stepped closer, and his confidence was a living thing.

"When one is resurrected through demonic channels, through the expenditure of a Devil's own power and essence, a contract is formed. Not consciously, perhaps. But the universe recognizes it nonetheless. You, Issei Hyoudou, were brought back from death by damnation itself. Your very second life is debt-marked. Your soul is collateral."

He spread the glowing contract between them. "Hell claims ownership of your resurrection. Through my authority, I am here to invoke that claim. You belong to Hell now. And through you—" his smile widened, "—your peerage belongs to Hell as well. They are your servants, and servants of Hell's servants are Hell's servants."

The document burned brighter. "This is not a negotiation. This is notification of ownership transfer."

But Issei did not flinch.

He did not rage. He did not argue. Instead, something cold and ancient rose in his eyes—something that had no place wearing a high school student's face.

He walked to Mephisto. Step by step, crossing the space between them as if crossing a battlefield. When he spoke, his voice was quiet and infinitely colder than any flame.

"I don't owe Hell anything."

Mephisto's confidence flickered—just for a moment.

"Rias resurrected me because she CHOSE to," Issei continued, and the words had the weight of absolute truth. "Not because Hell demanded it. Not because I asked for it. She made that choice for herself, as the heir to her house, as the one with the power to make that decision. Her choice. Her sacrifice. Her will."

Issei's hand extended, and the contract ignited—not in Hell's flames, but in draconic fire, golden and transcendent.

"I will NEVER accept being Hell's property. Not through contract. Not through technicality. Not through any loophole your lawyers can find."

And then Ddraig's voice emerged—not from Issei's Sacred Gear, but from the very core of his being. It was the roar of a dragon unbound, ancient and absolute.

**DRAGONS RECOGNIZE NO HELL BUT THE HELL WE CHOOSE. YOUR CONTRACTS DO NOT BIND US.**

Power erupted outward—not in attack, but in assertion. Issei's draconic essence blazed through him, a sovereignty that refused to be diminished or claimed. The very space around him shifted, acknowledging that here stood something that could not be owned, could not be bound, could not be made into debt.

Mephisto's confidence did not merely flicker. It broke.

He retreated a step, then two, his hand still extended toward the burning contract, watching as draconic flame consumed every word, every legal stipulation, every loophole and clause designed to entrap a soul. The flames didn't just destroy the document—they burned away the very concept of Hell's authority over the boy before him.

## Theatre Reactions: Gacha Pull 1

The theatre erupted in controlled chaos.

Supernatural lawyers jumped to their feet, frantically cross-referencing texts that glowed in their hands. One devil adjusted his tie nervously and whispered something urgent to his colleague, who shook his head in disbelief. Another legal expert scribbled notes at furious speed, her pen barely keeping up with the implications.

"That can't be valid," one of Hell's advocates hissed. "The resurrection contract is established precedent—"

"Not when the resurrected subject is a dragon," another voice cut in sharply. "Different jurisdiction entirely."

Rias rose from her block, her composure absolute, but her satisfaction unmistakable. She was no longer a girl defending her choice to resurrect a stray. She was the heiress of House Gremory, watching her decision affirmed before the entire cosmos. 

The devils in her peerage shifted uncomfortably—several of them appeared to be reconsidering their assumptions about who exactly held authority over their lives.

Loki leaned back in his seat and laughed—a genuine, delighted sound that echoed through the theatre. "Oh, this is magnificent. Hell tried to use the rules to cage a dragon and got bit for it."

Ghost Rider—Johnny Blaze—was already grinning, but the grin deepened into something approaching amusement. He understood the language of souls and contracts, and he recognized genuine rejection when he saw it.

Robbie Reyes nodded slowly. "Dragons don't negotiate with devils. Different game. Different rules. Different everything."

Hela's smirk could have cut glass. "Two kingdoms that refuse to bow," she murmured to no one in particular. "Both dragons and devils claiming sovereignty. This will end interestingly indeed."

Doctor Strange watched through steepled fingers, his expression thoughtful. "Hell cannot bind dragons easily," he said quietly to Wong, who nodded in agreement. "Their cosmologies don't overlap cleanly. Where Hell sees contract, dragon sees assertion. Where Hell sees law, dragon sees defiance."

The Asgardians appeared unsurprised—their own experience with draconic entities had taught them similar lessons. Dragons answered to themselves first, last, and always.

## Gacha Pull 2: "Theatre Response"

The screen shifted. No vision this time, but pure information. The theatre's judgment materialized before them all, rendered in the neutral, clinical format that characterized the system's assessments.

Mephisto's claim document appeared in full, projected for all to see. And then—systematically, line by line—the theatre began its analysis.

**CLAIM OF SOUL OWNERSHIP VIA RESURRECTION CONTRACT:** 

The first line burned red.

**DISPUTED.** DRACONIC LAW SUPERSEDES DEMONIC CONTRACTUAL FRAMEWORK. RESURRECTION BY CHOICE IS NOT EQUIVALENT TO RESURRECTION BY SERVITUDE.

**HELL'S JURISDICTION OVER DEVIL-CREATED SERVANTS:** 

Also red.

**LIMITED.** RIAS GREMORY'S PEERAGE ANSWERS TO RIAS GREMORY, NOT TO HELL ADMINISTRATIVE. DEVIL STRUCTURE DOES NOT MANDATE HELL GOVERNANCE OF SUBORDINATE DEVILS' SERVANTS. NO OVERARCHING CLAUSE APPLIES.

**RIGHT TO COLLECT DEBT:** 

Red.

**NONE.** NO DEBT WAS INCURRED BY SUBJECT ISSEI HYOUDOU. DEBT, IF ANY, WAS INCURRED BY RIAS GREMORY TO HERSELF AS SACRIFICE FOR RESURRECTION CHOICE. SUBJECT IS NOT LIABLE FOR RESURRECTION EXPENSES NOT OF HIS CHOOSING.

And then the final verdict appeared, bold and absolute:

**MEPHISTO'S SOUL CLAIM: REJECTED.**

**ANALYSIS: SUBJECT WOULD BITE CLAIMANT.**

**RECOMMENDED ACTION: ACCEPT LOSS AND MAINTAIN DISTANCE.**

The theatre's assessment wasn't merely a legal ruling. It was a blunt, almost amused observation that attempting to claim Issei's soul would result not in contract, but in conflict. Not in law, but in violence. Not in dominance, but in disaster.

## Theatre Reactions: Gacha Pull 2

Mephisto's face was a study in controlled rage. His horns burned brighter, and Hell's fire rippled across the stage. But he did not move. Did not argue. He had gambled, and he had lost, and he was ancient enough to know when the game was finished.

"A temporary setback," he said to his lawyers, his voice still smooth despite the flame in his eyes. "We will revisit this claim when—"

"You won't," Ghost Rider interrupted, his tone conversational but carrying the weight of finality. "Even Hell doesn't want to tangle with him. You know it. We know it. The theatre knows it."

Robbie Reyes nodded. "Dragons and devils. Different game. You played by Hell's rules, but he was never playing by those rules at all."

Hela watched with obvious enjoyment, her smile predatory. "Two sovereignties have collided, and Hell retreated. Fascinating. I wonder what claims the Underworld of Norse mythology might attempt..." She trailed off thoughtfully, apparently filing this information away for future use.

Doctor Strange exchanged a meaningful glance with Wong. "That answers several questions I had about his nature," he said quietly. "Half-Devil, half-Dragon, and somehow entirely his own authority. The cosmologies don't overlap the way we assumed they would."

Loki practically cackled with delight. "Oh, this is delicious. Mephisto brought contracts to a dragon fight. He brought law to someone who simply refuses to recognize its authority over his soul."

Rias watched from her block with the satisfied expression of someone who had just been vindicated before the entire universe. Her decision to resurrect Issei hadn't created a debt to Hell. It had created an alliance with someone who would never be Hell's property—and through that, had effectively claimed a sovereignty beyond even her own house's authority. It was a brilliant gambit, executed by accident.

The other devils murmured among themselves, clearly processing what this meant. If Issei's soul couldn't be claimed by Hell, if his nature as a dragon provided him protection from demonic legal structures, then he wasn't just Rias's servant. He was something far more complex. Something they would need to reassess.

Several supernatural lawyers continued frantically checking their references, but their expressions had shifted from confusion to awe. A hybrid creature who answered to neither Hell nor Heaven, bound only by his own choice and his dragon's pride. It was almost unheard of.

## Conclusion: The Weight of Sovereignty

Mephisto rose from the stage, Hell's flames dimming as he prepared to depart. He paused at the theatre's edge, and when he looked back, his expression was unreadable.

"The matter is tabled," he said, addressing the entire assembly. "But understand this: Hell does not forget claims rejected. And we do not forget those who reject us."

It was not a threat. It was something colder and more absolute—a statement of fact from someone who had walked through damnation for eons and knew the price of defiance.

But Issei's defiance, the theatre had judged, was worth that price.

The stage dissolved into neutral space once more, and the theatre settled into an anticipatory hush. Mephisto's claim had failed, but the rejection had raised new questions. What would come next? Would other cosmic powers attempt to stake claims? Would the dragon within Issei continue to assert this impossible sovereignty?

Ghost Rider caught Johnny Blaze's eye, and both wore the same expression: amusement mixed with respect. They recognized a soul that refused to be bound, and in their experience, such souls either changed the world or burned it down.

Either way, it would be interesting.
