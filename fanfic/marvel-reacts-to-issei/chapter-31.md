# Chapter Thirty-One: Emperor of Hope

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Avengers Leadership (Steve Rogers, Tony Stark) • Doom • Magneto • Cosmic Observers (Uatu, Silver Surfer)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Title Announcement: Emperor of Hope" — **Forbidden**
2. "Apophis Kill" — **Epic**

---

The theatre did not smell like anything anymore.

That was the first thing everyone noticed, and no one said. For ten chapters the air had carried breakfasts and birthday cake, and then a photograph had opened like a wound, and now the tiered dark smelled of nothing at all — scrubbed, neutral, institutional — and the steel-grey band above the stage lit segment by segment with a sound like a rifle being cleaned.

**ARC FOUR: HEROES, KINGS AND MONSTERS.**

**YOU HAVE SEEN WHAT THE DRAGON GUARDS. NOW YOU WILL SEE WHAT THE WORLD MADE OF HIM — AND WHAT IT CALLED HIM AFTERWARD.**

"Cheerful font," said Tony Stark, who had not slept. He'd spent the dark hours between chapters doing what half the room had done: running the erased child's static through every instrument his mind could build without a workshop, and getting a fear-shaped nothing. "Anybody notice we've been promoted? Arc One was the tutorial. Arc Three was the home life. This one's got *war memorial* kerning."

"It has *armory* kerning," Steve Rogers said quietly, beside him. The shield was across his knees. It had been there since the photograph. "Grief first. Then the recruiting poster. I've seen this order of operations before."

Down in the front row, the eight fiancées sat closer together than the seats were designed for. Koneko was still watching the screen the way a sentry watches a treeline.

The gacha wheel descended into the grey light, and the room saw at once that something about it was wrong. Every banner before this had displayed its rarities like a menu. Tonight there was a segment the other segments had visibly drawn away from: a thin seam, darker than the theatre's own dark, fenced on both sides by hazard-striping the machine had never used for anything.

The wheel spun. It slowed. It passed the seam once, twice — the whole theatre leaning with it like a coin circling a drain — and stopped there dead.

**GACHA PULL: 1 OF 2.**

**"TITLE ANNOUNCEMENT: EMPEROR OF HOPE" — RARITY: FORBIDDEN.**

No vision came.

That was the thing, the thing that made three thousand beings hold their breath: every pull in thirty chapters had *opened*, had swallowed them into blood or fire or breakfast. This one just — stood there. Four words, white on black, hanging over the stage with no memory attached, like a headstone delivered before the death.

**EMPEROR OF HOPE.**

Then, beneath it, printing slowly, the way the machine had printed only once before — on the night it said *record not found*:

**THE TITLE IS TRUE. THE TITLE IS EARNED. THE MEANING IS SEALED.**

**YOU COULD NOT YET AFFORD TO KNOW WHY.**

---

"Well," said Doctor Doom, into the silence, and the silence made room for him, because it always did. "At last. Thirty chapters of adolescence, matrimony, and grilled meat, and the machine finally addresses the only question of consequence." He had risen — Doom reacted to thrones the way hawks react to movement. "*Emperor.* Of course. Doom has watched the dragons of a hundred pantheons kneel to this boy; Doom has watched him arbitrate a household the size of a small court. The title is not surprising. The title is *overdue*."

"The title is wrong."

Steve hadn't stood. Somehow that made it land harder — Captain America, seated, saying it to the screen rather than to Doom, in the tone he used when the thing he disagreed with was an idea rather than a man.

"No offense to the boy. Everything we've seen says he's one of the finest people this theatre's ever aired. The problem is the word." He said it slowly, like tasting rust. "*Emperor.* I've spent my whole life on one side of a line, and on the other side of that line there is always a man whose title means *no one is allowed to tell me no.* Kings at least belong to a place. An emperor is what a king becomes when he stops accepting borders. You can be the best man alive and the title is still a loaded gun on the table." He finally looked at Doom. "I've met good men with crowns. I've never met a crown that stayed good."

"Because you have never met a crown worn by someone sufficient to it," Doom said, with the serene contempt of a man who owned one. "Your objection is not to emperors, Captain — it is to *unworthy* emperors, and you have simply never conceived of the other kind. The dragon-brat is vulgar, sentimental, and — Doom concedes — incorruptible in a manner Doom has verified and finds professionally irritating. If the multiverse insists on producing emperors regardless of your comfort — and it does, Captain — it could do far worse than one whose empire appears to consist of fed children and kept promises."

"Then answer the only question that matters."

Magneto's voice came down from the mutant tiers like a gavel. Erik Lehnsherr had said almost nothing since the photograph; the room knew what his silences weighed.

"Every crown I have ever seen was *issued*," he said. "By a church. By an army. By a border drawn in other people's blood, by a mob, by fear — always by *someone*, and the someone is the crown's true name. The machine says the title is true and earned. I do not ask what it means. I ask the older question, the one every throne is built to make you forget: *who gave it to him?* Whose hand is on that crown? Show me the grantor, and I will tell you what the empire is."

The theatre turned, more or less as one body, to the front row.

Rias Gremory was already shaking her head — slowly, and the room realized with a small collective chill that the Crimson Princess, who had named every power and title of this boy's world for them across thirty chapters, looked *unsettled*.

"We know the title," she said. "It's real — it followed him home from a war, it spread the way titles spread, from below, and when anyone uses it to his face he turns red and changes the subject. And none of us has ever found where it started. No herald announced it. No throne issued it. No one we ever asked said it *first*. It was simply, one morning, what people called him." She looked up at Magneto. "You want the grantor. So did I. I know how to trace a title through a bureaucracy. This one has no root. I stopped digging because it frightened me that there wasn't one."

"A crown with no grantor," Magneto said softly, sitting back down, unsatisfied. "In my experience there is no such thing. Only grantors who do not wish to be found."

High above, the Silver Surfer spoke from where his board hung motionless in the dark, his voice quiet and carrying anyway, the way falling snow carries.

"I served an emperor once. His empire was hunger, and no hand crowned Galactus; he simply *was*, and the universe adjusted. I hear the word *emperor* and I look for the hunger underneath it — that is what the title has meant everywhere I have flown." A pause. "Emperor *of hope*. In all my lifetimes of crossing this cosmos, I have never heard those two words joined. I do not know what it would even mean — to be the fact that hope adjusts to."

And higher still, Uatu the Watcher — who had stood, last chapter, for the first time; who had confessed blindness for the first time — said nothing at all. But those nearest his tier noticed he had turned fractionally, and was not watching the screen.

He was watching the seam in the wheel. The Forbidden band. As though checking whether it, too, was somewhere he could not see.

**GACHA PULL: 2 OF 2.**

**"APOPHIS KILL" — RARITY: EPIC.**

**CONTEXT: THE EVIL DRAGON WAR.**

"The what," said Tony — and the theatre swallowed them.

---

There was no sun.

That was the fact the vision built itself around. A battlefield on the roof of the world, a plain of glassed rock and dead wards, armies of devils and angels and fallen ranked together under a sky that should have held a dawn by now and instead held a *lid* — blackness with a texture, blackness that moved — and the theatre understood with borrowed dread that they were not looking at night. They were looking at coils.

**Apophis,** said Ddraig's voice, in the theatre and in the vision at once, old and flat and entirely without its usual humor. **Not a dragon as I am a dragon. The Egyptians knew him best: the serpent at the rim of the world, who swallows the sun before it can rise. Understand what you are seeing. Most evil is a choice — a man deciding. This is not that. Apophis is un-choice. He wants no treasure, territory, or worship. His victory condition is that tomorrow does not occur. Every dawn you have ever seen was a battle he lost.**

The vision moved among the armies, and the theatre heard what the soldiers heard: nothing. The serpent's shadow ate sound the way it ate light, and under it veteran soldiers stood shaking, because armies run on the assumption of *afterward*, and afterward was being digested overhead.

And then a red light got up off the ground.

He was different than they'd last seen him — the armor still Ddraig's scale mail, still dragon-shaped, but *deeper* somehow, crimson gone the color of arterial certainty, scarred and scorched and patched mid-campaign, a suit that had been lived in through a war. He rose alone through the dead air, a single ember under all that ending, and the theatre's audible-heart trick opened, and they braced for battle-fury.

There wasn't any.

*Okay,* thought Issei Hyoudou, tiredly, at the serpent that eats the sun. *Here's the thing. Down there, there's a guy who promised his kid he'd be home for her birthday. There's a unit that hasn't slept in three days and still lined up this morning. Asia's at the field hospital and she hasn't cried yet, because she's decided crying happens after. Everybody down there is carrying a "tomorrow" around like a coin in their pocket.* The ember rose. **[BOOST.]** *You eat those. That's your whole deal. You're not a person who did something terrible — you're the reason "terrible" would stop being a category, because categories need a next day to exist in.* **[BOOST.]** **[BOOST.]** *So this isn't like the others. There's nobody in there to spare.*

The serpent noticed him — a pressure like the attention of an avalanche — and the black lid of the sky peeled open along one seam into an eye, and then a mouth, and the mouth was the horizon.

What followed, the theatre would mostly remember in still frames, because motion at that scale defeated the eye. Ascalon, the dragon-slaying blade, running with light in a world that had none left. Coils falling across mountain ranges like dropped cable. The armor caving along one side and re-knitting slower than it caved. And through it all that exhausted, bookkeeping calm, boost stacking on boost — ten seconds, ten seconds, ten seconds, a man paying installments of himself — until the serpent gathered its whole blackness to swallow him the way it had swallowed ten thousand dawns—

—and he went *in*.

**[PENETRATE.]**

The theatre had seen the word before. They had never seen it used like this: not to bypass an armor but to refuse a concept — the sword and the ember passing through devouring-itself, into the cold coil-heart where the eaten dawns went, and the vision went white, and the white was not an explosion.

The white was sunrise.

Morning came up over the glassed plain like a debt being repaid. The serpent's body fell for a very long time, and was ash before it landed, and did not leave a wake — Kuroka, in the front row, flinched at that; *clean, it reads clean, nya, nothing left* — and across the battlefield, thousands of soldiers of three species stood in the first light any of them had truly believed in for weeks, and looked up at a small red figure hanging in it, armor cracked, blade dimming, visibly barely conscious.

Nobody in the vision said any title. The theatre noted that, hard. No herald, no chant, no coronation. The armies just stood there in the sun that had been given back to them, looking up.

And the boy's dying-tired thought, as the vision closed, was: *good. Okay. Everybody gets a tomorrow. Worth it.* And then, fainter, almost asleep: *man, I hope somebody kept my breakfast.*

---

The lights came up on a very quiet room.

"So that's the confusion, right there," Sam Wilson said at last, from behind Steve. "Machine calls him Emperor of *Hope* and then shows us a kill. A clean, deliberate, no-mercy kill, from the guy whose whole file says he doesn't do those. Anybody else's title generator would've gone with Dragonslayer. Sun-bringer. Something with *teeth*."

"Because the kill isn't the subject," Steve said slowly. He was looking at the afterimage the way he looked at maps. "Watch it again in your head. He never fought the serpent. Not really. He fought for the *sunrise* — for the birthday, the tired unit, the girl saving her tears for afterward. Every one of us in this room has made the hard call to end a threat. He wasn't making that call. He was making a different one, the same one, over and over, ten seconds at a time: *other people get a future, and I'll pay whatever that runs.*" He stopped, frowning, feeling the edge of something too big for the sentence. "I don't think hope is his slogan. I think it's his *casualty list*. Everything he spends comes out of his own column and the futures keep — " He shook his head. "I don't have it yet. But the word isn't wrong the way I wanted it to be."

"It is also," said Doom, "not granted, Lehnsherr — you heard the devil-princess. No root, no grantor, no throne. Doom has considered the problem for the duration of the sunrise and finds only one precedent for a title that issues from nowhere and is nonetheless *true*." A pause, precisely measured. "Prophecy. And this room has already been given one of those, concerning things *we* cannot stand before."

Tony's head came up. So, three tiers away, did Kang's.

"Say the quiet math," Tony said. "Come on. Somebody besides me is doing it. Last chapter: a thing that eats *children out of photographs* — futures, unhappened. Tonight, lesson one of the war college: here's a monster whose entire nature is eating tomorrows, and here's the boy who went *into* one and gave the morning back. This isn't a gacha anymore, if it ever was. It's a syllabus. And it just showed us the entry-level version of whatever took that kid."

Nobody argued. That was the frightening part; the theatre had argued about everything for thirty chapters, and nobody argued.

The screen let the silence sit for exactly as long as it took to become unbearable, and then printed, in the steel-grey band's cold type:

**AN EMPEROR REQUIRES NO GRANTOR, MAGNUS. BUT YOU ASKED THE CORRECT QUESTION — AND ONE ANSWER EXISTS AT YOUR SCALE.**

**NEXT: THE KINGDOM THAT ASKED FOR HIM.**

The wheel rose into the dark, the Forbidden seam still fenced in hazard stripes, still sealed, four white words fading last of all — **EMPEROR OF HOPE** — like a debt the theatre had just been shown the existence of, and not the amount.
