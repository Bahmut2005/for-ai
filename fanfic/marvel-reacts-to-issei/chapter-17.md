# Chapter Seventeen: Fenrir's Teeth

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Asgardians (Thor, Loki, Sif, Valkyrie, Heimdall) • Mutants (Wolverine) • Healers (Night Nurse, Elixir, Doctor Strange)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Fenrir's Ambush" — **Super Rare**
2. "Ravel's Ledger" — **Rare**

---

Wolverine knew before the lights did.

The theatre was still warm from the forge chapter — Eitri and Shuri were two rows apart and three hours deep into an argument about holy-sword lattice structures, Thor was re-enacting the replica Mjolnir loan for anyone who would hold his horn, and Loki was still sulking about it with the committed elegance of a man who intended to sulk professionally. It was a good night. It had been a good night for almost an hour.

Then Logan's head came up, slow, the way it did in forests.

"Someone's about to bleed," he said, to nobody.

The lights went storm-grey. Empires braced. The wheel came down out of the impossible ceiling without any of its usual showmanship, rings turning heavy and deliberate, and dropped its token into the crystal bowl with a sound less like a chime and more like a tooth hitting a plate.

**PULL ONE: FENRIR'S AMBUSH — SUPER RARE.**

Every Asgardian in the theatre stopped moving at the same time.

"That is a name," said Heimdall quietly, golden eyes fixed on the dark screen, "that should not travel between worlds."

---

The screen opened on mountain air. Northern Japan, canon-side: a summit ground ringed in wards, banners of the Underworld hanging beside older, stranger sigils, devils and gods attempting the most dangerous act in any cosmology — peace, catered.

And there, holding court at the center of it, was an old man with one eye, a white river of beard, a wide-brimmed hat, and an aura the gacha helpfully rated **ALLFATHER-CLASS**.

The Asgardian rows leaned forward as one, reverent, ready to behold a mirror of their own dread majesty.

The old man on screen was, at that moment, attempting to sneak away from the negotiations to visit what he cheerfully described as "an establishment of the flesh," and being dragged back by the ear by his own bodyguard.

"No," said Sif.

"Their Allfather," said Valkyrie, delighted beyond words, "is a *dirty old man.*"

"Their Allfather is a dirty old man with an *expense account,*" corrected the bodyguard on screen — a young valkyrie in white-and-blue armor, silver hair to her waist, radiating the specific fury of the criminally underpaid. Her name, the gacha noted, was **ROSSWEISSE**. She was, in rapid succession: cataloguing the old god's receipts, lamenting that battle valkyries received no retirement plan, and telling a bemused Issei Hyoudou that everything she owned came from hundred-yen shops and she was *proud* of it, and then bursting into tears about it, which rather undercut the proud part.

"I like her," announced Valkyrie, pouring. "Underpaid, over-armored, screaming at management. That's the whole sisterhood in one woman."

Then the sky went wrong.

It happened the way weather happens — no entrance, only a change of pressure. The wards over the summit ground frosted. The banners stopped moving. And down out of the bruised air came a figure in pale robes, silver-haired, beautiful the way a knife is beautiful, smiling the smile of a man who has rehearsed being hated.

**LOKI,** said the gacha. **EVIL GOD OF THE NORTH. STATUS: UNINVITED.**

Every eye in the theatre swiveled to the Trickster's seat.

"Don't," said Loki — Marvel's Loki — without looking away from the screen. His voice was flat in a way it almost never allowed itself to be. "Whatever you are all about to say. Don't."

The Loki on screen spread his arms and *monologued.* He objected to the peace. He objected to gods sitting at tables with devils. He said the word "Ragnarök" the way some men say the name of a woman who left them. And then, having explained his entire motive, position, and schedule to his assembled enemies, he put two fingers to his lips and whistled for his son.

The mountain answered.

It came up over the ridge like the ridge had grown it — a wolf the size of a house, grey as the underside of a glacier, moving with a wrongness the eye kept refusing: it did not seem to cross the distance between places so much as decline to acknowledge it. Its eyes were two setting moons. And its teeth—

The gacha, which annotated its highest rarities, wrote it across the sky in letters of ice:

**FENRIR. FANGS RATED: CONCEPTUAL. FUNCTION: THE DEATH OF GODS. NO KNOWN EXCEPTIONS.**

"...Aye," said Thor, very quietly, all the horn-swagger gone out of him. "That is the shape. That is the very shape of it."

On screen, the summit's defenders moved — and so did a boy in red armor. Scale Mail flared crimson against the frost; the borrowed replica of a hammer hummed in his gauntlets, thunder on loan; the green gem pulsed its steady heartbeat.

**[BOOST.]**

The wolf looked at him the way a lifetime looks at an afternoon.

**[BOOST.]**

"Partner," said Ddraig, and the theatre had learned to read that voice through stone and centuries, and what was in it now was fear. **"This creature was made to end things like gods. You are not yet a thing like a god. Do not let it touch you."**

"Kinda planning on that," Issei said, and moved.

The fight was brief, and it was not a fight. It was a demonstration with interruptions. Issei transferred stacked boosts into the hammer and split the sky; the wolf was elsewhere. Sif was narrating under her breath in the audience — *watch the hindquarters, watch the — no, it doesn't telegraph, it doesn't* telegraph — and the summit's assembled powers hurled everything a pantheon and half an Underworld could hurl, and the grey shape flowed through all of it like a rumor.

Then the wolf feinted at the old god.

It was a lie, and it was aimed at exactly one person on that field — the person guaranteed to answer it. Issei saw the moons swing toward the one-eyed old man and the silver-haired valkyrie planting herself in front of her terrible boss with her spear up and her eyes wide, and he did the thing the theatre had watched him do for sixteen chapters, the thing that made him *him*, the thing the wolf had been counting on.

He put himself in between.

The jaws came from an angle that was not an angle. There was no arc to track, no shadow, no sound until the sound, and the sound was Scale Mail — armor that shrugged off mountain-erasing blasts — parting like wet paper.

The theatre came out of its seats.

The screen did not flinch and did not gloat. It showed the fangs going in through the crimson chest-plate and it showed the points of them coming out through the back of the armor, and it showed the boy's body seize around the bite, and it let the audience hear, very clearly, over the screaming of a girl named Asia somewhere off-frame:

**[WARNING. VITAL DAMAGE. VITAL DAMAGE. PARTNER — WARNING.]**

**"PARTNER!"** Ddraig's roar shook two worlds and a theatre.

And Issei Hyoudou, with the death of gods buried to the gum-line in his torso, coughed red inside his helmet, got one hand turned around on the haft of the borrowed hammer, and said — wetly, horribly, *cheerfully*—

"Gotcha. You're in range now, doggy."

The point-blank thunderclap lifted the wolf off its feet.

It was the first time in the entire engagement that anything had moved Fenrir even an inch, and it bought exactly one thing: the jaws opened. The boy dropped out of them. The armor peeled away in dissolving motes of red light, and what was underneath made the healers' section of the theatre go silent in the specific way of professionals doing arithmetic they hate.

The gacha did not linger on the wound. It lingered on the faces around the wound, which was worse.

Asia Argento reached him first, both hands already pouring green light, Twilight Healing at full desperate flood — and the theatre watched the wound *drink* the light and stay open. Fangs made to unmake gods did not respect the healing of heaven. The green light poured and poured and the dark kept welling up through it, and Asia's voice climbed into a register that several hundred hardened heroes would hear in their sleep.

And then a small, imperious, furious voice cut through it like a letter opener.

"Move. *Move!* Weep on your own time — do you want him to die while you water him?!"

She came through the smoke at a dead sprint in a dress utterly unsuited to a battlefield, blonde hair in two ringlet drills, hem soaked dark, carrying a flat velvet case the way soldiers carry ammunition. She could not have been more than fourteen. She hit her knees in the mud beside the dying boy, snapped the case open on a row of crystal vials, and went to work.

**RAVEL PHENEX,** said the gacha. **HOUSE PHENEX. FOURTH DAUGHTER. THE FIRE OF RENEWAL.**

"Phenex," murmured Doctor Strange, sitting forward. "As in—"

"As in the roast chicken," said someone from the cheap seats, because the theatre remembered Riser Phenex, the immortal arrogance Issei had punched out of an engagement, and the theatre held grudges. "That's the little sister."

The little sister was performing field trauma medicine with the composure of a career anesthesiologist. Phoenix Tears — the gacha priced them, and the number had commas in alarming places — went into the wound not in a panicked flood but in *sequence*: the great rent first, then each fang puncture individually, her thumb finding them by count, her voice a steady metronome of instructions to devils three times her age. Flame the color of dawn crawled through the wounds and where it passed, flesh remembered itself. If some of the moisture hitting the mixture was salt and came from her eyes, her hands never acknowledged it, and neither did she.

The boy breathed. Shuddered, rattled, and breathed.

"Honestly," Ravel Phenex said, to the unconscious near-corpse of Issei Hyoudou, in the tone of a woman reviewing a subordinate's expense report, "the *state* of you."

The pull wound down in fragments: the evil god sealed under a hammerblow fat with transferred boosts; the summit saved; the great wolf dragged from the field in restraints by figures the gacha — deliberately, visibly — refused to focus on, a smear of censored light in which, for one frame, something glinted *white.* The Watcher's head tilted at that. So did Loki's.

The last image before the pause was small. Ravel, in the torchlight of the aftermath, bloody to the elbows, taking a little book out of the velvet case where the vials had been, and writing in it with a hand that shook, and then — line by line — stopped shaking.

The lights came up.

---

Thor did not boom. That was how the theatre knew.

He stood with his horn hanging forgotten from one hand, and when he spoke it was in the register he saved for barrows and brothers.

"We have one," he said. "Understand this, all of you. In our sky, under our branches — we have one. Fenris the wolf, who is promised the Allfather's life when the last day comes. We could not kill ours either. We bound him — and do you know what held him, when chains of iron and uru failed? A *ribbon.* Gleipnir. Woven of the sound of a cat's footfall, the roots of a mountain, the breath of a fish, the beard of a woman, the spittle of a bird — impossible things, because only the impossible holds a thing whose teeth are an argument against gods." He looked around the theatre, and his face was old. "And now I learn that a sky we have never touched, gods we have never met, grew the *same wolf.* The same shape. The same teeth. As if every heaven, wherever it rises, builds its own ending — and gives it fur."

"I have watched our wolf sleep for nine centuries," said Heimdall. "I know those teeth the way a man knows the crack in his own ceiling. When the boy stepped in front of them, I looked away. I have never looked away."

"It never telegraphed," Sif said. She was still watching the frozen screen, professional and pale. "I have fought beside thunder my whole life and I am telling you that animal was not fast. Fast is a quality of moving through space. That thing had an *exemption.*"

"And it was wasted," said Loki.

He rose. He brushed his sleeve. The theatre braced for silk and got, instead, ice.

"Let us review my counterpart's masterwork," he said. "He objected to a peace — not a scheme, note, a *tantrum with a pantheon attached.* He arrived uninvited, announced his name, his grievance, and functionally his itinerary, to an audience of his assembled enemies. Where I come from that is not called a plot. That is called a *confession.* And then—" and here something moved under the silk of his voice that the nearest rows would argue about for chapters "—then he whistled, and spent his own son like a coin. You will hear it said in my world, in the old poems they insist upon, that the wolf is *my* child. So take it from the man with standing: a father who files his son's teeth into a key for murdering old men has surrendered every claim to cleverness. A knife does not get to be proud of the hand that grips it — but the hand does not get to be proud of itself either."

"Compassion, brother?" Thor said, wondering.

"Taxonomy," said Loki, sitting. But his eyes had already gone back to the screen — not to the wolf, the nearest observers noted, but to the *restraints* being fitted over it in the censored smear of the final frames. Somewhere in that other cosmology existed a leash rated for a god-killer. Loki's expression filed that away in the drawer marked *unexplored categories*, alongside a certain green gem, and closed softly.

From the high dark of the monster galleries, Fin Fang Foom rumbled once: "Wolves. Dogs that unionized." And said nothing further, which for Foom was a eulogy.

---

They turned to Logan eventually. They always did when there was a wolf in it.

He was at the back with his boots on the seat in front of him and a beer he had threatened someone to obtain, and he let the silence sit until it was done squirming.

"First thing," he said. "Don't put this on the animal. The wolf's a weapon somebody grew. It didn't choose the teeth, didn't choose the leash, didn't choose the target. You wanna be angry — and you should be — you look at the hand holding the rope. Same as always." He took a pull of the beer. "Me and Frank got a list. The bird-girl with the light spear's on it. The wolf ain't. The guy who *whistled* is."

"And the second thing?" asked Night Nurse, because somebody had to.

Logan was quiet a moment.

"Everything that can't die has a door built into it somewhere," he said. "I heal from damn near anything. There's still a sword, back home, made just to turn that off. One thing in all the world with my name on it. That's the tax on being hard to kill — the universe keeps one key cut for you, special." He nodded at the screen, at the frozen frame of grey fur and moonlight eyes. "Their gods' key has four legs. Kid found out his door has teeth. He'll never walk the same after knowing that. Nobody does."

"He swung the hammer," said Elixir — Josh Foley, golden-skinned, the X-Men's living cure, who had been sitting very still through all of it. "The teeth were still *in* him and he swung the hammer."

"Yeah," Logan said. "That's the part." He looked at the kid healer, and for a second the six people close enough to see his face saw the thing he almost never let out of the basement — recognition, the kind that costs. "You want to know what that takes? Not strength. Strength's the first thing that leaves when you're bit that deep. All that's left is deciding you're not done. Most people never find out if they've got it, and they're the lucky ones." He settled back. "Kid's got it. I hate that he's gonna need it."

Strange had his fingers steepled, surgeon's habit, eyes half-closed over the replay he was running behind them. "Four punctures and a crush injury crossing everything a torso keeps important," he said clinically. "In my operating days I'd have called it unsurvivable and been right. And the divine healing *failed* — that's the detail that should frighten this room. Wounds from those fangs resist restoration the way the wolf resists distance; the injury itself carries the concept. Which makes what the girl did—" he opened his eyes "—not a miracle. A *protocol.* Sequenced application, punctures triaged by depth, dosage rationed under fire. I held hands steady for a living. Hers are steadier at fourteen than mine were at forty."

"And can we discuss," said Night Nurse, in the dangerous voice of a woman who had spent decades stitching heroes in a back room for free, "the part where healing in that world comes in a *bottle,* with a *price,* set by a *noble house?* Total restoration as a supply chain. Miracle as commodity. I've bandaged half this theatre on donations and stubbornness, and that little girl was carrying more medicine in one velvet case than I move in a year, and it's all *proprietary.*"

"FINALLY," said Mephisto, from the villains' section, wiping away a sincere red tear, "someone in that cosmology understands product."

"Sit down," said seven people at once.

But Elixir was still looking at the paused screen, at the small blonde figure bloody to the elbows, and he said, quietly, to no one who'd asked: "She didn't ration the last three vials. Check the case at the end of the pull. She emptied it. Whatever those cost — she emptied it."

The wheel, which had been listening — it listened now; the theatre had not forgotten that it listened — began to spin.

**PULL TWO: RAVEL'S LEDGER — RARE.**

---

The vision opened on peacetime, days later, and on a book.

It was a serious book. Leather-bound, brass-cornered, four silk ribbons, tabs in five colors. It lived under the arm of Ravel Phenex the way Excalibur lived at Faiza's knee, and the gacha — with what could only be called professional respect — gave it a full itemized overlay.

**SECTION I: INJURIES.** Date. Cause. Severity, on a ten-grade scale of her own devising, recently and grimly recalibrated so that the Fenrir bite could define the top. Treatment administered. Recovery time projected versus actual — *versus actual*, because she followed up. Recurrence risk. And at the bottom of the section, carried forward page after page, a running total of near-death events that made the theatre inhale as one.

"That number's wrong," said Peter Parker, from the street-level rows. "Tell me that number's wrong."

The number was not wrong. The number had an *audit trail.*

**SECTION II: EXPENDITURES.** Training equipment, destroyed, itemized, with depreciation schedules. Food, graphed, with a trend line that Volstagg saluted. Clothing destroyed by Balance Breaker transformations, tracked to the yen, with a marginal note reading *investigate flame-retardant uniform contract — bulk rate?*

**SECTION III: DEBTS & FAVORS.** Who owed Issei Hyoudou, and what, and since when. Whom Issei owed. A footnote in small fierce handwriting: *favors compound.* On screen, Issei told a shopkeeper "I owe you one!" and Ravel materialized beside him like a summoned spirit of accountancy, saying, "Define 'one.' In writing."

**SECTION IV: PROMISES.** Every promise he made, dated, witnessed, status-tracked. *To protect Asia Argento — Status: PERPETUAL. Non-negotiable. No expiry.* *To repay Rias Gremory for his life — Status: ONGOING; see all other sections.* *To become Harem King — Status: OUTSTANDING. Feasibility review: quarterly.* The theatre laughed at the last one. Some of the theatre noticed she had never once marked it *implausible*, and stopped laughing thoughtfully.

And then the gacha turned, on its own, to the newest page, and let it fill the sky.

**INJURY: FENRIR (F-GRADE — SCALE CEILING, REVISED).** Treatment: Phoenix Tears, six vials, full case. Market value: a number with commas in alarming places, multiplied by six. And the cost column — struck through. One line, in that small fierce hand:

*Drawn from personal reserve. Charged: nothing. He is not to be told.*

And beneath it, in the margin, smaller still, where she had plainly believed no one would ever read it:

*The tears erase the scars, so no one remembers what it costs him to keep being who he is. Someone must keep the account. The ledger will not forget, even when his body is made to.*

The theatre was quiet for a moment, in the way of people rebalancing an opinion.

Then Pepper Potts stood up.

---

"I want it minuted," Pepper said, "that I called it first. That is the most competent person we have seen in seventeen chapters of either universe."

"She's *fourteen,*" said Tony.

"She reconciles *daily,* Tony. She has recurrence-risk columns. She tracks projected-versus-actual recovery — versus *actual,* Tony, she *follows up* — and she maintains a depreciation schedule for a teenage boy's gym clothes." Pepper's eyes had the specific light of a chief operating officer beholding, across an unbridgeable dimensional gulf, a peer. "She negotiated her own family's product at cost and then didn't charge him and hid it in the notation. That's not bookkeeping. That's *governance.*"

"I feel personally attacked," Tony announced.

"You should," said Pepper and Rhodey together.

"I have an AI for this. I have several AIs for this—"

"You have several AIs for this," Pepper said, "because no human being could survive doing for you, by hand, what that child does for him *by choice.* She is running the back office of a one-boy natural disaster with a fountain pen."

Sue Storm was nodding slowly from the Baxter rows, and when Sue Storm nods slowly, veterans check their exits. "I've been the ledger for four superhumans and a building for fifteen years," she said. "Grants, insurance, property damage, school forms, Reed's patents — Reed once filed our entire insurance situation under 'solved' and moved on. Nobody ever *sees* that work. It's only visible when it fails, and hers never will." She looked across the aisle at Pepper, and something passed between them, treaty-shaped. "The girl has my respect. Formally."

"Formally," Pepper agreed.

"Oh no," said Tony. "They're founding something. Rhodey, they're founding something."

"The Committee," said Sue and Pepper, simultaneously and without consultation, which was the most frightening thing the theatre had witnessed all chapter, wolf included.

Mephisto leaned over the villain rail, avuncular, smoldering gently. "Ladies. That ledger is the finest instrument of obligation I have seen produced by mortal hands. Sections Three and Four alone — the *enforceable promises* of a boy purchased for eight Pawns? I'd trade three midwestern souls just to hold the—"

"They're not contracts," said Pepper, without turning around. "They're care."

Mephisto sat back. His smile stayed; his eyes did the arithmetic again and did not like the remainder. "...Worse," he murmured, to himself, in the tone of a man watching his thesis confirm itself unprofitably. "Priceless things ruin markets."

Night Nurse said, to the healers around her, "Every patient in this theatre should have one of her. I've been saying it for years: the chart is half the medicine."

"Kid'll outlive all of us," Logan said, from the back, boots still up. "Not because of the bird tears." He tipped the last of the beer at the screen, at the small figure with the serious book. "Because somebody's keeping score for him."

The lights began their end-of-chapter dim. The screen, unprompted, turned the ledger back to Section I — and let the running total of injuries sit there in the dark, and then, gently, horribly, tick upward by one as some off-screen training accident was appended in real time, the fountain pen scratching somewhere beyond the frame.

"...What does the doubling cost him?" Bruce Banner asked softly, in the front third, not really meaning to say it aloud. "Every ten seconds, double. Bodies aren't ledgers. Where does the debit go?"

Above them all, the great wheel — which listened, it *listened* — turned one soft click on its own, like a promise being scheduled.

## Theatre Ledger

- **New Pulls This Chapter:** *Fenrir's Ambush* — Super Rare; *Ravel's Ledger* — Rare. The wheel's end-of-chapter click in response to Banner's question is the second confirmed instance of the gacha reacting directly to the audience.
- **Secrets Revealed:** Issei's cosmology contains its own Norse pantheon — an Allfather-class (and shamelessly lecherous) Odin, a petulant evil-god Loki, and a Fenrir whose fangs are conceptual god-killers that even Scale Mail cannot stop and divine healing cannot mend. Issei was mortally wounded shielding others and survived only via House Phenex's Phoenix Tears — bottled, commodified, market-priced healing. Ravel Phenex keeps an exacting ledger of Issei's injuries (with a running near-death total), expenditures, debts, and witnessed promises; she secretly paid for his six-vial resurrection from her personal reserve ("He is not to be told"). The captured wolf was hauled away by figures the gacha itself censored — one frame glinted *white.*
- **Alliance / Opinion Shifts:** Pepper Potts and Sue Storm formally extend respect to Ravel and found "the Committee"; Tony Stark, personally attacked, is privately designing her a tablet. Marvel's Loki publicly disowns his counterpart's crudeness and cruelty-to-offspring — while quietly adding "a leash rated for a god-killer" to his unexplored categories. Wolverine claims kinship with Issei's survive-and-swing instinct and rules the wolf blameless, the whistler culpable (the Raynare list stands; DxD-Loki joins it in spirit). Valkyrie adopts Rossweisse as an honorary sister-in-arms. Night Nurse endorses the ledger as medicine; Elixir is unsettled by healing-as-commodity; Mephisto's "Issei is priceless" thesis is reinforced against his own financial interest.
- **Unresolved Hooks:** Who took Fenrir off the field — and why did the gacha censor a *white* glint (see Chapter 13: Albion's unknown wielder)? Where is the leash that held a god-killer, and how long before Loki asks? Ravel's secret expenditure remains unknown to Issei. Wolverine's warning stands: every unkillable thing has a door, and Issei now knows his has teeth. And Banner's question — *where does the debit of Boosting go?* — has been scheduled by the wheel itself. The next bill is the power's own price.
