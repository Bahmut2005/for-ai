# Chapter Twenty-Five: Ravel's Ledger

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Tony Stark • Maria Hill • Sue Storm • Shuri • Financial Experts (Pepper Potts, Emma Frost, Wilson Fisk, Danny Rand)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Ravel's Injury Ledger" — **Comedy-Class**
2. "Hyoudou Household Budget" — **Comedy-Class**

---

The theatre was still tender from Asia's chapter.

That was the only word for it. A room that had spent twenty-four chapters learning to brace for mountains and god-wolves had been undone the night before by a boy cooking rice for a girl who prayed, and nobody had fully recovered. Wolverine was being suspiciously kind to people. Mephisto was still sitting in the small penalty spotlight the theatre had assigned him, radiating the sulk of a demon who had learned there were house rules. And in the family box — the warm-gold gallery the machine had built when it summoned them — eight fiancées of Issei Hyoudou sat in various states of embarrassment and pride, having watched two universes fall a little in love with their idiot.

So when the ceiling lit brass and the little vaudeville bulbs chased each other around the wheel's rim, the theatre actually relaxed. They knew that fanfare now. Comedy-Class meant nobody died tonight.

They were half right.

**PULL ONE: RAVEL'S INJURY LEDGER — COMEDY-CLASS.**

"Wait," said Peter Parker. "The ledger? The ledger's back? The scary book from the wolf chapter?"

"COMEDY-CLASS PULLS," announced the screen, with the dignity of a butler introducing a pie fight, "MAY INCLUDE PHYSICAL EXHIBITS."

A pedestal rose out of the theatre floor, front and center, lit like a museum piece. Onto it, in a curl of dawn-colored flame, materialized a book. Leather-bound. Brass-cornered. Four silk ribbons. Tabs in five colors, plus — new since chapter seventeen — a sixth tab, red, near the back.

And approximately half a second later, out of a second and considerably angrier circle of Phenex fire, materialized a fourteen-year-old girl in mid-lunge, both hands locked around the book, blonde drills swinging, shouting the tail end of a sentence that had begun in another universe:

"—UNHAND MY FILING SYSTEM—"

She landed on the pedestal steps in a three-point crouch that would have credited a Dora Milaje, straightened instantly, and took in the cosmic theatre, the several thousand staring heroes, gods, and monsters, and the family box, where eight women she knew extremely well were waving at her with expressions ranging from delight (Irina) to open predatory glee (Kuroka).

Ravel Phenex smoothed her skirts, tucked the ledger under her arm, and curtsied to an audience of two universes as if she had been sent an invitation and simply mislaid it.

"Ravel Phenex, of the House of Phenex," she said. "I apologize for my entrance. I was given to understand by Rias-sama's situation reports that this establishment abducts people." A beat. Her eyes narrowed at the wheel overhead. "I was *not* given to understand that it abducts *documents.*"

"Situation reports," said Maria Hill, quietly, to no one. "The teenager files situation reports from inside a hostage dimension."

"Ravel-chan!" Irina called down happily. "It's actually very nice here once you stop screaming!"

"The popcorn is unlimited," added Xenovia, as though citing scripture.

"Welcome to the box, little bird," said Kuroka, patting the empty seat they had all, the theatre noticed, somehow known to leave open. "We saved you an aisle."

The screen dimmed the houselights, spread itself wide, and — with what could only be called professional courtesy toward a fellow archivist — opened the exhibit.

**RAVEL'S INJURY LEDGER,** it wrote. **AUDIT PRESENTATION. THE ROOM IS ADVISED THAT ALL FIGURES ARE ACTUAL.**

---

It began as pure comedy, and the theatre laughed the way you laugh at a tax return in fancy dress.

The vision showed the ledger's life. It showed Ravel Phenex trailing Issei Hyoudou through his days like a small, immaculate auditor of chaos: materializing at his elbow when he said "I owe you one" to a ramen vendor ("Define 'one.' In writing"); confiscating receipts out of his pockets while he slept face-down on a training field; interviewing Kiba as a *witness* to a sparring injury, with a form, in triplicate. It showed her chasing the boy around the Hyoudou yard with a measuring tape because his shoulders had broadened two centimeters and every uniform contract needed re-negotiating. It showed Ddraig's gauntlet flashing **[BOOST.]** during a training run and Ravel, without looking up from her book, calling out "That's number nine today, I'm starting a new column for you," and the great Welsh Dragon Emperor answering, with the weariness of eons, **"She is the only one who counts them, partner. Mind the ninth."**

"Okay," said Sam Wilson, wiping his eyes. "Okay, the dragon respects her. The apocalypse lizard *reports* to her."

The gacha unfolded the sections one by one, and the room settled in — Pepper Potts and Sue Storm already leaning forward with the specific hunger of two women who had founded a Committee about this exact book eight chapters ago.

**SECTION I: INJURIES.** Expanded since chapter seventeen. Recalibrated again. The ten-grade severity scale now had annotations in three inks, cross-referenced by cause, by opponent, by *which promise he was keeping at the time.*

**SECTION II: EXPENDITURES.** The depreciation schedules had grown sub-schedules.

**SECTION III: DEBTS & FAVORS.** *Favors compound* was now underlined twice, with a worked example.

**SECTION IV: PROMISES.** Status-tracked. Witnessed. One entry — *To protect Asia Argento — Status: PERPETUAL* — had been re-inked where the original had begun to fade, because some records were not permitted to fade.

**SECTION V,** new, **RATING GAMES:** damage projected versus sustained, per match, per opponent, with a marginal war of handwriting where Ravel's projections argued with reality and — the theatre noted with dawning awe — usually won.

**SECTION VI,** new, **EXPERIMENTAL INCIDENTS:** a curated catalogue of every time a certain fallen-angel researcher's prototype training equipment had detonated, malfunctioned, achieved sentience, or "performed as designed, which was worse." Each entry ended with the same stamped notation: *INVOICE SENT TO AZAZEL. STATUS: IGNORED. INTEREST: ACCRUING.*

"She's charging a *fallen angel* compound interest," said Shuri, delighted beyond language. "She has an accounts-receivable file for *Heaven's rejects.*"

And then the red tab. **SECTION VII: UNAUTHORIZED ACCESS,** one page, one line: *If you are reading this section, the book has already notified me, and I have already forgiven you, because what is about to happen to you is punishment enough.*

Wilson Fisk, in the villains' gallery, made a low sound that the people nearest him eventually identified as approval. It was the first noise he had made in twenty-five chapters.

"Whoever keeps the books," the Kingpin said, to the theatre at large, in that soft cathedral voice, "runs the city. I have killed men for tidiness one-tenth of hers."

"That's a compliment," Emma Frost translated, checking. She tilted her head at the small figure by the pedestal, telepath-to-ledger, CEO-to-CEO. "It is. My word. It actually is."

---

Tony Stark had been silent for four minutes, which those who loved him recognized as a medical event.

"Question," he finally said, standing, pointing at the screen with a mug he'd forgotten was empty. "Small question. Tiny. The recovery column. Projected-versus-actual. Who *verifies* actual? Because I've had JARVIS, FRIDAY, and at various points a small nation-state of interns, and not one of them has ever successfully verified whether I actually rested after—"

"I visit," said Ravel Phenex.

"You—"

"I visit," she repeated, in the tone of a woman explaining stairs. "At the projected recovery date, I arrive at his residence, and I inspect him. If he is training when he should be resting, I confiscate the equipment. It is itemized under Section Two as a recurring cost, because he *keeps buying it back.*" A pause. Her chin lifted a fraction. "Mr. Stark. You are the one with the flying suits, yes? The red ones. May I ask — do you amortize them, or do you simply build a new one every time you have a feeling?"

The theatre held its breath.

"...Pepper," said Tony. "Pepper, the fourteen-year-old is auditing me."

"She's *right,* Tony."

"I KNOW she's right, that's the ATTACK. I've been personally attacked since chapter seventeen and it's ESCALATING. She has *follow-up visits.* She has an interest-bearing grudge against an angel. I have seventeen degrees and a satellite network and I have never once known where my own receipts are—" he sat down heavily "—and I built her a tablet."

The theatre swung toward him.

"You *what?*" said Rhodey.

"I built her a tablet! Eight chapters ago! It's ruggedized, it's magic-shielded — Strange consulted, don't look at him, he *loved* it — it does double-entry, it does recurrence forecasting, it has a stylus that never runs out because I got emotional about the fountain pen, okay, and I've been carrying it around since the wolf chapter waiting for her to *exist* in here—" He was already climbing over the seats. He arrived at the pedestal, straightened his jacket, and held it out with both hands like a squire presenting a sword. "Ms. Phenex. From one attacked professional to the person who attacked him."

Ravel Phenex set down the ledger — the theatre gasped; she *set it down* — and examined the tablet for ninety silent seconds. She tested the stylus. She opened the depreciation module. Her eyebrows performed a small, grudging ceremony.

"The forecasting suite is competent," she pronounced at last. "The interface is garish. The security is adequate against machines and *insulting* against curses — I will forward specifications." She looked up at the most famous engineer of a universe, and something under the imperiousness was, for a moment, twelve years old and pleased. "It is accepted. As a *secondary system.* Paper cannot be hacked, Mr. Stark. Paper does not run out of power in a war zone. Paper survives an electromagnetic pulse, a dragon sneeze, and the collapse of civilization." She tucked the tablet under her arm, next to the ledger, a duchy annexing a province. "But it is a very fine backup, and I thank you."

"Secondary system," Tony repeated, walking back to his seat, hollow and glowing. "I peaked. That's it. That was the peak."

"Formally," said Pepper Potts, standing, "the Committee recognizes Ravel Phenex."

"Formally," said Sue Storm, also standing.

"Oh no," said every married person in the theatre.

"I have run the back office of the Baxter Building for fifteen years," Sue continued, "and I want it minuted, *again*, that this young woman does by hand, in a war, at fourteen, what entire universities of accountants could not do for Reed on a quiet Tuesday. The Committee has three members now." She glanced at Pepper. Something treaty-shaped passed between them for the second time in nine chapters. "We are no longer a committee. We are a *board.*"

Up in the family box, Rias Gremory was smiling the particular smile of a king watching her people be recognized in a foreign court. Rossweisse was taking notes. Koneko said, deadpan, to the room's general air: "Ravel-senpai made me a snack budget. It has a growth-forecast column." She paused. "...I'm in the book. It's nice, being in the book." And the theatre filed that sentence away to be devastated by later, because that was the thing about tonight — the laughter had a floor, and the floor was about to open.

---

Because Maria Hill had stopped laughing several minutes ago.

She was standing at the intelligence rail with her arms crossed, reading Section I as it scrolled, and she was doing what she did — arithmetic, the kind with consequences — and when she spoke, the brass light seemed to cool.

"Put the summary page up," she said. "The audit total. You've been dancing around it for ten minutes." She looked at the wheel, flat cop-eyes in a cosmic theatre. "You built the pedestal. Show the number."

The screen, which had never once obeyed anyone, obeyed.

**SECTION I: INJURIES — AUDIT SUMMARY,** it wrote, and the vaudeville bulbs went out one by one.

**CATALOGUED INJURIES TO DATE: 247.**

**SUSTAINED IN TRAINING: 121. SUSTAINED IN RATING GAMES: 43. SUSTAINED IN EXPERIMENTAL INCIDENTS (SEE: AZAZEL, INVOICES, IGNORED): 22. SUSTAINED IN ACTUAL BATTLE: 61.**

**OF THE 61: INCURRED WHILE SHIELDING ANOTHER PERSON WITH HIS OWN BODY: 58.**

**INJURIES OF GRADE SEVEN OR HIGHER: 31. NEAR-DEATH EVENTS: SEE RUNNING TOTAL, CHAPTER SEVENTEEN. THE TOTAL HAS NOT DECREASED.**

**INJURIES FOR WHICH HE APOLOGIZED TO OTHERS WHILE STILL BLEEDING: 247 OF 247.**

**SCARS REMAINING ON HIS BODY (PHOENIX TEARS, TWILIGHT HEALING): 0.**

**SCARS REMAINING IN THIS LEDGER: ALL OF THEM.**

The theatre did not laugh.

The theatre sat in the dark with two hundred and forty-seven line items hanging over it, each one dated, each one witnessed, each one in that small fierce handwriting, and the room performed, collectively, the exact recalculation the machine had built the whole evening to produce: *this is what it has cost him. This whole time. Every chapter we cheered. It cost this, every ten seconds, and the tears wiped his body clean so that nobody — including him — would ever have to look at the bill.*

"Fifty-eight of sixty-one," said Steve Rogers, very quietly, from the coaches' row. He didn't finish the thought. He didn't need to.

Natasha Romanoff was looking at the screen the way people look at a language they didn't expect anyone else to speak. "I keep a ledger," she said, and the nearest rows went still, because Natasha volunteered nothing, ever. "Everybody who does what we do keeps one. Mine's got red in it — red I put there, that I'm still paying down. His—" she nodded at the floating page, the columns, the zero scars and the two hundred forty-seven records "—his is nothing but other people's red. Every line is a wound that was headed for somebody else, and he *filed himself* under it instead. That's not an injury log." Her voice did something small. "I've never seen books like that. I didn't know you could keep books like that."

"You can't," said Matt Murdock, who had been listening to the fountain-pen scratch under every vision for eight chapters. "She can."

And then the screen turned, gently, to the newest pages — and the family box stopped breathing.

Because the wives had not been in the theatre in chapter seventeen. They had never seen the entry the audience had. And now it filled the sky again: **INJURY: FENRIR (F-GRADE — SCALE CEILING, REVISED),** six vials of Phoenix Tears, market value with commas in alarming places, and the cost column struck through with one line in that small hand — *Drawn from personal reserve. Charged: nothing. He is not to be told* — and beneath it, in the margin, smaller:

*The tears erase the scars, so no one remembers what it costs him to keep being who he is. Someone must keep the account. The ledger will not forget, even when his body is made to.*

Ravel Phenex went the color of her family's fire and moved to slam the exhibit shut, and the screen, mercilessly, kindly, held it open one second longer.

"Ravel." Rias Gremory's voice, from the box, and it was not the king's voice; it was younger than that, and unsteady. "Your personal reserve. That was — Ravel, that was your—"

"It was a *rounding error,*" snapped Ravel, chin high, eyes bright, fooling no one in two universes. "House Phenex produces the tears; I acquired them at cost; it was fiscally *trivial* and I will not have it discussed as—"

Koneko was already out of the box. Nobody had seen her move. She crossed the theatre floor, sat down on the pedestal steps next to Ravel Phenex, and leaned against her shoulder, deadpan and immovable, the way cats decide things.

"...You're in my book too," Koneko said. "It's shorter. One page." A pause. "It just says thank you, a lot of times."

"That is *not proper bookkeeping,*" said Ravel Phenex, wetly, and sat down next to her, and the theatre, very carefully, pretended not to watch a fourteen-year-old duchess of Hell put her face in order line by line, the way she had once watched her own hands stop shaking over a dying boy.

From the vision's last frame, before it dimmed, Ddraig's voice rolled out over the theatre one more time — a scene the gacha had saved: the dragon's gem glowing softly in a dark room where Issei slept off entry number two hundred forty-seven, and the small blonde auditor writing beside his bed by candlelight.

**"I have known every Red Dragon Emperor since the beginning,"** the old dragon said to her, quietly, so as not to wake the boy. **"All of them burned. None of them were *counted.* Dragons hoard treasure, little phoenix — I spent eras wondering what your hoard was."** The gem's light touched the ledger. **"It is him. Guard it well."**

"Obviously," said Ravel Phenex, on the screen and on the pedestal steps, in perfect unison, and the theatre's laugh came out cracked down the middle, which was exactly where the machine had aimed.

---

**PULL TWO: HYOUDOU HOUSEHOLD BUDGET — COMEDY-CLASS.**

The brass bulbs relit, apologetic, and the room accepted the mercy of comedy the way soldiers accept coffee.

The vision opened on a kitchen table, a mountain of receipts, Gorou and Miki Hyoudou looking politely shell-shocked, and Ravel and Rossweisse sitting across from each other like rival delegations at Yalta, co-signing what the gacha titled **THE UNIFIED HOUSEHOLD BUDGET: ONE (1) DRAGON, EIGHT (8) FIANCÉES, TWO (2) HUMAN PARENTS, ASSORTED DEPENDENTS, FLUCTUATING.**

The food line had a graph. The graph needed a logarithmic scale. Volstagg, in the Asgardian rows, rose and saluted it with tears in his beard. The clothing-destroyed-by-transformation line now had the flame-retardant bulk contract *executed* — the theatre cheered the marginal note from chapter seventeen paying off like a bond maturing. The residential line showed the Hyoudou house rebuilt and expanded to absurd devil-funded proportions, with one incendiary unresolved sub-item — **ROOM ALLOCATION: PENDING; DISPUTE ESCALATING; SEE NEXT AUDIT** — that made the entire family box abruptly stop making eye contact with itself, and made Loki take out a small notebook.

Rossweisse had a section of her own, flagged **HUNDRED-YEN COMMENDATIONS,** itemizing everything she had saved the household; Valkyrie led three rows in a standing ovation while the on-screen Rossweisse burst into proud, thrifty tears.

"I have," Danny Rand said dreamily, "literally billions of dollars, and I have never once known where any of it is." Ravel Phenex, from the pedestal steps, made a sound like a small kettle being personally insulted, and Emma Frost said "We know, Daniel," with corporate pity.

And then the gacha did the thing it always did — it let the comedy walk the room into the truth.

Because the last page of the budget was Issei's personal column, and it was almost empty. Allowance in. Almost nothing out. No gear — the household covered gear. No luxuries, unless one counted a line item reading *magazines (confiscated; see disciplinary appendix)*, which the theatre, out of respect for the presence of his fiancées, laughed at for a merciful three seconds only. What the column held instead was small outgoing entries, months of them, each tagged in Ravel's hand: *gift — Asia (hair ribbon, saved 4 weeks). Gift — Koneko (specialty snacks, saved 3 weeks). Gift — Rias (teacup, saved 6 weeks; broke it in transit; saved 6 more weeks; bought it again.)* On and on. Eight names. Fourteen names. His parents. A boy sitting on two hundred and forty-seven injuries and a power that could edit mountains, saving pocket change like a schoolboy, because it was the only currency he had that hadn't been given to him.

At the very bottom, the summary, in that small fierce hand:

*ASSETS: see every other page of this book.*

*LIABILITIES: none that he counts.*

*NET POSITION: he is owed more than any of them will ever repay. He has never once checked the balance. That is why I check it.*

"That's the whole boy," said Sue Storm softly. "In double-entry."

"That's not a ledger," Pepper Potts said, and her voice was not entirely a COO's voice anymore. "It never was. It's a love letter. Two hundred and forty-seven entries long, written in his injuries, in her handwriting — because he'd never write it, and she'd never say it."

"I would *thank* the board," said Ravel Phenex, standing, smoothing her skirts, restored to imperiousness with her eyes still slightly bright, "to refer to it as a *governance instrument.*"

"Of course," said Pepper and Sue together, in the tone of women minuting the words *love letter* forever.

Mephisto leaned out of his penalty spotlight, unable to help himself. "One copy," he said. "One page. Section Three alone. Name your—"

"The book," said Ravel Phenex, without turning around, "has already notified me."

Mephisto looked down at his hand, which had begun, very gently, to smoke. He sat back in the spotlight, extinguished himself, and said, with genuine reverence, "*Magnificent.*"

The wheel began to rise. As it went, the screen showed one last frame — the ledger's inside cover, which the gacha had saved for the exit, where a much younger hand had written the book's founding line, dated back before Fenrir, before Kyoto, before the theatre: *Entry the first. He caught me when the ceiling fell, and ruined his second-best blazer, and laughed. Somebody ought to be keeping track of all this. It may as well be me.*

And beneath the frame, in warm gold, a title card the family box did not know how to read yet, and the theatre did not either:

**MANAGER CANDIDACY: LOGGED. STATUS: PENDING. SEE FUTURE PULLS.**

"Pending," muttered Tony Stark, watching the small duchess climb up into the family box, into the seat they had saved on the aisle, tablet and ledger and Koneko in tow. "God help that kid when it clears. He'll be the first natural disaster in two universes to file on time."

The doorbell chime sounded, cozy and ominous, and the lights went down on a line item flickering at the bottom of the dark: **NEXT AUDIT: ROOM ALLOCATION. DISPUTE ESCALATING.**

In the family box, eight women very deliberately looked at eight different walls.
