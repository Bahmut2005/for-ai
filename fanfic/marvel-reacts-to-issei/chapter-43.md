# Chapter Forty-Three: Rocket's Worst Idea

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Rocket Raccoon • The Guardians of the Galaxy • The Wakandan Science Delegation (Shuri and the Golden City engineers)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Simulation: Steal One Item from Issei" — **Comedy-Class**
2. "Every Trap and Countermeasure" — **Secret**

---

Three nights into the Ravager arc, the theatre had learned two things about letting pirates operate the gacha: the machine insulted them constantly, and the machine kept receipts.

So when Kraglin Obfonteri hauled the operator's lever and the wheel came down in a shade of felted bank-vault warmth the room had seen exactly once before, nobody needed the banner explained. The banner arrived anyway, because the machine believed in ceremony the way executioners believe in sharpening.

**FILED: CHAPTER THIRTY-EIGHT. PLAN RECEIVED. SIMULATION — PENDING.**

The word **PENDING** flickered. Died. Was replaced.

**ACTIVE.**

"Oh no," said Peter Quill, delighted.

"Oh *yes*," said the Grandmaster from his booth, already posting odds.

At the end of the Ravager row, Rocket Raccoon stood up on his seat, wearing the purest expression his face owned — the one that meant *something is about to explode and it might legally be my fault.*

**PULL ONE: "SIMULATION: STEAL ONE ITEM FROM ISSEI" — COMEDY-CLASS.**

**SIMULATION IS IMMERSIVE FOR ONE (1) PARTICIPANT,** printed the screen. **PARTICIPANT HAS BEEN PRE-SELECTED BY HIS OWN HANDWRITING. OBJECTIVE: REMOVE ONE ITEM FROM THE ARCHIVE OF THE RED DRAGON EMPEROR. ALL DEFENSES ARE RENDERED FAITHFULLY. FAILURE RESETS THE PARTICIPANT TO THE HEDGE.**

A column of light opened on the theatre floor, shaped exactly like a raccoon-sized door.

"Rocket," said Gamora. "You don't have to—"

He was already gone.

---

The screen split: on the left, a night-blue rendering of the Hyoudou residence, suburban and warm and softly lit, six known basements deep; on the right, a live feed of Rocket in a hedge, unpacking four hundred contingency plans from a bag that should not have held nine.

**PLAN 1** was the front door. Rocket wore a delivery uniform, a fake package, and a mustache whose existence Drax would question for the rest of his life. He made it up the walk, onto the porch, finger to the doorbell — and inside, at a breakfast table the length of a treaty, Issei Hyoudou paused with a bowl of rice halfway to his mouth and sniffed once.

"Ddraig," said the simulated boy, frowning at the door. "Why does the mailman smell like plasma residue, gun oil, and premeditation?"

**FAILURE 1. CAUSE: DRAGON SENSES. THE NOSE IS NOT AN INSTRUMENT. IT CANNOT BE JAMMED.**

The hedge received him.

**PLAN 7** respected the intent-reading wards from Chapter Thirty-Eight: Rocket sent in a drone with no intent whatsoever, a machine so lobotomized it barely knew it had rotors. It crossed the lawn in perfect spiritual innocence. Three floors up, behind a closed window, Koneko Toujou's ear twitched over a parfait. She did not look up. She opened the window, tracked a servo-whine no instrument in Wakanda could have isolated from cricket-song, and punted the drone into a suborbital trajectory that the simulation lovingly rendered burning up on re-entry.

**FAILURE 7. CAUSE: NEKOMATA HEARING. SHE WAS NOT EVEN ON DUTY. THERE IS NO DUTY. THERE IS ONLY KONEKO.**

**PLAN 19** went under. Rocket tunneled from a neighboring yard, beautifully, geometrically, straight toward the vault's fold — and the tunnel came out in the Hyoudou kitchen, because the property's divine blessing (requested by Asia, granted by an archangel, *notarized*) gently re-curved all hostile topology toward hospitality. Miki Hyoudou looked down at the raccoon emerging from her pantry floor, decided he was a school friend of her son's, and fed him. The wards would not let him refuse. He ate three helpings. He complimented the pickles. He *meant it*, which was the worst part.

**FAILURE 19. CAUSE: DIVINE ENCHANTMENT. ALL ROADS INTO THIS HOUSE LEAD TO DINNER.**

**FAILURES 20 THROUGH 33** scrolled in montage. Rocket in a bush at three in the morning, on the theory that even dragons sleep — bisected hedge, because Xenovia Quarta drilled Durandal in the garden at three in the morning *precisely* because that was when enemies attacked. Rocket disguised as a stray cat — a plan the machine declined to render in full, printing only **DO NOT IMPERSONATE A CAT IN A HOUSE THAT CONTAINS KUROKA** over audio of the theatre's simulated participant screaming. Rocket bribing Asia's familiar with snacks, and Rassei, a small lightning dragon of enormous principle, accepting the snacks and then electrocuting him on the grounds that the two transactions were unrelated.

And **PLAN 33** — the one Shuri would ask to see again — was *good*. Rocket fabricated a replica of Ascalon II accurate to the molecule, swapped it in a nine-second window, and was four blocks away when, in an office in the Underworld, Ravel Phenex's asset ledger flagged a mass discrepancy of zero point four grams, ruled out humidity, and issued an audit. She arrived at the vault with a clipboard and the expression of a young woman who had found a rounding error in reality. Recovery time: eleven minutes.

**FAILURE 33. CAUSE: PHENEX ACCOUNTING. THE VAULT IS NOT GUARDED BY WALLS ALONE. IT IS RECONCILED.**

"Stop the tape," said Shuri, standing, to a machine that had never once obeyed anyone. "Everyone is watching the swords. Watch the *ledger*. Half this room has shield domes; shield domes fail. He has a seventeen-year-old with double-entry bookkeeping and a grudge-tier attention span, and *that* layer responds in eleven minutes across dimensions." She sat back down and began writing with both hands. "We are building an accounting department. A *terrifying* one."

The montage accelerated. **PLANS 34 THROUGH 301**, compressed into a scrolling column of cause-of-failure stamps while sim-Rocket cycled through disguises, drones, decoys, and one unexplained submarine:

**AKENO NOTICED AND SMILED. (PARTICIPANT WITHDREW VOLUNTARILY.)**

**ROSSWEISSE'S RUNES ARE ANALOG AND CANNOT BE HACKED. ALSO, EACH TRIGGERED WARD GENERATES AN ITEMIZED FINE. PARTICIPANT NOW OWES THE HOUSEHOLD 3,400 YEN.**

**THE BOAT TOLD ON YOU.**

**THE BOAT TOLD ON YOU AGAIN.**

**IRINA COUNTS THE FRAGMENTS. SHE COUNTS THEM EVERY DAY. WE DID WARN YOU THAT SHE COUNTS THEM.**

**RIAS GREMORY DOES NOT PATROL. RIAS GREMORY IS SIMPLY AWAKE WHENEVER IT MATTERS. THIS HAS NEVER BEEN EXPLAINED AND WILL NOT BE.**

In the Guardians' row, Mantis had both hands pressed to her mouth, antennae glowing. "He is so *happy*," she whispered, horrified. "He is failing and terrified and it is the happiest I have ever felt him. Should we be worried?"

"I am Groot," said Groot, with the serene cruelty of the vindicated, which everyone understood to mean *the boat is avenged and I have been sending it highlights.*

---

Then the montage stopped scrolling, and the theatre leaned forward, because the counter read **PLAN 402** — and Chapter Thirty-Eight had been very specific that Rocket owned four hundred contingencies. This was the one he had never written down.

Sim-Rocket stood in a shipping depot beside a crate stenciled with the seal of a pantheon that did not exist, addressed in flawless celestial bureaucratese: *TO THE RED DRAGON EMPEROR, WITH THE UNDYING GRATITUDE OF THE ASCENDED PEOPLES OF RACCOON-PRIME. TRIBUTE. DO NOT BEND.*

"No," breathed Tony Stark, half-rising. "No, no, no — the *tribute corner*. The unopened tribute. The wards read intent to *take*—"

"— and he's not taking anything," finished Nebula, and there was something in her voice the theatre had never heard aimed at Rocket before. "He's *giving*. Himself. Nothing in that vault was taken, so nothing can be — so he enters as a gift." A pause. "I broke into the Kyln in four hours. I would not attempt this house. And *that* is the correct door."

It worked.

The theatre watched, cheering in open defiance of five chapters of better judgment, as the crate was accepted, blessed, logged, fined nothing, suspected by no nose — gifts, the caption noted, *smell like intent to give, because they are* — carried past a napping Koneko, entered into Ravel's ledger as *TRIBUTE, UNOPENED, VALUE: SENTIMENTAL*, and shelved, by Issei Hyoudou personally, in the warm golden dark of the archive itself.

Silence. Vault-light. Ranks of kept promises receding into the distance.

The crate opened one seam. Rocket Raccoon slid out onto the floor of the most protected room in a universe not his own, alone, inside every wall, past every wife, under every ward, nine layers deep — closer, the counter admitted in small stunned font, **THAN ANY UNAUTHORIZED ENTITY IN THE ARCHIVE'S HISTORY** — and allowed himself one small fist-pump of blasphemous joy.

"HELLO!" said a bright, chirpy, synthesized voice. "I AM VAULT-KUN, THE ARCHIVE'S AUTOMATED CURATORIAL ASSISTANT! YOU APPEAR TO BE CONDUCTING A THEFT. WOULD YOU LIKE HELP WITH THAT?"

The theatre, as one organism, stopped breathing. In the Ravager row, the real Rocket whispered, "Don't. Buddy. It's me. *Don't.*"

Sim-Rocket, flush with victory, nine layers of unbeatable security behind him, did what Rocket Raccoon has always done when the universe offers him a lever: he pulled it. "Yeah, actually," he said. "What's the good stuff?"

"EXCELLENT QUESTION, VALUED INTRUDER! THE HIGHEST-APPRAISED ITEM IS THIS WAY! PLEASE MIND THE PRICELESS RELICS! YOU ARE DOING A GREAT THEFT!" And the cheerful voice led him — past the Ascalon rack, past the wax-sealed swords of dead dragons, past everything a sane burglar would have grabbed — deeper and deeper down the golden aisles, to a plinth in a pool of welcoming light, bearing a display case of curiously specific dimensions. Raccoon-sized, roughly. "THE TREASURE IS INSIDE! SIMPLY STEP UP TO CLAIM IT!"

He was inside the case, hands on the item — a single gleaming orb — before three things happened in swift succession: the orb dissolved into light; the case sealed with the sound of a vault accepting its combination; and the chirpy voice slid down two octaves, ten thousand years, and one entire species, into a register the theatre knew like weather.

"**Four hundred and two plans,**" said Ddraig, Welsh Dragon Emperor, from every wall at once, warm as a hearth and inescapable as arithmetic, "**and not one of them accounted for the possibility that the dragon was bored.**"

A label printed itself neatly on the case, in Issei's diligent-collapsing-into-scrawl handwriting:

*RACCOON. DRAGON-CLASS NERVE. FOUGHT WELL. CHOSE WRONG.*

**SIMULATION COMPLETE,** stamped the screen, over the frozen image of a raccoon in a display case being regarded, through the glass, by a genuinely impressed teenage dragon holding a snack he was clearly about to offer the exhibit. **ITEMS STOLEN: ZERO. LAYERS DEFEATED: NINE OF THIRTEEN. MEALS INCURRED: FOUR. OUTSTANDING FINES: 3,400 YEN. RESULT: CATALOGUED.**

---

The theatre came apart. Drax laughed so hard he damaged his own seat and then apologized to it. Quill and Kraglin settled seven separate bets in three currencies. The Grandmaster paid out, weeping with joy, to everyone who had bet on "the house wins but the raccoon medals."

Rocket climbed out of the simulation column with his ears flat and his dignity in a bag, and the Ravager section — the theatre's hardest room — gave him something it had never once given the screen: a standing ovation. "Nine layers, rat," said Yondu, boots finally off the seat back, grinning like a knife that had learned pride. "Nine. Off a *dragon god's* house. Hell, I've robbed *planets* with worse doors."

"I was *caught by customer service*," said Rocket.

"You were caught," said a cold, precise, metal-wrapped voice, "by the single finest security architecture Doom has ever audited."

The room turned. Victor von Doom had come down to the rail — Doom, who had audited Asgard's vault and found it quaint — and he was studying the frozen schematic with the absolute stillness he reserved for things he intended to remember forever.

"Attend, all of you, because you laughed through a masterpiece," said Doom. "Layered defense is trivial; any fortress stacks walls. This is *heterogeneous* defense. A biological layer that cannot be jammed. A divine layer that cannot be dug through — that converts *attack into hospitality*. A procedural layer — the Phenex girl's ledger — that detects what no sensor can, because theft is ultimately an accounting error. A social layer of god-tier combatants who patrol out of *love*, on no schedule an infiltrator can learn, because love does not keep schedules. And beneath it all, a final intelligence that does not stop the thief — that *recruits his own greed as an escort* and walks him into inventory." A pause, and then, from Doom, the unthinkable: "Latveria will be adopting elements of this doctrine. Doom is — " the word cost him nothing at all, which was somehow worse, " — impressed."

"Which layer got you into it?" asked Shuri, still writing.

"The accounting," said Doom and Shuri's own pen, simultaneously.

**PULL TWO: "EVERY TRAP AND COUNTERMEASURE" — SECRET.**

The schematic bloomed — the whole house rendered in golden wireframe, thirteen concentric layers annotated in the machine's cold font. The theatre read greedily: the nose, the ears, the runes and their itemized fines, the notarized blessing, the ledger, the patrols, the boat that told on you, the dragon playing customer service *because he thought it was funny and no one had ever gotten deep enough to meet him*. A sidebar noted the previous unauthorized record — **KUROKA, PRE-REFORM: SEVEN LAYERS** — and, in the front row, a woman in a black kimono took a bow nobody had asked for.

But the Secret was at the bottom, and it rearranged the room the way Chapter Thirty-Eight had:

**NOTE: NO LAYER WAS BUILT TO PROTECT THE ITEMS. EACH LAYER WAS BUILT BY ONE MEMBER OF THE FAMILY TO PROTECT THE OTHERS. THE ARCHIVE IS MERELY STANDING IN THE MIDDLE.**

**COUNTERMEASURE THIRTEEN IS THE ONLY ONE THAT HAS NEVER BEEN TESTED. IT IS: ASK HIM. PROJECTED SUCCESS RATE: 100%. NO ONE HAS EVER TRIED IT.**

The laughter went quiet in the specific way this theatre had learned to go quiet — not sobered, exactly; *retuned*. Rocket stared at the last line for a long time, list in his pocket, and did not say anything at all. One tier up, Yondu Udonta was looking at the frozen frame of the boy offering a snack to a burglar he'd caught — at a kid who trapped nothing and adopted everything that got close enough — and whatever moved behind the Centaurian's eyes, the machine saw it, because machines like this always did.

The wireframe rotated once as it faded, and at the archive's very back one small child-height case rendered not in gold but in static, annotated: **EXCLUDED FROM SCHEMATIC. NOT DEFENDED BY ANY OF THE THIRTEEN. IT DOES NOT NEED THEM.** High in the dark, the crack in the screen ticked, once, softly, and was ignored by everyone except Uatu.

**NEXT,** printed the screen, as the lights came down on a raccoon being handed, by Groot, a small commiserative leaf, **THE ARROW AND THE DRAGON: A CONVERSATION THAT NEVER HAPPENED ABOUT SONS WHO DID.**

"Fifty units says the blue guy cries," said Rocket, voice not quite steady, and the theatre, kindly, pretended to believe he was talking about Yondu.
