# Chapter Three: The Red Gauntlet

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Tony Stark, Reed Richards, Shuri, Thor Odinson, Beta Ray Bill, the Wakandan delegation, Doctor Doom

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Transfer Training Session" — Rare
2. "First Boosted Gear Scale Mail" — Super Rare

---

The theatre had settled into something almost like routine, which disturbed the more thoughtful occupants far more than panic would have.

Between pulls, the great amphitheater hummed with the low politics of a hundred stranded factions. The Wakandan delegation had claimed a tier of seats near the eastern arc and quietly fortified it with nothing but posture and the occasional flat stare from the Dora Milaje. Doom had a section entirely to himself, which was less territory and more moat. The Avengers held the center out of habit, the way people sit in their own living rooms even when the house is on fire.

Tony Stark had spent the intermission doing what he always did with helplessness: converting it into engineering. He had no lab, no fabricators, no satellite uplink — the theatre allowed nothing in and nothing out — but the armor's onboard suite still worked, and he'd burned six hours running spectral analysis on recordings of the previous pulls.

"Riddle me this," he said, flicking a hologram toward Reed Richards, who caught it with a hand that stretched four seats over to do so. "The kid's gauntlet. Every 'Boost' event, ambient EM in the recording spikes in a pattern. Not random. Not thermal. It's *periodic*. Ten seconds, metronome-clean."

"I saw it," Reed said, in the tone of a man who had seen it, modeled it, discarded three hypotheses about it, and slept ninety minutes. "The interval is too clean. Biological systems don't produce clock signals that stable. Whatever governs that gauntlet, it isn't the boy's nervous system."

"It is the dragon," Thor said from the row behind them, not looking away from the dark screen. He said it the way other people say *it is Tuesday.*

"That's not data, Point Break, that's vibes."

"It is *memory*," Thor said. "There is a difference, though you have never learned it."

Shuri, sprawled sideways across two seats with her kimoyo beads projecting her own analysis — smaller, denser, and, she would note to anyone who asked, arrived at without a billion-dollar suit — snorted. "You are both wrong about what matters. The interval is not the mystery. The *doubling* is the mystery. Show me where the energy comes from, then we can argue about clocks."

The screen chose that moment to wake up.

The whole theatre stilled the way it now always did, that ripple of a thousand conversations dying mid-word. The great golden wheel of the gacha assembled itself out of cold light, spun with its usual indifferent theatricality, and shuddered to a halt.

**[ PULL: TRANSFER TRAINING SESSION ]**
**[ RARITY: RARE ]**

"Rare," someone in the Latverian section read aloud, and Doom's voice cut across his own retainers like a blade: "Silence. Watch."

---

The screen opened on a forest clearing at an hour no teenager should be awake, gray pre-dawn light and frost still on the grass. Issei Hyoudou was on his hands and knees, vomiting.

Not dramatically. Routinely. The way an athlete throws up when the body has simply been asked for more than it contains, wipes his mouth, and gets back up because the alternative is not getting back up.

"Again," said the small white-haired girl standing over him — Koneko, the theatre had learned her name in Chapter Two's fragments — holding out one small hand with all the ceremony of someone waiting for bus fare.

The green gem in the red gauntlet flared. **[BOOST]**, said the deep resonant voice the theatre had come to know, and a visible shimmer of power gathered along Issei's arm. He gritted his teeth, pressed his palm to Koneko's, and —

**[TRANSFER]** —

— the power discharged in every direction *except* into her. The grass flattened in a ring. A tree lost its leaves. Koneko received approximately nothing and did not change expression.

"You are pushing," she said. "Pour."

"That's the same thing!"

"It is not."

The montage that followed was, by the standards of the theatre's earlier viewing, brutal in a wholly new register. Chapter Two's training had been comedy — the running, the falling, the perverted motivational structures. This was different. This was *repetition*, the unglamorous kind, hundreds of iterations of the same failure. Boost. Gather. Transfer. Spill. Boost. Gather. Transfer. Spill. Issei's hands blistered where the power grounded out through his own skin. At one point his nose bled from nothing but internal pressure, and he plugged it with grass and kept going.

"Oh, I *hate* that I respect this," Tony muttered.

"He is learning impedance matching," Reed said quietly, leaning forward, and now his voice had the particular hush it got when the universe was showing him something new. "Look. Watch the discharge pattern. Early attempts — the energy reflects off the interface between him and the girl. It's like connecting two circuits with mismatched impedance; the power has nowhere to go, so it reflects back and radiates as loss. But each attempt, the reflection coefficient drops. He's not learning a technique. He's learning to *tune himself to another person.*"

"He's learning to give it away," Shuri said.

Reed blinked at her.

"That is what you are all missing, because you are all — " she gestured at Tony, at Reed, at the distant black silhouette of Doom, " — hoarders. Every one of you builds power the same way: accumulate, concentrate, control, *keep*. Your suits, your fields, your fortresses. This boy has a machine on his arm whose entire mythology is 'become the strongest,' and the first hard thing they are teaching him is how to hand the strength to someone else. Look at the design of it. Boost *and* Transfer. Whoever built that gauntlet built generosity into the weapon."

Silence in the science rows for a moment.

"That's..." Tony started, and then, uncharacteristically, didn't finish.

On screen, weeks compressed. The failures got narrower. Issei transferred power into a sword and the blade sang and held it; transferred into Kiba mid-sprint and the knight blurred into a line of light; transferred into a rock, which exploded, which everyone agreed was still progress. And then — one cold morning, on the four-hundred-somethingth attempt — he pressed his palm to Koneko's, and the power went *through*, clean, no spillage, no flare, and the small girl's next punch cratered a boulder into gravel, and Issei whooped like an idiot and got punched in the arm for it, and grinned anyway, blistered hands and all.

**[TRANSFER: MASTERY THRESHOLD ACHIEVED]**, the screen noted clinically, and dimmed.

"Huh," said Sam Wilson, from the Avengers block. "Force multiplier."

"Say more," Steve Rogers said.

"I've watched a lot of strong guys, Cap. Strong guys who get stronger, whatever — that's a bigger problem, but it's the same *shape* of problem. You contain it, you outlast it, you unplug it." Sam nodded at the dark screen. "A guy whose power is making *everyone around him* stronger? That's not a bigger problem. That's a different kind of war."

In the eastern tier, Okoye said something low in Xhosa to General Ayo, who nodded once. Shuri, without turning around, translated for the room's benefit, because she was constitutionally incapable of not having the last word: "She said: 'One spear is a man. One man who sharpens every spear is an army.' Wakanda has a threat file open on him now, by the way. As of ten minutes ago."

"Wakanda has a threat file on a seventeen-year-old," Tony said.

"Wakanda has a threat file on *you*, Stark. Do not feel special."

---

The wheel came back before the laughter fully died, and this time it did not spin so much as *lunge*, gold light shading toward something hotter, redder. The theatre's ambient illumination dropped. Several people who had learned the system's tells sat up straight.

**[ PULL: FIRST BOOSTED GEAR SCALE MAIL ]**
**[ RARITY: SUPER RARE ]**

"Super Rare," Loki read from his perch in the shadows of the upper gallery, tasting the words. "The machine is proud of this one."

---

It opened in the middle of a losing fight.

Context arrived in fragments, the way the screen liked to deal it: a mountain wilderness, night, and the air itself *wrong* — panes of distorted space stacked like broken glass, a barrier of violet-black geometry sealing off the battlefield from the world. Inside it, Issei's friends were down or pinned. And moving through the sealed space like smoke given a smile was a woman with black cat ears and golden eyes, power rolling off her in lazy, poisonous waves.

**[SUBJECT: KUROKA — ROGUE NEKOMATA — SS-CLASS CRIMINAL]**, the screen supplied, and offered nothing else about her, which those paying close attention noted the screen almost never did.

"Spatial partition," Reed said instantly, on his feet, arms unconsciously lengthening toward the screen as if he could palpate it. "That barrier — she's folded a volume of space out of alignment with its surroundings. That's not a wall, that's a *topology*. You don't break that with force. Force stays inside the fold with you. Sue's fields don't do that. *I* can't do that."

"I can," Doom said, from his moat of empty seats. It was the first time he'd spoken to the room in an hour. "With three days' preparation, the correct grimoire, and a price I would not pay twice. The cat-witch does it while *bantering*, Richards. Adjust your estimates of this world accordingly."

On screen, Issei was on one knee, bleeding, boosted to the edge of what his body could hold, and it was not enough, and everyone in the theatre could read on his face the exact moment he understood it was not enough. The green gem blazed.

**[Partner,]** Ddraig's voice rolled out, and the theatre's acoustics did something unfair with it — it did not come from the speakers, it came from the *walls*, from underfoot, from the base of the skull. **[You know what to do. It will cost you. It always costs. Are you willing?]**

"Take it! Whatever it is, take the deal!" someone shouted from the street-level heroes' section, and was shushed.

Mephisto, alone in his velvet dark, leaned forward with his chin on his knuckles and smiled at the word *cost* like a sommelier hearing a good year.

Issei stood up. Said something the screen let them hear only as a raw, cracked-voice shout — a promise, the shape of it unmistakable even where the words blurred: *for them.* And the gauntlet answered.

**[WELSH DRAGON — BALANCE BREAKER]**

**[BOOSTED GEAR: SCALE MAIL]**

The armor did not assemble the way Stark tech assembled — no flying components, no nanite crawl. It *erupted*. Red plate flowed out of the gauntlet like the dragon exhaling its own body around the boy: overlapping crimson scales, a horned helm with burning green lenses, jets of raw power flaring from the back like the memory of wings. In two seconds flat, the flailing teenager the theatre had spent a chapter laughing at was gone, and something out of an old, old story stood in his place.

The theatre made a sound. Not a gasp — lower than that. The sound a thousand professionals make when a threat assessment revises itself in real time.

"Oh, that's *gorgeous,*" Tony breathed, before his brain caught up with his mouth. His HUD was already going insane. "J— okay, no JARVIS, fine, doing this the artisanal way. Full-body articulated plate, zero visible joint gaps, manifested in under three seconds from a *wrist-mounted seed unit*. That's — I want to be clear, my nanite suit needs a housing the size of my sternum and it still has deployment lag. His deploys from a bracelet and it's *load-bearing everywhere at once*."

"It is not manufactured," Doom said.

"Excuse me, it's clearly—"

"It is not *manufactured*, Stark." Doom had risen. Against every law of his own theatrical instincts he had walked forward, down toward the screen, and the firelight of the projection slid across his mask. "Manufactured armor is imposed on the body from outside. That — " one gauntleted finger, uncharacteristically not quite steady, indicating the screen, " — is *conjured anatomy*. The dragon is lending the boy its own form, plate by plate, mediated through the Sacred Gear as a translation layer. It is armor as *incarnation*. Doom has fused sorcery and machinery for thirty years and Doom will tell you plainly: whoever engineered that gauntlet solved problems I have not yet finished *naming*." A pause, and then, with visible cost: "It is... masterful."

"Did Doom just compliment somebody," Peter Parker whispered.

"Write down the date," said Johnny Storm.

**[Note the exchange, partner,]** Ddraig's voice was saying on screen, conversational now even as power screamed around them. **[While the Scale Mail holds, the ten-second law does not bind you. Boost as you will. The armor pays the toll your flesh cannot.]**

"*There,*" Reed said, snapping his fingers, and the science argument that had been simmering for two chapters finally boiled over. "There it is — did you hear that? *The armor pays the toll.* The doubling was never free. There's a cost function, and until now his body was the collateral. The armor is a *buffer*. Tony — the doubling. Ten seconds, two-x, uncapped. Say it violates conservation of energy. Say it out loud so we can all hear how it sounds."

"It violates conservation of energy," Tony said. "Sounds insane. Isn't, necessarily. Option one: it's not creating energy, it's a *tap*. The dragon's the reservoir — an entity that old could have a stockpile that just looks infinite from where we're standing. The gauntlet's a spigot with a very weird valve timing."

"Option two," Shuri said, beads flashing as she threw her model up between them, a lattice of light. "It is not borrowing from some*where*, it is borrowing from some*when*. Look at my decay curves from the training footage. When his boosts collapse, the energy does not disperse — it *vanishes*, below my detection floor, faster than any dissipation mechanism allows. Energy that arrives from nowhere and leaves into nothing. That is not a reservoir. That is a *loan*. The books balance — just not *now*, and not *here*."

"Option three," Reed said softly, "and I like it least, so it's probably closest. The doubling is real, the energy is real, and conservation holds — but the conserved system isn't this universe. Sacred Gears were built by *something*, embedded in human souls, and run on rules written above the physics we can see. We are ants arguing about the thermodynamics of a power outlet. The energy comes from the grid. We have never seen the grid. We have only ever seen the wall."

The three of them looked at each other.

"Table it," Tony said finally, which from him was a white flag with equations on it. "Live mystery. Top of the board."

"Agreed," said Shuri.

"Agreed," said Reed, who hated every letter of the word.

"Children," Doom said, without turning from the screen. "You are about to miss the mountain."

---

On screen, the boy in the red armor had stopped dodging.

He stood in the center of Kuroka's folded space, and the gem on his chestplate was chanting — **[BOOST] [BOOST] [BOOST] [BOOST]** — a metronome with the limiter ripped out, each pulse arriving on top of the last, the air around him beginning to bend the way air bends over a runway in summer. Kuroka's smile, for the first time, lost a few degrees of its lazy warmth.

He raised one clawed palm toward the barrier wall — and beyond it, incidentally, a mountain.

"Dragon Shot," half the theatre said in unison, because Chapter Two had taught them the words as a punchline. It had been a joke. It had gone through a hill sideways and terrified a farmer.

This was not that.

The blast that left his palm was a column of red so dense it read as solid, and the sound arrived in the theatre a half-beat late like the recording itself flinched. It struck Kuroka's spatial barrier — the fold in the world that Reed Richards had just finished explaining could not be broken by force, because force stays inside the fold —

— and the fold *tore*. The violet geometry shattered outward pane by pane like a cathedral window in a bomb blast, and the column of red kept going, out into the unsealed night, and hit the mountain behind it.

There was no explosion. That was the horror of it, the detail every after-action report in the theatre would fixate on. Explosions leave rubble. The beam simply continued, and where the mountain had been intersecting its path, the mountain *stopped being there* — a clean, glowing absence carved from foothill to summit, snow flash-boiled into a rising white halo, the topology of the region revised.

**[TARGET BARRIER: DESTROYED]**
**[INCIDENTAL TERRAIN LOSS: 1 (ONE) MOUNTAIN]**

The screen held the shot: the boy in red armor, steaming, swaying, victorious, framed against a skyline that no longer matched its own maps. Kuroka, at the edge of the frame, was already leaving — but she looked back, once, at the thing in red, with an expression the screen lingered on a moment longer than it needed to. Interest. Reassessment. Something filed away.

Then black.

---

Nobody spoke for a while.

It was Beta Ray Bill who finally broke it — the great horse-skulled Korbinite had sat through both pulls in total stillness beside Thor, Stormbreaker's twin resting across his knees, and when he spoke his voice was low thunder kept respectfully indoors.

"Thor. You hear it too."

"Aye," Thor said. "I hear it."

"The voice in the gauntlet does not speak as a beast speaks." Bill's strange eyes stayed on the dark screen. "I have fought dragons of a dozen skies. Star-eaters. Burrowers in dead worlds. Mighty, all — and all of them *animals*, however vast. Hunger with wings. That is not what lives in the boy's arm. When it says *partner*, it speaks with the weight of one who has had ten thousand partners and buried them all, and chooses to hope again anyway. That is not a monster's voice, son of Odin. That is an *elder's*."

"When I was young," Thor said slowly, "my grandfather's skalds sang of the truly ancient wyrms — not the beasts we hunted, but the ones from before. The ones who fought over questions of *dominion* while our ancestors were still learning fire. The songs said such a dragon's presence could be felt through any seal, any prison, any— " he gestured around them, at the theatre, at the screen — "any *wall*. I dismissed it as skald's inflation. And then that voice spoke, and my hands remembered the songs before my head did."

"And it is *bound*," Bill said. "That is the part that should keep this room awake. Something of that age and that weight, sealed into a trinket on a mortal boy's wrist. Ask the right question, Thor."

"Who binds an elder dragon," Thor said, "and *why* — and how strong must the boy become before the seal is the lesser of the two powers in that arm?" He shook his head. "There is honor in it, though. Did you mark it? The dragon does not command him. It *counsels*. It asks 'are you willing.' A bound god who has kept his courtesy through the binding." He was quiet a moment. "I should like to drink with that dragon."

"You should like to drink with everything."

"And I am usually right."

Two galleries up, folded into shadow, Loki listened to all of it with his chin on his laced fingers and revised his own private ledger. *An elder power, bound, courteous, patient, sealed in a boy who gives his strength away.* Every word of the description was a lock, and Loki had never in his long life met a lock he believed was addressed to someone else.

---

Down in the center rows, the mood had shifted in the way weather shifts — nothing announced, everything different.

Steve Rogers was watching the frozen final frame with the expression he reserved for footage he intended to memorize. "Chapter Two he couldn't hit a target ten feet away," he said, to no one in particular. "How much time passed between these pulls? Months?"

"If that," Natasha said.

"That's the number that matters. Not the mountain. The *slope*." He looked around the room — at Wakanda's tier, where Shuri's threat file had just acquired a second page; at Doom, still standing apart, still facing the dark screen like a man who had found a rival cathedral; at Tony, Reed, and Shuri's floating lattice of unresolved equations; at Thor and Bill, two old soldiers of myth speaking quietly of elder things. "Whatever the screen meant — 'the one who will stand before the things you cannot' — as of tonight, I stop assuming it was being dramatic."

The theatre's lights rose gently for intermission, indifferent as always.

The laughter of Chapter Two did not entirely die — someone in the back was already doing an impression of Issei's victory whoop, and it landed, and people grinned. But the grins were different now. It was the particular fondness that soldiers develop for a rookie the day they realize the rookie is going to outgrow every one of them, and the only real question left is whether he'll be standing in front of them or across from them when he does.

On its dead black glass, faint and brief, almost too quick to catch, the screen flickered a single line — and was blank again before more than a dozen sets of eyes could confirm they'd seen it:

**[THE CAT WILL RETURN.]**

## Theatre Ledger

**New Pulls This Chapter**
- "Transfer Training Session" — Rare
- "First Boosted Gear Scale Mail" — Super Rare

**Secrets Revealed**
- Boost operates on a mysteriously precise ten-second clock originating from Ddraig, not Issei's biology.
- Transfer requires "tuning" to the recipient — Issei's true early mastery is giving power away, not hoarding it.
- Scale Mail (Balance Breaker) suspends the ten-second restriction while worn; "the armor pays the toll."
- The doubling has a cost function; the energy source remains unidentified (reservoir? temporal loan? extra-universal grid?).
- Ddraig is confirmed, by Thor and Beta Ray Bill, as an *elder* being — bound, but courteous, and possibly stronger than his own seal in time.

**Alliance / Opinion Shifts**
- Wakanda opens (and immediately expands) a formal threat file on Issei.
- Tony, Reed, and Shuri form a de facto research bloc around the "conservation problem" — mystery tabled, unresolved by mutual agreement.
- Doom publicly praises the Boosted Gear's design — his first compliment on record — and now regards the Sacred Gear's unknown maker as a rival intellect.
- Sam Wilson reframes Issei doctrinally: not a bigger threat, a *different kind* — a force multiplier.
- Steve Rogers stops treating the screen's prophecy ("he will stand before the things you cannot") as theatrics.
- Loki's covetous interest sharpens: an elder power in a bindable vessel is, to him, an addressed invitation.
- Room-wide: Issei graduates from comic relief to acknowledged rising power; the comedy survives, but the grins have changed.

**Unresolved Hooks**
- Where does the doubled energy actually come from? (Three competing theories, none confirmed.)
- Who built the Sacred Gears — and who bound an elder dragon, and why?
- Ddraig's warning: "It will cost you. It always costs." What was the price of Balance Breaker?
- Mephisto's fascination with debts: eight Pawns, and now a dragon's "toll."
- Kuroka escaped — the screen abnormally withheld information about her, lingered on her parting glance, and flashed: **THE CAT WILL RETURN.**
