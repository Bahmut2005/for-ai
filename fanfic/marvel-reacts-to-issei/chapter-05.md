# Chapter Five: The Boy Who Returned

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Doctor Strange & the Mystic Arts Contingent (Scarlet Witch • Doctor Voodoo • Clea) • Asgardians

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Resurrection by Rias" — **SR**
2. "Sacred Gear 101" — **UC**

---

The theatre had moods. Everyone had learned to read them by now, the way sailors learn a sea.

After the Asia Argento pull, the mood had been an argument — a low ecclesiastical thunderstorm rolling through the rows where Nightcrawler and Daredevil and Kamala Khan sat, with the Ghost Rider burning quietly at its edge like a votive candle with opinions. That storm had not finished. It had merely been tabled, the way everything here was tabled, because the wheel had begun to tick again, and the wheel outranked God, grief, and Matt Murdock's cross-examination voice, in that order.

The wheel slowed. Stopped. The screen went a deep, arterial crimson, and gold letters stamped themselves across it with the smugness of a dealer turning over the river card.

**"RESURRECTION BY RIAS" — RARITY: SR.**

"A rerun?" Rocket said, disgusted. "We *saw* the kid die. We saw the chess pieces. I want my pull back."

"It is not a rerun," said Doctor Strange, and something in his tone made half the mezzanine turn. He had come down over the past few sessions from wherever he'd first landed, and now he sat front and center of what the theatre's population had started calling the wizard block: Wong-less, home-less, but flanked. Clea sat at his right, silver-haired and faintly luminous, a piece of the Dark Dimension wearing a person the way other people wear coats. Jericho Drumm — Doctor Voodoo, Sorcerer Supreme once himself, and never once allowed to forget he'd had the job — sat at his left with the staff of Legba across his knees. A row behind them, alone by choice, in red: Wanda Maximoff, who had said almost nothing since the theatre took her, and whom everyone had very politely not pressed.

"The first showing was the event," Strange said. "This is the autopsy. Look at the framing."

And then the theatre took its breath, and swallowed them, and he was right.

---

They stood — all of them, invisibly, impossibly — in the park at dusk. The fountain. The dead boy by the fountain, a hole through his chest the exact shape of a light spear, blood going black in the last of the light. Ten thousand watchers who had already seen this happen once braced for the pain of seeing it again.

But the vision did not care about the boy yet. The vision cared about the *girl*.

She arrived the way royalty arrives: out of a circle of crimson light that unfolded from the ground like a rose deciding to be a door, the Gremory seal turning within it, and she stepped out of it without hurrying, because hurrying was for people whose arrival changed things, and her arrival *was* the change. Crimson hair to her waist, moving in a wind that didn't exist. A school uniform worn like a court gown. Blue-green eyes that went to the dying boy and performed, in the space of one second, an entire committee meeting.

The theatre — folded inside the moment as it always was — could *feel* that second from the inside this time. That was the new thing. That was the SR of it. They felt her feel it:

*The summoning slip pulled me here. He called with his dying breath — his desire reached my seal. Sacred Gear, then, and a strong one; ordinary want doesn't punch through like that. He's still warm. Still fresh. Still viable.*

And underneath the calculation, quieter, almost buried: *He's just a boy. She left him like garbage.*

"There," Natasha Romanoff said softly, from the dark. "Both of them at once. Remember that."

Rias Gremory knelt beside the corpse of Issei Hyoudou, and produced from nowhere — from an inventory, Tony Stark thought, the girl has *hammerspace* — eight small chess pawns, red as garnets, and set them on his ruined chest.

"Evil Pieces," Strange said, to the room, inside the vision, and the vision, obligingly, *slowed down* for him. Everyone noticed that. The theatre had never done that for anyone before. Several people made a note that the theatre appeared to like wizards, or at least liked being explained.

"Watch closely," Strange said. "Because I want to kill a rumor before it breeds. Half of this room has been calling this a resurrection. It isn't one. Watch what the pieces actually do."

The first pawn sank into Issei's chest like a stone into still water, and it *lit up* — not the boy, the *world*, the local weave of it, the part of reality that held the boy's pattern. Every sorcerer in the theatre saw it at once and sat forward at once, a whole congregation leaning into the same scripture.

"His soul never left," Doctor Voodoo said. His voice was deep and certain, the voice of a man who had personally escorted souls in both directions and would know a returning one on sight. "Look at it. There is no retrieval. No door opens. Nothing is called back across any river, from any gate, out of any hand. The soul is *there* — dimming, untethering, but there. It never went anywhere." He shook his head slowly, and the bones woven into his staff clicked. "This is not necromancy. I have raised the dead, and I have paid what raising the dead costs, and this — this pays a different bill entirely."

"Then what is it?" Sam Wilson called from the Avengers block. "Because from the cheap seats it looked a lot like a dead kid sitting up."

"It's an *edit*," said Wanda Maximoff.

She hadn't meant to say it out loud, perhaps. But the vision had slowed to a crawl now — the second pawn sinking in, the third — and inside that slowness the mechanism was naked, and Wanda Maximoff knew this mechanism. Knew it the way you know your own handwriting in someone else's letter.

"It's not calling him back," she said. "It's not healing him, either — look, the wound closes as a *side effect*, an afterthought. The pieces are rewriting what he fundamentally *is*. Human is a fact about him, and the fact is being — replaced. Crossed out. 'Devil' written over it, in the same hand that wrote the original. His body was fresh enough that the pattern hadn't unraveled, so the new fact takes. If she'd come an hour later, there'd have been nothing coherent left to edit." A pause. Her voice went flat and careful, a person walking on their own grave. "I've done this. To more people than one, all at once, for a lot worse reason than saving a boy's life. The difference is that when I did it, it broke the world and it broke me. And she's doing it *kneeling in a park*, with a *game piece*, and reality isn't even filing a complaint."

"Because reality has already agreed to it," Clea said.

Everyone in earshot of the wizard block turned. Clea rarely spoke to the room. When she did, it was with the mild, unsettling courtesy of someone raised in a dimension where the landlord ate people.

"That's what should frighten you about this cosmology," she said. "Not the demons. Not the spears of light. *This.* In my uncle's realm, remaking a being's nature is an act of overwhelming force — Dormammu rewrites what things are because nothing in the Dark Dimension can stop him. It is violence. Here—" she gestured at the screen, at the fourth pawn sinking home, "—it is *paperwork*. Standardized. Reproducible. Every Devil noble carries a set. Which means someone in Issei's world sat down and turned the transmutation of souls into an *industrial process*, with regulations, and denominations, and a pricing scheme. Someone made ontology into currency."

"And currency," said a warm, delighted, rotten voice from higher up, "is my *native language*."

Mephisto had been so quiet, these last few chapters. That should have worried more people than it did.

But before he could develop the thought, the vision seized their attention and *squeezed* — because on the screen, something had gone wrong. Or gone strange. The fourth pawn had sunk into Issei's chest, and Rias Gremory had gone very still, because the boy had not stirred.

They felt her surprise. It was the first crack they had ever seen in her, and the theatre served it up in close-up: *Four pawns. Four pawns and the reincarnation isn't binding. Four pawns is a mid-tier Sacred Gear user, four pawns is a talented exorcist, four is — not enough? For* him*?*

She placed the fifth. The sixth. Nothing. Her jaw set. The audience — folded into her at this distance, marinating in her interior — felt the arithmetic she did next, and the arithmetic was cold and clean and jointless: *Six pawns is more than I have ever heard of anyone spending on a Pawn. Six pawns is a scandal. If I stop now I lose the pieces AND the boy. If I continue and it takes eight, I lose my entire front line, forever, on one bet. There is no reincarnating twice. There is no refund.*

And then, riding under the arithmetic like a keel under a warship: *Something in this boy is refusing a price that would buy a small army. Which means something in this boy is worth more than a small army.*

*Take the bet.*

Seventh pawn. Eighth. And with the eighth, all of them ignited at once, crimson light welling up out of the dead boy like the world's most beautiful infection, and the wound in his chest unhappened, and the fact of him was quietly, thoroughly rewritten, and Issei Hyoudou — who had been a corpse for the better part of an hour — took a breath.

The vision froze there, on the breath, and let them all out.

---

The theatre held a silence with a very specific texture. It was the silence of several thousand extremely dangerous people revising a file.

"Eight," Loki said, into it. He was sitting forward, chin on laced fingers, and his eyes were bright in the way that had preceded most of Asgard's worst decades. "You all felt her ledger. A Pawn costs *one*. A gifted human, two, three. A small monster, four. She poured in *eight* — her entire allotment, every future soldier of that rank she will ever have — and the system didn't overcharge her out of cruelty. It *appraised* him. The pieces refused to close the transaction until the price matched the goods." He smiled, thin and marveling. "Do you understand what that means? Underneath their reality there is a *valuation engine*. Something down in the plumbing of that cosmos that looks at every soul and knows, to the decimal, what it is worth. And it looked at a lecherous seventeen-year-old failure and priced him like a *siege weapon*."

"That's the part," Strange agreed quietly. He was rubbing his scarred hands together, slow, unconscious, the way he did when a problem was good. "Not the resurrection. The *appraisal*. Magic that transforms, I know. Magic that bargains, I know far too well. But a standing, automated, universal assay of worth, built into the substrate, that a teenage noblewoman can query by touching game pieces to a corpse—" he exhaled. "Every system has an architect. Someone *built* that scale. I want to know who, and I want to know what they were weighing souls *for*."

From his aisle — his own aisle; it was understood by now that Doom had an aisle — Victor von Doom spoke without turning his head. "You are asking the wrong question, Strange. Not who built the scale. Who *audits* it." The mask tilted a fraction toward the screen. "A currency is only as honest as its enforcement. Eight pawns entered that boy and reality *honored the payment*. There is a bank underneath that world. Banks have vaults. Vaults have doors." A pause, silken. "Doom merely observes."

"Doom merely *shops*," muttered Shuri, but she was writing all of it down.

And that was when Mephisto stood up.

He did it theatrically, because he did everything theatrically: rising from the villain rows in his long red glory, dusting velvet from velvet, smiling the smile that had closed a million deals in fine print and screaming. "Well," he said, addressing not the room but the *screen*, the way one lawyer addresses another. "This has all been marvelously educational, and I do thank the management. But now that we've established the facts of the case, I'm afraid I must raise a small — *procedural* matter."

"Oh no," said Peter Parker.

"The boy *died*," Mephisto said, and his voice put on robes and a wig as it went. "Died in sin, I might add — lust in his heart, on Church-adjacent business, slain by a fallen angel; you couldn't *draft* a cleaner intake form. And between the moment of his death and the moment of this — charming — *rewrite*, his soul sat unclaimed on open ground for the better part of an hour. Unclaimed. Unattended. *Uncontracted.*" The smile widened past the point where smiles should stop. "In every jurisdiction I operate in, and I operate in nearly all of them, an unclaimed soul in transit is *salvage*. I hereby assert prior lien. Whatever the little redhead paid, she paid it for goods that were already forfeit. The Devil girl may keep the body." He spread his hands, magnanimous. "I'll take the rest. You may deliver him at the management's convenience."

The silence this time had a different texture. It was the texture of a room waiting to see if the universe would actually allow this.

The screen considered him. There was no other word for it. The great expanse of it went blank and thoughtful, and then white letters printed themselves, one line at a time, with what could only be described as *relish*.

**CLAIM RECEIVED: MEPHISTO.**

**REVIEWING.**

A pause, exquisitely timed.

**FINDING ONE: THE SOUL OF ISSEI HYOUDOU WAS NEVER IN TRANSIT. IT NEVER LEFT. THERE WAS NOTHING TO SALVAGE. YOU WATCHED THE SAME FOOTAGE AS EVERYONE ELSE.**

**FINDING TWO: THE SUBJECT IS NOT OF YOUR COSMOLOGY. YOUR JURISDICTION DOES NOT EXTEND TO HIM. YOUR JURISDICTION, AT PRESENT, DOES NOT EXTEND TO YOUR OWN SEAT.**

**FINDING THREE: THE SUBJECT'S VALUATION IS ON RECORD. EIGHT PAWNS. THE COURT HAS REVIEWED YOUR ASSETS.**

**YOU COULD NOT AFFORD HIM.**

**CLAIM DENIED. THANK YOU FOR YOUR INTEREST IN ISSEI HYOUDOU.**

The theatre did not laugh at Mephisto. The theatre *howled*. Rocket fell out of his seat. Loki laughed the way he hadn't laughed since before Thanos, high and helpless. Even up among the Asgardians, Heimdall — Heimdall — was seen to smile, which several witnesses would later swear was the single most unsettling event of the chapter. Deadpool started a chant of *"reviewed your as-sets"* that mercifully failed to catch on.

Mephisto sat down. He was still smiling, because he was always smiling, but the smile had gone fixed and lacquered, and his eyes had gone the flat red of a closing furnace door. A demon lord who trafficked in worth had just been publicly appraised and found *insufficient*, in front of every hero, god, and rival he had, by a piece of architecture. He folded his hands in his lap with terrible calm.

"Everything," he said softly, to no one, "is negotiable. Eventually."

Nobody sitting near him slept especially well that cycle.

---

The wheel ticked again almost gently, as if the theatre were feeling generous after its own joke. Grey-green light this time, modest, workmanlike.

**"SACRED GEAR 101" — RARITY: UC.**

"Uncommon," Kraglin narrated dutifully. "That's the tier that's mostly, uh. Homework."

It was homework. It was also, everyone agreed afterward, some of the most valuable footage the machine had ever dispensed — a stitched-together seminar of moments: Issei sitting in the Occult Research Club's absurd Victorian clubroom while Rias, backlit by lamplight and self-assurance, laid it out for him; flashes of other people in other places whose names arrived unbidden; glimpses of things that made the gods in the room stop breathing.

The shape of it, as the audience assembled it out loud between scenes:

Sacred Gears were *artifacts born inside people*. Not forged, not granted in adulthood, not earned — installed in certain human souls at birth, invisibly, by the God of the Bible of that world, as a system for enacting miracles through mortals. Most carriers never knew. A man whose crops never failed. A surgeon whose patients beat the odds. A girl in a church whose hands closed wounds. The gears ranged from that — from *lovely and small* — up a long, long ladder.

"So it is a lottery," Thor said slowly. The Asgardian block had been leaning in since the word *God* — Thor and Sif and Brunnhilde, with Loki adjacent and Heimdall above, and even Hela, far apart in her dark corner, listening the way a knife listens. "Their god seeded weapons through mankind like a farmer scattering grain, blind, and let them sprout where they would. In peasants. In children. In *fools*." He didn't say it with contempt. He said it with something almost like vertigo. "Father locked Mjolnir behind a *worthiness clause*. Their god apparently just — left the armory unlocked and walked away."

"Walked away," Heimdall repeated, in the tone of a man filing the phrase under *later, and important*.

And then the seminar reached its final chapter, and the screen said the word, and the temperature of the room changed.

*Longinus.*

Thirteen of them, the vision explained, through Rias's own lecturing voice — thirteen Sacred Gears out of countless thousands, named for the spear that pierced Christ, set apart for a single shared quality: each one, fully realized, could kill a god. Not defy. Not wound. *Kill.* The screen was tactful enough not to show all thirteen. It showed silhouettes, suggestions — a fading tapestry of a beast with too many wings, a blade that ended what it cut, and then, last, lovingly, in full color and no silhouette at all —

A red gauntlet. A green gem. Two doubled voices, boy and dragon.

**BOOSTED GEAR. LONGINUS-CLASS. THE POWER TO SURPASS GOD AND SATAN.**

"There it is," said Ddraig's own audience — because by now, half the theatre thought of the dragon as one of theirs. In the vision's depth the gem flickered, and the old rolling voice added, dry as a hoard: *"They always leave out the fine print, partner. The power is real. So is the bill."*

Down in the wizard block, Strange had gone very still. "The doubling every ten seconds," he said. "No cap but the vessel. We knew that. What we did not know is that his cosmology has an official *category* for 'weapon that kills gods,' that it manufactured thirteen of them, put them in random mortal infants — and that the boy we watched die over a text-message date is carrying one." He looked up at the frozen image of the gauntlet. "Rias Gremory did not know which Sacred Gear he had when she spent those pieces. She couldn't have. All she had was the price refusing to settle."

"Which means," said Natasha, picking the thread up smoothly, the two of them reasoning across half a theatre like colleagues, "she read one data point — *he's expensive* — and bet her entire hand on it, at seventeen, kneeling in a park, in under a minute." A beat. "I've recruited for three intelligence agencies. I would hire her tonight."

"She gambled," Sif said, unconvinced. "It happened to pay. We honor the win, not the throw."

"No," said Doom, and even Sif turned, because Doom disagreeing with someone at normal volume was rarer than the Longinus. "Examine the throw. She did not scatter her pieces over years on safe, modest servants, as her rivals surely do. She *held* them — all eight — waiting for a purchase worthy of the whole purse. That is not luck. That is a doctrine. The girl decided long ago that she would rather have one priceless thing than eight adequate ones." The mask turned back to the screen, to Rias Gremory frozen mid-lecture, regal in lamplight, one hand resting — they all noticed it now, the theatre made sure they noticed it — protectively on her new Pawn's shoulder. "Doom has employed many servants. Doom has met perhaps three people who understood that. He was one of them."

"Was that a compliment?" Peter whispered.

"It was a *warning*," said Miles, watching Doom watch her.

Wanda Maximoff spoke last, quietly, and mostly to Clea, but the theatre had good acoustics when it wanted to. "Everyone keeps asking what she saw in him," she said. "I keep thinking about the other thing. She rewrote what he *is*, and he woke up thanking her. One day he's going to understand what was actually done in that park — what it cost, what it took, what he never consented to because the dead can't consent." Her eyes, on the screen, were very old. "What I want to know is what she does *then*. That's the day you find out what the girl is."

The screen, as if it had been waiting for exactly that, dimmed the frozen image until only two things were left glowing in the dark: the green gem of the Boosted Gear, and Rias Gremory's blue-green eyes.

Underneath them, in small white letters, unhurried:

**SHE WILL TELL YOU WHY SHE BET EVERYTHING.**

**BUT NOT YET.**

The wheel began, softly, to tick.

## Theatre Ledger

**New Pulls This Chapter:**

**Secrets Revealed:**
- The Evil Piece system is confirmed as **not** necromancy: Issei's soul never departed and nothing was called back — the pieces rewrote his fundamental nature from human to Devil while his body was still fresh enough for the new pattern to bind (verified independently by Doctor Voodoo, Wanda, and Clea).
- The pieces carry an automatic appraisal: reincarnation refuses to bind until the price matches the recipient's true worth — implying a hidden, universal **valuation engine built into the substrate of Issei's reality**.
- Rias did not know Issei carried the Boosted Gear when she paid; she bet all eight Pawns on a single data point — the price refusing to settle — with no refunds and no second chances.
- Sacred Gears are miracle-artifacts installed by that world's God in random human souls at birth, ranging from mundane blessings to the thirteen god-slaying **Longinus** class — and Boosted Gear is one of the thirteen, billed as "the power to surpass God and Satan."

**Alliance / Opinion Shifts:**
- Mephisto formally asserted a salvage-lien on Issei's soul and was denied by the theatre itself ("YOU COULD NOT AFFORD HIM") — publicly humiliated, privately patient, and now genuinely dangerous on this subject.
- The mystic-arts contingent has cohered into the theatre's analytical core; the theatre visibly slows footage for Strange's benefit, which several parties have noted.
- Wanda Maximoff broke her silence: she recognizes the Evil Pieces as industrialized, sanctioned versions of what she once did by force — and has fixed her attention on the day Issei learns what was done to him without consent.
- Doom and Loki have both shifted from studying Issei to studying the **system underneath him** — Loki covets the valuation engine; Doom wants to know who audits the "bank" beneath that reality.
- Room consensus on Rias Gremory sharpened from "the girl who saved him" to "a strategist who held her whole hand for one priceless purchase" — Natasha would recruit her; Doom paid her his highest compliment/warning; Sif remains a skeptic.
- Asgardians are newly disturbed by a cosmology whose God scattered god-killing weapons into random mortal infants and "walked away" — Heimdall flagged that phrasing for later.

**Unresolved Hooks:**
- Who built — and who audits — the valuation engine that priced an "ordinary" boy like a siege weapon? What were souls originally being weighed *for*?
- Mephisto's parting note: "Everything is negotiable. Eventually."
- The screen's direct promise: Rias's true reasons for betting everything **will** be revealed — "BUT NOT YET."
- Wanda's question hangs over the story: what does Rias do on the day Issei fully understands the transaction in the park?
- Why the God of Issei's world "walked away" from an unlocked armory — the theological storm from Chapter 4 now has fuel.
