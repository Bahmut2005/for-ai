## THEATRE LEDGER — SECTION I: THE IMPOSSIBLE GATHERING

**[HUD Status: All Factions Present | Threat Level: INTERDIMENSIONAL | Consensus: FRAGILE]**

The Devourer had made its intentions clear three hours ago.

It existed in the spaces between possibilities—a being that consumed unrealized futures, erased potential timelines, unmade worlds that hadn't even finished being born. It was *not* a destroyer in the traditional sense. It was a pruner. A gardener of destruction. And it had set its attention on the Marvel multiverse with the casual hunger of something that had never encountered resistance it couldn't simply erase.

The Theatre had attempted observation but found only static. The Archive couldn't capture what the Devourer touched because the Devourer unmade the very concept of "having been touched." Reality revised itself in its wake. Records rewrote themselves. Time loops broke and reformed around the gaps it left behind.

For the first time in the Theatre's existence, survival required more than witnessing.

It required alliance.

"This is insane," Tony Stark said, looking around the coalition assembly chamber they'd hastily constructed in neutral space. "We have Doom over there actively planning world conquest. We have Magneto who—no offense, Erik—has a history of, you know, genocide. We have Loki. We have *literal cosmic gods* sitting next to street-level heroes. This is a recipe for a civil war, not a defense."

"And yet," Doctor Strange said quietly, his hands folded within his crimson robes, "it is the only recipe we have."

The gathering itself was unprecedented. The Avengers formed a tight bloc—Rogers, Stark, Danvers, T'Challa, and a dozen others. The X-Men occupied a section that bristled with psychic potential—Professor Xavier, Jean Grey, Magneto (who had been invited under duress and extraordinary circumstance), and their contingent. The gods had manifested in physical form: Odin, Thor, Amaterasu, various Asgardian and cosmic entities. The Ravagers—Rocket, Nebula, Kraglin—occupied a corner with the casual disregard for protocol that defined their entire existence. Latveria's delegation was tiny but formidable: Doom, his cabinet, and a contingent of Doombots that technically weren't alive enough to count as attendees but were impressive anyway.

And then there were the magic users. Strange and his sanctum-sanctum colleagues. Loki, whose motivations were suspect but whose power was undeniable. Doom again—because Doom was many terrible things, but he was also *competent*. Agamotto's lingering presence in the room like a watchful parent. Scarlet Witch, Wanda Maximoff, whose chaos magic could rewrite probability itself if pushed hard enough.

The wives were there too. Rias and her contingent from the underworld. Akeno, Xenovia, Rossweisse. Issei stood apart from everyone—not isolated, but distinct. His wives had positioned themselves in concentric rings around him. A living fortress of love and protection.

"The Devourer has consumed forty-seven potential timelines," the Living Tribunal spoke, its multiple faces aligned in perfect consensus. "In each case, the timeline in question ceases to exist retroactively. No records. No echoes. It is as if those futures were never possibilities at all. We estimate it will reach your multiverse's primary timeline in approximately seventy-two hours."

"Then we don't have time for trust-building exercises," Rocket said, his small clawed hands folded across his chest. "So let's skip the part where we all pretend we're not terrified and get to the part where we divide up the labor."

Steve Rogers stood. The room's attention shifted to him—not because he was the most powerful, but because he was, in most people's estimation, the most trustworthy. "We need to know what we're defending. Strange, Doom, someone give us the technical assessment."

Victor Von Doom rose, his metal mask catching the light. When he spoke, his voice was precise, controlled, devoid of the theatrical menace he might have employed in other circumstances. "The Devourer is not a being in any conventional sense. It is a *principle*. An entropy harvester. It exists to prune possibility trees—to eliminate futures that might threaten some larger cosmic equilibrium. My analysis suggests it operates on instruction from a force considerably larger than itself."

"The Void," Strange said. His voice was hollow. "We've theorized about it for years. A cosmic force dedicated to elimination. To simplification. To removing complication from the universal equation."

"So we're not fighting the Devourer," Jean Grey said, standing. Her red hair drifted slightly as her psychic power manifested. "We're fighting something that treats the Devourer like a tool."

"Yes," Strange confirmed. "Which means killing the Devourer doesn't solve the problem. We need to protect the timelines *from* being erased. We need to create a space where unrealized possibilities can exist safely."

Magneto leaned back in his seat, his hands folded before him. "The roles, then. If we are to undertake this foolish enterprise, we must divide responsibilities according to expertise."

"Tech and theoretical engineering," Doom said flatly, looking at Stark. "Stark Industries. My Latverian research division. Wakanda's manufacturing capacity. We build defenses."

"Magic," Strange said. "Loki, myself, Agamotto if it will manifest further. We'll establish a binding circle—a space outside the Void's reach."

"Psychic infrastructure," Charles Xavier spoke for the first time, his voice carrying the weight of someone who had coordinated millions of minds. "Jean and I can link consciousness across the defense. We'll be the nervous system. If the Devourer moves, we'll know. If it targets a specific timeline, we'll respond."

"Wakanda coordinates logistics," T'Challa added. "Transport, supply lines, communication networks. We've experience managing impossible supply chains."

"Latveria handles security and counterintelligence," Doom said. "Any infiltration attempt, my systems will identify it."

"The Ravagers handle stealth ops," Rocket said, grinning without humor. "If we need to slip into places that don't officially exist, hit targets that can't be targeted, we're your crew."

Yondu's whistle—that legendary sound—echoed through the chamber. "Going to work alongside gods and space-kings," the old Ravager mused. "Better than I expected, actually."

"And the wives?" Issei asked quietly. His voice cut through the tactical discussion like a blade. "What are their roles?"

The room went quiet.

"They," Rias said, standing, "are the heart and morale. They are the reason this coalition doesn't collapse under the weight of its own contradictions. You put a bunch of conquerors, scientists, warriors, and gods in a room, they'll tear each other apart. They put us here—women who have chosen love over power, chosen unity over conquest, chosen each other's survival over personal victory—and suddenly this impossible thing becomes possible."

"My wife is a priestess," one of the X-Men's allies said. "Rossweisse is an Valkyrie. She'll help Jean with the psychic network. She's got military discipline and magical understanding."

"Akeno is a demoness," Rias continued. "She has command authority that transcends faction. The underworld answers to her, and through her, they'll answer to this coalition."

"Xenovia is holy magic incarnate," Issei said, understanding now. "She can consecrate spaces, bless territory, make it hostile to erasure."

"And you," Akeno said, smiling with genuine affection at Issei, "will do what you've always done. You'll hold us together. You'll be the proof that impossible alliances can work, because you're living proof that enemies can love each other."

Magneto made a sound—not quite a laugh, but something close. "The dragon-man becomes the symbol. How inevitable."

"So we have assignments," Steve Rogers said, pulling everyone back to tactical focus. "Doom and Stark: defensive infrastructure. Strange and Loki: magical binding. Xavier and Jean: psychic coordination. Wakanda: logistics. Latveria: security. Ravagers: special operations. The wives: morale and secondary support roles. Issei: primary symbol and adaptive response."

"What about me?" Odin asked, and his eye blazed with the light of ages.

"You," Rogers said carefully, "maintain divine neutrality. If the Void targets the universe's theological foundations, we need someone who can negotiate with entities older than creation itself. You're the backup plan if this goes sideways."

Odin nodded slowly. "I have warred and negotiated for millennia. I can do both simultaneously."

"Then we begin," Doom said, rising. "Section Seven of Latveria's deep archives can serve as primary coordination hub. It is defended by means Stark's technology and Xavier's psychics will struggle to penetrate. If we're going to trust each other, we might as well do it in a space where betrayal is literally impossible."

"How would that work?" T'Challa asked.

"Magical sealing," Loki said, understanding immediately. "The space becomes isolated—nothing can leave until everyone agrees to leave. Perfect trust enforcer."

"Exactly," Doom said. "We coordinate from within sealed space. We coordinate or we die together. There is no third option."

Around the room, people began to rise—heroes and villains and gods and rogues, all beginning to move toward the exit that would lead them to Doom's fortress.

"One thing," Rocket said, his voice cutting across the general shuffle. "This only works if we all agree on one thing: none of us are trying to conquer each other while we're doing this. No hidden agendas. No 'I'll help you save the universe but I'm positioning myself to take it afterward.' This works only if we're *actually* allies."

"Agreed," Magneto said simply.

"Agreed," Stark said.

"Agreed," Doom said.

The consensus rippled through the room. Not trust—trust would be impossible. But something more useful: a shared interest in survival that temporarily overrode all competitive instincts.

As they filed toward the exit, Steve Rogers stood with Strange and the Living Tribunal.

"Will this work?" Rogers asked.

Strange closed his eyes. "No," he said finally. "I don't think it will. But it's the only thing that might. The Devourer has never faced opposition before. It's only ever encountered beings it could erase. It's never met an enemy that could protect itself. Maybe that's enough."

The Living Tribunal's faces tilted toward each other—the gesture, among such entities, equivalent to a shrug.

"Unknown," one of the faces said. "For the first time in observable history, we reach toward genuine uncertainty. It is terrifying and somehow... exhilarating."

## THEATRE LEDGER — SECTION III: THE RELUCTANT UNITY

**[HUD: Coalition Status — FORMED | Trust Level — MINIMAL | Commitment — ABSOLUTE | Motivation — SURVIVAL]**

In Doom's sealed fortress, in a chamber that existed partially in Latveria and partially in spaces magic had no name for, the coalition took their positions.

Stark and Doom began laying out infrastructure specifications. Magical binding circles took shape beneath Strange and Loki's combined will. Xavier and Jean began weaving consciousness-networks that would allow real-time coordination across impossible distances. T'Challa started calculating supply routes. Latverian security systems came online, not to keep enemies out, but to keep the allies *in*—locked in accountability.

Rocket looked at Yondu and grinned. "Never thought I'd be working alongside Odin and Doom and still be the least threatening person in the room."

"That's because you're the sanest," Yondu said, his whistle barely audible. "Everyone else in here's got something to prove. You just want to survive and get paid."

"Damn right," Rocket said. "And maybe get home to my family."

In another section, Rias sat with her contingent, watching the work happen. "It won't hold," she said quietly to Akeno. "Not under real pressure."

"No," Akeno agreed. "But it'll last long enough. And that's all we've ever been able to count on—a little bit longer. A few more chances."

Issei stood at the chamber's highest point, looking down at the gathering. His wives surrounded him—Rias below with her demon contingent, Akeno at the psychic coordinates with Xavier, Xenovia at the magical binding points with Strange, Rossweisse at the logistics with T'Challa. Four points of a star, with him at the center.

"Do you think this can work?" Akeno asked through the psychic link Jean had established.

"No," Issei said honestly. "But I think we can survive long enough that something else will. I think we can hold the line long enough for a better solution to present itself. And sometimes, that's all hope actually means."

Below them, the coalition continued its work—enemies turned temporary allies, united against an apocalypse that cared nothing for their differences.

## THEATRE LEDGER — SECTION IV: THE RECKONING

**[HUD: Archive Status — RECORDING PERMITTED | Subject Consent — VERIFIED | Coalition Authority — ACCEPTED | Next Phase — IMMINENT]**

The Theatre had been silent since Issei's confrontation forty chapters ago. It had learned—slowly, painfully—that observation without consent was violation. That spectatorship carried moral weight.

Now it watched as an alliance formed—not out of love, not out of friendship, but out of necessity. The purest form of unity: *we survive or we die together*.

Doom's voice echoed through the sealed chamber: "Defenses are online. We can hold for seventy-two hours minimum. After that... we will need Issei to hold the line."

"I know," Issei said simply.

"The form," Loki said, his usual mischief replaced by something almost serious. "You'll need Apocalypse AnswerArms."

"I know," Issei said again.

"It will burn you out," Strange said. "Weeks of recovery, minimum. Years off your lifespan."

"I know," Issei said a third time. "But that's the choice. That's always been the choice. Everything has a price. This time, the price is measured in years I won't live. And I'm paying it anyway. Because they—" He gestured at his wives, at the assembled coalition, at the impossible alliance humanity and its allies had managed to create, "—are worth it."

In the sealed space of Doom's fortress, the coalition held.

Not with pride.

Not with hope.

But with grim determination and the understanding that the only thing worse than an uncertain survival was a guaranteed death.

The Theatre recorded it all.

And when the gacha notification echoed through the Archive, it was different from before.

It carried weight. Consequence. The knowledge that the next seventy-two hours would determine whether this impossible alliance could actually do the impossible thing it had assembled to attempt.
