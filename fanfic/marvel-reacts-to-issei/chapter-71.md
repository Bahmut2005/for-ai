## THE WEIGHT OF RETURN

The theatre had grown colder after Arc Seven's finale. The observation that Issei could destroy planets—and the cost of that ability—had settled into the room like snow. Seventy chapters in, and the novelty of spectating had worn away. The theatre's tone had shifted from *how powerful is he?* to something quieter and more terrible: *what did it cost him?*

In the wives' block, Rias sat with her hands folded. Behind her, Akeno held Xenovia's hand. Asia's eyes were fixed on the empty stage, her healing light dimly kindled between her palms. Koneko's small frame was rigid, her ears flat. Rossweisse's usual composure had thinned to almost nothing.

They knew what was coming. In the canon that the theatre drew from, they had lived it.

The machine's voice—rarely heard directly—resonated through the chamber:

**SESSION SEVENTY-ONE OF ONE HUNDRED.**

**THE QUESTION NOW BECOMES: WHAT DID YOU SACRIFICE TO BECOME THE STRONGEST?**

**THE FIRST HALF ANSWERED POWER.**

**THE SECOND HALF ANSWERS COST.**

**GACHA PULL INITIATED.**

The stage darkened. No colour wheel. No spinning symbols. This pull came differently—not as random revelation, but as *summoning*. The theatre was drawing deliberately now, reaching into memory, and the wives gripped each other's arms.

The wheel appeared anyway, an afterthought, and landed on gold.

## THE FIRST DEATH

The stage reconstructed a park at dusk. Autumn leaves, the smell of cooling earth, and a teenage boy in an unsupposed high school uniform walking beside a beautiful girl with dark hair and wings that shimmered like oil on water.

Issei—younger than he'd appeared in any vision the theatre had yet shown—was talking. Earnestly talking. Something about how lucky he was, how he'd never expected someone so beautiful to like him back. The theatre recognized his voice in those early words: uncertain, aching, hopeful. The pervert-loudmouth was still there, but muted, almost shy.

Raynare smiled, and the theatre watched him fall for it like watching a boy step onto ice that's about to crack.

"Thank you for doing this," she said sweetly. "For me."

The knife came out of her sleeve like a magician's trick. Not a blade, though—a spear of pure, golden light, materialized from her hand, shaped like a rapier of radiance. The theatre heard Issei's sharp intake of breath. Saw his eyes widen—not with rage, but with confusion. With hurt. With the specific, particular pain of betrayal by someone you believed in.

"Sorry," Raynare said, and she sounded almost sorry—almost professional about it. "You're in the way."

She drove the light-spear through his chest.

The stage didn't cut away. It held on his face—the moment his mind caught up with the fact of it. The knowledge that he had been walked to slaughter. That the person he'd wanted to trust had been waiting the entire time to do this. That no kindness had been real.

Blood fell to the autumn leaves. His knees buckled.

In the wives' block, Rias made a sound—not quite a cry, but close. Akeno gripped the armrest of her seat hard enough that the metal creaked. Asia's healing light blazed and dimmed, blazed and dimmed.

Raynare let him fall. She watched him bleed out on the grass, and the vision held the full arc of it—minutes that felt like hours as the light left his eyes, as his body went cold, as death claimed him with neither mercy nor dignity.

Then the vision cut to Rias.

The camera found her in a basement of some house—the Gremory base, the theatre supplied, unhelpfully. She was cradling Issei's corpse, and her face was a mask of rage and decision. Resolve crystallizing in real time.

"Eight pieces," she whispered. "All of them."

The theatre understood: she was using her entire set of reincarnation Evil Pieces—all eight pawns—to drag him back from death. Mephisto in the villains' block made a small, delighted sound. "She's gambling *everything* on a corpse," he said happily. "That's the sound of someone who sees something."

The light from Rias's magic was golden, warm, and desperate. It lifted Issei's body. The wounds closed. The colour returned to his face. And when he gasped back to life—gasped like a drowning man breaking the surface—the vision showed his first confused, terrified moment of waking: *Where am I? What happened? Am I—?*

Dead and resurrected before he'd truly begun living.

The stage went dark.

## REACTIONS TO PULL ONE

In the heroes' block, Matt Murdock had his face turned away. Steve Rogers was gripping the arms of his seat. Frank Castle was still.

"That's the move," Frank said quietly. "That's the kill-move that every soldier fears. Send them on a date with someone they trust. Then make them understand the betrayal *before* they die."

Logan nodded slowly. "Kid came back wrong. You don't come back the same after learning that lesson at fourteen."

Deadpool was silent for once, which was worse. "Resurrection without consent is just kidnapping," he said. "He got kidnapped back to life because somebody else decided he was valuable. That's not salvation. That's a new kind of debt."

In the cosmic judges' section, one of the elder beings spoke: "The subject died. Resurrected by another's will. He did not choose to return. This is the foundation of his story—not chosen power, but *imposed* obligation."

Rias had not moved. Her eyes were still on the dark stage. Her hands had stopped shaking.

Nightcrawler was wrestling visibly with something. "I've seen the Church justify a lot," he said finally. "But watching an angel play a teenage girl to murder a boy? That's new. Even for me."

## THE SECOND DEATH

The wheel spun again before the reactions had finished settling. Gold to silver, and the stage erupted into a different scene—wind and snow and mountains.

**GACHA PULL TWO OF THREE: "CRITICAL INJURY BY FENRIR" — RARITY: SUPER RARE**

Issei stood on a battlefield, older now—older than the boy who'd died in the park. He was in Scale Mail, that red dragon armor that had first impressed the room seventy chapters ago. But armor doesn't matter much when what you're facing is a god.

Fenrir materialized—not a wolf, but the *concept* of wolf. A creature the size of a mountain, covered in fur like storm clouds, with eyes that had eaten stars. The ancient wolf-god-demon, bound and ancient and *furious*.

When it moved, it was not quick—it was inevitable.

The jaws opened like the earth splitting, and the theatre saw fangs like swords, each one the size of a human body. Issei tried to dodge. His dragon speed should have been enough. Scale Mail should have been armor enough.

Neither mattered.

Fenrir's bite closed on his shoulder and torso, and the theatre heard the *sound*—not screaming, though there was that. The wet, terrible sound of flesh being shredded by force that no defensive magic could parse or redirect. Blood painted the snow in spirals. The armor cracked. The bones beneath the armor *splintered*.

Issei's health bar would have read critical if he'd been wearing a video game. Instead, the theatre simply showed what critical looked like: a teenage god-in-training hanging from the jaws of an ancient beast, watching his own blood freeze before it hit the ground.

The wives were on their feet.

Akeno's lightning ran wild across her seat. Koneko hissed—a sound that didn't belong in a human throat. Rossweisse was summoning ice without meaning to, frost crawling across the theatre's walls. Asia's healing light had become a beacon.

"No—no, he should—" Xenovia couldn't finish the sentence.

On the battlefield, Issei forced power into his form. Transferred energy from the Boosted Gear to his limbs, muscles shredding and reforming under the strain. His wives were there—the vision showed them, Akeno's healing magic pouring into him, Asia channeling everything she had, Rias literally pulling power from her own reserves, pushing it into him like a life transfusion.

Rias spoke through gritted teeth: "Stay with us, Issei. *Stay with us.*"

He shouldn't have survived. The bite wound was catastrophic. The internal damage was apocalyptic. But his wives *would not let him die*, and Ddraig's voice in the Boosted Gear was a roar of refusal:

**[TRANSFER ACCEPTED. PARTNER. BURN. BURN EVERYTHING.]**

Issei burned. The recovered strength came from somewhere primal. Fenrir released him, and he fell to the snow. His teammates caught him before he hit ground.

The vision held the aftermath: Issei's shoulder, where the bite was, festooned with scar tissue that would never fully heal. Pink and white and wrong. A permanent mark of how close he'd come to not coming back.

The theatre's clinical display appeared:

**NEAR-FATAL INJURY. RECOVERY TIME: 47 DAYS. PHYSICAL SCARRING: PERMANENT. PSYCHOLOGICAL IMPACT: SEVERE.**

The stage went dark again.

## REACTIONS TO PULL TWO

Wolverine had his claws out, unsheathed, just sitting there. His jaw was working. He'd taken hits like that. Knew what recovery looked like. Knew what the scar meant—not weakness, but *experience*. A body that had been broken and willed itself back together.

"Forty-seven days," he said to no one. "In a world where his wife can heal with magic, he still took forty-seven days. That wound was *bad*."

Steve Rogers was watching the wives' block. "His people saved him," he said quietly. "Not his power. His people. That's a different kind of strength."

In the cosmic judges' section, a being of pure mathematics spoke: "The subject has now died once and nearly-died once. Each resurrection marked an *escalation*—a crossing of thresholds. He is becoming something that cannot be easily ended by conventional means. This is not weakness. This is selection."

Frank Castle had moved. He was now standing next to Daredevil, and when Matt turned to look at him, Frank was studying the scarred warrior in the stage's afterimage.

"That scar is going to remind him every time he looks in a mirror," Frank said. "Every morning shower. Every time one of his wives sees him. That's a weight they carry too."

## THE THIRD DEATH

The wheel did not hesitate.

**GACHA PULL THREE OF THREE: "POISONING BY SAMUEL" — RARITY: RARE**

A different scene. A garden courtyard, somewhere cultured and carefully maintained. Issei older still—this was later canon, the outline whispered to those who'd studied it. He was meeting with an enemy. Not in Scale Mail this time. Civilian clothes. Playing politics, the way a High-Class Devil sometimes had to.

The poison came in tea.

Samuel—the detail appeared in subtle letters at the bottom of the stage—was an older demon, half-human, skilled in alchemies that didn't quite fit any magic the theatre had studied before. He'd offered Issei a drink as a gesture of parley.

Issei had accepted, because he'd been training himself to trust, to believe in covenants and promises.

The poison didn't burn. It *spread*.

The vision showed it at the cellular level—toxins spreading through his bloodstream like ink in water. His healing factor fighting it, desperately, burning its own energy to neutralize the compounds faster than they could propagate. But this poison had been *designed* for this. Designed by someone who'd studied his physiology. Designed to be faster than his regeneration.

Issei's body convulsed. The theatre held on his face in real time as pain registered—not clean pain, not sword-pain, but the invasive, total pain of his own body turning against him.

He collapsed.

His wives were there—they were always there, it seemed, which was both mercy and tragedy. Akeno's healing magic was frantic, desperate. Koneko was brewing something in her hands, some kind of antidote by instinct. Asia was praying and channeling and crying. Xenovia was holding him down because sometimes a dying man thrashes and has to be held.

Rias was calculating, her mind working through every countermeasure in the archives. "Identify the poison," she was saying, even though she was crying. "Identify it, we can counter it, we can *fix* this."

The poison spread for what felt like hours. The tissue in his digestive tract started to degrade. His organs were receiving the message: *accept this foreign substance or die resisting*. Issei chose to die resisting.

His healing factor pushed back. Burned calories he didn't have. Burned reserves of life force he couldn't spare. But it worked—slowly, agonizingly, incrementally, it worked.

He survived.

The theatre's notation appeared:

**INSIDIOUS POISONING. RECOVERY TIME: FOUR WEEKS. LINGERING WEAKNESS: PERMANENT. SECONDARY DAMAGE: CELLULAR DEGRADATION IN GASTROINTESTINAL SYSTEM. HE WILL NEVER EAT WITHOUT FEAR AGAIN.**

The stage went dark.

## REACTIONS TO PULL THREE

The wives were no longer trying to hide tears. Rias had her face in her hands. Akeno was holding her. Asia was making sounds that didn't need translation—just grief.

In the antiheroes' section, Daredevil was the first to speak. "He's been poisoned. Poisoned *after* everything else. And now his own body is an enemy. You can't trust food anymore when you've learned that lesson."

Natasha Romanoff understood this in her bones. "Slow kill. Assassin's choice. It doesn't kill you fast—it makes you afraid of living slowly. He'll choke on every meal thinking this could be it."

Deadpool was quiet still, which had become his default in Arc Eight. "Three deaths," he said finally. "Not resurrection story, not transformation story. The story of a guy who keeps *almost* dying and whose body keeps saying no and keeps saying no and he keeps—" He didn't finish.

Bucky Barnes understood. "He keeps choosing to wake up."

In the cosmic judges' section, the mathematics-being spoke again: "The subject has now approached death three times by three methods: murder, wound, and poison. He does not die because something in him *refuses*. This is not explained by luck. This is a structural property of his existence. He has been selected to survive. The question is: selected by whom? Selected for what?"

## THE THEATRE'S SUMMATION

The stage remained dark as the message appeared, white letters burning slowly into being:

**SUBJECT HAS SURVIVED THREE FORMS OF DEATH: SIMPLE MURDER. CATASTROPHIC WOUND. INSIDIOUS POISON. EACH RESURRECTION MARKED A THRESHOLD. EACH COST: PHYSICAL, PSYCHOLOGICAL, AND CIRCUMSTANTIAL.**

**THE FIRST DEATH WAS CHOSEN FOR HIM. HE DID NOT CONSENT TO RESURRECTION.**

**THE SECOND DEATH WAS NARROWLY AVOIDED THROUGH THE INTERVENTION OF THOSE WHO LOVED HIM.**

**THE THIRD DEATH WAS SURVIVED BY DEPLETING RESERVES HE CANNOT REPLACE.**

Another message appeared below it:

**OBSERVATION: IN SEVENTY CHAPTERS, THE SUBJECT HAS BEEN SHOWN BECOMING STRONGER. IN THIS CHAPTER, THE TRUTH IS REVEALED: HE DID NOT BECOME STRONGER. HE LEARNED TO DIE BETTER. HE LEARNED TO SURVIVE BETTER. THE POWER CAME AFTER—NOT BEFORE—EACH LESSON IN MORTALITY.**

**THIS IS THE COST OF BECOMING THE STRONGEST.**

**NOT GIFT. NOT DESTINY. SURVIVAL. REPEATED SURVIVAL. MOUNTING DEBTS CALLED RESURRECTION.**

A third message, in red text:

**NEXT CHAPTER: "THE SMILE AFTER TRAUMA." THE ROOM WILL LEARN HOW ONE SURVIVES WHEN SURVIVAL HAS BECOME A SCAR.**

The lights went down on the wives' section, where Rias was holding her servants and Issei's other partners, and where the weight of those three near-deaths hung like a fog none of them could name.

## THEATRE LEDGER

**New Pulls This Chapter:** *Death by Raynare* (SSR—first scene, Issei's murder by the fallen angel and his nonconsensual resurrection by Rias using all eight pawn pieces); *Critical Injury by Fenrir* (Super Rare—Fenrir's catastrophic bite, survived only through combined spousal magical intervention, resulting in permanent scarring and 47-day recovery); *Poisoning by Samuel* (Rare—later-canon alchemical assassination attempt, survived through Issei's healing factor at the cost of cellular degradation and permanent fear of eating).

**Secrets Revealed:** (1) Issei's resurrection was not his choice—Rias made that decision unilaterally. He woke from death as an obligation, not a gift. (2) His wives' magical support is not incidental to his survival; it is *essential*—without Akeno's healing, Asia's power, and Rias's reserves during Fenrir's attack, he would not have lived. (3) In later canon, even his elevated healing factor has limits; poisoning designed for him can degrade his organs and force him into a state of permanent vulnerability. (4) Survival is not the same as strength. He survives because something in him refuses death, not because he is powerful enough to avoid it.

**Alliance / Opinion Shifts:** The wives' bond is no longer abstract to the theatre—it is now understood as *structural*. They are not romantically attached to him; they are necessary to his survival. Antiheroes (Daredevil, Natasha, Deadpool, Bucky) have shifted from seeing him as a superpowered anomaly to seeing him as a trauma survivor. Cosmic judges are reconsidering their assessment: Issei's continued existence may indicate selection by forces unknown, not random luck. Frank Castle and Logan have privately recognized in him a kindred pattern: a body that has learned to live with damage. Nightcrawler's faith is being tested by the sight of angelic cruelty. In the wives' block, the emotional weight has shifted from pride in Issei's power to terror at how close they came to losing him multiple times.

**Unresolved Hooks:** (1) Arc Eight is now framed not as "the cost of power" but as "the cost of survival." Each remaining chapter in this arc will explore how one lives after learning you can be murdered, catastrophically wounded, and poisoned—and that you might face these things again. (2) Raynare's motivation and current status remain unresolved; Frank Castle and Wolverine have developed an off-screen vendetta. (3) The Fenrir attack raises questions: how did he recover so quickly? Who authorized this battle? What triggered it? (4) Samuel and the alchemical poisoning suggest a network of enemies still hunting Issei, but with more sophisticated methods. (5) The statement "HE WILL NEVER EAT WITHOUT FEAR AGAIN" suggests psychological scars that even healing magic cannot address. (6) The cosmic judges' inference—"selected by whom? Selected for what?"—reopens the thread of the theatre's own selection criteria and suggests the gacha pulls are not random, but curated. Chapter 72 ("The Smile After Trauma") will show how Issei copes with this weight—specifically, through humor and performance, with private breakdowns hidden from public view.
