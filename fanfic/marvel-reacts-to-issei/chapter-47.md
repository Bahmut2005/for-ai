# Chapter Forty-Seven: Nebula's Compatibility Roll

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Guardians of the Galaxy (Star-Lord, Gamora, Nebula, Rocket, Drax, Groot, Mantis) • Ravagers (Yondu, Kraglin, Stakar Ogord & assorted captains) • Villains (Thanos, Mephisto, Loki, Doom)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Compatibility Analysis for Guardians" — **Rare**
2. "Nebula's Unexpected High Score" — **Secret**

---

By the seventh night of Arc Five, the theatre had a gambling problem.

It had started, as most institutional decay does, with Yondu Udonta. The machine had made the Ravagers its operators for the arc — a decision the room's scholars still could not explain and the machine still refused to — and within an hour of taking the console the Ravagers had done to the cosmic theatre what Ravagers do to everything: unionized it, insulted it, and opened a book on it. A rust-red auxiliary scoreboard now hung beneath the great screen like a barnacle on a cathedral, tracking side-bets in units, teeth, and in one distressing column, *favors owed to Kraglin*. The machine had labeled the scoreboard **UNAUTHORIZED** on the first night, **TOLERATED** on the third, and had, on the fifth, quietly begun correcting its odds.

The crack in the upper right quadrant had not spread since the arc began. It had not healed, either. It sat there through pirate codes and treasure planets and one prophecy Ddraig still refused to translate, ticking very occasionally, like a patient creditor.

Tonight the console chimed under Kraglin Obfonteri's hand, and Kraglin — acting operator by virtue of losing a coin toss he was starting to suspect had used two-headed currency — read the banner aloud in the tone of a man defusing something.

**GACHA PULL: 1 OF 2.**

**"COMPATIBILITY ANALYSIS FOR GUARDIANS" — RARITY: RARE.**

**QUERY: OF THE SO-CALLED GUARDIANS OF THE GALAXY, WHOM WOULD ISSEI HYOUDOU COME TO TRUST? WHOM WOULD HE BE WISE NOT TO?**

**TRANSLATION FOR RAVAGER OPERATORS: WHICH OF 'EM WOULD SELL HIM, AND FOR HOW MUCH.**

"Now *that's* a pull," said Yondu Udonta, boots already off the seat in front of him, fin catching the screen-light. "Machine's finally speakin' the language."

"It is a betrayal audit," said Loki from the villains' dark, with the reverence he otherwise reserved for himself. "Forty-six chapters of feelings, and at last — a *statistic*."

"Book's open!" bawled a Ravager captain from the cheap seats, and the scoreboard lit like a slot machine paying out. Odds scrolled: the flame-haired boy's trust, handicapped runner by runner. Quill drew middling numbers. Rocket drew insulting ones. Drax drew a symbol the scoreboard used for *don't ask*.

Peter Quill stood up with the wounded dignity of a man about to make things worse. "Okay, first of all, we don't need a machine to tell us we're trustworthy—"

**SUBJECT: PETER JASON QUILL,** printed the screen, and the theatre settled in with the particular joy of an audience watching a friend's mail get read aloud.

The analysis was not a vision so much as a *dossier assembling itself* — footage of Issei the room already knew, spliced against footage of the Guardians the room knew better, two lives held up to the light until the grain aligned. Issei charging a god with a countdown on his chest; Quill charging Ronan with a dance-off. Issei taking a mortal wound for a girl who healed strangers; Quill taking a mortal storm for a tree.

**COMPATIBILITY: HIGH,** the machine allowed. **TWO ORPHANED IDEALISTS WITH DELUSIONS OF LEADERSHIP AND VERIFIED WILLINGNESS TO DIE FOR THE TEAM. PROJECTED RELATIONSHIP: IMMEDIATE MUTUAL RECOGNITION, FOLLOWED BY A PERMANENT ARGUMENT OVER WHO IS THE PROTAGONIST.**

**BETRAYAL RISK: NONE. INCOMPETENCE RISK: CONTINUOUS.**

"I'll take it," Quill said, over the Ravager section's howling.

**SUBJECT: ROCKET.** Footage: the Chapter Forty-Three simulation, Rocket's ninety-first contingency plan dying to a dragon impersonating a security AI. **COMPATIBILITY: HIGH, AGAINST THE SUBJECT'S OWN STATED PREFERENCES. BETRAYAL RISK: PETTY THEFT ONLY, NEVER TREASON. ANALYSIS: THE SUBJECT WOULD STEAL ISSEI HYOUDOU'S POSSESSIONS AND DIE BEFORE SELLING HIS LOCATION. ISSEI HYOUDOU WOULD NOTICE THE THEFT, LAUGH, AND ASK HIM TO STAY FOR DINNER. THE SUBJECT WOULD FIND THIS UNBEARABLE. HE WOULD STAY.**

Rocket, who had opened his mouth to object at *petty theft*, closed it at *he would stay*, and became very interested in a bolt on his armrest.

**SUBJECT: DRAX THE DESTROYER. COMPATIBILITY: HIGH. TWO BEINGS INCAPABLE OF DECEPTION FOR STRUCTURAL REASONS. PROJECTED RELATIONSHIP: PENDING. SEE NEXT CHAPTER.**

"See next — is it *advertising* at us now?" said Sam Wilson, somewhere in the mid-tiers.

Drax stood, gravely pleased. "The machine promises me a chapter. I will not sit down until I receive it." He remained standing for the rest of the night. No one argued. Arguing with Drax about literalism was a war with no veterans.

**SUBJECT: GROOT. COMPATIBILITY: MAXIMUM. ENDORSEMENT ON FILE FROM THE WELSH DRAGON. NO FURTHER ANALYSIS WILL BE OFFERED, AS NONE WOULD SURVIVE TRANSLATION.**

"I am Groot," said Groot, satisfied.

"**He is,**" agreed Ddraig, from wherever Ddraig's voice lived, and the cosmic linguists' section quietly tore up another week of research.

**SUBJECT: MANTIS. COMPATIBILITY: HIGH. WARNING APPENDED: EMPATHIC CONTACT WITH SUBJECT ISSEI HYOUDOU'S SURFACE THOUGHTS IS NOT RECOMMENDED BEFORE LUNCH.** Mantis, who had once touched Thor's forehead and announced his grief to a room, giggled — then read further and went faintly pink on behalf of a boy she had never met.

**SUBJECT: GAMORA. COMPATIBILITY: HIGH. THE DEADLIEST WOMAN IN THE GALAXY AND A BOY WHO STRUCTURALLY REFUSES TO GIVE UP ON PEOPLE. PROJECTED RELATIONSHIP: SHE WOULD TEST HIM FIRST. HE WOULD PASS FOR THE STUPIDEST POSSIBLE REASON, WHICH IS THAT HE WOULD NOT NOTICE HE WAS BEING TESTED.**

Gamora's mouth did the thing that on anyone else would be a smile. "Accurate," she said.

And then the dossier stopped assembling, and the theatre noticed what the scoreboard had noticed thirty seconds earlier — the odds column beside one name had frozen, mid-calculation, and the name was the one sitting at the end of the Guardians' row with her arms crossed and her chin up like a fixed bayonet.

"Skip it," said Nebula.

The machine did not skip it.

---

**SUBJECT: NEBULA,** the screen printed, and then, instead of a dossier: a delay.

The theatre had learned the machine's silences the way sailors learn weather. There was the silence of contempt, the silence of secrets, and — rarest — the silence of a thing double-checking its own arithmetic because it did not expect to be believed.

"Get on with it," Nebula said, to the screen, evenly. She had come to this arc braced. The room knew her file; the room had *seen* her file, in fragments, across a dozen reaction shots — the daughter Thanos rebuilt one confiscated piece at a time, the sister who lost every sparring match and paid for each loss in flesh and machinery, the traitor who sold out Ronan, sold out the Guardians, sold out anyone, until the day she didn't. She had watched the betrayal audit crawl toward her seat all night with the calm of a woman who had already written the verdict herself. *Betrayal risk: total. Compatibility: none. Projected relationship: he should shoot first.*

"Twenty units says traitor-class," someone offered from the Ravager tiers, not quietly enough.

"Fifty says the machine don't even score her," said someone else. "Machine's got *standards*—"

The whistle was very soft, and the arrow's glow was very red, and it hovered outside the second speaker's ear at a courteous distance for exactly as long as it took the Ravager section to remember its manners. Yondu didn't look away from the screen. "Board's closed on this one," he said, mild as milk.

**GACHA PULL: 2 OF 2,** said the screen.

**"NEBULA'S UNEXPECTED HIGH SCORE" — RARITY: SECRET.**

The theatre stopped breathing. Secrets were never comedy. Secrets were the machine opening a vein.

And the vision, when it came, was not of Nebula at all.

It was Issei — a triptych of him, three memories the room had already lived through in earlier arcs, replayed now with new captions, like evidence resubmitted under a different charge. A black-haired nekomata in a purple kimono, an SS-class criminal who had fought his family and fled her sins, standing awkwardly at his family's dinner table while he pushed a bowl toward her: **KUROKA. FORMER ENEMY. CURRENT FAMILY.** A white-haired rival descending on wings of light that had tried, more than once, to kill him — and Issei grinning up at him like a man greeting weather: **VALI LUCIFER. PERPETUAL RIVAL. TRUSTED AT HIS BACK IN EVERY WAR SINCE.** A man with a spear that could kill gods and had very nearly killed *him*, hollowed and remade by his own defeats, and Issei's voice, off-screen, unbothered: *"If he's trying to be better, then that's that, right?"* **CAO CAO. THE MAN WHO NEARLY ENDED HIM. FORGIVEN.**

**OBSERVE THE PATTERN,** said the machine. **THE SUBJECT OF THIS THEATRE DOES NOT TRUST PEOPLE FOR THEIR RECORDS. HE TRUSTS THEM FOR THEIR DIRECTION.**

**NOW OBSERVE THE ANALYSAND.**

And there was Nebula's file after all — but cut the way no one had ever cut it. Not the ledger of betrayals. The *other* ledger. Nebula putting herself in the torture rig in her sister's place. Nebula flying alone into Thanos's ship with nothing but rage and a plan made of grief. Nebula, in a ruined future the room had watched with their hands over their mouths, holding an orange stone out to strangers because the person she had been would have kept it. Every clip captioned with the same word, stamped like an auditor's chop: **CHOSEN. CHOSEN. CHOSEN.**

**SUBJECT: NEBULA. DAUGHTER OF THANOS. REBUILT AS A WEAPON BY A FATHER WHO GRADED HIS CHILDREN AGAINST EACH OTHER.**

**COMPATIBILITY WITH ISSEI HYOUDOU: NINETY-FOUR PERCENT. HIGHEST OF ALL GUARDIANS ANALYZED.**

The number hung there, patient, while the theatre made the sound of two thousand assumptions failing at once.

**BETRAYAL RISK,** the screen continued, and here it did the thing it did perhaps once an arc, dropping the auditor's voice for something older and quieter, **REQUIRES CLARIFICATION. THE ANALYSAND HAS ALREADY COMMITTED THE ONLY BETRAYAL THAT INTERESTS HER. SHE BETRAYED THE PERSON SHE WAS BUILT TO BE. THE QUOTA IS SPENT.**

**HE WOULD SEE THAT IN HER BEFORE SHE SAID A WORD. HE HAS PRACTICE. HALF THE PEOPLE AT HIS DINNER TABLE HAVE HER FILE.**

**PROJECTED RELATIONSHIP: HE WOULD TRUST HER WITH HIS LIFE. SHE WOULD FIND THIS INFURIATING. IT WOULD BE THE FIRST TRUST SHE NEVER HAD TO EARN IN COMBAT, AND SHE WOULD SPEND YEARS TRYING TO DESERVE IT ANYWAY. THAT IS THE COMPATIBILITY. THAT IS THE WHOLE SCORE.**

Nebula sat entirely still. The theatre — which had watched her take a disintegrating beam to the face in one vision without blinking — watched her stare at a number, and blink.

"That's wrong," she said. Her voice was level. Her hand, on the armrest, was not. "Run it again."

"It isn't wrong." Gamora had turned in her seat, and she was not looking at the screen; she was looking at her sister with the expression of someone who has been holding a translation for years, waiting for the original to be published. "Nebula. Every person that boy loves most, he met at their worst. The cat ran from her crimes. The rival was raised by a monster. The man with the spear put a hole in him. He doesn't keep score the way Father kept score — wins, losses, parts replaced." The old words, deliberately, turned in her hands like a blade being reversed and offered hilt-first. "He counts direction. And nobody in this room — *nobody* — has turned harder against what they were made to be than you."

"I sold you," Nebula said. "More than once."

"And then you crossed a galaxy for me more than once. The machine watched both." Gamora's voice went dry. "Apparently it can do arithmetic."

"I am Groot," said Groot, gently.

"Yeah," said Rocket, thick, not looking at anyone. "What he said."

High in the villains' dark, Thanos had not moved for the length of the entire pull. The Mad Titan watched the number — ninety-four, higher than the assassin he'd perfected, higher than all of them, pinned to the daughter he had taken apart most often — and the theatre nearest him would later swear the great chin lifted, just slightly, as though absorbing a blow from an unexpected quarter. "I made her a blade," he said at last, to no one who had asked. "Every piece I replaced was meant to remove the part that hesitated." A pause of geological length. "The boy's machine scores the hesitation highest. Interesting. Wrong — but interesting."

"It is not wrong, Titan; it is *pricing*," said Mephisto, lounging three seats down with his fingers steepled and his eyes gleaming like a market opening. "Redemption is the single scarcest commodity in every realm I hold paper on — nearly no one genuinely wants it; they want *absolution*, which is cheaper. She wants the real article. The dragon-boy stockpiles the real article. Of course the exchange rate is obscene." He sighed happily. "Ninety-four percent. I couldn't get terms like that with a *contract*."

"You couldn't get terms like that with a *soul*," said Doom, who had been taking notes again and pretending otherwise.

---

The scoreboard, which had been holding its breath along with everyone else, remembered it was a scoreboard.

**SIDE-BET LEDGER — UPDATING,** it announced, and the rust-red columns spun. Half the Ravager section had lost its shirts betting *traitor-class*; the units flowed in a great migratory river toward the three individuals who had bet otherwise. The theatre craned to see the winners' column: **GAMORA. GROOT. Y. UDONTA.**

Quill turned around slowly. "You bet on Nebula?"

"Bet on the *machine*," Yondu said, with the serenity of a man counting money. "Thing's been runnin' the same con for forty-six nights — shows you a body everyone's given up on, then shows you the boy not givin' up. Warn't gonna suddenly change genre." He jerked his chin at the console. "'Sides. I got history with the category. Folks the galaxy scores wrong." He did not look at Kraglin when he said it, which was how everyone knew it was about Kraglin, and Quill, and himself, in roughly that order.

Then, because sincerity had a strict half-life in the Ravager tiers: "Machine! Double or nothin'. Run my first mate."

"Cap'n—" Kraglin began, alarmed.

**SUBJECT: KRAGLIN OBFONTERI,** the machine printed, with what the room could only describe as relish. **NOT A GUARDIAN. ANALYZED ANYWAY, AS THE OPERATOR'S HAND IS STILL ON THE CONSOLE AND THE MACHINE FINDS HIM SOOTHING.**

**COMPATIBILITY: NINETY-ONE PERCENT. ANALYSIS: LOYALTY THAT SURVIVED ONE MUTINY, ONE REGIME CHANGE, AND YEARS OF BEING THE ONLY ADULT ON A SHIP OF FERAL CHILDREN. ISSEI HYOUDOU'S HOUSEHOLD CONTAINS FOURTEEN WOMEN, A DRAGON, ASSORTED GODS, AND NO ADULTS WHATSOEVER. HE WOULD TRUST THIS MAN INSIDE A WEEK AND PUT HIM IN CHARGE OF LOGISTICS INSIDE TWO.**

**BETRAYAL RISK: HE FEELS GUILTY BORROWING TOOLS.**

The Ravager section detonated. Kraglin went a blotchy, delighted crimson and mumbled something about the arrow incident that the machine, mercifully, declined to caption. Yondu collected his second payout of the night and put his boots back up, wearing the smug of a man whose entire parenting philosophy had just been peer-reviewed.

Stakar Ogord, who had watched the whole night from the captains' row with his arms folded, spoke for the first time. "Kid scores exiles high," he said, to Yondu, quietly, and something decades old passed between them. "Whole roster of 'em. The cat. The rival. Now her." He nodded once at Nebula, a captain's nod, the kind that had once meant *you fly with us*. "Machine keeps this up, gonna run out of people to give up on."

**THAT IS THE INTENTION,** said the screen, which was not supposed to answer, and the silence after it had teeth.

Nebula was still looking at the number. Mantis, two seats away, had been vibrating with restraint for ten full minutes; Nebula, without turning her head, said, "If you touch me, I will remove the hand," and then — a hesitation of nearly a full second, the longest the theatre had ever seen her spend on anything — "…later. You can do it later."

Mantis beamed like a sunrise.

High above, in the upper right quadrant, the crack ticked once — soft, mineral, patient — and the frozen light along its seams shimmered with that faint static-edged wrongness the room had learned not to discuss after midnight. The machine dimmed the scoreboard, filed the night's ledger, and printed its closing banner over the fading red columns:

**COMPATIBILITY ANALYSIS: COMPLETE. ONE SUBJECT REMAINS UNSCORED.**

**THE DESTROYER HAS REQUESTED A CHAPTER. THE MACHINE PAYS ITS DEBTS.**

**NEXT: SIMULATION — DRAX VERSUS THE RESTRICTED DRAGON.**

Drax, still standing, cracked his knuckles with the sound of a small avalanche, and smiled at the screen the way other men smile at old friends. "Finally," he said. "I will make an excellent chapter."
