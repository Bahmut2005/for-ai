## OPENING: THE ARCHIVE

The theatre's walls had shifted again. What had been the living room of a New York penthouse now resembled something between a library and a cosmic surveillance center. Banks of monitors cascaded down the walls—each one displaying a different timeline, a different thread of reality. The TVA had requested this configuration, and the theatre, in its mysterious way, had obliged.

Kang stood at the center of the room, arms crossed, his robes crackling with temporal energy. His variant form was sharp and predatory—all angular features and barely restrained impatience. Beside him, Immortus maintained a more subdued presence, watching everything with the careful eye of someone who had lived too long to be surprised by anything.

The TVA agents flanked the far wall, their formal posture betraying genuine concern. This was not standard procedure. The TVA did not attend gacha pulls from reaction theatres—theatres existed outside their jurisdiction, in narrative spaces that pre-existed their own temporal authority.

Yet here they were.

"You're certain this is accurate?" Kang demanded, gesturing at the primary screen.

"The timeline data is comprehensive," one of the TVA agents replied carefully. "Though... there are anomalies."

"Anomalies?" Kang's tone suggested this was an insult to his person.

"Yes, sir. Approximately 23% of recorded events involving the subject exist outside standard chronological markers. We're... struggling to locate them in the main timeline."

Victor Von Doom materialized at the back of the room, summoned by some instinct of entertainment. Strange had arrived via portal moments before, looking distinctly uncomfortable about the whole affair. Loki had invited himself and was currently perched on an armchair, pretending to read a book from the theatre's infinite library but actually watching everything with dangerous amusement.

The theatre's opening sequence began, and the screens flared to life.

## GACHA PULL 1: "TIMELINE ANALYSIS OF ISSEI'S EVENTS"

**RARITY:** Rare  
**PULL TIME:** 14:32 (Theatre Standard)  
**CLASSIFICATION:** Chronological Investigation

The screens erupted with data—cascading timelines, probability threads, branching possibilities. The TVA prided itself on seeing everything, cataloging everything, pruning anything that threatened the "sacred timeline." Their surveillance was absolute. Their control was complete.

Or so they believed.

The first wave of data confirmed what they already knew. **Timeline 19,847-Prime (Issei's Primary Sequence):**

*Birth—October 22, 1994 (subjective Marvel dating system). Kuoh Academy. Raynare's betrayal. Death by spear thrust. Resurrection by Rias Gremory. The familiar sequence of DxD events integrated into Marvel's multiverse.*

The TVA agents nodded. Expected. Documented. Safe.

But then the screens shifted.

**TERTIARY TIMELINE BRANCHES:** Events that existed *outside* the main sequence. Events that had no anchor points in Marvel's official chronology.

*The Celestial encounter—Chapter 67. Where was it located? Not in Kuoh. Not in Japan. Not in any mapped Marvel coordinate. The timestamps didn't match. The spatial markers were... absent.*

"That's impossible," one agent said flatly.

Kang leaned closer to the screen, his temporal robes fluttering. "What am I looking at?"

"Pruned events," Immortus said quietly. "Or rather—events *from* pruned timelines that somehow persist in the gacha's records."

The screens showed more. A battle with cosmic entities. Issei, somehow, exchanging words with something that predated the Marvel Timeline itself. Encounters with beings whose designation files were classified even from the TVA's view. These events had *markers*—they were real memories, real experiences—but they existed in branches that the TVA had cut away.

Cut away. Deleted. Unmade.

Yet they remained in the theatre's database.

"How?" Kang's voice was controlled, but the temporal energy around him crackled with agitation. "How are records of pruned timelines accessible? Those timelines *don't exist*. They were erased."

"Not erased," the theatre's HUD whispered, almost conversationally. "Archived. There is a difference."

The TVA agents exchanged glances. The theatre was... responding to them? That wasn't supposed to happen.

"The boy's timeline," Immortus observed, "has connections beyond your jurisdiction, Kang. Perhaps beyond anyone's jurisdiction. The pruned branches—the theatre preserved their memories even as the timelines themselves were unmade."

"This suggests," Strange said, his voice carrying the weight of mystic certainty, "that Issei's experiences aren't confined to a single timeline. Or perhaps to Marvel's timeline at all. He may have traveled between branches. Moved through pruned sequences."

"Impossible," Kang snapped. "No one travels through pruned branches. There's nothing *there* to travel through."

"Clearly," Doom said from the shadows, his tone dripping with amusement, "someone did not brief you on the full parameters of your 'sacred timeline.'"

"The TVA," Immortus continued, a hint of something like sympathy in his ageless voice, "believes itself omniscient. Yet the theatre holds records of events you cannot see. Events in branches you cut away. The boy's existence spans more than you understand."

Kang's jaw tightened. The TVA agents began backing away from him slightly—a universal sign that temporal authority was about to express itself through violence or temporal manipulation or both.

"Mark these branches for investigation," Kang ordered. "Full resource allocation. I want access to every aspect of—"

The screens flickered. Text appeared, clinical and absolute:

**ACCESS DENIED: INSUFFICIENT CLEARANCE.**

A pause. Kang stared at the message as if it had personally insulted his entire being.

"Run that again," he said. "Authorization code Kang-Temporal-Prime."

**ACCESS DENIED: TIMELINE VARIANCE CLASSIFIED.**

"Classified?" Kang's robes were actively smoking now. "I am Kang. I am the TVA's temporal authority. Nothing is classified from—"

**ACCESS DENIED: THIS ISN'T EVEN YOUR JURISDICTION.**

Loki began to smile. A small smile, but genuine.

Immortus, to his credit, said nothing. But his expression suggested he had been expecting this for quite some time.

## GACHA PULL 2: "KANG'S BLOCKED FILES"

**RARITY:** Comedy-Class  
**PULL TIME:** 14:33 (Theatre Standard, +1 minute)  
**CLASSIFICATION:** Bureaucratic Frustration (Feat. Temporal Authority)

The second gacha pull was, the theatre had clearly decided, going to be *entertaining*.

Kang attempted diplomatic channels first. His version of diplomacy, which meant demanding information with the assumption it would be provided. When the theatre's response was again a flat denial, he shifted tactics.

The screens displayed his attempts like a comedy sequence—each one more desperate than the last.

**14:33:04 — BRUTE FORCE ACCESS ATTEMPT**

*Kang's consciousness lashing out through the theatre's data streams, attempting to force his way through encrypted files, timeline locks, narrative safeguards.*

**ACCESS DENIED: STOP ASKING. IT'S PATHETIC.**

Strange actually laughed. A short, sharp sound that echoed through the theatre.

**14:33:23 — DIPLOMATIC REQUEST**

*Kang, maintaining his composure, formally petitioning the theatre for access to classified timeline data pertaining to Subject Issei Hyoudou.*

The theatre's response came back almost immediately:

**ACCESS DENIED: WE'RE BUSY. HAVE PATIENCE.**

**14:33:41 — BARGAINING PROTOCOL**

*Kang offering temporal resources, reality-stone fragments, promises of favorable timeline treatment in exchange for data access.*

**ACCESS DENIED: YOUR CURRENCY HAS NO VALUE HERE.**

Loki was openly grinning now. Even Immortus's stone-faced expression seemed to be fighting against something that might have been amusement.

"The theatre is mocking him," Immortus said to no one in particular.

Doom moved closer to the screens, genuinely fascinated. "I have not seen anyone reduce Kang to this level of... what is the word? Desperation?"

**14:34:09 — FINAL TEMPORAL AUTHORITY ASSERTION**

*Kang, all pretense of civility abandoned, asserting his full temporal dominion. Every code, every override, every quantum probability key at his disposal. The TVA standing behind him, their own authority lending weight to his demand.*

The theatre seemed to pause. The screens went dark for a moment—a moment that stretched long enough that even Immortus appeared uncertain.

Then, the response came. All caps. Indisputable. Final.

**NO. KANG VARIANT—REJECTED. KINDLY STOP BOTHERING US. WE HAVE 100 CHAPTERS TO GET THROUGH.**

## THE MOMENT OF REALIZATION

The silence that followed was absolute. Complete. The kind of silence that occurs when the fundamental nature of reality has just been questioned and found wanting.

Kang didn't move. His robes, which had been crackling with temporal energy, went completely still. His eyes, which had been flashing with the certainty of one who commands time itself, lost focus.

"The theatre," he said quietly, "just told me to stop."

"Yes," Immortus confirmed.

"The theatre... just referenced..."

"A 100-chapter narrative structure," Strange finished, his voice carrying equal parts horror and fascination. "The theatre isn't just displaying events, Kang. The theatre is *aware* of its own structure. Its own purpose. It knows this is Chapter 69 of 100."

Doom began to laugh. Not a cruel laugh—though that would have been in character—but a laugh of genuine respect. "The theatre has personality. It has exhaustion. It is *tired* of your interference, Kang. And it is not afraid to say so."

Loki was on his feet now, eyes bright. "The theatre is *sassy*. It has a sense of humor. It broke the fourth wall and used it as a weapon."

The TVA agents had actually backed away from Kang entirely now, recognizing that their leader was experiencing something approaching an existential crisis. Their control over the multiverse—absolute, omniscient, unquestionable—was not nearly as absolute as they believed.

"The theatre," Strange said carefully, "is not merely displaying information. It is *editing* information. Choosing what to reveal, what to withhold, what to mock. And it just told Kang—the man who believes himself master of all timelines—to stop bothering it."

"It's condescending," Kang breathed. "A theatre. A narrative space. Is *condescending* toward me."

Immortus placed a hand on Kang's shoulder. An ancient gesture from an ancient being. "Perhaps that is the lesson, variant. The theatre exists outside your authority. Outside anyone's authority. It preserves what it wishes, discards what it wishes, and judges what it wishes. And it has clearly judged you... insufficient."

Doom was still laughing, a sound of pure appreciation. "This is magnificent. Kang the Conqueror, humiliated by a theatre with attitude. I will remember this moment with great fondness."

The screens flared once more—this time with something almost like an emoji in the old Earth style. A little face, sticking its tongue out.

Kang's temporal authority surged. His robes flamed. Reality around him began to crack with the force of his indignation.

And then he simply... stopped. Composed himself. Forced the energy back within.

"Note this," he said to the TVA agents. "Note every moment of this interaction. The theatre has declared itself beyond our jurisdiction. It has mocked our authority. It has blocked access to critical timeline data pertaining to Subject Issei Hyoudou."

"And?" one of the agents asked carefully.

"And," Kang said, "we return. Eventually. But not today. Not to this theatre. This theatre requires... a different approach."

He turned to leave. The TVA agents followed, maintaining a respectful distance.

Immortus remained behind, looking at the screens with something approaching satisfaction.

Strange had already begun constructing a portal. His expression suggested he would need to meditate on what had just occurred for several hours.

Loki was looking at the theatre's empty screens with genuine affection. "I like you," he said to the space. "You're chaotic. You remind me of me."

The screens flickered one final time:

**NOTED. RECOMMENDATION: THERAPY FOR KANG. AVAILABLE ON CHAPTER 45, SUBSECTION B.**

Loki burst out laughing.

How had he traveled through branches that no longer existed? And what events remained in those archived memories—events so critical that even the theatre itself had classified them?

The answers would require a different approach. A partnership. An alliance that crossed dimensional lines and power hierarchies.

Enter Apocalypse and the AnswerArms—Arc 7's final convergence.

The theatre was ready. Kang would have to learn readiness as well.
