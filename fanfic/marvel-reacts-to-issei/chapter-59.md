## Theatre Frame

The auditorium had settled into an eerie quiet as the cosmic entities took their seats in the upper tiers. The Living Tribunal did not sit so much as *manifest*, three faces converging on a single point of intention. The Watchers, silent and eternal, arranged themselves with the kind of patience that outlasted continents. Galactus's Herald—today manifesting as a figure of burnished starlight—radiated an almost curious hunger, not for flesh but for *outcome*.

Below them, Wakanda's delegation occupied their section with quiet dignity. T'Challa leaned forward slightly, his fingers steepled. Shuri watched the theatre's mechanisms with barely concealed fascination, already theorizing how they worked. Beside them, the ancestral voices hummed faintly—the Black Panther always carried the weight of the past.

Across the auditorium, Victor Von Doom sat motionless, a statue of dark green and crimson. His mask had not moved in hours. But his fingers—his long, methodical fingers—drummed once against the armrest. Just once. In the rows behind him, his council whispered in Latverian, though no one truly believed Doom was listening to anything but the stage.

The cosmic sorcerers formed a constellation of their own: Scarlet Witch, Ancient One (still present in some iteration of continuity), Doctor Strange's other selves, and practitioners whose names would fold the tongue. They had grown quieter as Arc 6 deepened. This was judgment territory now.

The Villain's Bloc—Thanos, Magneto, Doctor Doom, and a dozen others—watched with the intensity of wolves appraising another predator. There was no mockery in their eyes now. Only assessment.

The Gacha Machine spun.

## Gacha Pull 1: Forbidden Simulation

The theatre dissolved.

What rose before them was not the pristine sky of canon, but a *fractured* Earth. The vision began at what might have been called Year Zero—the moment when multiple catastrophes converged into singular collapse.

First, a cosmic incursion: a corrupted Celestial death-throes, poorly managed by Avengers and mystics alike. The resulting energy cascade poisoned atmospheric layers and disrupted magnetic field patterns. Supply chains withered within weeks. Technology failed in cascading wave patterns. Then came the political domino-fall: governments, already fragile, shattered under resource scarcity. Military installations fractured into warlord factions. The wealthy fled; the poor remained.

In this darkness, *he* moved.

Issei Hyoudou did not ascend to the throne. The vision showed him resisting it—actually *refusing* a crown offered by desperate remnants of the old world's power structures. But resistance was not sufficient. Vacuum abhors vacancy.

His wives, however, had always understood authority differently.

**Rias Gremory** emerged as the first regent-queen. The vision showed her in a vast hall beneath the Himalayas, her scarlet hair catching candlelight as she arbitrated disputes between warring factions. The high-level devil in her *knew* rulership—had been born to it. She established protocols. She created administrative hierarchies. Her peerage, reorganized and expanded, became the nervous system of a fragile eastern state. She was beautiful and terrifying in equal measure, and in the simulation, genuinely exhausted. The vision showed her alone, makeup removed, staring at her hands as if they belonged to someone else. *"I was supposed to be choosing my loves and my throne, not choosing between million-death scenarios,"* her voice whispered, though her lips did not move.

**Akeno Himejima** took military command. The half-demon general understood warfare in ways that transcended her human half. She organized defense networks. She orchestrated humanitarian corridors through war zones. But warfare meant casualties. The vision did not shy from showing her ordering interventions that resulted in collateral damage—civilians in the wrong square when thunder fell from her fingertips. She moved through ruined cities like a ghost, armor never removed, because removing it meant confronting what she'd done. *"Necessary cruelty is still cruelty,"* she said to empty air, and the theatre felt the weight of that paradox.

**Asia** moved among the suffering as a healer and angel-diplomat. In the simulation, her blessing power had evolved; she could purify corrupted land, restore poisoned water, mend disease. She saved millions. But her power had limits. In refugee camps and bombed-out hospitals, she had to choose who received aid and who did not. The vision showed her weeping—genuinely, utterly, breaking-apart weeping—when she failed to save a child. She comforted them anyway. She sat with them anyway. And in doing so, she found a form of peace, however fragile. *"I cannot save everyone. But I will stay with whoever I can,"* she murmured, and some of the cosmic entities actually leaned forward at that.

**Koneko** managed resources and distribution with mathematical precision. She became something between administrator and quartermaster—unglamorous, necessary, and absolutely ruthless about efficiency. Food stocks were allocated based on population density and strategic necessity. Supplies went where they would save the most lives per unit resource. It was logical. It was also cold. The simulation showed her moving through markets she'd reorganized, watching hungry people receive rations, and feeling nothing because feeling would compromise the calculus. *"Numbers. Always numbers. Millions alive, millions dead. All reducible to mathematics,"* her quiet voice said, and there was no self-pity in it—just observation.

**Xenovia and Irina** worked together across religious and cultural divides, engaging in diplomacy that would have seemed impossible in the old world. With institutional religion largely shattered, they became intermediaries between communities, people, and Issei's makeshift governance. They negotiated with warlords and local powers. They brokered temporary truces. They were less queen or general than *diplomat*, but diplomats, the vision showed, were the threads holding fragile peace together.

But Issei—*Issei* did not want any of this.

The core of the vision centered on him: an overworked arbitrator, moving between regions, using his authority sparingly because he understood that authority, once seized through power rather than earned through consent, corrupts everything it touches. He did not command. He negotiated. He provided options. But when options were exhausted, when regions descended into violence despite every diplomatic avenue, he *intervened*—and each intervention cost him pieces of his soul.

The vision showed him alone in a war-torn administrative center, armor removed, sacred gear dormant. His hands were shaking. Not from fear. From exhaustion. From the weight of knowing that his presence, his decision, his *will*, had determined whether billions lived or died.

**Regional Outcomes in the Simulation:**

*East Asia* prospered under Rias's governance—not because she was authoritarian (she was not), but because she had been trained to rule, and she did so with genuine attempt at fairness. Democratic structures, fragile but present, were restored. Some warlords resisted; she crushed their resistance with efficiency born from both martial power and administrative strategy. Casualties were lower here than elsewhere, though "lower" was still millions of lives.

*Europe* remained fragmented. Doom—*Doom himself, even in this apocalyptic simulation*—maintained Latveria's independence, respecting Issei's authority enough not to contest it, but absolutely refusing to submit to it. Wakanda similarly stood sovereign, though there was an alliance of mutual benefit: Issei provided security guarantees; Wakanda provided technological and economic structures that stabilized surrounding regions. Both T'Challa and Doom recognized that Issei was not their tyrant-king. This made him unique.

*The Americas* descended into the most chaotic scenario. Warlords backed by fragmentary military remnants refused centralized authority. Issei's attempts to install governors failed repeatedly. The collateral damage from his intervention in these conflicts was massive. Hundreds of millions died, though billions would have died without his intervention. The mathematics were unsalvageable. The moral arithmetic impossible.

*Africa* benefited from international coalition-building: Wakanda's economic expertise, Issei's raw power, and a network of local leaders who recognized that cooperation meant survival. Death tolls were lower. Institutions, though new and fragile, took root.

But the wives—oh, the wives.

Some of them thrived. Rias discovered she was capable of genuine rulership, and while she would never choose this path under normal circumstances, there was dignity in it. Xenovia and Irina found purpose in bridge-building. But others *broke*.

The vision showed Akeno, years into the simulation, having become something harder and colder—still loyal, still powerful, still protective, but fundamentally altered by the cost of making hard choices. She was a better general than any human could be, and it had destroyed her in ways that armor concealed.

It showed Koneko, increasingly isolated, her efficiency making her invaluable and impossible to approach—a calculator that happened to have once been a girl.

It showed Asia, present and warm and doing genuine good, but with an underlying sadness that suggested she would never fully recover from the suffering she'd witnessed, even as she'd alleviated it.

And it showed some of the other women—Rossweisse, Irina in a different iteration—choosing to step *away* from power entirely, retreating to quiet lives, refusing the crown not out of humility but out of self-preservation.

**The Simulation's Verdict:**

"OUTCOME ANALYSIS: NEITHER DYSTOPIA NOR UTOPIA. MORAL CORRECTNESS: UNCLASSIFIABLE."

The words scrolled across the theatre's consciousness like a denial. This was not victory. It was not defeat. It was *cost and compromise*, rendered into dominion.

The theatre **froze**.

No one spoke. Not immediately.

## Theatre Reaction: The Weight of Ambiguity

In Latveria's section, Doom's mask remained motionless, but his council exchanged glances. Slowly—almost imperceptibly—Doom inclined his head. A gesture of *acknowledgment*, not approval.

T'Challa spoke first, quietly: "The Panther recognizes what I see here. Issei would not *want* to rule. But if forced to rule, he would rule with respect for sovereignty and a refusal to erect monuments to his own power. That is... rarer than you might think."

In the cosmic section, the Living Tribunal's three faces turned toward each other in consultation. The Watchers observed. Galactus's Herald remained still.

Wanda Maximoff's eyes were red. Strange's hands were shaking. The Ancient One, in whatever form she occupied, seemed almost *sad*.

The Villain's Bloc sat in unusual silence. Thanos's jaw tightened. Magneto leaned back, genuinely considering. Doctor Doom actually smiled—a thin, predatory thing—but it was a smile of respect.

## Gacha Pull 2: Outcomes (Secret)

The machine spun again.

This time, the revelation came as data, analysis, *confession*.

**"Outcomes"** bloomed across the theatre in luminous detail.

*Civilian lives saved (relative to cascade-collapse scenario): 2.3 billion.*

*Civilian lives lost (from Issei's governance): 47 million—casualties of war, collateral from his interventions, failed attempts to maintain stability without authoritarian force.*

*Net calculation: Positive. Mathematically, unambiguously positive.*

*Moral calculation: Unknown. Unknowable.*

The vision shifted. It showed Issei in the simulation, years into his involuntary reign, sitting with his wives in a room stripped of all grandeur. Rias held his hand. Akeno said nothing. Asia simply wept. Koneko arranged tea with mechanical precision.

His internal monologue echoed through the theatre:

*"I didn't start this collapse. I could have hidden. Could have let it unfold without my intervention. Billions would have died. I'm responsible for how it ends. Not for the ending itself, but for the shape it takes. And that responsibility is heavier than any armor I've ever worn."*

Rias's response, spoken into the quiet: *"You did what you could with the tools you had."*

*"I did what I had to do,"* he corrected. *"That's not the same as what I should have done. And I don't know if there's a difference."*

The secret pulled further back, revealing the wives' own assessments:

Rias was supportive but worn—a woman who had discovered capacity within herself and would never be entirely free of it. She would always be Rias Gremory, High-Level Devil and Queen-Regent, whether she wanted to be or not.

Akeno questioned the necessity of some decisions, and in questioning, fractured. Her protective instinct toward Issei remained absolute, but her faith in his judgment—and her own—had cracked.

Asia was simply sad. Not bitter. Not broken. But possessed of a kind of comprehensive grief that suggested she understood something the others hadn't: that saving people and suffering with them were not separate things.

Koneko was pragmatic. Numbers were numbers. People lived. She had facilitated that. The cost was the cost. There was no redemption in pretending the cost was smaller than it was.

And some of the other women—those who retreated, those who chose quiet—they made peace by choosing *not* to bear the weight at all.

The theatre understood: this simulation showed not a tyrant in-waiting, but a man who would rule *reluctantly, incompletely, with genuine anguish*, and that anguish itself would be the thing that prevented tyranny.

## Marvel's Debate

Doctor Strange stood. "He would be bound by his own morality. That's a stronger chain than any we could forge."

"But would that be *enough*?" Tony asked the question with genuine uncertainty. "A benevolent dictator is still a dictator. Even if his benevolence is real."

Cap nodded slowly. "He'd do it if he had to. And he'd hate himself for it. That's... that's actually more trustworthy than most rulers."

Doom's voice emerged, measured and certain: "He would rule better than most mortals could. But he would hate himself for ruling at all. That hatred would be *honest*. That is rare. That is valuable."

From Wakanda: "Issei as ruler would actually respect our sovereignty. That is *precisely* rare."

The cosmic entities conferred in frequencies beyond hearing. Finally, the Living Tribunal spoke: "The dragon-boy is not *destined* for rulership. The multiverse does not incline toward that outcome. Yet in the scenario where he is forced into it, he resists its corruption better than most gods have done."

Scarlet Witch: "He's not a tyrant. But that doesn't mean he wouldn't be *trapped*."

The Villain's Bloc's assessment was more practical: "He would be nearly impossible to overthrow. Inconvenient for those who thrive on chaos. Inconvenient, but not destructive. We could work with that."

Galactus's Herald simply radiates approval at the notion of a ruler who understood the limits of power, even at cosmic scale.

## Hook into Chapter 60

The Master of the Theatre allowed the silence to extend, then spoke: "But this is simulation. Forbidden because it treads into territories of what-if that are themselves dangerous to contemplate. The true path of Issei Hyoudou diverges from rulership. His destiny is not crown-bearer, but *power-wielder*—and those are profoundly different things."

"In the final chapter of Arc 6," the Theatre continued, "we will witness what the Forbidden Simulation could not fully reveal: the true forms that Issei approaches. The Diabolos form. Pseudo-Dragon Deification. True Dragon Deification. These are not about ruling worlds. They are about becoming *transcendent*."

"The question is not: Would Issei rule well? The question is: What will Issei *become*?"

The theatre's awareness shifted. The gacha machine was already spinning, its drum already turning. One final pull before Arc 6 climaxed.
