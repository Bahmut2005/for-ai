# Chapter Sixteen: The Blacksmith

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Doom • Tony Stark • Shuri • Eitri • Phastos

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Forging the First Ascalon" — **Legendary**
2. "Replica Mjolnir Loan" — **Rare**

---

Sixteen chapters into their shared captivity, the theatre's audience had done what audiences always do: they had self-sorted.

The gods had their high tiers and their thunderheads. The street heroes had colonized the middle rows into something resembling a neighborhood, complete with borrowed blankets and an unspoken rule about saving seats. The monsters had the galleries. And down on the left flank, in a cluster of seats that had somehow acquired workbench-height armrests and improved lighting without anyone asking, sat the quarter of the room that thought with its hands.

Tony Stark. Shuri of Wakanda, who had claimed the seat beside him specifically so she could disagree with him at conversational volume. Phastos of the Eternals, who repaired things when he was anxious and had been visibly anxious since chapter one. And Eitri, king of the dwarves, who occupied three seats and made them look like a throne, and who had spent fifteen chapters saying almost nothing — because until now the boy on the screen had punched, boosted, bled, and roared, and none of that was *craft.*

Victor von Doom did not sit with them. Doom sat in the aisle adjacent, alone, at the precise distance that communicated *I am not with these people* and *I can hear every word these people say* simultaneously. Nobody had ever seen him arrive at that seat. He was simply always in it, like a load-bearing pillar with opinions.

The previous chapter had been hunts — strays and monsters, wages and mercy, Blade's slow approving nod and the Punisher's slower one. Tonight, the theatre's weather changed early. The house lights sank not to storm-grey or pub-gold but to a deep working orange, the exact color of metal thinking about becoming something else, and the air filled with a smell that had no business crossing dimensions: coal smoke, scale, quenching oil.

Eitri was on his feet before the wheel even descended.

"That," he said, in a voice like a rockslide clearing its throat, "is a forge. I would know that smell in my grave."

The gacha wheel dropped from the dark, spun its rings with ceremonial smugness, and delivered its first token into the crystal bowl. The chime, tonight, sounded like a hammer on an anvil.

**PULL ONE: "FORGING THE FIRST ASCALON" — LEGENDARY.**

"Ascalon," said Shuri, already leaning forward. "That is not a Japanese word."

"It's not a devil word either," said Stark. "That's a *saint's* word. Why does the pervert dragon have a saint's word?"

The screen bloomed, and — as it did for its highest rarities — it *illustrated.*

---

Legend first, painted in the gacha's memory-fire, hedged in the soft brushwork it used for things older than reliable witnesses.

An age when dragons were a weather that ate villages. A forge at the edge of that age, and a smith whose face the vision never showed — only the hands, and the work. The theatre watched a blade being made the old way, which is to say slowly, which is to say *honestly*: folded, hammered, blessed, quenched, prayed over, hammered again. Sanctified not once at the end but continuously, the way you don't season a stew by dumping the salt in last. Whatever went into that steel went in at every stage, until the vision made one thing wordlessly clear: this was not a sword that had been taught to cut. This was a sword that had been taught, hammer-blow by hammer-blow, exactly one lesson, and the lesson was *how dragons end.*

A knight carried it. The legend called him George, and later called him Saint, and the vision gave the theatre one single frame of him standing over something vast and scaled and finished, and then, mercifully, moved on. Ascalon, the dragon-slayer. Every dragon it ended it *understood*, and every one it understood made it better at ending the next. A blade with a curriculum.

"Oh, that's obscene," said Stark, delighted and appalled. "That's a learning system. Somebody built machine learning out of hagiography and murder."

"Somebody built a *purpose* and gave it an edge," Eitri rumbled. "Be silent. This is the good part."

But the vision jumped forward instead — centuries collapsing like a closing bellows — and the forge-light gave way to a school's meeting room, and the theatre's collective stomach dropped, because standing in that mundane little room, wings like sunrise given anatomy, was an Archangel.

**MICHAEL,** the screen captioned, unnecessarily. Nobody in the theatre needed the caption. Several of the room's gods sat up straighter without meaning to.

And Michael, chief of the armies of Heaven, gentlest smile in creation, held out the dragon-slaying sword of Saint George — to Issei Hyoudou. Devil. Red Dragon Emperor. The boy who, six chapters ago, had made the entire theatre's threat-meter climb by swearing a dragon-backed oath against Heaven itself over what Heaven's church had done to Asia Argento.

"Hold on," said Sam Wilson, from the middle rows. "Hold *on.* Heaven heard that kid declare war on it, and Heaven's response was to *arm him?*"

"Heaven's response," murmured Doctor Strange, eyes narrowed with professional interest, "was to hand the dragon an anti-dragon weapon and call it a gift. That is either the most generous act in this story so far, or the most elegant containment strategy I have ever seen. Possibly both."

On screen, Issei took the sword — and yelped, because the holy aura of the thing met his devil flesh like a stovetop. He held on anyway. The theatre had learned by now that *holding on anyway* was roughly eighty percent of his skill set.

And then, quietly, before the exchange ended, the boy did the thing that made the Workshop Quarter — who had come tonight for metallurgy — go suddenly still.

"Um. Michael-san. Since you're here. Can I ask for one more thing? Not for me." On-screen Issei bowed, badly, the bow of someone who had never had a reason to learn formality and was improvising with his whole heart. "There's a girl. Asia. The Church threw her away, but she still — she still prays. Every day. And it *hurts* her now, because she's a devil, because of me, kind of. Could you — is there a setting, or —" he gestured helplessly at the ceiling, at the sky, at the entire apparatus of Heaven — "could you make it not hurt? When she prays? That's all. You can take the sword back if it costs extra."

Phastos put his hand over his mouth.

Michael's smile changed. The screen did not caption what it changed into, and didn't need to. "It will be done," the Archangel said. "Keep the sword."

---

The vision moved to a basement lab, and the real work began — the part the pull had been named for.

Because Ascalon, it turned out, would not simply be *carried.* A devil's hands blistered on its hilt; a dragon's aura made it hum with the low, eager note of a dog scenting the exact thing it was bred for. So the boy and the dragon in his arm did the only thing they could think of, which was also the maddest thing available:

They fed the dragon-slaying sword to the dragon.

The theatre watched Issei brace the blade against the Boosted Gear's green gem, watched him pour boosts into the join — **[BOOST.]** **[BOOST.]** **[BOOST.]** — watched holy light and dragon-fire argue like two rivers meeting, watched the boy grit his teeth as the backwash burned up his forearm in bright, forgiving welts.

**"Partner,"** said Ddraig, with the tone of an ancient being reading his own eviction notice, **"I wish it noted, formally, for whatever record survives us: I am a dragon. You are asking me to hold a dragon-slayer in my mouth."**

"You said we needed a weapon with an edge! You literally said that! Last week!"

**"I meant a knife. A spear. A sharpened complaint. Not the single most anti-me object ever manufactured. Do you understand that every dragon that has ever lived would call this blasphemy?"**

"Do YOU want to give it back to the Archangel? *You* explain the refund."

A pause. The green gem flickered — the theatre had learned to read that flicker as Ddraig sighing, and Thor, in the high tiers, was already grinning in anticipation.

**"…No,"** said the Welsh Dragon, at last, with terrible dignity. **"It is a good sword. I sigh a proportionate amount, and this occasion merits one. Continue."**

The join took. Light snapped inward like a door closing, and when Issei raised his arm again the gauntlet spoke in its cold system voice — a new word, added to its small vocabulary of dooms:

**[BLADE.]**

Ascalon slid out of the Boosted Gear's crest and locked home, holy steel rising from dragon scale, and the two auras — which had spent the whole scene fighting — settled against each other like a border going quiet. The gacha stamped the frame with its rotating Legendary seal and, because it had developed a taste for editorializing, wrote across the bottom of the screen in letters of forge-fire:

**THE PERFECT ANTI-DRAGON WEAPON EXAMINED THE DRAGON — AND CHOSE TO SERVE.**

**RE-FORGED IN DRAGON-FIRE. CARRIED BY ITS OWN PREY. IT HAS NEVER BEEN HAPPIER.**

"It has never been *happier,*" Phastos repeated, softly. "It said happier. The sword has a mood now. I need everyone to be more upset about that sentence."

The screen paused itself between pulls. The lights came up.

And then the gacha did the thing it had only done once before — in chapter thirteen, when it had spun uninvited to humiliate the British. It reacted to its audience. The wheel's rings rotated with unmistakable glee, a shaft of light stabbed down into the open floor in front of the Workshop Quarter, and out of the light rose a plinth of black stone.

On the plinth lay Ascalon. Or something wearing its shape — steel and gold, faintly warm, impossibly *present.*

Text hung in the air above it, in the gacha's coldest font:

**LEGENDARY BONUS: ANALYSIS OPPORTUNITY.**

**EXHIBIT IS AN ECHO. ECHO IS FAITHFUL. INSTRUMENTS PROVIDED.**

**THE MANAGEMENT ACCEPTS NO LIABILITY FOR THE EXHIBIT'S OPINIONS.**

Beside the plinth, a workbench had appeared, laden with instruments so beautiful that Tony Stark made a small, involuntary sound.

"It's a trap," said Shuri, standing, already rolling up her sleeves. "It has provided us perfect instruments to watch us fail with. This is a rigged casino."

"Obviously it's a rigged casino," said Stark, also standing. "Deal me in."

---

Stark went first, because of course he did.

He didn't touch it — sixteen chapters had taught even Tony Stark that much — he *scanned* it, with a spectrometer of no earthly design that fit his hand like it had been printed for him. The readings came back promptly, confidently, and uselessly.

"Okay. Composition analysis says — " he squinted — "it says *faith.* Not an element. Not an alloy. The word. Mass reads as 'depends.' Depends on *what?* Edge geometry is — the edge doesn't resolve. I've got magnification down to angstroms and the edge just keeps *going,* it's not an edge, it's a — " he turned the display so Shuri could see, and his voice did the thing it did when engineering became religion — "it's a boundary condition."

"Give me that." Shuri took the instrument, ran her own protocol, cross-checked it against a second device, then a third. Her eyebrows climbed. "He's right. The edge of this sword reads like the walls of this theatre read. It is not sharp, colonizer. Sharp is for knives. This thing is *definitional.* It does not cut dragons." She looked up, delighted in the way only a scientist meeting a beautiful monster can be. "It decides where dragons *stop.*"

She tried to lift it then, properly, with an insulated cradle rated to eight hundred tons, and the sword — without moving, without glowing, without any drama at all — weighed eight hundred and one. She swapped for a heavier rig. The sword weighed more than that instead. Exactly, precisely, insultingly *one unit more* than whatever she brought.

"It is doing this on purpose," she announced, to the room. "It has a sense of humor. I want everyone to know I respect it and I hate it."

Phastos came forward third, and he did not bring an instrument at all.

He stood over the blade with his hands loose at his sides, and he looked at it the way he looked at engines and doorways and dying machines — reading not the material but the *intention*, because to Phastos every made thing was a sentence and he had never met one he couldn't parse. The theatre quieted. Even the Workshop Quarter had learned that when Phastos went still, something true was about to get said.

"Every machine wants something," he said at last. "Weapons are usually easy reads. They want use. This one — " he tilted his head — "this one doesn't want use. It wants to *keep a promise.* Someone stood at a forge with the whole world burning behind them, and instead of making a weapon, they made a vow you can hold." He stepped back, and did not once try to touch it. "It's not mine to pick up. You don't try on somebody else's wedding ring."

"You're not even going to *try?*" said Stark.

"Tony. It's a promise with an edge, and it already belongs to someone." Phastos sat back down. "I fix things. That is not broken."

Eitri went fourth, and Eitri did the thing none of them had dared: he touched it.

Not the hilt. Never the hilt. He laid one enormous scarred knuckle against the flat of the blade, gently, the way you greet a strange horse — and the whole theatre heard it: a low tone rose off the steel, warm, resonant, *acknowledging.* The sword had ignored three geniuses and their instruments, and it was humming for the dwarf.

"HA!" barked Stark. "Favoritism!"

"Fellowship," Eitri corrected quietly. And then the tone faded, and the warmth went out of the metal, and the king of the dwarves withdrew his hand with a rueful, ancient smile. "There it is. It let me read the maker's mark — smith to smith, one nod across a very long room. It did not let me read the work." He looked down at the echo of Ascalon for a long moment. "Forged once. *Finished* ten thousand times — every hand that ever carried it rightly was another tempering. That is craft beyond me, and I have forged suns into rings." His voice dropped, and the old grief moved under it like a fish under ice. "I made a thing, once, that answered any hand that held it. Any hand at all. That was my great mistake, and half a universe paid for it. This smith was wiser than I. He made a thing that *chooses.*"

The silence after that held for three full seconds, which in that theatre was a standing ovation.

And then Doom rose.

He did not hurry. Doom had never hurried anywhere in his life; the world was expected to wait, and it did. He crossed to the plinth, and the Workshop Quarter drew back without discussing it, and the armored king of Latveria looked down at the dragon-slayer of Saint George the way one head of state examines another's border.

"You have refused a merchant," Doom said to the sword — to the *sword*, in the second person, as an equal, and no one laughed, "a princess, a god's engineer, and a king of smiths. Very well. Doom does not scan. Doom does not beg artifacts for their consent." He extended one gauntlet, mystic circuitry waking along the knuckles in threads of green. "Doom *takes measure.*"

He laid his hand upon the hilt.

Nothing happened.

Not a burn. Not a shock. Not a hum, a glow, a refusal, a *weight.* The sword did to Victor von Doom what the theatre's floor had done to the Hulk's fists in chapter one: it declined, utterly and without effort, to acknowledge that anything had occurred. Doom stood with his hand on the greatest blade any of them had ever seen, and the blade treated him as weather. Less than weather. Weather, at least, gets a forecast.

The theatre held its breath. Everyone in it understood, instinctively, that the sword had just done the single most dangerous thing you can do to Doom. It had burned Stark's curiosity, teased Shuri, saluted Eitri, moved Phastos.

It had *ignored* Victor.

Doom removed his hand. He regarded the blade for a further moment, and when he spoke, his voice was as even as a chess clock.

"Adequate," he pronounced, and returned to his aisle.

Those seated nearest him — and several of them compared accounts afterward, so the record is considered reliable — observed that he wrote precisely one line in the small book he thought no one had noticed he kept, beneath the line he had written three chapters ago about dragons:

*Latveria possesses no dragon-forged blade; oversight.*

Up on the screen, the paused image chose that exact moment to resume for three seconds: on-screen Issei, in his world, flexing his arm — **[BLADE.]** — and Ascalon leaping joyfully out of the gauntlet into his scarred, unremarkable, teenaged hand.

On its plinth, the echo *leaned.* Toward the screen. Like a dog hearing the car in the drive.

"It rejected five of the finest makers in the multiverse," said Shuri, into her hands, "and it is wagging its tail at a *television.*"

---

The wheel spun again before the Workshop Quarter had finished arguing about whether "faith" could be a boundary condition (Stark: no; Shuri: evidently; Phastos: *please stop instrumentalizing faith*; Eitri: silence, but weighted silence). The second token dropped with a cheerful clatter.

**PULL TWO: "REPLICA MJOLNIR LOAN" — RARE.**

Two reactions occurred at the same instant, in different tiers.

Thor stood up so fast his bench overturned.

And Loki said, "I beg your pardon — *loan?*" — and then the screen opened on the vision, and the theatre got to watch the God of Mischief experience the rare pleasure of seeing his own name captioned under someone else's face.

**LOKI,** said the screen, over a sneering, silver-haired Norse deity of the canon world — haughty, radiant with malice, monologuing at a boy in red armor about the coming twilight of the gods and the tedium of peace.

The theatre's Loki examined his counterpart in silence for four seconds. "The hair is derivative," he announced at last, to no one and everyone. "The cape is a cry for help. The monologue lacks structure — he's leading with Ragnarok, you *close* with Ragnarok. I am being defamed across the multiverse by an amateur."

"Brother," said Thor, not looking away from the screen, voice climbing toward the ceiling, "*hush.* Look. LOOK WHAT THE BOY IS HOLDING."

The vision showed it plainly: into Issei Hyoudou's hands, before the battle, someone had delivered a war hammer. Squat. Rune-carved. Impossibly familiar. The screen captioned it with what might have been the driest text it had ever produced:

**MJOLNIR (REPLICA). DWARF-FORGED. BLESSED FOR THE BEARER'S USE. TERMS AND CONDITIONS APPLY.**

"THAT IS MY HAMMER!" Thor roared, with total joy.

"That is a *copy* of your hammer," said Sif.

"THAT IS A COPY OF MY HAMMER!" Thor amended, with, if anything, more joy. "Do you see? Do you all SEE? Across the whole of the multiverse, when the gods of another cosmology needed to arm a mortal champion against a Loki — and I note, brother, that *every* world appears to need arming against a Loki, you are a constant, like the speed of light — what did they reach for? What was the *obvious* answer? MY HAMMER. Accept no substitutes! Except, apparently, this one substitute, which I hereby endorse!"

On screen, the fight was not going well. The canon Loki was god-tier and bored; Issei's boosts were climbing but slowly; and at the trickster's side loped something that made the theatre's warmer conversations die mid-word — a wolf the size of a house, grey as famine, moving like an apology being retracted. The screen captioned it **FENRIR**, and then, in smaller text, as if reluctant: **HIS FANGS SLAY GODS.**

The boy in red armor set his feet. The hammer was too heavy for him, too holy, too *much* — and the theatre watched him do the thing that made Tony Stark rise slowly out of his seat with the expression of a man watching a stranger solve his life's problem with a rock.

**[BOOST.]** **[BOOST.]** **[BOOST.]** **[BOOST.]** —

**[TRANSFER.]**

He poured it into the *hammer.*

The replica Mjolnir drank ten boosts of Red Dragon Emperor and grew — swelled — became a hammer for a giant's hand, wreathed in borrowed lightning and doubled dragon-power, and Issei Hyoudou gripped it in both gauntlets, screamed something wordless and sincere, and hit the God of Mischief with it so hard the vision's horizon flinched.

The canon Loki left a trench. The trench smoked.

The vision's frame stayed tight on Issei throughout — suspiciously tight, cropped like a photograph of a crowded party sent to an ex. At the very edge of the final frames, something white and swift blurred past, wing-light and contemptuous, and the gacha *conspicuously declined to focus on it,* panning away with the smooth haste of a museum guide passing a door marked PRIVATE.

The last thing the screen showed before the lights came up was the wolf.

Fenrir, lunging at the frame, jaws opening — and the vision cut to black on his teeth.

---

"Okay," said Stark, standing in the open floor now, pacing, hands sketching architecture in the air. "Okay. Everyone's watching the hammer. Stop watching the hammer. The hammer is a *peripheral.* Watch the kid. Sixteen chapters, and I finally have the spec sheet." He spun to face the Workshop Quarter. "He's not a weapon. He never was a weapon. He's an *outlet.* A universal power bus with legs. Transfer works on allies, sure, fine, support class, whatever — Transfer works on *hardware.* Hand that kid any weapon in this room and ten seconds later it's the finest version of itself that has ever existed. He turned a licensed replica into a god-killer in one move. Do you understand what I would *pay*—"

"Nothing," said Shuri. "You would pay nothing, because you cannot buy it, and that is the actual finding, Stark." She ticked it off on her fingers. "The holy sword: chooses him. The hammer: *blessed* for him — permission, notarized by a pantheon. The gauntlet: a partner, not a device — it has a name and it sighs at him. Every piece of that boy's arsenal runs on the same operating system, and the operating system is *consent.* You cannot steal it. You cannot reverse-engineer it. You cannot mass-produce it." She sat back, deeply satisfied. "It is the only arms industry in existence with no aftermarket."

"A covenant," rumbled Eitri, nodding slowly. "Every true smith knows it, and every empire forgets it. The work is not the metal. The work is the *agreement.*"

"Which is why the sword ignored Victor," said Phastos — quietly, but not quietly enough, and he knew it, and said it anyway. "It's not that he's unworthy. Worthiness is the hammer's gimmick. The sword just knows the difference between a hand that asks and a hand that takes."

Doom did not turn his head. "The Eternal is invited," he said, to the air, "to speculate about Doom's hands when his own have finished trembling over a moody letter-opener." But those nearest the armored king noted that he had opened the small book again; that he was looking at the screen, where a boy who could not be bought, copied, or commanded had just field-upgraded a borrowed god-weapon by *sharing*; and that his pen hovered a long time over the page without adding anything — which, from Doom, was the loudest fascination anyone had ever heard.

High above, Loki had draped himself over his seat-back in a full sulk, being consoled, appallingly, by Thor.

"He beat me," Loki said, "with a *knockoff.* Of *your* hammer. There are Lokis in infinite worlds, brother, and every one of us felt that. It travels. Indignity is quantum-entangled."

"Aye," said Thor, patting him with a hand like a friendly landslide, absolutely radiant. "And what a blow it was! Be of good cheer, brother — you were felled by the finest imitation of the finest weapon in all the realms, wielded by a most worthy dragon. Truly, there is no shame in it."

"Every word of that sentence was worse than the last."

The house lights began their end-of-chapter dim. The plinth with Ascalon's echo sank back into the floor — though several witnesses swore the blade's tone sounded once more, softly, toward the darkened screen, like a goodnight — and the great screen offered its final frozen frame, held long past comfort:

Grey fur. A wet black nose.

And teeth.

Text typed itself out beneath the image, one cold letter at a time, and then the dark took everything:

**NEXT: THE FANGS THAT SLAY GODS FIND HIM.**

In the workshop quarter, nobody moved for a while. It was Eitri who finally spoke, to no one in particular, in the tone of a man closing up his forge for the night.

"A sword that chooses. A hammer that consents. A boy who asks." The dwarf king shook his enormous head, and there was something almost like peace in it. "Whoever is arming that child, they are better smiths than all of us. I should like to shake their hands before this theatre is done with us."

"Assuming," said Stark, watching the teeth fade from the dark screen, "there's anything left of him to arm."

## Theatre Ledger

- **New Pulls This Chapter:** *Forging the First Ascalon* — Legendary; *Replica Mjolnir Loan* — Rare. The Legendary pull came with an unprecedented bonus: a tangible "echo" of Ascalon manifested on a plinth for the audience to examine — the second confirmed instance of the gacha interacting with the theatre directly, and the first time it has put an object in the room.
- **Secrets Revealed:** Ascalon is the dragon-slaying holy sword of Saint George — gifted to Issei by the Archangel Michael, meaning Heaven answered his oath against it with a *gift*. Issei used the gift's moment to ask nothing for himself, only that Asia be allowed to pray without pain; Michael granted it. Ascalon was integrated into the Boosted Gear (**[BLADE.]**) — re-forged in dragon-fire, an anti-dragon weapon that examined a dragon and chose to serve. Issei's cosmology contains a Norse pantheon, including its own Loki and Fenrir ("fangs that slay gods"); the gods there loaned Issei a dwarf-forged replica Mjolnir, blessed for his use, and his Transfer ability works on *objects* — ten boosts poured into the replica turned it giant and flattened that world's Loki. The Workshop Quarter's consensus finding: Issei's entire arsenal runs on consent (choosing sword, blessed hammer, partnered gauntlet) — it cannot be stolen, copied, or mass-produced.
- **Alliance / Opinion Shifts:** The Ascalon echo burned no one but ranked everyone: it teased Shuri's instruments, hummed once in fellowship for Eitri ("it let me read the maker's mark, not the work"), moved Phastos ("a promise with an edge" — he refused to touch it), and *ignored* Doom entirely, which the room understood to be the most dangerous outcome available. Doom pronounced it "Adequate" and wrote in his private book: *Latveria possesses no dragon-forged blade; oversight.* Eitri's respect for Issei's unseen armorers is now total; he publicly contrasted the sword-that-chooses with his own "great mistake" that answered any hand. Stark has reclassified Issei from "weapon" to "outlet — universal power bus with legs." Thor is proprietary and delighted about the replica ("Accept no substitutes! Except this one substitute, which I hereby endorse!"); Loki is in a formal sulk over being beaten-by-proxy with a knockoff of his brother's hammer ("indignity is quantum-entangled").
- **Unresolved Hooks:** The pull was titled the *First* Ascalon — implying more Ascalons to come. During the Loki battle the gacha kept its frame suspiciously tight and *refused to focus* on a white, winged blur at the edge of the fight — the audience suspects Albion's wielder and notes the gacha is now actively curating what they see. The exhibit-echo sank back into the floor: is the gacha building a museum, and why? Heaven's true motive in arming the dragon remains debated (gift, containment, or both — Strange suspects both). And the chapter closed on a freeze-frame of Fenrir's god-slaying teeth with the caption "NEXT: THE FANGS THAT SLAY GODS FIND HIM" — the theatre has been promised, in writing, that the boy is about to be bitten.
