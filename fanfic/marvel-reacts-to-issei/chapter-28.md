Eight chapters into the Family-Class banner, the theatre had stopped pretending it wasn't a household.

The wives had a box now — the theatre had grown it for them somewhere between Chapters Twenty-One and Twenty-Two, a wisteria-hung terrace at stage left that no one had voted on and no one dared question, from which Rias Gremory watched her husband-to-be's life get dealt out to forty thousand strangers with the poise of a woman grading their reactions. The "WHO WOULD HE ADOPT?" betting board from the previous session still hovered over the mezzanine, updating its odds with quiet malice. Kamala Khan was at four-to-one. Kamala Khan had noticed.

The dragon bloc held its old ground from Arc One — Thor with his boots up, Valkyrie with her whetstone, Loki draped across the end seats like a knife pretending to be a scarf, and Fin Fang Foom occupying his eleven reupholstered chairs with the coiled stillness of a being who had, over twenty-seven chapters, made the journey from contempt to grudging respect and hated every step of the itinerary.

And up in the smugglers' balcony the Ravagers had colonized in Arc Two — chosen for its sightlines and its proximity to nothing anyone would miss — Yondu Udonta had his flame-crested silhouette tipped back, Kraglin beside him, Stakar Ogord one row behind them both, present in the way a court is present.

The wheel came down. The room went quiet the way it had learned to.

It spun once, twice — and stopped on the black wedge. The one with the keyhole. The room had seen it only twice before, and both times it had cost somebody a secret.

**"BEAUCEPHALIS BACKSTORY" — RARITY: SECRET.**

"Beaucephalis," Loki read aloud, and sat up by degrees, un-liquefying. "Well, *now* we're getting somewhere. That's a dragon's name if I've ever forged one. And I'll tell you exactly what this is—" he was on his feet, warming to it, playing to the mezzanine, "—it's *his*. The boy's true-name. Dragons hide their real names, everyone knows this; 'Issei Hyoudou' is the registry entry, the bait, the name you give the tax collector. Beaucephalis is what the gauntlet calls him when no one is—"

The betting board flickered. **BEAUCEPHALIS = ISSEI'S SECRET DRAGON NAME: 2:1.** Money — the theatre's strange, pointless, irresistible money — began to move.

The screen let it move for exactly four seconds. Then, in letters that managed to convey a sigh:

**INCORRECT.**

**BEAUCEPHALIS IS A SEPARATE ENTITY. A SEPARATE DRAGON. NOT AN ALIAS. NOT A TITLE. NOT ISSEI HYOUDOU.**

**DESIGNATION: NEST-BROTHER.**

A beat.

**THE AUDIENCE WILL NOT BE PERMITTED TO CONFUSE THEM. THE AUDIENCE IS BEING TOLD THIS IN ADVANCE BECAUSE THE AUDIENCE HAS A RECORD.**

"It's developing opinions about us," muttered Sam Wilson. "That's new and terrible."

In the wisteria box, Rias Gremory had gone very still, and then very soft, in the way the theatre had learned meant the machine had reached into an actual drawer of her actual life. "Oh," she said. "Oh, they're showing *Beau*."

Koneko Toujou, chin on the box rail, produced one of her rare, small, dangerous smiles. "Roof hog," she said, with enormous affection.

Forty thousand legends turned to look at the box.

"There's *another* dragon in that house?" said Peter Parker.

"There's another *brother*," said Rias, "in that family," and the screen, as if it had been waiting for the correction, opened the vision.

---

Purple mountains, under a sky the color of a bruise that had decided to be beautiful about it. The Underworld's dragon country — and the theatre knew it at once, because Arc Two had made them memorize it: Tannin's territory, the Blaze Meteor Dragon's fostering grounds, where a former Dragon King ran orchards and broods with the same volcanic patience, and where Issei Hyoudou had once been sent to train by being chased, burned, bitten, and improved.

The caption set the scene with its usual brutal economy: **TRAINING PERIOD. PREVIOUSLY DOCUMENTED. SEE: RAVEL PHENEX'S LEDGER, ENTRIES 31 THROUGH 68.**

("Sixty-eight," said Pepper Potts faintly, from the analysts' rows. "Thirty-one *through* sixty-eight.")

But the vision wasn't following Issei. That was the first strangeness. It followed a shape at the edge of Tannin's brood-fields — a young dragon, iron-grey shading to storm-slate, wings a full size too large for the body, folded wrong, the way a boy wears a coat he's meant to grow into. He was watching the other fledglings from a distance that had been carefully chosen to look voluntary.

**BEAUCEPHALIS,** said the screen. **FLEDGLING. FOSTERED. UNAFFILIATED.**

"Unaffiliated," Fin Fang Foom repeated. It was the first thing he'd said all session, and his voice had gone flat and exact. "That word does not mean what these apes will assume it means. For a fledgling, *unaffiliated* is not a status. It is a wound. Watch his throat."

The theatre watched his throat. The other fledglings, wheeling and squabbling above the orchards, trailed little banners of flame when they called to each other — bright, casual, wasteful, the way children are wasteful with anything they'll never run out of. The grey fledgling called too, once, at the sky.

Nothing came out but sound.

"Cold-throat," said Foom, and for once there was no disdain in the delivery at all, which from him was its own kind of shout. "His fire has never been lit."

**A HATCHLING'S FIRST-FIRE IS KINDLED FROM THE NEST-FIRE OF ITS BROOD,** the screen supplied. **BEAUCEPHALIS'S NEST WAS DESTROYED BY EVIL DRAGONS BEFORE HE WOKE FROM THE SHELL. HE HATCHED ALONE, IN ASHES, THE SOLE SURVIVOR. THERE WAS NO FIRE LEFT TO GIVE HIM.**

The theatre's street rows made the sound they always made when the machine did this — set a joke's worth of whimsy on the table and then turned it over to show the grief soldered underneath.

**THE OTHER BROODS WOULD NOT NEST WITH HIM,** the screen went on, pitiless. **NOT FROM CRUELTY. FROM LAW. A NEST SHARES FIRE. HE HAD NONE TO SHARE. THREE BROODS DECLINED HIM. TANNIN FED HIM AND COULD DO NO MORE; A FOSTER'S FLAME IS DUTY, NOT KINSHIP, AND EVERY DRAGON ALIVE KNOWS THE DIFFERENCE FROM THE INSIDE.**

"That is *correct*," Foom said, quietly, to nobody, "and I have never once heard it said aloud," and eleven seats' worth of ancient Makluan went silent in a new way.

Then Issei arrived in the frame, and the theatre exhaled, because it knew this boy: sixteen, scorched, bandaged in Ravel Phenex's meticulous wrappings, dropped in the dirt of Tannin's mountains at the end of a training day that had visibly tried to kill him. He was failing to build a campfire. He was failing at it *comprehensively* — a devil resurrected into a chess piece, wielding a gauntlet that could erase mountains, defeated by damp kindling.

The grey fledgling watched him from his carefully voluntary distance.

Issei looked up, saw him, and did the thing that twenty-seven chapters had taught the theatre he would always, always do. He held up half his rations.

"You gonna hover, or you gonna eat?"

No ceremony. No negotiation. The theatre watched a boy split dried meat with a dragon the size of a delivery truck and complain, at length, about their mutual foster-tormentor — *"and THEN he says 'dodge faster,' like, incredible coaching, Tannin, really, write a book"* — and watched the fledgling settle, one degree at a time, from a distance that looked voluntary to a distance that was honest.

It became a pattern. The vision moved in cuts: night after night, the boy and the grey fledgling at the edge of the brood-fields, sharing food, sharing complaints, sharing the specific silence of two creatures who had both been laughed at by the better-equipped. Beaucephalis started copying Issei's training — the failing, the getting up, the failing differently. Issei started saving the fledgling the good half.

"Oh no," said Kraglin Obfonteri, up in the Ravagers' balcony, with the tone of a man watching a recruitment happen. "Kid's crew now. He don't even know it yet."

Then came the cold night.

Deep mountain winter, the brood-fires banked and distant, and the vision found the two of them on the high slopes after a training exercise had run long — Issei blue-lipped and shaking, the fledgling curled around him like a levee, both of them staring at a pile of kindling that neither of them could light. Issei, because he was hopeless. Beaucephalis, because he was Beaucephalis.

The fledgling tried anyway. The theatre watched him gather himself, neck arched, wings mantled — and produce nothing but a cough of hot air and humiliation while, far below, the bright fledglings of the other broods trailed their wasteful banners across the dark.

And Issei Hyoudou looked at his own left hand.

"Hey, Ddraig. Transfer works on anything, right? Power into a sword, power into Rias, power into—" a nod at the fledgling's throat— "a pilot light?"

**[Partner,]** said the dragon-voice, low and suddenly very careful, **[think about what you are asking.]**

"I'm asking if my little buddy freezes tonight or not."

A pause with grammar in it.

**[...No,]** said Ddraig. **[He does not.]**

**[BOOST.]** said the gauntlet, into the mountain dark. **[BOOST. BOOST. TRANSFER.]**

The green gem flared, and Issei pressed his gauntleted hand to the fledgling's breastbone, and the theatre watched a spark that was not the fledgling's own — a mote of banked-ember red, freely given, compounding — catch somewhere behind that cold grey throat and *take*.

Beaucephalis breathed out a ribbon of flame the color of a forge nine hours after the smith went home. Ddraig's color. Issei's color, now, and forever after his own.

The kindling lit. The fledgling stared at the little campfire as if it were a continent.

Fin Fang Foom was on his feet. All eleven seats of him.

"That is not *possible*," he said, and it came out cracked in the middle. "Fire is hoard. Fire is the *first* hoard, the one a dragon is given before he is given a name, and no dragon parts with it — not for love, not for ransom, we do not *give fire away*, it is the one—" his claws closed on the seat-back in front of him, and the seat-back filed no complaint— "it is the one thing that cannot be asked for, because to ask is to admit you are empty, and to give is to make yourself less forever. The rite of first-fire passes from nest-fire to hatchling because a *fire* has no self to spend. What that boy just did—"

**WHAT THE BOY DID,** said the screen, **WAS GIVE HIS OWN, WITHOUT BEING ASKED, WITHOUT KNOWING THE PRICE. UNDER NEST LAW, FIRE FREELY GIVEN IS THE OLDEST BOND THERE IS. THE RITE HAS ONE STEP REMAINING.**

"The name-gift," Foom said. He sat down slowly, like a landslide changing its mind. "No. Surely the whelp doesn't know the — he's a *cold-throat*, no one will have taught him—"

On screen, the fledgling had gone very still, firelight moving on his storm-grey scales. And then Issei flinched, and put a hand to his own temple, and the theatre heard it the way he heard it — a voice arriving from inside, young and rough and shaking with the size of what it was doing:

***{My name is Beaucephalis,}*** said the fledgling, mind to mind, giving away the only hoard he had ever owned. ***{It was my mother's last word. I have never told anyone. Now it is yours too.}***

The theatre did not breathe.

"...okay," said Issei Hyoudou, out loud, wet-eyed, entirely failing to rise to the occasion in the way that was, the room had long since concluded, his actual superpower. "Okay. Wow. So — I'm Issei. I don't have a secret name. You can have the regular one twice."

The silence on the mountainside lasted four full seconds. Then a sound came out of the fledgling that no one in the theatre had ever heard a dragon make, and it took the room a moment to identify it, because it was so foreign to everything the word *dragon* was supposed to carry.

He was laughing.

**[It is done,]** said Ddraig, over the sound, and there was something in the old voice like a door being propped open. **[Witnessed by the Welsh Dragon, who has seen every rite this custom owns and never once seen it done like THIS. Congratulations, partner. You have a brother. He is going to eat everything in your parents' house.]**

**RITE COMPLETE,** stamped the screen. **BEAUCEPHALIS HYOUDOU: NEST-BROTHER. BOND: TELEPATHIC, PERMANENT, MUTUAL. RANGE: ANY.**

And then, because the machine had learned exactly how this audience grieved and exactly how to stop them, it cut to the present day: dawn over the Hyoudou household, a storm-grey dragon the size of a bus arranged proprietarily across the roof, and Issei's voice floating up from a window in the key of every younger sibling in every universe:

*"BEAU. You ate my pudding. I KNOW it was you, I can hear you being smug FROM HERE—"*

***{The nest shares food,}*** came the telepathic reply, unbearably prim. ***{It's the law. I don't make the law, brother.}***

*"You QUOTE the law like a LAWYER—"*

The theatre laughed — the whole-room laugh, Hela to the cheap seats — and in the wisteria box Koneko said, to no one and everyone, "He does the voice better than the screen does," and Kuroka draped an arm around her little sister and did not say anything at all.

---

The lights came up to half, and the arguments started where the machine had aimed them.

"NEST-BROTHER!" Thor was already standing, already radiant, already halfway into a toast with nothing in his hand. "Do you hear it, Loki? The boy has a brother of the FIRE, sworn on a mountain, witnessed by an elder wyrm! In Asgard we'd feast a week for less! Oaths of brotherhood are the oldest oaths — older than kingship, older than—"

"Older than honesty, certainly," said Loki, who had not stood. He was examining the paused frame — dragon on the roof, boy in the window — with the fixed expression of a man being shown a mirror at a bad angle. "Do let's be precise about what we just watched. A creature no nest would take, collected by someone with power to spare, on an impulse, without being consulted — and now it's family, and everyone applauds, and no one asks the obvious question." His voice stayed light. His knuckles didn't. "What happens on the day the boy regrets the impulse? Adoption is a story the strong tell the stray. The stray spends the rest of his life waiting to hear how it *ends*."

The theatre went carefully quiet. There was exactly one being in the room who could answer that, and everyone knew who he was, and he was already turning in his seat.

"Yes," Thor said. "I imagine that is precisely how it feels."

"Don't—"

"Father chose you once," Thor said, "and told you badly, and late, and that was his failure and it was real. But *I* am not our father, and I did not choose you once." He was not performing now; the hall-toast voice was gone, and what was left was worse, because it was just a brother talking. "I have chosen you every day for a thousand years. Including the days you stabbed me. Including the day you were a snake. I chose it again this morning, when you took the end seats so you could pretend you might leave." He turned back to the screen, and let it stand. "*That* is what the fire-gift is, trickster. Not the impulse. The mornings after it. Ask the grey one on the roof whether the boy has ever once made him wait to hear how the story ends."

Loki said nothing at all. But those seated closest observed that he did not move from the end seats — and that he did not, for the rest of the session, sit as though he might leave.

From the wisteria box, a voice — deadpan, small, and carrying to every corner of the theatre because the theatre wanted it to:

"Blood is who you start with," said Koneko Toujou, whose blood sister sat beside her with an arm around her shoulders, and whose road back to that arm the theatre had watched paid for in full. "Nest is who you keep."

The betting board quietly retired the 2:1 line and did not offer a replacement.

The wheel spun and stopped on honest silver.

**"DRAGON-NEST CUSTOMS" — RARITY: RARE.**

"A rules pull," said Sam Wilson. "It knows we love the rules pulls. We're being managed."

They were, and it worked anyway. The screen laid itself out like a codex — the theatre had seen it do this for the Evil Piece system in Arc Two — and began, with visible relish, to legislate:

**NEST LAW. THE CUSTOMS OF DRAGON KINSHIP, AS PRACTICED SINCE BEFORE THE PRACTICE OF WRITING THINGS DOWN.**

**ONE. THE NEST IS CHOSEN. BLOOD MAY APPLY FOR MEMBERSHIP.**

The laugh started in the Ravager balcony and rolled downhill.

**TWO. FIRST-FIRE IS KINDLED FROM THE NEST-FIRE. FIRE FREELY GIVEN MAKES KIN. FIRE CANNOT BE ASKED FOR, ONLY OFFERED.**

**THREE. THE NAME-GIFT. KIN EXCHANGE TRUE NAMES, MIND TO MIND. THEREAFTER THEY SPEAK ACROSS ANY DISTANCE, AND NEITHER IS EVER ENTIRELY ALONE, WHICH IS THE POINT.**

**FOUR. HOARD-RIGHT. NEST-KIN TAKE FROM THE HOARD WITHOUT ASKING. THEFT IS A CRIME THAT ONLY EXISTS OUTSIDE THE NEST.**

"*Imprecise,*" snapped Fin Fang Foom, who had been vibrating with the suppressed agony of an expert watching amateurs handle his field, and could bear it no longer. "Hoard-right extends to the *shelf* of the hoard — food, shelter, comfort, coin — never to the *heart* of it. Every dragon keeps a heart-hoard of things that are not property. No kin touches those. The whelp's pudding is shelf. The whelp's *name* was heart, and he gave it anyway, which is why the rite—" he stopped, aware of the entire theatre listening to him explain, with helpless scholarly tenderness, the customs of a species he'd spent twenty-eight chapters disowning.

The screen amended its own caption: **FOUR (REVISED, PER MAKLUAN CONSULTATION): SHELF, NOT HEART.**

"Stop *agreeing* with me," Foom told the screen, in Karnak's exact aggrieved cadence, and three rows of Inhumans turned to look at Karnak, who declined to acknowledge the citation.

**FIVE. NEST-DEBT. YOUR BROTHER'S FIGHT IS YOUR FIGHT. YOU MAY ARRIVE UNINVITED. YOU MAY NOT ARRIVE LATE.**

**SIX. NEST-SEVERANCE. THE ONE UNFORGIVABLE CRIME: TO FEED ON YOUR OWN NEST. THE SEVERED ARE NOT HUNTED AND NOT HARMED. THEY ARE SIMPLY NEVER SPOKEN TO AGAIN, AND THEY BURN ALONE.**

The balcony had gone quiet somewhere around Five.

"Krags," said Yondu Udonta, slowly, "you readin' this?"

"Readin' it, Cap'n."

"Read it back to me in *ours*."

Kraglin counted it off on his fingers, half-grinning, and the grin thinning as he went. "Crew's who you pick, not who spawned you. You fly your colors 'cause somebody gave 'em to you, you don't buy 'em. Crew shares the take. You come when a Ravager whistles, don't matter whose territory. And if you..." He slowed. "If you eat off your own crew. Deal in what a Ravager don't deal in. You get your flame put out, and hundred ships don't say your name no more."

"Huh," said Rocket, from the seat he had annexed at the balcony rail on the grounds that he was, quote, *dragon-adjacent, it's a whole thing, don't look it up.* "So the pirate code and the dragon code are the same code. Somebody owes somebody royalties."

"Ain't no code," said Yondu.

He said it quietly, and the balcony turned, because Yondu Udonta did not do quiet. He was looking at the codex-screen with his jaw set, and every soul in that balcony knew exactly which line he was looking at, and exactly which years of his life it was reading back to him — and one row behind, Stakar Ogord, who had spoken the severance once and unspoken it harder, put one hand on the back of Yondu's seat. Not on his shoulder. On the seat. It was the same gesture, in Ravager.

"Ain't no code," Yondu said again. "Never was. Codes is what you write down 'cause you're scared you'll forget. That grey lizard on the roof, this whole damn theatre-full of that boy's people — wives, dragon, church girl, fox kid, all of it—" he flicked two fingers at the screen, at the roof, at the dawn. "That ain't law. That's just the folks you feed. Dragons just got the guts to put it in writing."

"He's Mary Poppins with scales, y'all," Kraglin offered, wetly, and was ignored with great love.

The screen, as if it had been waiting for the balcony to finish, delivered its closing argument. The codex dissolved, and in its place the machine did something it had never done: it re-ran the story so far — twenty-seven chapters in as many seconds — with the nest-law diagram laid over the top. Rias's eight Pawns: *fire freely given.* Asia at the Hyoudou dinner table: *shelf and heart alike.* The peerage, the wives' box, Kunou, Ophis, the adopted and the sheltered and the kept: node after node after node, kin-lines igniting one by one across the whole map of a sixteen-year-old's life until it blazed like a city seen from orbit.

**FINDING,** said the screen. **THE SUBJECT HAS BEEN BUILDING A NEST SINCE CHAPTER ONE. HE HAS NEVER ONCE CALLED IT THAT. HE HAS NEVER ONCE STOPPED.**

And then, for exactly one frame — one — the diagram stayed on screen but the *nodes* changed, and what the kin-lines connected was not Issei's people but a seating chart. Mezzanine. Aisles. Boxes. Balcony. Forty thousand seats, mapped in the same diagram-language, kin-lines dim and unlit, like a city before dawn. Like something waiting to be kindled.

Then it was gone, and most of the theatre would swear it had imagined it.

Uatu the Watcher did not swear that.

---

The last thing the machine showed them was the roof at dusk: Beaucephalis, storm-grey and content, and Issei sprawled against his brother's flank with a pudding cup he was defending with his whole body, and the two of them bickering mind-to-mind about nothing — the theatre given subtitles for it, sibling nonsense, *your roof*, *OUR roof*, *the LAW, brother* — until, mid-sentence, the young dragon's head came up.

He looked at the horizon. The subtitles stopped.

The horizon had wings in it. Dozens. Then more than dozens — a sky filling from every direction with shapes out of every mythology the theatre's oldest members had personally survived, all of them inbound, all of them slow, the way you fly when you are coming to something rather than at it.

***{Brother,}*** said Beaucephalis, and the young voice had gone formal, and underneath the formality it was shaking again — pride, this time. ***{They're coming to see you. All of them.}***

*"...why?"* said Issei Hyoudou, pudding forgotten.

The screen went dark on the question. In the dark, Fin Fang Foom rose to his feet, all eleven seats, staring at where the horizon had been with an expression the theatre had no file for at all.

"Foom," said Shang-Chi carefully. "What did we just see assembling?"

"Everyone," said Fin Fang Foom.
