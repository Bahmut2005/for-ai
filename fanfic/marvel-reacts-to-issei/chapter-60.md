## Part One: The Cosmic Block

The theatre had changed. Something in the atmosphere had shifted over the past fifty-nine chapters, some indefinable pressure that had been steadily mounting. The cosmic entities—the tier of being that did not usually deign to react to anything—sat with the focused intensity of predators scenting blood.

Galactus occupied his seat in the ventral rows, that monstrous, geometric form still and coiled like a spring. The Living Tribunal had ceased its meditative silence and now studied the screen with what might have been tension in its triplicate eyes. The Celestials, those ancient architects who had watched countless civilizations rise and crumble, had arranged themselves in an unusual configuration—closer together than was their norm, their forms radiating an energy that made the theatre's very walls hum.

The Watchers—Uatu chief among them—exchanged glances that spoke of recognition. Of knowing what was about to unfold.

The Thor-block (for such it had become across five arcs) was absolutely silent. The God of Thunder sat with his arms crossed, Odin beside him, both kings of pantheons wearing the same expression: the look of those who sense a change in the cosmic order.

Professor Xavier sat in the X-Men's section, his hands folded before him, his psychic presence a low, constant hum in the shared consciousness of his team. "Something is coming," he said to them silently. "Can you feel it? The theatre is afraid."

Afraid was not a word that fit a supernatural gacha system. And yet.

The screen remained dark. The wheel did not spin.

Instead, a single line of text appeared, burning white against the black:

**THE DRAGON ASCENDS. THE THEATRE ASKS: WHAT IS HE CAPABLE OF?**

Below it, a timer began to count down from ten seconds.

## Part Two: Gacha Pull One — Diabolos Dragon God vs. Rizevim

The timer reached zero. The screen erupted.

**DIABOLOS DRAGON GOD VS. RIZEVIM LUCIFER**

*Ultra Rare — 0.3% acquisition rate*

The battle opened not with preamble but with **transformation**.

Issei stood in the High School DxD verse, his body already in full consciousness of what he was becoming. Rizevim Lucifer—possessor of the Sacred Gear Canceller, the power that nullified the supernatural itself—regarded him with absolute certainty. The Canceller glowed in his hand, an artifact of such fundamental negation that even gods had learned to fear it.

Issei closed his eyes.

And then—the transformation.

It was not the bright crimson of Scale Mail. It was not the tactical precision of Cardinal Crimson. It was something **other**.

Crimson-black armor materialized across his body like living shadow and living fire intertwined. Not forged by a dragon—*worn by infinity*. The armor's surface was smooth as obsidian in some places, jagged and fractal in others, as if reality itself could not quite decide what shape to give a form powered by Ophis's infinite power.

Four wings unfurled from his back—not the leather-veined draconic wings of Scale Mail, but wings of **pure cosmic energy**, each one trailing wisps of power that seemed to bend the visible light around them. They were the wings of something that had transcended the physical entirely, that existed partially in this realm and partially in others.

His eyes opened. They were **gold** and **black** simultaneously, a pupil-less void that contained everything and nothing.

"Rizevim," Issei said, and his voice was different. It was his voice, but underneath it—layered beneath like harmonics—was the voice of something vast and ancient. It was Ddraig, the Welsh Dragon. It was Ophis, whose power was *infinity itself*. They spoke through him, with him, as him.

Rizevim's expression flickered. Just once. Just for a moment. But it flickered.

He raised the Canceller. Energy surged—a wave of negation that unmade the supernatural, that stripped power from existence itself.

Issei did not move.

The negation wave hit him and—

**Failed.**

The Canceller's power broke against Diabolos Dragon God like waves against stone. Worse—it *bounced*, rippling back toward Rizevim in a cascade of unresolved force that sent him skidding backward across the battlefield, blood spraying from a wound that should not have been possible against a god-tier being with the universe's ultimate nullification power.

Internal monologue bloomed across the screen, Issei's thoughts rendered visible:

*I am beyond cancellation now. This power does not belong to me—it belongs to what I carry. This is Ophis. This is the power of infinity, and infinity cannot be cancelled. It cannot be negated. It exists beyond the framework of power and nullification. This is Ddraig, and Ddraig is every dragon ever to claim supremacy. This is what happens when a dragon stops counting the cost.*

Issei raised his hand. Reality bent. Space folded. The **Infinity Blaster** began to charge—not a beam, but a *cascade of dimensional collapse*, a weapon that didn't merely destroy but *unmade* the target at a fundamental level.

The power buildup was visible even to the Marvel theatre. The air around Issei became thick, almost syrupy with accumulated force. The ground beneath him cracked and splintered. The sky above developed fractures, as if reality itself was about to split open.

Rizevim, bleeding and furious, activated the Canceller again. He poured everything into it—his own godhood, his stolen powers, the fundamental negation that had broken a hundred supernatural artifacts.

Issei fired.

The Infinity Blaster was not a beam of light. It was a *line of nothing*—a path through existence that denied the existence of everything in its way. It reached Rizevim's position and—

Rizevim was simply **gone**. Not killed. Not destroyed. He was erased from the battle, pulled backward through space-time and thrown into a restraint field that Marvel's observers realized was part of the DxD verse's own binding magic.

The Infinity Blaster faded. Issei lowered his hand.

And then—Issei *felt it*. The form destabilizing. His own existence fracturing.

Internal monologue again, this time darker:

*This form is beautiful and it is *fatal*. Every second I hold this shape, I can feel my own being unraveling. Not the power—my *self*. My identity. My consciousness fragmenting under the weight of carrying infinity. I am dissolving into Ddraig. Into Ophis. Into the infinite power that is not mine to hold, only to borrow. I can feel the edges of my awareness cracking. If I maintain this form much longer, there will be nothing left of Issei Hyoudou. Only the dragons. Only the power. Only the void where a person used to be.*

Issei cancelled the Diabolos Dragon God form. He collapsed to his knees, bleeding cosmic energy, trembling with the aftershock of temporary self-dissolution.

The screen went dark.

## Part Three: Theatre Reaction to Pull One

Silence.

Not the contemplative silence of cosmic entities. But **horror**.

The street-level heroes—Spider-Man, Daredevil, the defenders—had gone pale. The power gap between what they could comprehend and what they had just witnessed was so vast it approached meaninglessness.

But it was the silence from the cosmic block that was truly terrifying.

Galactus had **moved**. The massive entity had shifted in its seat, an adjustment so minute it should have been invisible. But every consciousness in the theatre felt it—recognition, acknowledgment, the awareness that it had just seen something approaching its own tier of existence.

The Living Tribunal remained motionless, but its three faces had turned toward each other in conference.

Odin leaned over to Thor, his single eye narrowed. "Did you understand what you just saw?"

"No," Thor said quietly. "I know what I saw—power beyond comprehension. But I cannot hold the meaning of it in my mind. It slips away."

The Watchers were communicating via channels beyond normal perception, their ancient telepathy singing through dimensions. Uatu's expression was something between awe and concern.

Professor Xavier had gone very still. When he spoke to his X-Men, his mental voice was shaken. "I cannot read him in that state. I attempted contact during the transformation and there is a *wall*—not hostile, but *other*. A consciousness so fundamentally alien to our own that my telepathy rebounds from it like a stone from steel. That is not human thought. That is *dragon* thought. Vastly older. Vastly more dangerous."

Jean Grey had her hands clenched in her lap. "The power level when he fired that final blast... it was touching Omega-tier multiversal scales. And he *survived it*. Just barely. But he survived."

## Part Four: Gacha Pull Two — Pseudo Dragon Deification vs. Vidar

The wheel spun again. The theatre had not fully recovered from the first pull. Many were still processing what they had witnessed.

The screen illuminated.

**PSEUDO DRAGON DEIFICATION VS. VIDAR THE GOD-KILLER**

*Legendary — 1.5% acquisition rate*

A different battlefield now. The canon had moved forward, and Issei had achieved something that the first form had never granted: **stability**.

Through spiritual discipline—through a treatment called Simplified Amrita that Marvel's cosmic physicians leaned forward to study with genuine interest—Issei had learned to stabilize the Pseudo Dragon Deification form. Not the infinite power of Diabolos. But something that could be *held*, that could be *sustained*, that did not require the dissolution of self to maintain.

Issei's second transformation was a revelation of *control*.

The armor that manifested was similar to Diabolos Dragon God in basic architecture, but where Diabolos was chaotic and reality-breaking, Pseudo Dragon Deification was *elegant*. The crimson-black colors were there, but they maintained consistent form. The four wings manifested, but they held true to physics in ways that Diabolos's wings had refused.

The form was still god-tier. It still radiated power that made the air shimmer.

But it was **manageable**.

Vidar emerged—a god-killer, one of the few beings in the DxD verse capable of threatening even the highest pantheons. He was brutal, efficient, ancient with the weight of eons.

The battle that followed lasted a full three minutes in the theatre's time-stream—an eternity for combat at this scale.

Issei did not obliterate Vidar. Instead, they fought. True combat. Blow for blow. Vidar's anti-god weapons clashed against Pseudo Dragon Deification's scaled armor. Neither warrior was overwhelmed. Neither yielded.

Landscapes reshaped themselves. Mountains crumbled. Oceans boiled. But the form *held*. Issei maintained perfect consciousness, perfect control. He was still *himself*, even holding infinite power.

Internal monologue:

*This is the form I can sustain. This is the shape I can wear without burning away the person inside it. Diabolos Dragon God is power without limit, but it costs me everything. Pseudo Dragon Deification is power without recklessness—it costs me time. Eighty-eight minutes. I can hold this for eighty-eight minutes and remain myself on the other side. This is the compromise between the infinite and the mortal. This is the form that makes me a warrior instead of a weapon.*

The battle ended not in victory for either side, but in a stalemate. Vidar had managed wounds. Issei had managed wounds. Both warriors recognized the other as a true peer in combat.

Pseudo Dragon Deification faded. Issei stood, breathing hard, but still *there*—still himself, still conscious, still *Issei*.

The screen displayed: **88-MINUTE DURATION LIMIT MAINTAINED. WARRIOR INTEGRITY PRESERVED.**

## Part Five: Theatre Reaction to Pull Two

The reaction to the second pull was different from the first.

It was not horror.

It was **profound unease**.

Professor Xavier's attempt to read Issei's mind had repeated during the Pseudo Dragon Deification transformation—and this time, he managed to brush the edge of that consciousness. Just barely. Just enough to feel it.

He opened his eyes, and his expression was one of deep concern.

"What did you sense?" Storm asked him.

"Scale," he said quietly. "And not the kind that exists in this universe. I felt *consciousness* on a scale that made the Omega-level mutant collective seem like children. I felt something that had been alive for longer than human civilization. And it was using a human body as a glove. Wearing it like clothing. The person inside was... present. But so dwarfed by what he was containing that it seemed almost quaint. As if a human being was piloting a god, rather than becoming one."

Thor was on his feet. "That's not right. A human cannot hold that form and remain unchanged. Something was different between the first and second transformation."

Odin's eye was narrowed. "He was learning," the All-Father said quietly. "In the first transformation, he was discovering what power felt like. In the second, he was learning to *survive* it without losing himself. The Simplified Amrita—some technique from his verse that stabilizes divine consciousness. He is teaching himself to *be* at that power level."

Galactus had shifted again. Not much. But enough that the message was clear: *I acknowledge a peer*.

The Living Tribunal's conference had intensified. The three faces rotated through expressions that had no clear translation, but the intensity was undeniable.

## Part Six: Gacha Pull Three — True Dragon Deification vs. Angra Mainyu

The wheel spun one final time. The theatre was tense. Everyone understood what was happening: an **escalation**.

The screen burned white.

**TRUE DRAGON DEIFICATION VS. ANGRA MAINYU**

*Legendary — 1.5% acquisition rate*

This was different from what had come before.

This was the **final form**.

Issei's third transformation did not announce itself with armor manifestation or wing-unfolding. Instead, it happened at a level of reality so fundamental that the theatre's projection struggled to render it.

Great Red emerged from within Issei—not possessing him, but *resonating* with him. The flesh of the infinite dragon, the being who had transcended duality itself, aligned with Issei's human form. For a moment, they occupied the same space. The same existence.

When the transformation completed, Issei was no longer wearing divine armor. He *was* divine armor, or rather, he was something beyond armor entirely. His body had become partially draconic—not a full dragon transformation, but a **transcendence**. Wings manifested from his back, but they were not armor. They were *real*—scales and sinew and the terrible beauty of something that had evolved across eons. His eyes were pure gold, no longer containing black voids. His skin had developed a faint iridescence that spoke of power held in perfect equilibrium.

Angra Mainyu rose to meet him—end-of-world incarnate, the evil that existed to unmake creation itself.

And then—

They fought.

The battle was **cosmically devastating**. Reality fractured. The concept of location became meaningless. The battleground existed simultaneously in the DxD universe and in dimensions adjacent, overlapping, parallel. Stars were born and died in the background. Galaxies wheeled. The very laws of physics negotiated with one another for the right to govern the space where these two beings clashed.

True Dragon Deification was not faster than Angra Mainyu. It was not stronger in any direct sense. But it was **complete**. It was perfect synthesis—Great Red's infinite existence, Ddraig's draconic sovereignty, and Issei's human will all speaking as one. There was no fragmentation, no dissolution, no cost to the self.

Internal monologue, transcendent:

*I am Great Red's echo. I carry the dreams of a dragon older than pantheons. I am the bridge between what is and what could be. I am the Red Dragon Emperor, but I am also the dream of the infinite dragon. I am what happens when mortality and infinity learn to dance together. I am—*

The monologue cut off as Issei struck. Not with weapons. With *being*. With the simple fact of his existence overwriting Angra Mainyu's existence.

Angra Mainyu was **defeated**. Not merely overcome, but *unmade*. The evil was stripped away, bound, imprisoned in a seal that Great Red's own flesh helped to establish.

The victory was *final*.

And then—**Hades struck**.

The God of Underworld emerged from the ether, and Issei, already depleted from the Angra Mainyu confrontation, barely managed to defend. The two clashed, and though Issei held, the cost was *visible*. Cosmic energy ran like blood from wounds that should have been impossible on a being of this scale.

Issei stood, trembling, barely holding form. True Dragon Deification flickered. Cracked. Faded.

The screen displayed, in letters that seemed to burn:

**ISSEI HYOUDOU RECOGNIZED AS SUPER DEVIL**

And then a sub-display:

**SCAR DETECTED. SOURCE CLASSIFIED. PROBABILITY: UNWRITTEN DEVOURER. SEVERITY: CONTAINED BUT UNRESOLVED.**

## Part Seven: The Cosmic Silence

The theatre went quiet.

Not the silence of confusion. But the silence of awe. The silence that follows the perception of something that rewrites the parameters of reality.

Galactus **stood**.

The massive entity rose from its seat, a movement so fundamental that the theatre's structure itself trembled. The being who consumed worlds, who had turned civilizations to dust without thought, stood in recognition of something that had just demonstrated power on a scale that approached its own.

The Celestials **began to whisper**.

And here was the horror: the whispers were *not translated*. The theatre's interface, which had translated every language, every thought, every communication that had ever occurred within its walls, suddenly went silent. The Celestials spoke in their own language—incomprehensibly old, incomprehensibly vast—and the theatre *refused* to provide subtitles. Whatever they were saying, it was not for the comprehension of lesser beings.

The Living Tribunal made a **formal announcement**:

**CLASSIFICATION ATTEMPT FAILED. SUBJECT DEFIES COSMIC TAXONOMY. ISSEI HYOUDOU CANNOT BE CLASSIFIED WITHIN EXISTING COSMIC HIERARCHY. NEW FRAMEWORK REQUIRED.**

The Watchers stood. All of them. Uatu and the others who monitored creation itself simply *stood*, acknowledging something that had transcended their usual categories of observation.

Thor was pale. Odin's single eye was wide—not with fear, but with the recognition of someone watching the order of the universe shift.

The gods—Zeus, Amaterasu, and the dozen other pantheon heads scattered throughout the theatre—exchanged looks that spoke of understanding that *something* in the cosmic order had just changed fundamentally.

The X-Men's psychic collective had gone completely silent. Professor Xavier's hands were shaking.

"What," Storm whispered, "is he?"

"Something new," Xavier said quietly. "The universe has not seen his like in eons, if ever. He is not a god. He is not a dragon, though he carries dragons within him. He is not mortal, though he began as one. He is... something the cosmic framework does not have a word for."

Jean Grey was staring at the faded screen, at the display that still showed: **SCAR DETECTED.**

"There's something else," she said. "Did you see it? That last note. Something hurt him. At *that* level of power. Something so dangerous that when it wounded him, Issei's own response was to *erase his memories of it*."

"Impossible," Charles said.

"And yet," Jean replied, "it's on the screen. True Dragon Deification bears wounds from something. Something classified as 'Unwritten Devourer.' Severity: contained but unresolved."

The implications hung in the air like a thundercloud.

## Part Eight: The Hook to Arc Seven

The theatre's lights dimmed. The screen faded to black. But before the darkness could consume the theatre entirely, a banner appeared, burning with letters of gold:

**ARC SEVEN — GODS, DEVILS AND COSMIC JUDGMENT**

**THE ROOM HAS NOW SEEN ENOUGH POWER. THE REMAINING CHAPTERS CONCERN THE TRUTH.**

**What injured a being that can defeat gods? What mystery is worth erasing from a dragon's own memory? What does it mean that such a wound remains unhealed, even at the level of True Dragon Deification?**

**The Marvel multiverse will not rest until the answers are known.**

The banner faded.

The theatre returned to silence—but it was a different silence now. Not the silence of discovery. But the silence of understanding that they had reached a **inflection point**. That the nature of what they were watching had shifted fundamentally.

The question was no longer: "How powerful can Issei become?"

The question was now: "What is he protecting Marvel from?"
