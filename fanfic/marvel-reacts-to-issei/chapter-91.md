## THE RETRIEVAL

The Theatre has changed. No longer are the seats arranged for grand observation of distant battles. Now they form something closer to a funeral gathering. The lighting is dimmer, more contemplative. Cosmic entities have moved closer to the central stage, as if proximity to truth demands a sacrifice of distance.

The wives sit in a block that Rias has claimed, and no one questions this. They are no longer observers. They are witnesses to reckoning.

On the stage, a single object floats in stasis: a child's drawing, framed in light.

It was found in the Theatre's archive, one of thousands of temporal fragments the Theatre collects from erased timelines. The paper is old—not aged, but carrying the quality of something that *should* have been discarded ages ago but wasn't. The drawing is crude, made by young hands perhaps eight or nine years old. It depicts three figures: a tall man with red spiky hair and a tired smile, a woman with black hair and eyes that somehow contain starlight, and a smaller figure—another child—holding both their hands.

At the bottom, in careful childish script: *"Family Day - Daddy said I'd be strong like him"*

The date on the frame is marked as 2047.

Twenty-one years in the future from the current timeline.

## THE NAME

A voice speaks from the Theatre itself. It is not the usual mechanical statement. This voice carries weight—sadness, perhaps, or the closest thing such an entity can experience to regret.

**"THE CHILD IN THIS FRAME WAS DESIGNATED: AIRI HYOUDOU."**

**"BIOLOGICAL TIMELINE DESIGNATION: PRIMARY-TIMELINE-DIVERGENT-BRANCH-2047-STABLE."**

**"PARENTAGE: CONFIRMED ISSEI HYOUDOU AND RIAS GREMORY."**

**"SIBLINGS: SEVEN OTHER CHILDREN, BORN ACROSS TIMELINE BRANCHES BETWEEN 2041 AND 2048."**

**"TIMELINE STATUS: ERASED.**"

Rias stands. The motion is sharp, sudden, as if her body cannot bear to remain still under this weight.

"Erased," she whispers. "By whom?"

**"INTENTIONALLY ERASED BY THE SUBJECT HIMSELF. ISSEI HYOUDOU ACTIVELY DESTROYED THE PRIMARY-2047 TIMELINE AND SIX SUPPORTING BRANCHES TO PREVENT THE EMERGENCE OF AN EXTRADIMENSIONAL ENTITY FROM THAT TIMELINE."**

A holographic display materializes above the stage:

**TIMELINE 2047-A: "THE LOST YEAR"**

- **POPULATION:** 8.2 billion sentient beings
- **NOTABLE FEATURES:** Stable draconic civilizations, integrated supernatural-human societies, established peace accords between cosmic factions
- **CRITICAL EVENT:** Emergence of proto-Devourer entity in year 2071, originating from accumulated timeline erasures
- **CONTAINMENT ATTEMPTS:** Seven separate interventions by Issei and allies; all failed
- **CASUALTIES:** 4.3 billion
- **FINAL OUTCOME:** Issei chose full-timeline erasure to prevent the entity's escape to adjacent timelines

Rias is not the only one standing now. Across the wives' section, every woman has risen. Akeno's hands are trembling. Xenovia has gone pale. Irina's usual composure has shattered. Asia's eyes are filling with tears. Koneko has made a small, wounded sound. Rossweisse looks as though the weight of cosmic knowledge is finally too much. Ravel is gripping the armrest so hard it might splinter.

"He had a daughter," Akeno says quietly. It's not a question.

**"ACROSS THE EIGHT ERASED TIMELINE BRANCHES: FOUR DAUGHTERS, THREE SONS, AND ONE CHILD OF INDETERMINATE GENDER FROM A TIMELINE WHERE SUCH DESIGNATIONS HAD EVOLVED BEYOND BINARY CLASSIFICATION."**

## THE CHOICE

The stage shifts. Now we see not the frame, but a memory—or perhaps a reconstruction, something the Theatre has assembled from fragments across probability space.

We see Issei, older than the current timeline's version by nearly three decades, standing in the center of the 2047 timeline. His hair is long, streaked with gray. His eyes carry the weight of a man who has made peace with atrocities. Around him, children play in a garden—Airi among them, older now, perhaps fifteen, with her mother's eyes and her father's stubborn jaw.

Then the alarms begin.

The proto-Devourer is emerging, and it is using the accumulated love in this timeline as a pathway. Every attachment, every relationship, every memory of joy—these are bridges that the cosmic horror builds itself upon. Because what is love but a kind of erasure? A forgetting of the self in service to another. And the Devourer is made of forgetting.

We watch, through the Theatre's terrible honesty, as Issei makes the calculation.

He can try to seal it in this timeline. But it will escape. It will infect adjacent branches. It will spread like cancer through probability space, and the cost in life will be exponential.

Or.

Or he can do what no parent should ever have to do.

He can unmake his own future. He can erase his own children. He can burn the timeline to cinders rather than let it become a vector for the Devourer's escape.

**"I CHOOSE THE TIMELINES OVER THE TIMELINE,"** a voice says—Issei's voice, but filtered through temporal distortion, as if it's coming from a distance of decades. **"I CHOOSE THE INFINITE OVER THE PERSONAL. I CHOOSE TO NOT EXIST TO SAVE EXISTENCE."**

The children in the garden do not scream. They do not feel pain. They simply cease to be, erased gently, carefully, with the kind of love that only a parent can pour into an act of destruction.

Airi is looking at her father when it happens. In that last microsecond of awareness, her eyes are full of confusion, and trust, and the absolute certainty that whatever is happening, her father will keep her safe.

The timeline collapses. Eight lives become probability shadows. Eight futures become fragments in the Theatre's archive.

## THE REACTIONS

The Theatre is silent.

Not metaphorically. Not dramatically. Literally silent. No sound enters the space. Sound itself seems to have abandoned this moment as inappropriate.

Rias has collapsed into her seat. Her hands are over her face. There is no sound from her, but her shoulders shake with the violence of grief she cannot even articulate. Around her, the other wives are breaking in ways that correspond to their natures: Akeno's lightning sparks weakly, uncontrolled. Xenovia is muttering prayers in every language she knows. Irina's light is flickering, struggling to maintain form. Koneko has returned to her child-form, curled in on herself. Asia is holding Koneko. Rossweisse is gripping the armrest. Ravel stares at nothing, and nothing stares back.

"He killed them," Xenovia whispers. "His own children. He unmade his own future."

"To save ours," Rossweisse says quietly. It is not forgiveness. It is the acknowledgment of an unbearable mathematics.

In the Marvel section, Steve Rogers is standing, fists clenched. "That's—" He stops. He cannot complete the sentence because there are no words. There is only the recognition of a terrible deed done for a terrible necessity.

Tony Stark's calculations have halted. His mind, always moving, is stuck on a single loop: *children, children, children, eight children, all his children, all erased.*

Thanos watches with something that might be respect. He knows the weight of this choice. He has made similar calculus himself. But even he seems momentarily diminished by the scale of it.

"How long has he known?" Magneto asks, his voice hollow.

No one answers because the implications are too vast. Has Issei been carrying this knowledge his entire life? Knowing about futures that could never be reached? Knowing he had children he would never meet? Knowing he had already chosen to kill them to save infinity?

The Watcher speaks, its ancient voice carrying the weight of entities who have witnessed ten thousand timelines end.

**"HE HAS KNOWN FOR THIRTY-SEVEN YEARS. IN HIS PERSONAL TIMELINE, THE EVENT OCCURRED WHEN HE WAS THIRTY-FOUR. HE HAS SPENT THIRTY-SEVEN YEARS MOURNING CHILDREN HE HAD NEVER HELD, GRIEVING FUTURES THAT NEVER EXISTED OUTSIDE HIS MEMORY, CARRYING THE WEIGHT OF EIGHT ERASED LIVES WITH NO ONE TO SHARE THE BURDEN."**

**"UNTIL NOW."**

## THE REVELATION

The Theatre speaks again, and this time its voice is almost gentle.

**"ISSEI HYOUDOU DID NOT BREAK WHEN HE MADE THAT CHOICE. HE DID NOT BREAK WHEN HE ERASED HIS OWN TIMELINES. HE DID NOT BREAK WHEN HE CARRIED THE WEIGHT ALONE FOR THIRTY-SEVEN YEARS."**

**"BUT HE BROKE WHEN HE REALIZED HIS WIVES WOULD EVENTUALLY KNOW. THAT IS THE REAL SACRIFICE. NOT THE ACT OF ERASURE. THE KNOWLEDGE THAT THOSE HE LOVED WOULD LEARN WHAT HE HAD DONE, AND KNOW THAT HE HAD DONE IT ALONE, IN SECRET, WITHOUT GIVING THEM THE CHANCE TO GRIEVE."**

Rias looks up from her hands. Her eyes are red, but her gaze is sharp.

"You're saying he's been grieving eight children he never had," she says slowly, "while lying to us about the reason. While letting us believe those timelines just... didn't happen."

**"YES."**

"And knowing we would find out eventually."

**"YES."**

"Because that's the kind of future-sight he has now. Not just seeing what will happen, but seeing what will be known, and when, and what the cost will be when truth breaks the surface."

**"YES."**

Rias's laugh is terrible, broken, a sound like glass cracking under pressure.

"My husband spent thirty-seven years mourning our children, and knew that someday I would have to mourn them too, and he carried that knowledge alone, and when that knowledge reached me—" Her voice breaks. "That was his real punishment. Not the erasure. Knowing that I would suffer when I learned."

The other wives are watching her, understanding now the full scope of the cruelty. Not the cruelty Issei inflicted—the cruelty of existence itself. The cruelty of being a being of power who can see futures, carry knowledge others cannot carry, make choices that isolate him even from those he loves most.

In the Marvel section, Steve Rogers finally understands. This is why the Theatre called them. This is why Marvel's greatest heroes are needed. Not to fight a cosmic enemy. But to understand that sometimes the greatest enemy is the weight of choices that should never have to be made.

The children's drawing remains on the stage, framed in light. A family that never was. A future that never existed. Eight lives erased so that infinities could continue.

## THEATRE LEDGER — SESSION 91

### **Entry One: The Erased**
Eight timelines. Eight children. Not theoretical casualties or statistical shadows, but real children who drew pictures and believed in their fathers and would have grown into adults with their own ambitions and loves and futures. Issei's choice was mathematically correct—the erasure of eight timelines to prevent the Devourer's escape into infinite others was the only viable calculus. But mathematics does not account for the soul-weight of what it costs a father to unmake his own children. The choice was necessary. The necessity does not make the choice less terrible.

### **Entry Two: The Thirty-Seven Years**
Issei's true horror is not what he did, but what he has carried since doing it. While the wives lived, believed, loved, and dreamed of futures with children, Issei knew the truth: he had already chosen those children out of existence. He carried their absence like a weight in his chest. Every time his wives spoke of future children, Issei knew he was listening to a timeline that might still end in erasure. Every moment of joy in the present was shadowed by the knowledge of futures destroyed. He has been alone with this. The greatest power in the Theatre—the ability to save infinite timelines—has been the source of the most profound isolation.

### **Entry Three: The Wives' Breaking Point**
Rias and her sisters will now face a choice that Issei has already made: do they love him despite this? Do they forgive a sacrifice he made without their consent, for reasons they cannot access, carrying children they never knew existed? Or is love, when tested at this scale, insufficient? The Theatre does not offer answers. It only presents the truth, and the truth is that no relationship survives unbroken when one partner has been carrying such vast and secret grief. What will emerge from this breaking is what matters. Not forgiveness. Not understanding. But the possibility of something new, built on the foundation of what was broken.

### **Entry Four: The Question of Isolation**
If Issei had told them—if he had said, "I have seen eight timelines where we have children, and I have erased them to save existence"—would the burden have been lighter? Or would it have been multiplied? The Theatre presents this not as philosophy but as operational fact: there are griefs too large to be shared, choices too cosmic to be collective. Issei chose isolation not out of cruelty, but out of love. The wives are now learning that love, at its highest and most terrible, often looks like abandonment. This paradox will shape what comes next.
