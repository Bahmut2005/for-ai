# Chapter Twenty-Three: The Gremory Household

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Wakanda (T'Challa, Shuri, Okoye, Ramonda) • Latveria (Doctor Doom) • X-Men (Xavier, Magneto, Storm, Nightcrawler, Rogue, Kitty Pryde) • Diplomats & statesmen (Namor, Everett Ross, Maria Hill, Valkyrie, She-Hulk)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Rias's Peerage Dynamics" — **Rare**
2. "Gremory Family Council" — **Epic**

---

Three nights into the Family-Class banner, the theatre had developed institutions.

It had happened the way institutions always happen: nobody voted, and suddenly they existed. The wives' box — eight thrones of theatre-grown velvet that had assembled themselves the night the gacha misfired and summoned Issei Hyoudou's fiancées into the audience — now had a low brass rail around it and, thanks to Rossweisse, a posted schedule for who was allowed to ask the wives questions and for how long. (The schedule was laminated. No one knew where she had gotten a laminator. No one asked.)

And across the aisle, a second institution had condensed out of the crowd like frost: the diplomats' section. It had begun as Everett Ross dragging a chair over to Maria Hill to compare notes on succession law, and it had accreted — She-Hulk with a legal pad, Valkyrie with a flask and a king's tired eyes, and then, on the high tier above them, the heavy furniture: the Panther Throne's delegation on the left, the Lord of Latveria on the right, and between them, arms folded, standing because sitting implied equality with the room, the Sub-Mariner.

"Two nights of marriage law," Namor said, to no one and therefore to everyone. "The surface world giggling at a boy's betrothals like fishwives. Show me the *structure*. Show me who holds what, and from whom, and what it costs. Then I will tell you what this Gremory girl's house is actually worth."

The gacha wheel came down through the dark with its new domestic chime — that warm little doorbell sound that still made Sam Wilson's eye twitch — and, for once in its smug mechanical life, gave a king exactly what he asked for.

The first token dropped. The bowl rang.

**PULL ONE: "RIAS'S PEERAGE DYNAMICS" — RARE.**

**YOU HAVE SEEN THE PIECES,** the screen wrote. **NOW SEE THE HOUSEHOLD.**

In the wives' box, Rias Gremory sat up very straight, crimson hair sliding off her shoulder, and said, with the dignity of a duchess and the dread of a teenager whose parents own photo albums, "Oh no."

---

The vision opened on snow.

A boy lay dying in it. White-haired winter, a mountain road in Italy, and a child in rags with his breath rattling out of him — and the captions did their brutal economy: **THE HOLY SWORD PROJECT. THE CHURCH'S ATTEMPT TO MANUFACTURE WIELDERS OF EXCALIBUR. THE SUBJECTS WHO FAILED TO MEET SPECIFICATIONS WERE DISPOSED OF. THIS ONE RAN.**

The theatre had watched a boy die in chapter one and had never gotten better at it. But this time the snow lit up crimson, and a girl no older than the dying child stepped out of a teleport circle with her hands already glowing, and the caption said only: **RIAS GREMORY, AGE THIRTEEN, FOUND HIM FIRST.**

"A Knight," she said on-screen, kneeling in the snow, holding out an Evil Piece like it was a blanket. "I need a Knight. Will you live for me instead?"

The vision moved like a hand through photographs. A shrine in Japan and a black-haired girl hiding from men with light-spears, her murdered mother behind her and her fallen-angel blood damning her to both sides at once — **AKENO HIMEJIMA. HUNTED FOR HER FATHER'S WINGS.** — and Rias again, older by a year, offering a hand and asking no questions about the wings at all. A cell in the Devils' capital and a small white-haired girl waiting, without crying, to be executed for her sister's crime — **KONEKO TOUJOU. SENTENCED FOR ANOTHER'S SIN.** — and the Gremory heir arriving with paperwork, a Rook piece, and an expression that several Wakandan generals recognized from their own queen mother. A sealed room in a European castle, garlic hung on the door by his own family, a half-vampire boy who could stop time and had been taught to apologize for existing — **GASPER VLADI. HIS HOUSE FEARED HIS EYES. SHE BOUGHT HIM FROM THEM.**

"Bought him," Magneto repeated, low and dangerous, from the X-Men's tier. "Note the verb. Everyone cooing at this — note the verb."

"Noted," said Charles Xavier quietly. "Keep watching, old friend."

Because the vision had arrived at the clubhouse.

The Occult Research Club, the screen had always called it, and the theatre had always read that as cover identity, headquarters, base. The gacha now corrected the record at length, and the correction was dinner. It was Kiba Yuuto — grown into the kind of handsome that made half the theatre's younger section sit up — burning eggs and being banned from the kitchen by a fourteen-year-old. It was Koneko asleep on Rias's lap while Rias signed territorial paperwork one-handed rather than wake her. It was Akeno and Rias arguing about a rating-game formation like sisters, which is to say viciously and with tea. It was a sealed cardboard box in the corner with eyes glowing through the hand-holes, and Issei Hyoudou — the theatre's boy, their mountain-erasing, spacetime-bruising constant — sitting cross-legged next to the box for hours, talking to it about video games until the vampire inside forgot to be afraid.

"Come on, Gasper," on-screen Issei said, to the box. "You don't have to come out. But if you *do* come out, I'll teach you the thing where we hide Kiba's sword polish."

A pause. The box said, in a tiny voice, "…he cries when he can't find it."

"He *weeps,*" Issei confirmed solemnly, and the box giggled, and the theatre — hardened, ledger-keeping, threat-assessing — made a noise like six hundred people trying not to be charmed and failing on institutional scale.

Then the gacha showed them the night it all became doctrine.

A church, half-destroyed. Kiba Yuuto kneeling among broken Excaliburs with his dead comrades' faces in his eyes — the vision compressed his whole vendetta into a single held breath, the survivor of a poison-gas purge finally facing the sword project that made him — and losing. Coming apart. Choosing revenge over his own life in real time. And Issei Hyoudou grabbing him by the collar, bleeding, furious, and screaming into his perfect face:

"You're not the only one who gets to carry them! You have comrades *now*, you stupid — Rias cried for you! Koneko's *terrified* for you! You're not a stray sword, you're OUR Knight, so live for the ones who died AND the ones who haven't, you absolute — *bishounen!*"

("Did he run out of insult," Kitty Pryde whispered, "and just call him handsome?" "Shh," said Rogue, wiping her eye, "it's workin'.")

It did work. The theatre watched the spirits of murdered children rise out of a broken holy sword and lend their gratitude to the survivor; watched Kiba's Sacred Gear crack past its limit into Balance Breaker; watched a boy forged as a weapon by one institution get reforged as a *person* by another — and the screen stamped the whole vision with a caption that landed in the diplomatic tier like a treaty draft:

**THE EVIL PIECE SYSTEM MAKES SERVANTS. THIS IS THE RULE. THE GREMORY DO NOT COLLECT SERVANTS. THEY KEEP FAMILY. THIS IS THE HOUSE.**

**THE PIECE BINDS. THE HOUSE CHOOSES HOW TO HOLD.**

---

The lights half-rose for the arguing. The theatre had learned the rhythm: the machine liked to let them fight in the intermission.

The X-Men went first, because the X-Men had just been shown their own mirror and everyone knew it.

"A refuge school," said Storm, and her voice had the stillness she reserved for weather about to change, "for children the world calls monsters. Gathered by a wealthy idealist. Trained to master the very gifts they were feared for." She turned her white eyes to Xavier. "Charles. She built yours. At *thirteen.*"

"With one difference," Magneto cut in, rising, cape and conviction. "Charles's students may leave. May graduate, may quit, may slam the door and curse his name — Scott Summers has done all four, twice." (From the X-Men rows: "Hey." — "He's not wrong, Scott.") "These children are *reincarnated into her service.* The piece goes in and does not come out. Strip away the tea and the sleepovers and what remains is the oldest machine in every aristocracy that ever ground my people to ash: hereditary power, purchasing the desperate, and calling the purchase love." He let it ring. "A leash held gently, my friends, is still a leash."

"Objection sustained *and* overruled," said She-Hulk, standing with the legal pad. "Because here's what's weird — I've been building the property-law argument for two nights, and this pull just entered evidence against me. Look at the exit clauses in what we were shown. Koneko was already sentenced to die — the piece commuted it. Kiba was already dead in the snow. Gasper's own blood family kept him in a *sealed room*; his purchaser's first act was unsealing it. Counselor Lehnsherr's leash theory requires that these kids had a better alternative on the table." She flipped the pad around. It said, in large writing, THEY DIDN'T. "You can't call it exploitation when the market rate for these children's lives was *zero* and she bid a piece of her own soul's army anyway."

"The alternative not existing," Magneto said, "is precisely how such systems perpetuate—"

"Yes," said a new voice, "it is."

The theatre turned. Rias Gremory had risen in the wives' box, and the brass rail did not stop her voice from filling the hall, because she had been trained since birth to fill halls.

"Lord Magneto is correct," she said, which surprised the room, and visibly surprised Magneto more. "The Evil Piece system is exactly what he says. It was built to rebuild a decimated species by binding the talented to the noble. Most houses use it exactly as designed. I have sat across rating-game tables from Kings whose pieces flinch when their master's voice rises. I will not defend the system." Her chin came up. "I will defend my *house.* We were taught — my brother, my mother, my father, every Gremory back to the pillar — that a piece is not a purchase. It is a promise you spend the rest of your life keeping. Ask my servants if the leash chafes."

"The fish here is good," said Koneko Toujou, deadpan, without looking up from the theatre-conjured snack she had acquired by unknown means. "Also she's the best thing that ever happened to me. Also Kiba would say something embarrassing and noble right now, so someone should say it for him." A beat. "Consider it said."

"Ara ara," Akeno added, chin on her laced fingers, smiling at Magneto with the precise sweetness of a lightning storm choosing a golf course. "The gentleman in the lovely helmet is welcome to inspect my leash. Many have tried."

Magneto, survivor of camps, breaker of nations, veteran of ten thousand debates, looked at the smiling priestess of thunder, performed an unhurried strategic assessment, and sat down. "The point," he said with dignity, "is entered into the record."

Which was when the heavy furniture spoke.

"Doom will now say what the diplomats are dancing around." The Latverian tier did not rise; Doom seated was somehow taller than most men standing. "The peerage is feudalism. Unapologetic, formalized, superior feudalism — vassalage with the honesty to write its bonds in chess notation rather than hide them in mortgages and employment contracts, as your republics do." The mask surveyed the hall's democratic quadrants with palpable contempt, then returned to the screen. "And the Gremory have solved the one problem feudalism never solved. Loyalty compelled decays; Doom has *watched* it decay, in others' kingdoms. Loyalty *owed to kindness* compounds. The girl does not command her Knight's death — she has made it so he would be *offended* not to offer it. Latveria takes note. This is statecraft."

"That's not statecraft, that's family," said Valkyrie, around the flask, "and the fact that you can't tell the difference, Victor, is the whole biography."

"T'Challa." Namor's arms were still folded. "Your house rules by blood, as mine does. Tell the room the truth its comfortable classes will not enjoy: the girl's kindness is *funded.* Compassion is what a great house purchases after the wars are already won. Her love is real, I do not doubt it — and it is armored in a fortune the size of a small sea. Strip the wealth, and what survives?"

T'Challa had been silent through all of it, watching the screen the way he watched the ancestral plane — as a man being addressed by his predecessors. Now he spoke.

"Strip the wealth," he said, "and you keep the thing the wealth cannot buy, cousin, which we both know because we have both stood in treasure vaults that could not cure our loneliness." He rose, and the diplomats' section quieted. "In Wakanda the throne is blood — and then the blood must stand at Warrior Falls and be *challenged*, every reign, in front of everyone, or it is nothing. Heredity proposes; the people ratify. What I see on that screen is a house that runs the same ritual at the scale of a dinner table. Every meal that girl cooks for her servants, every night she does not wake the sleeping child on her lap — that is her Warrior Falls. Consent, renewed daily, by people with every power to withhold it." He glanced at the wives' box, and inclined his head, king to heir. "The pieces bind. The house chooses how to hold. Your machine writes better law than most parliaments, Miss Gremory."

"It steals it from the good ones," said Rias, and returned the nod, "and shows it to the rest. I'm beginning to suspect that is its entire purpose."

Overhead, the gacha wheel — which had absolutely been waiting for a line that good — dropped the second token.

**PULL TWO: "GREMORY FAMILY COUNCIL" — EPIC.**

"Oh *no,*" said Rias again, with feeling. "Mother, please be normal."

---

The Gremory estate did not fit on the screen, so the screen apologized and gained altitude.

**THE GREMORY TERRITORY,** the captions said, over castle grounds that unrolled past the horizon in every direction, **IS LARGER THAN THE JAPANESE ARCHIPELAGO. THE HOUSE IS ONE OF THE SURVIVING PILLARS OF THE UNDERWORLD'S ARISTOCRACY. ITS SIGIL IS KNOWN FOR THREE THINGS: THE POWER OF DESTRUCTION, THE COLOR CRIMSON, AND AN AFFECTION FOR ITS SERVANTS THAT OTHER GREAT HOUSES CONSIDER AN ECCENTRICITY BORDERING ON SCANDAL.**

("Larger than Japan," Everett Ross said faintly, doing GDP math with his eyes. "The *duchy* is larger than Japan. Maria. The duchy—" "Breathe, Ross.")

The council chamber was crimson and gold and old the way Wakandan vibranium mines were old, and the vision seated its principals with a herald's care. **LORD ZEOTICUS GREMORY** — silver-haired, elegant, with the well-fed serenity of a man whose worst enemies were seating charts. **LADY VENELANA GREMORY** — brown-haired and mild-eyed, and the theatre noted that every other devil in the room tracked her the way sailors track a calm sea, and the caption confirmed the instinct: **BORN OF THE HOUSE OF BAEL. THE POWER OF DESTRUCTION IN THE GREMORY LINE IS HERS.** At the table's end, in white and silver, a maid stood so precisely that Okoye's spine straightened in professional respect — **GRAYFIA LUCIFUGE, HEAD MAID OF THE HOUSE, STRONGEST QUEEN OF THE UNDERWORLD** — and beside the lord's chair, red-haired and radiating the gentle, bottomless good humor of a man who could unmake the room, the elder brother: **SIRZECHS LUCIFER. BORN GREMORY HEIR. SURRENDERED HIS FAMILY NAME TO TAKE THE THRONE OF A SATAN — A CROWN THE NEW UNDERWORLD GRANTS BY POWER AND SERVICE, NOT BY BLOOD.**

The diplomats' section made a sound like forty analysts finding the same footnote at once.

"There it is," breathed Maria Hill. "That's the whole debate in one family. The hereditary house's own firstborn — and the merit system took him, and the house *let* it."

The council's business, the captions explained, was the low-born Pawn.

The theatre watched envoys of two other pillar houses — all cheekbones and heirloom sneers — present their concern with poisonous courtesy: the Gremory heir's *servant*, a reincarnated human of no lineage, was being spoken of in the same breath as the heir herself. Was being *entertained at the family table.* Was rumored — the sneer deepened — to be the reason the heir's prior, politically immaculate engagement had ended with the groom punched through a ceiling. Surely the house understood how this looked. Surely standards—

"I'm told," Lord Gremory interrupted pleasantly, "that when my daughter was taken from that arranged altar, the boy in question fought his way through a Phenex's immortality with a broken body and a borrowed blessing, on the sole logic that she had cried where he could see it." He turned a page of the envoys' own complaint as if it were a wine list. "You are correct that I did not raise my daughter to be won by pedigree. I raised her to be *worth* what that boy paid." He set the page down. "The house's position: Issei Hyoudou dines at our table whenever he can be persuaded to hold the correct fork, which is never, and it is the happiest our table has been in a century. This council is adjourned on that point."

"Husband," said Venelana Gremory, mildly, "you've skipped the important part."

She rose, and the envoys did not quite flinch, and the theatre understood that in this family the Power of Destruction wore pearls. "My children," she said, "were born owning everything. It is the great disease of houses like ours — Bael taught it to me with a whip, and I will not repeat the lesson here. Children who own everything must be *given* the one thing ownership cannot reach: someone who would have loved them poor. My son found it in a maid who outranks every soul in this room." Grayfia's composure flickered by exactly one millimeter, which for Grayfia was a blush. "My daughter has found it in a Pawn who did not know her name was worth anything when he died for it. The house of Gremory does not apologize for treasuring what cannot be inherited." Her mild eyes swept the envoys. "You may tell your pillars we said so, with our love."

And Sirzechs Lucifer, Satan, Super Devil, leaned back in his chair with his eyes closed and said, to the ceiling, in the tone of a man settling an old account, "I gave up this name because the Underworld needed a crown that anyone could earn. Best decision I ever made — and I have regretted it at every family dinner since." He opened his eyes, and the good humor in them had red at the bottom, like coals. "Issei-kun is going to be my brother. The pillars can put *that* in their genealogies."

The vision held one last frame — the whole improbable household on the estate's endless lawn, lords and maids and reincarnated strays and one loud Pawn being ceremonially taught the correct fork by a thirteen-pillar dynasty and getting it wrong on purpose to make Gasper laugh — and the screen wrote, quietly, without capitals for once:

**hereditary power built this table. earned power sits at it. the house cannot tell you which of them holds it up. neither can the house's enemies. that is the trick.**

---

The lights came up on a diplomats' section that had stopped taking notes on the screen and started taking them on each other.

"Doom will concede a single sentence." The hall quieted; Latverian concessions were rarer than the gacha's Secret tier. "The brother is the proof. A merit crown that a hereditary house *willingly feeds its heir to* — that is the only version of aristocracy Doom has ever seen that deserves to survive its founders. Latveria's succession plans are hereby under review." A pause, precisely timed. "The review committee is Doom. The committee's findings are Doom. Nevertheless: under review."

"The Underworld got lucky," Magneto said, but the iron had gone contemplative. "One good family at the top of a rotten machine. It changes nothing structurally. And yet." He looked at the screen a long moment — a boy teaching a vampire courage, a duchess destroying a marriage market with pearls on. "I have burned better-defended systems for less cause than those envoys gave. I find I do not want to burn this one. It is an unfamiliar feeling. I am *watching* it."

"That's called hope, Erik," said Xavier.

"It is called *provisional data,* Charles, and if you smile at me I am leaving the theatre." There was no exit from the theatre. Magneto knew this. It was understood by all parties to be the point of the sentence.

Namor unfolded his arms. The theatre's betting board (Rocket, proprietor) had him at 40-to-1 against conceding anything, and forty rows of gamblers leaned in.

"The mother," the Sub-Mariner said at last, "would have been welcome in Atlantis's war councils, and I have said that of two surface-dwellers in my life." He turned from the screen. "My challenge stands answered. Strip the wealth from that house and the wealth was never the load-bearing wall. Imperius Rex — and *noted.*" (In the back, Rocket paid out, bitterly.)

T'Challa said nothing for a while. His mother had come down from the Wakandan tier during the second pull, and stood now at his shoulder, watching the frozen frame of Venelana Gremory the way one queen mother studies another across an impossible sea.

"He was resurrected into a feudal contract," T'Challa said finally, "by the one house in that world that treats the contract as a cradle. Eight Pawn pieces." He looked across the hall to where Mephisto sat, and the demon — who had priced that transaction in chapter five and been dining out on the arbitrage ever since — lifted a clawed hand in weary acknowledgment before the king even asked.

"Yes, yes," Mephisto sighed. "The market has updated. I believed the girl saw the dragon and overpaid on *insider information.* I now believe something far more fiscally upsetting." He steepled his fingers. "The Gremory always overpay. For family, they structurally, doctrinally, *deliberately* overpay — the maid who is a queen, the heir traded to a merit throne, eight pieces for one dying pervert — and they have compounded that idiotic strategy into the richest and most beloved house in their Underworld." His smile returned, thin and professionally wounded. "Somewhere in that is an insult to everything I do for a living. I am *choosing* not to locate it precisely."

"And that," said Okoye, to Ramonda, quietly, in Xhosa, subtitled by a theatre that missed nothing, "is why the boy fights the way he fights. We have all been asking what he is loyal *to.* It is not the girl. It is not the piece. It is the table."

Ramonda nodded slowly. "You do not break a man like that by defeating him," she said. "You would have to defeat the table. All of it. Every chair."

The screen, which had been listening — the screen was always listening — dimmed to warm gold, and the doorbell chime rang, and the next banner's tease rose in the cozy font that fooled no one:

**NEXT: THE PAWN AND THE BISHOP. "ASIA'S HUSBAND."**

In the wives' box, Asia Argento turned the color of the Gremory sigil and attempted to hide behind Xenovia, who did not understand the maneuver and helpfully stepped aside. Seven fiancées turned toward Asia with the loving, terrible unanimity of a family that has found the photo album.

"Oh *no,*" said Asia.

"It gets us all eventually," said Rias, patting her hand, duchess to sister. "Chin up. It's the house rule."

The lights went down on a theatre that had come to argue about power, and stayed to envy a dinner table.
