# Chapter Nineteen: Dragon Pride

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Thor • Namor • Doctor Doom • The Ravagers (Yondu, Kraglin, Stakar Ogord)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Accepting a Duel Because of Pride" — **Rare**
2. "Friends Restraining Him" — **Common**

---

The theatre had spent the previous chapter learning arithmetic.

That was how Bruce Banner had described it, anyway — quietly, to no one, while the screen tallied what every Boost actually cost the boy's body: torn muscle, burned reserves, a heart doing work it was never budgeted for. *The Price of Boosting* had not been an exciting chapter. It had been a ledger chapter, and the room had left it subdued, the way people leave a doctor's office holding a pamphlet.

So when the lights shifted tonight, the audience checked the weather out of habit — and found something new.

Not the storm-grey of tragedy. Not the buttery gold of comedy. The theatre had gone the deep, banked red of a forge left unattended. It was warm the way an open oven is warm: pleasant right up until you understood it as a warning.

In the high dark of the monster galleries, Fin Fang Foom lifted his head before the wheel had even begun to spin.

"Ahhh," he said, with the satisfaction of a connoisseur. "Tonight the mammals learn what he actually is."

The gacha wheel descended, rotated with none of its usual showmanship — no flourish, no teasing clatter — and dropped its token into the crystal bowl like a judge dropping a gavel.

**PULL ONE: ACCEPTING A DUEL BECAUSE OF PRIDE — RARE.**

"Rare," said Sam Wilson, reading the ceiling. "Him doing something reckless is *Rare?* We've watched this kid punch a fallen angel cadre, swear a blood oath at Heaven, and try to out-stubborn a wolf that eats gods. Reckless is his resting state."

**RECKLESSNESS FOR OTHERS: COMMON,** printed the screen, primly. **RECKLESSNESS FOR HIMSELF: RARE. ATTEND THE DIFFERENCE.**

The room attended. The screen opened.

---

The Underworld, in the vision, was doing its best impression of a summer afternoon — violet sky, training fields of the Gremory territory rolled out below the mountains like a green tablecloth. The audience had seen these fields before: this was where Issei Hyoudou did his failing, daily and on schedule, the honest blue-collar failing that had slowly won over half the theatre.

Today there was a guest instructor.

He arrived with a retinue and a reputation, a High-Class devil in a war-coat older than several member nations of the audience, and the gacha — which had grown fond of statistics — hung his introduction in the air beside him rather than bothering with a name:

**VETERAN OF THE OLD WAR. HIGH-CLASS. FORMAL DUELS: 4,119. FORMAL DUELS LOST: 0.**

"Ah," said Doctor Strange, softly. "One of those."

The Duelist watched the young devils train with the expression of a man inspecting produce. He corrected Kiba's footwork with two words. He nodded once at Koneko, which from him appeared to constitute a parade. And then he came to Issei — mid-collapse, as usual, face-down in the dirt after his four-hundredth gauntlet drill, arms shaking, getting up anyway, because getting up anyway was the entire syllabus of his life.

The Duelist looked at him for a long moment.

"So this is it," he said. "The era's Red Dragon Emperor."

Issei got up. "Yes, sir."

"I fought two of your predecessors." The old devil's voice wasn't cruel. That was the terrible part; the theatre noted it immediately. Cruelty can be dismissed. This was *assessment.* "Belzard nearly killed me in the war. Two strongest hosts the red dragon ever took — and even the weak ones, even the mad ones, were *dragons* the moment the gauntlet woke. And now." He gestured, taking in the dirt, the shaking arms, the boy. "Now the Welsh Dragon rides a Low-Class reincarnate who loses his Rating Games and faints when he boosts. Eight pawn pieces. I heard what Gremory paid, and I laughed. The great Ddraig, reduced to choosing failures. A legend spent on a boy who will die of his own arithmetic before he ever matters."

It would have been fine.

The theatre watched it be almost fine. On-screen, Issei took the insult to himself the way he took all insults to himself — a flinch, a grin, fuel for the next four hundred drills. The audience had seen it a dozen times. Cyclops was already nodding along: *good, use it, that's coachable.*

Then the boy's eyes changed.

It was small. The screen made sure no one missed it. A flicker of ember-light behind brown irises — and on his arm, unbidden, the Boosted Gear shimmered into being, and the green gem was not entirely green. Something older moved in it, red at the edges, like coals breathing under ash.

"Say that about me," Issei said, and his voice came out wrong — level, carrying, with a low double-register underneath it that had never been there before. "Say whatever you want about me. It's all true. But you don't get to say Ddraig chooses failures."

**Partner,** said the gauntlet, quietly. **Partner — wait.**

"I challenge you," said Issei Hyoudou. "Formal duel. Right now."

The training field went silent. Rias Gremory was already moving, already saying his name in the tone of an owner watching her house catch fire — and the Duelist smiled the smile of a man who has been handed exactly what he came for, and spoke the formal acceptance before anyone could reach the boy, and the dueling circle rang shut around them both like a bell.

House rules. Sealed circle. No interference until yield or incapacity.

"Oh no," said Peter Parker, from the street-level rows. "Oh no no no. He's fifteen. Somebody call time out. Does that world have time out?"

"It has yield," said Shang-Chi, watching the boy's stance, which had gone proud and wide and nothing like anything Kiba had taught him. "He will not use it."

---

The duel took four minutes. The theatre experienced it as considerably longer.

It was not a massacre in the way of Fenrir — the audience had already survived watching him bitten nearly in half; they had calibration for horror. This was worse in a way several of them would struggle afterward to name. Fenrir had been an ambush. *This* was a door Issei walked through on his own, with his chin up.

He opened with Scale Mail and every trick the room had learned to cheer for. **[BOOST.] [BOOST.] [BOOST.]** — the crimson armor blazing, the doubling stacking, the mountain-erasing output the gacha had crowned Legendary nine chapters ago. And the Duelist, who had four thousand years of exactly this, took him apart with the unhurried economy of a man carving a roast. Every Boost bought Issei power; the veteran simply declined to be where the power went. Ten seconds is an eternity to someone who has been waiting out dragons since before Rome.

By the second minute Issei was boosting past the line. The audience knew the line now — last chapter had drawn it for them in Banner's careful red ink — and they watched him cross it with his teeth bared. Blood in the helmet. The armor's gem stuttering. **[BOOST,]** said the Gear, and then, for the first time in nineteen chapters, the system voice hesitated: **[BOOST. WARNING. BOOST. WARNING.]**

**Partner, stop,** Ddraig said, and the dragon's voice had lost all its dry slate calm. **Listen to me. Your body is at its end, and that fire in your chest — partner, that fire is not all yours. I know it. I have felt it in twenty-seven hands before yours. It ends the same way every time. STOP.**

"He insulted you," Issei said, through blood, rising again.

**And I am four billion years old and could not care less! Boy — you are the only host who ever cried for ME—**

The Duelist put him down with something almost like regret, an open-palm blast that cratered the boy through the circle's edge and ended the duel by incapacity, formally, cleanly, on the record: 4,120 and 0.

And Issei Hyoudou, who could not stand, stood.

The circle was open. The duel was over. His armor was in shards and his right arm hung wrong and the double-register in his voice was stronger now, ember-light crawling in his eyes, and he took one dragging step toward the veteran devil and said, "Again."

The screen froze on that frame — the ruined boy, the old killer's first flicker of genuine unease, the green gem burning red — and the theatre lights came up on four hundred held breaths.

---

Thor stood.

He did not raise the horn. Those who knew him — and by now the whole theatre knew him — understood that this was the actual alarm, the one Asgard never got to hear twice.

"That," he said, "was not temper."

"Looked like temper," said Yondu Udonta, from the Ravager rows. "Looked like grade-A, aged-in-the-barrel temper. I've *started* that temper in forty bars."

"No." Thor's voice rolled out flat and level, filling the room the way weather fills a valley. "I have seen temper. Temper is a fire in a hearth — it burns what is near and dies hungry. What woke in that boy's eyes has a *lineage.*" He looked around the theatre, and for once there was no performance in him at all. "Dragon pride is not a flaw of character. It is a force, as real as the weight of a mountain, and older than any pantheon in this room. It is what a dragon IS — the certainty of the summit, the law that nothing above you may be suffered to remain there. Every dragon carries it as surely as fire. And a dragon's power poured into a mortal soul pours *that* in with it." He sat down slowly. "In Asgard we had a saying: you may borrow a dragon's strength, or a dragon's wings, and pay only in gold. Borrow a dragon's pride and you have already paid everything, and the collector is merely in transit."

"The wielder before him," said the Valkyrie Brunnhilde, quietly, from the Asgardian benches. "The ones before him. Twenty-seven hands, the dragon said. Where are they now?"

The screen, which had been listening — the theatre had learned, since the Welsh chapter, that it listened — answered.

**PREVIOUS HOSTS OF THE BOOSTED GEAR: [COUNT WITHHELD].**

**PREVIOUS HOSTS UNDONE BY EXTERNAL ENEMIES: SOME.**

**PREVIOUS HOSTS UNDONE BY THEMSELVES: THE REMAINDER.**

**THE REMAINDER IS THE LARGER NUMBER.**

Nobody said anything for a moment. In the villain tiers, Mephisto — who had spent eighteen chapters delighted by everything — was, for the first time, taking notes with a completely straight face.

"So the kid's biggest threat," said Yondu, slowly, working it through, "ain't the wolf, ain't the fallen angels, ain't whatever's got a white gauntlet out there waitin' on him."

"It's the mirror," said Kraglin.

"It is the *inheritance,*" said Doom.

---

Victor von Doom did not stand to speak. Doom never stood for the theatre; the theatre, his posture suggested, should consider standing for him. But he had turned his chair — an event in itself — and the green hood faced the frozen screen, the boy mid-snarl in his broken armor.

"Observe the sequence precisely," Doom said, "because the rest of you will sentimentalize it within the hour. The insult to himself, he absorbed. We have watched him absorb worse than insult — humiliation, defeat, death itself — and convert all of it to fuel. That conversion is his single genuine talent." A pause. "The insult to what he *loves*, he could not absorb. And note what the pride did with that noble opening: it did not make him protect the dragon. It made him *stake* everything the dragon is invested in — his body, his future, his master's eight pieces — on a duel arithmetic said he would lose. Pride always disguises itself as loyalty on the way in. It presents as love, as honor, as *standards.*" The metal fingers laced. "Pride dooms kings. Not peasants — peasants cannot afford enough of it to be dangerous. Kings. It waits until you are strong enough that no one dares correct you, and then it eats the kingdom."

The theatre waited. Everyone in six rows was extremely careful not to look at Latveria's delegation of one, or to mention a face, or a scar, or a lifelong war with a man Doom could have simply stopped hating.

"Doom speaks," said Doom, "from observation."

"Sure," said Ben Grimm, from very far away, in the tone of a man valuing his own life at slightly above one wisecrack.

"From *extensive* observation," said Doom, and turned his chair back, and under the heading in his notebook that still read *Latveria possesses no dragon; oversight,* he added a second line the screen was rude enough to enlarge for the entire room: *Amendment — a dragon, it transpires, is a citizen who cannot be ruled. Fortunate that Doom admires him from a distance.*

---

And then there was Namor.

He had been quiet through all of it, arms folded, ankle-wings still, watching the broken boy on the screen with an expression the surface-dwellers around him had no file for. When he finally rose, it was without any of his usual ceremony, and what he said first was not addressed to the room.

"Again," Namor repeated — the boy's word, the bleeding boy's word to a foe he could not touch. "*Again.*" He let out a short breath through his nose. "Draconian. In the oldest sense. Not cruel — *draconian:* the law of the dragon, which recognizes no verdict delivered by anyone above it, because it does not acknowledge the existence of *above.*" His jaw worked. "I have said that word about myself, you understand, usually while a Fantastic Four member was pulling me out of something I had personally set on fire."

"Is this," said Sue Storm, from across the theatre, "*growth?*"

"It is *recognition,*" said Namor, with dignity. "There is a difference that royalty understands." But he did not smirk when he said it, and he did not ascend his customary two smug inches, and his eyes stayed on the screen. "I know that fire exactly. When the surface insults me, I can be persuaded, eventually, at great cost to the furniture. When they insult *Atlantis* — when they call my people primitive, my throne a puddle —" The ankle-wings flicked once, hard. "There is no *eventually.* There is the flood. Half of what this theatre holds against me began in the exact moment that just played: the moment an old man said *your beloved thing is worthless* and something ancient stood up inside me wearing my voice." He sat back down, slowly. "The boy is fifteen and it is already strong enough to hold formal challenges in his mouth. Imperius Rex, little dragon. I am grudgingly and genuinely afraid for you."

From the gallery, Fin Fang Foom laughed, low and long, like distant rockfall.

"Now you understand," the great dragon said. "You have spent eighteen chapters asking whether the boy is worthy of the dragon. Wrong question. The question was always whether the boy can carry the dragon without *becoming* one. None of the others could. Foom has met dragons' hosts before, little kings. They are the saddest creatures in creation — all of our pride, none of our wings."

The wheel spun again before anyone could answer him. Perhaps mercifully.

**PULL TWO: FRIENDS RESTRAINING HIM — COMMON.**

"Common," said Stakar Ogord, speaking for the first time all night, arms crossed like a closed gate. "Meaning it happens a lot, or meaning it's cheap?"

**MEANING IT IS THE ORDINARY MIRACLE,** said the screen. **WATCH.**

---

The vision resumed exactly where it had frozen. One dragging step. "Again."

Kiba reached him first — a flicker of Knight speed, and then the swordsman had Issei's unbroken arm locked behind him, heels plowing furrows, being *moved* anyway, because a boy who had boosted past his limit was briefly stronger than sense. "Issei. Issei, it's over, it's called—"

"AGAIN—"

Koneko hit him at the waist.

The smallest member of the peerage wrapped both arms around him with full Rook strength, planted her feet, and became geology. The theatre had watched this tiny girl stop charging monsters; now she stopped her friend, cheek pressed against his shattered breastplate, and she was not looking at the Duelist or the circle or anything at all. She was listening to his heartbeat with an expression of open fear.

"Senpai smells wrong," she said. "Senpai smells like *someone else.*"

And Issei — eyes still burning, voice still doubled, one arm pinned and his waist in a vise — kept pulling toward the old devil, dragging both of them, snarling something in a register the subtitles declined to render, and it might have gone somewhere genuinely unforgivable except that the sound cut through everything else:

Asia Argento, crying.

Not healing him. She *was* healing him — the green glow of Twilight Healing already soaking through the ruin of his ribs — but that wasn't what stopped him. She was crying the way she cried the night he died, hiccupping and horrified, hands shaking against his chest, saying his name over and over like a prayer that wasn't working.

The ember-light went out of his eyes between one heartbeat and the next.

The double-register vanished. The pride drained out of his frame so fast that Koneko's grip became the only thing holding him up, and Issei Hyoudou looked down at Asia's tears, and at Kiba's white-knuckled lock on his arm, and at the crater, and at his own blood, with the dawning, seasick expression of a man waking up at the wheel of a moving car.

"...What," he said, in one voice, his own, very small, "was I just doing?"

**That,** said Ddraig, heavily, **is the correct question, partner. Hold on to it. Most of the ones before you never thought to ask it.**

---

The gacha did not cut away. The theatre had expected the usual mercy-fade; instead the vision followed them home, to the Hyoudou living room, that evening — and the audience realized it was being shown the *real* pull. The restraint had been the trailer. This was the feature.

Because they were all waiting for him. And nobody was proud of him.

Rias Gremory sat in the center of the sofa with her hands folded, and she did not shout. It was infinitely worse than shouting. "Eight pieces," she said. "I gave eight pieces, Issei. Do you know what I actually bought with them? Not a weapon. Not a dragon. *You.* Your ridiculous, kind, stubborn self — the one who gets up for other people. Tonight I watched something wearing your face gamble all of that to defend the *feelings* of a being who predates feelings. If you ever put my cute servant in that kind of danger again, I will—" Her voice cracked, hairline, and every empath in the theatre flinched. "You *died* once already. I was there. I will not do that twice."

"Ara ara," said Akeno Himejima, and there was no smile under it at all, which several audience members found genuinely frightening. "And you didn't even bring us. You know I'd have held the nasty old man down for you, Issei-kun. That's what hurts. You forgot you *have* us."

"Duel expenses," announced Ravel Phenex, materializing a ledger with the crisp violence of a slammed door, and Pepper Potts sat up straight in the audience. "Phoenix tears, one vial, market rate. Twilight Healing, four hours, priced at Asia-san's tears, which are *not renewable at market rate.* Training ground repairs. One formal apology to a war veteran, drafted by me, because your draft opened with 'Dear Old Guy.' Total in the margin. *Look at the margin,* Hyoudou Issei."

"Stupid," said Koneko, with maximum precision. "Senpai was stupid. Senpai is not allowed to smell like someone else again."

"I have decided," said Xenovia, who had been thinking hard in the corner, "that if pride requires an outlet, you may duel *me* each morning. I will defeat you very kindly."

"That's not—" said Irina. "That's not the *lesson,* Xenovia—"

And Asia, who had cried herself out hours ago, simply took his hand in both of hers and held it against her cheek and said nothing whatsoever, which broke him — the theatre watched it break him — far more thoroughly than the ledger, the lecture, and the geology combined.

Issei bowed his head over their joined hands. "I'm sorry," he said. "I don't — that wasn't — I could hear myself saying it, and it felt *right,* that's the scary part, it felt like being sure of something for once—"

**The apology is also mine to make,** said Ddraig, and the gauntlet manifested, gem glowing low, addressing the room of furious girls with what could only be called formality. **Some of that fire was mine. I burn; it is what I am; he holds the flame and it warms him and it will always, always try to spread. And some of it was older than mine — the ones who came before, whose endings I carry. I could not save them. They had power, every one.** The ember-light banked, and the ancient voice did something the theatre had never heard it do. It went gentle. **None of them had this room. Keep scolding him. I mean this as a dragon who has buried twenty-seven partners: the scolding is the shield. It is the only one that has ever worked.**

The screen held on the living room — the boy surrounded, chastened, held — and then, before fading, it printed one line across the image, without fanfare, in small letters, as if for its own records:

**HYPOTHESIS LOGGED: THE ASSET'S PRIMARY CONTAINMENT SYSTEM IS DOMESTIC.**

---

"HA!" Yondu was on his feet, pointing his whole arm at the screen. "That's it! That's the thing! Krags — that's the thing don't nobody put in the recruitment brochure!"

"The scoldin'," Kraglin agreed, nodding along. "Cap'n, you remember when you flew us into that Kree interdiction line 'cause the checkpoint officer laughed at your fin?"

"He laughed at the *fin,* Kraglin, the fin is *cultural*—"

"Lost the aft section. Lost my second-best knife. And weren't nobody on that bridge told you no, is my point." Kraglin turned to the theatre at large, warming to the first philosophy of his life. "That's Ravagerin'. Whole crews of proud idiots and not one Asia in the bunch. Pride's the house drink. It's *why* half of us signed up and *how* most of us go out."

"It's why he flew colors alone for twenty years," said Stakar Ogord, and the temperature in the Ravager section dropped. Yondu went still. Stakar looked at the screen instead of at him — at the frozen living room, the circle of angry, loving faces. "I exiled a man once for breaking the code. Right call. Then pride kept me from ever once checking what the exile was doing to him, and by the time I looked, there was almost nothing left to look at." He let that sit. "That boy got dragged home and yelled at by six people the same night he embarrassed himself. You know what that is? That's *wealth.* Damn kid's rich and doesn't know it. Ravagers should take notes."

"Ravagers can't read," said Yondu, hoarsely, which in Ravager was *thank you, brother,* and everyone present had the grace to pretend otherwise.

Thor raised the horn at last — but he raised it away from the screen, toward the memory of the living room.

"Not to the dragon tonight," he said. "Nor even to the boy. To the shield-wall! To the red-haired king who counts her pieces, the little stone one who holds the door, the healer whose tears outrank armies, the keeper of the terrible ledger! In Asgard we sang ten thousand songs of dragons and none — *none* — of the ones who talk them down. An oversight I intend to correct." He drank. "The pride is real, and it will come again, stronger, each time his power grows. Mark me. But so will they."

"The strategic summary," said Doom, without turning his chair, "for those requiring one: the boy's ceiling is not determined by the dragon. It is determined by whether that living room still exists on the day the ceiling matters. His enemies would be advised to understand this." A pause, arid as Latveria in August. "Doom advises no one."

Namor said nothing at all. But those seated near the Atlantean section observed that he watched the frozen frame of the scolding for a long time, unblinking — a king with no living room, memorizing someone else's — and that when the lights finally began their end-of-chapter dim, he inclined his head to the screen by perhaps two degrees. Monarch to monarch. Or perhaps to the girls.

The wheel gave its parting clatter in the dark, and the letters came up gold and ominous both at once:

**NEXT: HIS BODY HAS LEARNED WHAT IT COSTS TO BE ONE THING. SO IT WILL BECOME THREE.**

"Three?" said Tony Stark, into the dark. "I'm sorry — three *what?*"

## Theatre Ledger

- **New Pulls This Chapter:** *Accepting a Duel Because of Pride* — Rare; *Friends Restraining Him* — Common. The gacha explicitly ranked recklessness-for-himself as rarer than recklessness-for-others, and followed the restraint home to the scolding — flagging the domestic aftermath, not the duel, as the true content of the pull.
- **Secrets Revealed:** Dragon pride is a real, transmissible force, not adolescent ego — Ddraig's nature bleeding into his host, amplified by residue of the previous Red Dragon Emperors (twenty-seven prior partners, per Ddraig; the screen confirmed most hosts were "undone by themselves," and that this is "the larger number"). Under its influence Issei's eyes ember, his voice doubles, and Koneko can smell "someone else" in him. A veteran duelist (4,120–0) provoked it by insulting Ddraig — insults to himself he converts to fuel; insults to what he loves bypass every defense. Asia's tears, not force, broke the state. The screen logged: "THE ASSET'S PRIMARY CONTAINMENT SYSTEM IS DOMESTIC."
- **Alliance / Opinion Shifts:** Thor reclassifies the pride as an ancient force and toasts the girls, vowing songs for "the ones who talk dragons down." Namor names the behavior "draconian" with open, unsmirking kinship and visibly envies the scolding. Doom (updating his *Latveria possesses no dragon* note) rules that pride dooms kings "from observation," and flags the living room as Issei's strategic center of gravity. The Ravagers claim pride as "the house drink"; Stakar calls Issei's scolding circle "wealth" and half-reconciles with Yondu in the process. Mephisto has stopped smiling and started writing. Rias, Akeno, Koneko, Asia, Xenovia, Irina, and Ravel established on-screen as the anti-corruption mechanism: his family does not blindly praise him.
- **Unresolved Hooks:** Ddraig has buried twenty-seven partners and says the scolding is "the only shield that has ever worked" — what exactly did the ones before Issei become, and what is the sealed weight Ddraig carries of their endings? The pride "will come again, stronger, each time his power grows" (Thor), making Issei's own inheritance the arc's shadow-threat. The Duelist's mention of "Belzard" gives the past hosts their first name. Doom is now openly modeling how to *unmake* Issei via the living room — as warning or as blueprint. And the wheel's tease stands: his body will "become three." The theatre has no idea what an Illegal Move is. It is about to find out.
