## THE REVELATION

The Theatre's stage shifts, and Issei does not move. He stands, watching, as his own past manifests before every observer present.

What is shown is not a battle. It is fragments. Pieces of a conflict so profound that history itself was damaged in its occurrence.

Issei, twenty years younger, in a probability space that was never meant to be witnessed. Alone. Standing in a dimension that is not quite space and not quite time. Around him, the proto-Devourer—not yet unified, not yet fully conscious, but already aware. Already hungry.

The Devourer in its nascent state is less horrifying because it is less coherent. It is a scream given form. It is the screaming of a thousand erased timelines that haven't yet learned to speak in unison. It is awareness without purpose, rage without focus.

Issei is smaller than the Devourer, even in this early form. He is also not using any of the forms the Theatre has documented. He is not in Apocalypse AnswerArms. He is not powered by divine gifts or dragon blood or any of the transformations that would come later. He is simply himself, and the proto-Devourer is already destroying him.

**"This was before,"** the Theatre's voice explains. **"Thirty-seven years ago. When the proto-Devourer first began to coalesce. Before it had enough erased timelines to form fully. Before it had unified voice and intention."**

**"Issei encountered it in probability space. By accident, or by the Theatre's guidance—this part is unclear, even to the Theatre's own records."**

The battle unfolds in fast-forward, not because time is accelerating, but because the Theatre cannot bear to show the full duration of Issei's suffering. The proto-Devourer attacks—not with weapons, but with the weight of its own existence. It attacks with the knowledge that it should not be, that every moment of its manifestation is an ontological crime against the order of realities.

Issei cannot defeat it. That much is immediately clear.

He cannot destroy it because it is already dead—already erased. He cannot seal it because it does not exist in accessible dimensional space. He cannot kill it because it is made of what was already unmade.

So instead, he does something else.

## THE SACRIFICE

What we see is Issei, surrounded, overwhelmed, facing annihilation. And then, in a moment of clarity that the Watchers say transcends normal causality, he makes a choice that even they did not anticipate.

He does not run.

He does not fight harder.

He walks *into* the Devourer. He permits it to consume him—not his body, but his memories of these moments. Every memory he possesses that can be used as a pathway, he erases it. Every moment of this battle, he unmakes.

His mind doesn't go dark. It simply forgets. His consciousness experiences the battle, and then his consciousness chooses to no longer possess the knowledge that the battle happened.

He is simultaneously fighting the proto-Devourer and fighting to forget that he is fighting.

**"DO YOU SEE WHAT HE DID?"** the Devourer's voice breaks through, even in the flashback. **"HE ATTEMPTED TO SEVER THE BRIDGE BETWEEN MYSELF AND CONTINUING EXISTENCE. HE TRIED TO ERASE HIMSELF FROM HIS OWN MEMORY. TO BECOME A BLANK SPACE IN PROBABILITY THAT COULD NOT BE USED AS A VECTOR FOR MY MANIFESTATION."**

The proto-Devourer's consumption of Issei stops. Not because it chooses mercy, but because what it would consume is being unmade as soon as it consumes it. Issei's memories are erasing themselves retroactively, becoming probability shadows instead of coherent thoughts.

For a moment—infinitesimal, unmeasurable, but real—the proto-Devourer hesitates. It has encountered something it does not understand. An entity willing to erase itself to prevent being used as a weapon against itself.

The flashback ends.

Issei, in the Theatre's present, is standing silently. His expression has not changed, but his wife can see that something in his eyes is different. Colder. Emptier.

**"He succeeded, partially,"** the Theatre continues. **"The battle was erased from his conscious memory. For thirty-seven years, Issei Hyoudou did not remember fighting the proto-Devourer. But erasure of memory is not erasure of effect. His body retained the scars. His spirit retained the damage. His soul retained the knowledge, even if his mind did not."**

## THE WIVES' RECKONING

Rias approaches Issei. The other wives follow. When she speaks, her voice carries the weight of someone who has just realized the full scope of what her husband has been hiding.

"Thirty-seven years," Rias says slowly. "You've been carrying this wound for thirty-seven years. You fought an incomprehensible enemy, erased your own memories to protect us, and then spent decades knowing—knowing on some level your conscious mind didn't access—that the wound was degrading. That eventually this moment would come."

Issei does not respond.

"You knew when we married. You knew when we had plans for children. You knew every time you looked at us that this was coming. That eventually, the memories would return, and the entity would wake up, and you would have to face it again."

Still, Issei does not speak.

"And you didn't tell us. You let us love you without knowing that loving you was a ticking timer."

Issei finally meets her eyes. What she sees there is not love or tenderness or any emotion she can name. What she sees is exhaustion so profound it transcends physical tiredness. It is spiritual exhaustion. It is the weariness of a man who has already lived multiple lifetimes of sacrifice and is about to enter another.

"I didn't tell you," Issei says slowly, "because telling you would not have changed the outcome. It would only have given you seventy-two hours to grieve what you couldn't prevent. I gave you thirty-seven years of not grieving. I thought that was mercy."

"It wasn't," Akeno says, and her lightning crackles with her anger. "Mercy would have been trusting us with the truth."

"Perhaps," Issei agrees. "But I was not capable of that kind of mercy. I was capable only of the kind that removed choice. That's all I've ever been capable of—removing choices for people I love to protect them. This current crisis is the consequence of that capability."

In the Marvel section, even Thanos is quiet. He has made terrible choices in service of necessary mathematics. But even he can recognize something he has never fully experienced: the isolation of carrying a burden alone because you cannot bear the thought of others carrying it with you.

## THEATRE LEDGER — SESSION 94

### **Entry One: The Hidden Battle**
Thirty-seven years before this moment, Issei encountered the proto-Devourer in probability space. The battle cannot be fully described because it was erased from standard causality by Issei's own actions. But fragments remain: a scar on his soul, missing portions of his consciousness, and a partial bridge between himself and the entity that continues to exist. Issei's victory was not to defeat the Devourer, but to survive it by removing his own memories of the encounter. This bought time—thirty-seven years of time. But it did not solve the problem. It only postponed the reckoning.

### **Entry Two: The Cascading Erasure**
To erase his memories of the battle, Issei was forced to fracture his own soul. Thirteen percent of his spiritual integrity was sacrificed to create barriers around the forbidden knowledge. These barriers have been holding for thirty-seven years, but they are not permanent. They are degrading at a measurable rate. In seventy-two hours, the barriers will fail completely, and the memories will return. When they return, the bridge to the proto-Devourer will become active again. This is not a prediction. This is an ontological certainty.

### **Entry Three: The Wives' Discovery**
Rias and her sisters now understand that they have been loved by a man who was simultaneously saying goodbye to them every day for thirty-seven years. Every moment of tenderness was shadowed by the knowledge that this moment would come. Every promise of future was made with the understanding that the future might be consumed. Issei kept the truth to protect them from grief—but in keeping the truth, he denied them the choice to grieve together, to face the crisis unified rather than learning of it only when the timer counted down to zero. This is the private cruelty hidden within his public sacrifice: isolation masquerading as protection.

### **Entry Four: The Seventy-Two-Hour Countdown**
The Theatre has gathered all of Marvel's greatest minds, all of the cosmic entities, all of the Watchers and abstract forces. It has given them the complete picture of the crisis. And it has given them seventy-two hours to solve a problem with no known solution: how to defeat an entity made of erased timelines before it becomes fully aware of the man whose erased memories it feeds upon. The clock is ticking not in minutes or hours, but in the space between probability branches. And it rings in a language no one has learned to speak.
