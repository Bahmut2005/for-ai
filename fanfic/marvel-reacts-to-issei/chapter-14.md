# Chapter Fourteen: A Dragon Picks Its Rider

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Ravagers (Yondu Udonta, Stakar Ogord, Kraglin, Martinex — with Guardians adjacent) • Symbiotes (Eddie Brock & Venom) • Cosmic Riders (Silver Surfer, Beta Ray Bill, Ghost Rider)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Ryuuteimaru's Origin" — **Epic**
2. "Choosing a Partner" — **Rare**

---

The theatre had rearranged itself again overnight, in the way it did — soundlessly, apologetically, like a butler who knew you'd argue if asked.

The British rows had been moved a respectful distance from the Asgardian ones, presumably to prevent any further litigation over dragons. And down along the theatre's port side — everyone had started calling it the port side; nobody remembered deciding to — a new section had assembled itself out of scorched hull plating, mismatched barstools, and a jukebox that only played songs from planets that no longer existed.

The Ravagers had made themselves at home the way Ravagers made themselves at home anywhere: by stealing the good chairs from adjacent factions and daring the universe to invoice them.

"Thing I don't get," Kraglin was saying, boots up on the back of an empty seat that had until recently belonged to an Atlantean, "is the *machine.* Wheel comes down, wheel spins, wheel decides what everybody feels about the kid tonight. That's a slot machine runnin' the whole room. Back home we'd have cracked that thing open by now and rigged the odds."

"Back home," said Stakar Ogord, who sat like a man who had been thrown out of better theatres than this, "you tried to rig a Contraxian slot machine and it took your thumb."

"Grew back."

"That weren't growin' back, that was Martinex printin' you a new one and you cryin' about the color."

Above them, Yondu Udonta said nothing. He'd said very little for thirteen chapters, in fact — just sat with his arrow across his knees like a sleeping animal, watching the screen with the flat red gaze of a man doing arithmetic on somebody else's life. He'd watched the boy die on a first date. He'd watched a rich girl in red spend everything in her pockets to buy him back. Yondu Udonta knew a few things about buying boys, and about what it cost you later, and he had opinions about the girl in red that he had not yet shared with the room.

The wheel descended. Rings within rings, sigils spinning — and, since last chapter, everyone watched it a little differently, because last chapter it had *listened* to them, and nobody had forgotten.

It clattered. It chimed. The token dropped.

**PULL ONE: RYUUTEIMARU'S ORIGIN — EPIC.**

"Ryuu-tei-maru," sounded out Rocket, from the Guardians' row just aft of the Ravagers. "That's a lotta name. Ten quatloos says it's a sword. This kid's world hands out swords like breath mints."

"I am Groot," said Groot.

"It is NOT always a sword. It's a sword like sixty percent of the time."

The screen bloomed.

---

A forest that was not a forest. Trees like candle-wax cathedrals, a sky the color of the inside of an eyelid, and paths that visibly rearranged themselves when no one was looking — the theatre's HUD helpfully stamped the location across the bottom of the frame:

**THE FAMILIAR FOREST. UNDERWORLD TERRITORY. ALL DEVILS COME HERE TO FIND THEIR LIFELONG COMPANION.**

And through it, arguing, came Issei Hyoudou and a wiry, weathered man in an explorer's getup with a net over his shoulder and the unmistakable energy of a man who has been bitten by everything he loves.

**FAMILIAR MASTER: ZATOUJI**, said the HUD. **PROFESSION: MATCHMAKER (BEASTS).**

"You again," Zatouji was saying. "The dragon boy. The one from the *incident.*"

"It wasn't an incident—"

"The lightning sprite chose the little blonde nun and personally electrocuted you off the premises. I keep records, boy. I have you filed under 'rejected by the forest.'"

The theatre — which had not seen this particular history — received it as a gift. Down in the Guardians' row, Rocket made a noise like a airlock failing.

"He got — *ha* — he got dumped by the *pet store?*"

"Twice, apparently," said Martinex, crystalline face refracting the screen-light. "It says rejected. Plural incidents implied."

On screen, Zatouji sighed the sigh of a professional about to do something unprofessional, and led Issei off the path — through wax-cathedral trees, down a slope of silver moss, to a pond.

It was not an impressive pond. It was barely a committed puddle. And floating in the middle of it, perfectly still, was—

The screen zoomed in. Then zoomed in again. Then, with an air of embarrassment, a third time.

A boat.

A tiny boat. A boat the size of a loaf of bread. Lacquered hull, a stub of a mast, a little carved prow curling up at the front like it had ambitions of one day being a figurehead. It bobbed on the puddle-pond with its sail furled, and as the audience watched, it produced a small, unmistakable sound.

A snore.

The boat was asleep.

The theatre's laugh started in the Ravager section and rolled outward. Rocket was face-down on the seatback in front of him, hammering it with one fist. "It's a *dinghy!* Thirteen chapters of doom dragons and murder angels and the kid's magic destiny animal is a *toy boat!* It SNORES! I'm gonna die here and I'm gonna die HAPPY—"

"**Epic rarity,**" the wheel noted overhead, in cold letters, and something about the stubbornness of it quieted the laugh by half.

On screen, Zatouji crouched by the water. "Don't laugh, boy. You're looking at a bloodline older than most gods' grudges. This is a skiff-spirit — hatched from the line of *Skíðblaðnir.*"

Two rows of Asgardians sat up at once, in unison, like a wave.

"*Skíðblaðnir!*" Thor was on his feet, horn sloshing. "The ship of the god Freyr! Finest vessel ever wrought — built by the sons of Ivaldi, the greatest smiths under any sky! A ship that always finds fair wind, that sails sea, sky, and land alike, and folds small enough to carry in a pouch! I have HEARD of her brood but never seen one whelped! BOY! *FEED IT SOMETHING!*"

"He cannot hear you," said Loki, without looking up. But the audience nearest him noted that he had gone very still — the specific stillness of a collector who has just seen an heirloom of his own pantheon float, snoring, into the possession of a Japanese teenager. First the dragon of Wales. Now the shipwrights of Asgard. Loki's expression suggested a man revising a very long list.

On screen, Zatouji was explaining, and the theatre hushed itself to hear.

"It's a growth-type, boy. Rarest kind of familiar there is, and the most honest. It's got almost nothing now — can't fight, can't fly, can't do much but sleep and drift. But it feeds on the aura of its master. It'll drink whatever you are, a little every day, for years. And it becomes — slowly, and only ever once — a vessel worthy of the one who fed it." He looked up, and his voice did the thing that voices in this story did right before the theatre's chest tightened. "Which means you'd best think hard about what you're going to be. Because whatever that is? One day you'll have to *ride* it."

The HUD flickered, once:

**GROWTH-TYPE FAMILIAR ACQUIRED. CURRENT CAPABILITY: NEGLIGIBLE. POTENTIAL: UNMEASURED.**

*Unmeasured.* The theatre had learned to fear that word. The screen had said it about a gauntlet once.

And then the little boat woke up.

It didn't do anything grand. It turned — a small, sleepy quarter-circle on the puddle — and drifted, unhurried, across the water, and bumped its tiny prow against Issei's outstretched hand. Once. Twice. Like a cat deciding.

Issei, who had been rejected by this forest twice, who had been rejected in worse ways by worse things, went very quiet. On the gauntlet, the green gem glowed.

"**It has chosen, partner,**" said Ddraig. "**Do not make a fuss.**"

"I'm not making a fuss."

"**You are crying on it.**"

"IT PICKED ME, DDRAIG. IT'S THE FIRST THING THAT EVER JUST — *PICKED* ME—"

"**I picked you.**"

"You were SEALED in me, that's different, you didn't get a vote—"

A pause, on screen and in the theatre both. The ember-voice, when it came, was even.

"**I have had many wielders, partner. A vote is not always cast with a hand. We will speak of it another time.**"

Issei lifted the boat out of the water — it fit in his two palms, mast and all — and held it up level with his eyes, and the little prow bobbed at him, and he grinned the grin the theatre had learned across fourteen chapters meant *incoming terrible decision, incoming with total sincerity.*

"Ryuuteimaru," he declared. "Because I'm gonna be the Dragon Emperor, and you're gonna be the Dragon Emperor's ship. We'll grow into it together. Deal?"

The boat snored.

"He'll come around," said Issei, and tucked the ship of the line of Skíðblaðnir into his jacket like a kitten.

The screen paused itself. Lights up.

---

"Okay," said Rocket. "Okay. I got questions."

"Nobody sanctioned questions," said Star-Lord.

"First question. That forest. Is that a *shop?* Is there a *shop* where they hand out mythical Norse boat-larvae to whoever wanders in? Because I've been to every black market in six galaxies and nobody TOLD me—"

But it was Stakar Ogord who stood, and when Stakar stood, the port side went quiet, because there were maybe four beings in the theatre with a genuine hundred-crew command voice and he was the one currently using it.

"Y'all are laughing at the boat," he said. "You're missing it."

He nodded at the screen — at the paused frame, boy and boat, eye to eye.

"I've flown with a hundred crews. Signed some, inherited some, stole a couple. And I'll tell you the one law that holds from the Quadrant to the far rim: you can *buy* a ship and you can *hire* a crew, and neither one of those is worth a damn when the burn gets hard. The only thing that holds a deck together at the bad end of a fight is the ones who *chose* to be there. Choosing's the whole cargo. Everything else is freight." He sat back down, slow. "That little boat drank nothing yet. Gained nothing yet. It looked at a boy the whole universe keeps turning down and it swam *toward* him. That's not a pet. That's a crewman signing on before the ship's even built."

"It's still funny," said Kraglin, loyally.

"It's hilarious," Stakar agreed. "Most true things are."

And from above them, quietly, Yondu spoke for the first time in three chapters.

"Growth-type." He turned the word over like a coin he suspected of being real. "Feeds on what you are. A little every day. For years." His jaw worked. "Hell of a thing to sign up to be somebody's diet."

"You'd know," said Stakar, not unkindly, and Yondu didn't answer, and the whole port side very carefully did not look at Peter Quill, and Peter Quill very carefully did not look at anything at all.

The wheel spun.

**PULL TWO: CHOOSING A PARTNER — RARE.**

---

The second vision was quieter, and it was not one memory but several, laid side by side like cards.

*First:* the incident. The theatre finally got to see it — the Familiar Forest, months earlier, and a tiny blue sprite dragon crackling with lightning, and every devil present cooing over it, and the sprite dragon looking at the assembled options, floating directly past Issei — pausing — and delivering unto him a bolt of pure rejection that parted his hair down to the scalp, before settling into Asia Argento's arms like a returning thought. Asia named it Rassei. Rassei spent the rest of the memory glaring at Issei with tiny, sizzling jealousy.

The theatre laughed. But the gacha wasn't done, and the cards kept turning.

*Second:* the gauntlet. A procession of half-lit figures, centuries of them — the previous Red Dragon Emperors, each seizing the Boosted Gear like a weapon, wringing it for power, burning down into madness or ruin. Ddraig's voice over all of it, older than tired: "**They wielded me. Some feared me. Many used me. My power was a thing that happened TO them, and their hatred was a thing that happened to me, and we called that partnership because neither of us had ever seen one.**"

*Third:* a bath. The theatre braced reflexively — fourteen chapters had taught them what this boy's bathing scenes usually meant — but no. It was Issei alone, soaking training bruises, holding his left arm up out of the water and just... talking to it. Not asking for power. Not asking for anything. Telling Ddraig about his day. About a math test he'd failed. About whether dragons liked melon bread, and whether Ddraig could taste it when he ate, and, if so, apologizing for the week he'd eaten nothing but instant noodles.

"**Why do you tell me these things, partner?**"

"'Cause you're stuck in there, man. You've been stuck in there for like a thousand years and I bet nobody ever tells you anything except 'MORE POWER' and 'AAAGH.' That's a terrible feed. I'm gonna be the Dragon Emperor with the good feed."

A silence, in the memory, that went on one beat past comfortable.

"**...Melon bread. Describe it again. Slowly.**"

*Fourth,* and last: night, and the Hyoudou household's little garden pond — barely a committed puddle — and Ryuuteimaru afloat on it, and Issei crouched at the edge in his pajamas, holding his hand over the water, letting a thread of warm red aura drift down like crumbs. A little every day. The boat's carved prow tipped up toward him. It had grown — the theatre checked, and it had, undeniably, grown.

By about a centimeter.

"Look at you!" Issei whispered, delighted beyond all proportion. "You're gonna be a battleship. Don't tell anyone I said that. Get some sleep."

The boat snored. The boy stayed anyway, ten more minutes, watching it, the way you watch something you have decided is yours to keep alive.

**PARTNERSHIP,** the screen printed, in small cold letters, **IS NOT ASSIGNED. IT IS FED.**

Lights up.

---

It was the symbiote that spoke first — which surprised everyone, because the symbiote section had been, by hard-won treaty, a quiet zone since chapter three.

Eddie Brock sat where he always sat, big shoulders hunched, and the black living skin of the Venom symbiote lay across them like a second, more honest posture. When it spoke, it spoke with his mouth and around it, layered, two voices deciding to be one.

"**We know what they will say about the boy's boat,**" it said. "**They will say: parasite. They will say: it takes and takes. They said it about us for years.**" A ripple crossed the black. "**Our kind's own name — Klyntar — do you know what it means, in our first tongue? It means 'cage.' We were made to be worn, and we were named a prison, and for a long time we believed it, and we made ourselves true to the name. A bond can rot into that. We are the room's leading experts.**"

Eddie's own voice came through alone for a moment, rough. "But that's not what's on the screen."

"**No. What is on the screen is the other thing. The thing our codex sings about and almost never gets. A host who feeds the bond instead of bleeding it. Who talks to what lives in him. Who apologizes for the noodles.**" The symbiote's voice did something no one in the theatre had heard it do: it went soft. "**The dragon has been worn by a hundred men as armor. This one wears him as a friend. We watched the boy die in chapter one and we did not understand why the red girl paid so much to bring him back. We understand now. Hosts like this are why our kind first left the void. He would have been chosen on any world. By any of us. It was only ever a question of what got to him first.**"

"And thank every god it was the boat," said Eddie, alone again, dry as dust — and the theatre, which had been holding a very tender feeling and not known where to put it, gratefully laughed.

The cosmic tier stirred next.

The Silver Surfer had watched both pulls without moving — a statue of liquid chrome in the high seats, board upright at his shoulder like a second self. When he spoke, he spoke mostly to the board.

"I was bound to a master once," he said. "I sold my will to save a world, and my master fed on worlds, and what he fed me in return was purpose with all the choice burned out of it. That is one kind of bond. I know its taste." His hand rose, rested on the board's edge. "This one chose me back. It is the only thing in my long service I am certain chose me. The boy on the screen has that — twice over now, dragon and vessel — and he is *fifteen*, and he got it not by bargaining away his soul but by talking to his arm in the bath." The chrome face turned, very slightly, toward the screen. "There are beings in this theatre who command galaxies and have never once been chosen. I count myself. Watch him closely. He is rich in the only coin that matters."

"SKUTTLEBUTT WOULD LIKE THE BOAT," boomed Beta Ray Bill, from the row above, with the delighted volume of a horse-faced Korbinite who has been waiting all evening to bring up his warship. "My ship is alive, little dragon-boat, wherever you are! She chose her crew too! She has opinions about everyone I bring aboard and she has been right *every time!* When your pond-ship grows, they should meet! Ships that choose their captains should know they are not alone!"

"Bill," said the Ghost Rider, "you cannot arrange a play-date across a dimensional barrier."

"THE THEATRE IS ALREADY IMPOSSIBLE. I AM MERELY ADDING AN ITEM TO ITS LIST."

The Rider — leathers, chain, skull wrapped in patient fire — looked back at the screen, and the flame that served him for expression banked low and blue.

"Bond with its own will," he said, quietly, to nobody. "Yeah. I got one of those." The chain shifted on his shoulder, restless, like it knew it was being discussed. "Mine came in screaming. Vengeance don't ask. It rides *you*, and you spend every night negotiating who gets the wheel, and some nights you lose." The skull-face turned toward the paused frame: the boy in pajamas, feeding a boat light. "Fifteen years old and he got the version they promise you in the fine print and never deliver. Something that eats what you are — and what he is, is *kind*, so that's what it'll grow up made of." The fire flickered. "Kid's building the only vehicle in creation I'd swap for."

The theatre sat with that. It was, by consensus, the touching part of the evening, and the theatre had learned to brace for what always followed the touching part.

"So, uh. Real quick. Logistics question."

Rocket was standing on his seat.

"The boat drinks the kid's mojo and grows into a bigger boat. The dragon lives in the kid's arm. The kid is, functionally — and I'm just doing the math the rest of you are too polite to do — the kid is a *vehicle-in-progress.* Dragon-ship. Ship-dragon. Some kinda escalating rideable situation." He spread his paws, reasonable as a lawyer. "So my question, and it's a NORMAL question, is: could I ride him? Like when it's all grown. Hypothetically. Could I strap a seat to—"

The response was not organized, which made it worse.

Frank Castle racked a shotgun that the theatre should not, by any physics, have permitted him to retain. Wolverine's claws came out with a sound like the world's shortest argument ending. Thor stood so fast his horn baptized an entire row of Eternals; three separate members of the Spider-family thwipped web-lines in Rocket's general direction on pure reflex; Elsa Bloodstone produced a pistol from somewhere philosophical; Faiza Hussain's sword hummed; and from the monster galleries, high above, Fin Fang Foom's voice rolled down like weather: "**If the vermin mounts the dragon-child, Foom will eat the vermin, the seat, and — on principle — the concept of saddles.**"

"WOW," said Rocket, hands up. "Okay! Tone! Noted! The theatre has adopted the kid, everybody's real normal about it—"

"I am Groot," said Groot, with crushing disappointment.

"Oh, don't YOU start, you let three kids ride you last—"

Overhead, the gacha wheel — which had listened to the audience once before, and evidently intended to make a habit of it — emitted a single low buzz in Rocket's direction, like a headmaster clearing his throat.

Rocket sat down.

"...I'm keeping the question on file," he muttered.

---

The lights had begun their end-of-chapter dim when Yondu Udonta stood up.

He didn't address the room. Ravagers didn't. He just looked at the last lingering image — the boy at the pond's edge in the dark, and the tiny ship drifting toward his hand for one more bump of the prow before sleep — and he whistled, once, soft. The arrow rose off his knees and hung in the air beside his ear, red trail glowing, close as a thought.

"Folks think the whistle steers it," he said, to nobody, to the port side, to a boy in another row who wasn't looking at him. "It don't. Whistle's just how I ask. It flies 'cause it *knows* me. Knows every rotten thing I am and flies anyhow." He watched the boat bump the boy's hand, on screen, one last time. "You don't own a thing like that. You feed it and you hope like hell you're worth what it grows into."

He sat. The arrow settled. And Peter Quill, twelve seats away, looked at the screen very hard and said nothing, and the entire port side, with the ferocious discretion of career criminals, pretended that was that.

The screen went dark — then flickered, once, with next chapter's appetite. A moonlit street. Something inhuman shrieking in the dark of the canon world, and Issei's silhouette walking toward it, gauntlet lit, unafraid.

**NEXT: THE HUNT,** said the HUD, and in the rows where Blade and the Punisher and Elsa Bloodstone sat, several professionals leaned forward at once.
