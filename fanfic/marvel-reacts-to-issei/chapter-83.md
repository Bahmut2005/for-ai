### SECTION I: FORMAL INTRODUCTIONS

**[HUD Status: Conflict Status — NEUTRALIZED | Atmosphere — CAUTIOUSLY OPTIMISTIC | Protocol Level — FORMAL]**

The Theatre's central platform had been reconfigured. Where before it had been a stage for memory projection, it now served as a meeting ground. Not observation, but genuine encounter.

Issei stood at its center, flanked by his wives. The anger had not dissipated—trust, once violated, takes time to rebuild. But beneath the anger, there was something else now. Curiosity. The dragon-man understood power when he saw it, and the Avengers—despite their violation through Theatre's archive—represented a particular species of strength that warranted assessment.

Captain America descended to the platform first. His approach was careful, deliberate, all respect in posture and bearing. This was a man who understood hierarchy, who recognized authority when confronted with it, and who possessed the discipline to establish peace before pursuing questions.

"Steve Rogers," he said, offering his hand in the human gesture of greeting. "I'm captain of the Avengers. We're Earth's primary defensive team against threats beyond our normal capacity. I want to apologize for the violation of your privacy. That wasn't our call, but we participated in it regardless, and that shares responsibility with us."

Issei considered the hand for a moment. Rias's grip on his arm tightened slightly—a warning or encouragement, he wasn't quite sure which. Then he took Cap's hand.

The grip was firm. The shake respectful. And in that simple gesture, something shifted in the Theatre's emotional register. The dragon-man was willing to hear them.

"I appreciate the direct acknowledgment," Issei said. "You took something that wasn't yours without permission. You're at least honest about it."

Tony Stark was already approaching, his suit's armor retracting enough to let him extend a hand. "Tony Stark. I'm the one with too much money and too many opinions about everything. Captain America's right—the violation wasn't our call, but we could have declined participation in the archive observation. We chose not to, which makes us complicit."

Where Steve had offered respect, Tony offered accountability without obfuscation. Issei found that he appreciated the honesty.

"Genius inventor?" Akeno asked, reading between the lines of Tony's presentation.

"Among other things," Tony confirmed. "I saw what you could do with magic and engineering combined, and I immediately wanted to understand it. Selfish impulse on my part. I treated your life as a technical problem to solve instead of a person to respect."

Issei actually smiled slightly at that. "At least you're self-aware."

Thor descended to the platform with the kind of gravitational impact that suggested his world's physics operated somewhat differently than Earth's. The Asgardian warrior was massive, radiating power in a way that made even Issei's draconic senses hum with recognition.

"Issei Hyoudou," Thor said, offering a wrist-clasp rather than a handshake—a gesture Issei recognized as indicating peer respect between warriors. "I am Thor Odinson, God of Thunder. I have witnessed your combat records through the archive. Your fighting style is formidable. Your strategic assessment of threats is sound. Your protective dedication to your partner-units is admirable."

The formality in Thor's speech suggested he was drawing from somewhere deeper than casual English. Issei responded with equal formality.

"Thor Odinson. Your reputation for combat excellence precedes you as well. Multiversal warrior-traditions speak of the Thunderer with respect."

"Then let us speak as warriors do," Thor said with a broad grin. "Tell me—does your armor double the effect of your Dragon Shots? I have theorized that the metal-craft could serve as amplification matrices, but I would prefer assessment from the dragon-kind who designed them."

Despite everything, despite the violation and the anger, Issei found himself genuinely interested in the question. "The armor doesn't double the output, but it does focus and distribute the power more efficiently. Three components—the core generation, the focal channeling, and the dispersal pattern. Without armor, the energy scatters too broadly. With it, you achieve concentration."

"Remarkable," Thor said, and there was genuine appreciation in his voice. "You have created a system of magical engineering that rivals our Asgardian artificers. Though I suspect your approach differs significantly in fundamental theory."

"It does," Issei confirmed. "Asgardian magic seems to work through fundamental reality principles. Draconic magic works through personal resonance. We're channeling the same forces through entirely different frameworks."

Carol Danvers was next, and she brought a different energy entirely. Where Steve offered formal respect and Tony offered honest confession, Carol offered challenge.

"Captain Marvel," she announced. "I've got cosmic-level power, and I've fought threats that could destroy entire galaxies. That makes me dangerous in ways that traditional military framework can't contain. I also happen to believe that power comes with responsibility to the people who don't have it."

She looked directly at Issei, and there was no apology in her gaze. Only recognition.

"The violation of your privacy was unacceptable. Consent frameworks matter. But I also think you and I should spar once you've settled with the Theatre issue, because I want to understand how a dragon warrior from an anime dimension fights, and I want you to understand that cosmic-level threats are real and sometimes require assistance."

Xenovia actually laughed—a sharp bark of genuine humor. "I like her."

"Sparring can be arranged," Issei said, and he sounded like he meant it. "Though I should warn you—dragon combat incorporates elements that most humanoid warriors find difficult to defend against."

"I've got cosmic awareness," Carol replied with a grin. "I think I can handle it."

The problem, as it turned out, was language.

Not English—Issei was fluent enough. But English as spoken by people from multiple different dimensional frameworks, each with their own cultural reference points and combat paradigms, created a translation layer that was more permeable than anyone expected.

When Tony asked if dragon armor could be "retrofitted with repulsor tech," Issei assumed he was asking about combining their fundamental power sources into a unified system. When explained that "repulsor" meant directed energy weapon, Issei spent five minutes trying to explain that his armor already *was* a directed energy system, just powered by draconic resonance rather than arc reactor technology.

"So it's like a magical repulsor," Tony concluded.

"It's nothing like a repulsor," Issei said with exasperation. "It's a entirely different architecture based on—" He stopped, recognizing the fundamental futility of trying to explain dimensional magic theory to a man whose entire framework was built on engineering principles.

When Steve asked Issei to "brief the team on his combat doctrine," Issei began describing his spiritual training and personal philosophy regarding the protection of those weaker than himself. When Steve realized he'd meant "brief us on your military strategy," there was a moment of awkward silence as both men tried to figure out how to bridge the gap between warrior-philosophy and tactical doctrine.

"You go into battle with *spiritual alignment*?" Carol asked, her tone hovering somewhere between respect and bemused confusion.

"It's called *intent*," Issei said carefully. "When you enter combat with clarity about what you're protecting and why, your power becomes more efficient. Your strikes are more precise. Your defense is more comprehensive. It's not philosophy. It's fundamentally how draconic warriors are trained."

"That's not something that translates well to combat theory," Carol admitted. "We mostly just... go in and hit things."

"That explains your causality damage rating," Akeno observed dryly. "Impressive power, less elegant execution."

The real comedy emerged when Thor asked if Issei had ever "tested his strength against an Asgardian warrior-champion." Issei, assuming this meant a formal combat tournament or ritual, began asking detailed questions about the rules and expectations. When Thor clarified that it meant "let's have a friendly fight and see who's stronger," Issei almost fell off the platform.

"You want to brawl. With me. In the Theatre."

"Is that not the custom of your people?" Thor asked, genuinely confused.

"Not in multiversal meeting spaces that aren't ours," Issei said flatly. "And definitely not without more discussion about what 'friendly' means in Asgardian context."

"Asgardians punch harder than we talk," Carol explained. "It's a cultural thing."

"Noted," Issei said, and there was something between exasperation and humor in his voice now. "When we have proper venue and when the Theatre is no longer actively being restructured, perhaps. But not today."

## THEATRE LEDGER — SECTION III: THE WIVES' ASSESSMENT

**[HUD: Wives' Strategic Evaluation — ONGOING | Threat Assessment — RECALIBRATED | Alliance Potential — HIGH]**

Rias, Akeno, Xenovia, and Rossweisse had positioned themselves strategically to observe every interaction. Not to protect Issei—he needed no protection from these heroes—but to assess what kind of people they were, what their actual values were beneath the power they wielded.

Steve Rogers, they determined, was someone who actually believed in the ideas he represented. His apology had been genuine because it wasn't calculated. He'd violated consent without knowing it, recognized his error, and was working to ensure it didn't happen again. That was credible reform.

Tony Stark was the interesting case. He'd treated Issei's life as technical data without considering the humanity involved. But once confronted, he'd immediately accepted responsibility and was actively working toward external oversight. He was the type to make mistakes while believing himself right, but he was also the type to correct course once evidence demanded it.

Thor was a warrior-culture representative from a dimensional framework that understood honor and combat hierarchy differently than the human world or the draconic world. His interest in Issei was genuine and came from a place of actual respect, not curiosity.

Carol was power recognizing power. She was someone who understood that cosmic-level beings had responsibility toward lower-dimensional inhabitants, but she also understood that this responsibility worked both directions. She treated Issei as peer, not subject, which was appreciated.

"They're not bad," Rossweisse said quietly to the other wives. "They're powerful and they made a violation decision, but they're actually trying to correct it."

"The blond one—Thor—reminds me of the older nobles in the draconic hierarchy," Xenovia observed. "All honor and straightforward combat philosophy."

Akeno was still assessing. "They violated us without hesitation when they thought the violation was acceptable. That tells me they don't naturally consider consent as a priority. But they're responding well to correction, which suggests their values are flexible enough to incorporate new understanding."

"In other words," Rias said slowly, "they're the kind of people who can grow beyond their mistakes. Assuming we hold them accountable."

The wives exchanged glances. There was more discussion to be had, but for now, the immediate threat assessment was shifting from "dangerous violation potential" to "potential allies with learning curve."

## THEATRE LEDGER — SECTION IV: WARRIOR TO WARRIOR

**[HUD: Meeting Status — PROGRESS | Consensus — BUILDING | Understanding — DEEPENING]**

As the day in the Theatre progressed, something unexpected happened. The initial tension—born from violation and anger and betrayed trust—began to ease into something more workable. Not forgiveness, not yet. Forgiveness would require time and consistent demonstration of changed behavior.

But there was possibility now. There was the foundation for something beyond simple opposition.

When Carol asked if dragon armor could theoretically withstand cosmic-level energy discharge, Issei took her seriously and worked through the calculations with her. When Thor wanted to understand the differences between draconic and Asgardian magic, Issei and Rossweisse spent an hour explaining fundamental force theory and how different dimensional frameworks approached similar problems through different lenses.

Tony sketched out rough designs for hybrid armor that might combine draconic resonance amplification with Earth-tech energy efficiency. Steve took notes on dragon-warrior combat doctrine, mentally cataloging how it might apply to team tactics.

And Issei, who had come to the Theatre in fury and was leaving it in something approaching cautious cooperation, began to understand that not all observation is violation if it leads to genuine encounter.

"If we're going to be in regular contact," Steve said as the formal introductions began to wind down, "we should establish protocols. Not surveillance. Not archive observation. Just... agreement on how we interact."

"Consent-based contact," Issei confirmed. "I can work with that."

"We'll need to coordinate on dimensional threats," Carol added. "Your world intersects with some dangerous cosmic activity. You should know about it."

"And we should know how to contact you if our world faces threats beyond our capacity," Issei said. "That's fair exchange."

"Then we have agreement," Steve said formally, and he offered his hand again.

This time, when Issei took it, there was something less wary in his grip. Not trust yet. Trust would come with time and consistent behavior. But there was acknowledgment of the possibility. Recognition that power could be wielded responsibly. Understanding that warriors from different worlds could find common ground.

The Avengers had come to the Theatre seeking spectacle and wonder.

They were leaving with something far more valuable—the understanding that wonder is not a resource to be taken, but something to be built together through respect and genuine encounter.
