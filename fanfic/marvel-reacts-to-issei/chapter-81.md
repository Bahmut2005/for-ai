### SECTION I: THE SUMMONING
**[HUD Status: All Spectators Present | Archive Access: GRANTED | Subject Location: UNKNOWN → LOCALIZED]**

The Theatre had endured centuries of reactions—shock, tears, laughter, fury—but it had learned to anticipate the moment when surveillance became reckoning.

Theatre Keeper stood at the precipice of that moment now.

Around the amphitheatre, heroes and villains alike shifted in their seats. The Avengers had settled into something approaching comfort over the past hours. Tony had stopped making quips about "supernatural broadcast networks." Carol had stopped calculating defensive strategies. Even Wanda, who had gently touched the edges of Issei's psyche through the archive memories, had come to a certain peace with what they'd witnessed.

That peace was about to shatter.

"Why has the feed stopped?" Doctor Strange asked, noticing the blank projection screen. Around him, the collective attention of the room sharpened. Something had changed in the Theatre's ambient pressure—a tightness that suggested consequence.

Theatre Keeper did not answer immediately. The being responsible for maintaining the archive had learned that some moments required ceremony. Some truths demanded proper staging.

"We have reached a critical juncture," Theatre Keeper announced, its voice resonating through the crystalline chamber. "The subject of our observations has become aware of his circumstances."

The room fell silent.

"You've breached his awareness?" Sue Storm leaned forward, her scientific mind already running calculations. "How? The archive is observational—temporally isolated."

"The archive observes," Theatre Keeper confirmed. "But observation, when conducted by thousands of consciousness across multiversal boundaries, creates resonance. The subject is sensitive to such resonances. More than sensitive. Attuned. His species—draconic hybrids of profound magical ancestry—experience collective awareness differently than humans. What you experience as passive watching, he experiences as presence."

Doom's gauntleted hand slowly curled against the armrest of his seat. "You allowed him to perceive."

"I did not allow. I acknowledge. There is a difference."

The debate might have continued, but the Theatre's central platform began to shift. The space where memories had been projected now rippled with a different quality of magic—not archive-drawn, but live-summoned. The air acquired weight. Temperature shifted.

Something was coming through.

The wives felt it first. Rias straightened in her seat, her crimson aura flaring briefly before she controlled it. Akeno's fan snapped shut, her expression hardening into something between protective fury and grim satisfaction. Xenovia gripped her sword's hilt. Rossweisse's magic crackled against her fingertips, barely contained.

"He is coming," Rias whispered.

They had watched his memories—intimate, private, deeply personal moments. They knew his face, his laugh, his touch. They knew how he loved them, the specific inflection in his voice when he held each of them close. They knew things that—by rights—they should never have known without his consent.

None of them were prepared for the three-dimensional reality of him.

Issei Hyoudou materialized on the Theatre's central platform with the force of a summoning spell backfiring. Reality *bent* around his arrival; he didn't simply appear but rather *crashed* into presence like an impact. His armor formed around him as he landed—not the casual battle-wear he might have summoned in his own world, but full formal regalia. Red scales overlaid with white-gold accents. Wings folded tight against his back like shields.

His eyes were *furious*.

For a moment, the dragon-man existed purely in the moment of confused rage. His gaze snapped around the Theatre, taking in the architecture, the spectators, the impossible scope of what he was seeing. On his face, the transition from disorientation to understanding happened like watching a predator recognize a trap.

When he spoke, his voice carried harmonics that made the Theatre's walls resonate. English, but layered with draconic undertones that seemed to bypass hearing and strike directly against consciousness.

"What—" He stopped. His eyes found his wives in the amphitheatre, and something in his expression cracked. Recognition. Verification. Confirmation of the terrible truth already crystallizing in his mind.

He moved.

The dragon-man pivoted to face the assembled spectators, and his posture shifted into something deliberately protective. Wings unfurled—not to attack, but to position his body between the audience and the section where his wives sat. A conscious, deliberate choice: *I stand between you and everything I treasure.*

"Who are you?" His voice was quieter now, but somehow more dangerous for it. "All of you. *Why* are you here?"

The silence in the Theatre was absolute.

"Who permitted this?" Issei demanded, his head turning to face Theatre Keeper directly. "Who brought me here? And more importantly—" His fists clenched, magic beginning to swirl around his fingers in patterns none of the assembled heroes had seen before. Draconic fire, but refined. Controlled. Weaponized by someone who understood exactly what he was doing. "—why do you know my wives?"

The Avengers exchanged glances. Even those with decades of combat experience felt the weight of his question. Tony's hand moved instinctively toward his suit-calling mechanism. Captain America remained still, but his body had shifted into alert readiness. Carol was already on her feet.

Theatre Keeper raised a hand—a gesture of deliberate non-escalation.

"Your anger is justified," the being said simply. "Your confusion is earned. Both are appropriate responses to your circumstances."

"My circumstances?" Issei's laugh was bitter. "My circumstances are that I appear to have been violated. That thousands—I can feel thousands—have been *present* in my memories. In moments that were not meant for witnesses. In times when—" He glanced back at his wives, the anger in his eyes mixing with something that looked dangerously like shame. "In times when they were mine alone."

Rias had stood. Akeno beside her. Both moved forward as if to descend to the platform, but Issei's wing-sweep stopped them—a gentle but firm gesture. Not *stay away*. Rather: *I need to understand this first*.

The room understood. They were watching the dragon-man process the unprocessable.

"The archive observations are not violations of consent in the temporal stream from which they originated," Theatre Keeper explained carefully. "The events you witnessed were historical fact in those moments. Observation occurred after completion. The subject—you—has not been invaded."

"But I'm being watched," Issei said flatly. "I'm being *studied*. Like—" He looked around the Theatre with fresh horror. "Like an experiment. A curiosity."

"No," Sue Storm called out. She'd risen as well, her scientific mind grasping for the framework that might defuse this situation. "Not an experiment. A reaction archive. We're here to observe your world, your circumstances, through the lens of—"

"Through the lens of violation," Issei finished. His magic was intensifying, the draconic fire around his hands now visibly burning. Not fire that consumed—fire that *refined*. Alchemical flame. "You watched me love them. You watched moments I could never recover if they were stolen from me. You watched without asking. Without permission. Without *consent*."

The word hung in the Theatre like a blade.

"Every single memory you've seen was a gift," Issei continued, and his voice was shaking now—rage so profound it had cycled through to something almost eloquent. "A gift from them to me. A gift I was privileged to receive and hold close. And now—now you've *stolen* them. You've made them public. You've violated the sacred nature of everything I hold dear."

Magneto had risen. So had Doom. Charles Xavier remained seated, but his psychic presence pressed against the edges of the room like a question. Around the Theatre, heroes and villains alike were beginning to understand the magnitude of what they had done.

The Theatre had shown them wonders. It had given them access to a warrior of extraordinary ability, a dragon-slayer who had chosen a path of love and protection over conquest. It had revealed beauty and tenderness and profound joy. It had been *educational*.

But it had done so without the consent of the person at the center of it all.

"I demand," Issei said, and his voice carried the weight of formal declaration, "to know who authorized this. Who decided my life was entertainment. And what, precisely, you intend to do about it."

The wives had stopped trying to reach him. They understood, with the clarity of long partnership, that their beloved needed this moment. Needed to defend the sanctity of what they had built together. Needed to remind everyone in this Theatre that privacy—that *intimacy*—was not a resource to be mined for spectacle.

Issei turned slowly, his draconic eyes taking in every face in the amphitheatre. His gaze lingered on the Avengers. On the X-Men. On Doom, who met his eyes with what looked almost like respect for the fury being displayed.

"You will explain yourselves," Issei said. "All of you. Or this conversation ends with fire."

And the Theatre, which had become accustomed to witnessing reactions, now faced its own reckoning.

Theatre Keeper did not move. To move would suggest the need for self-defense. To move would grant legitimacy to the threat.

Instead, Theatre Keeper simply stated: "The archive was convened by the Multiversal Council, in response to a dimensional anomaly centered on your person. The study was meant to—"

"I don't care about your justifications," Issei cut in. "I care about *consent*. Did anyone ask them?" He gestured at his wives. "Did anyone ask me? Or was I simply convenient? A dragon-man from another world with no power to resist what you decided?"

The silence that followed was answer enough.

Rias descended the amphitheatre steps before anyone could stop her.

"He's right," she announced, her voice cutting through the Theatre like a blade. "Every memory you watched was sacred to us. You observed our most private moments without permission. That's not scholarship. That's violation."

Akeno followed, her smile sharp as broken glass. "The dragon-king has spoken his truth. Should we remind you all what happens when you violate the privacy of draconic nobility?"

Xenovia and Rossweisse formed up beside them—not in attack posture, but in solidarity. Four women who had invited a broken boy into their hearts and helped him rebuild into a dragon-king.

Four women who were now remembering that they were forces of nature in their own right.

Tony Stark slowly lowered his hand from his suit-summoning mechanism.

"Okay," he said quietly. "Okay, I think we're starting to understand the actual problem here."
