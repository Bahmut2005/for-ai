# Chapter Thirty-Seven: Issei Hyoudou vs. Diplomacy

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Political Leaders (T'Challa, Doom, Namor, the Wakandan delegation, Atlantis, Magneto and the mutant nation) • Nick Fury

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "International Summit Speech" — **Epic**
2. "Accidental Insult to a God" — **Comedy-Class**

---

Seven chapters into the steel-grey banner, the theatre had quietly rearranged itself again.

It had happened the way all the seating changes happened — without discussion, by gravity. The night a village had elected Issei Hyoudou its protector, the crowned heads of the audience had drifted into one block to argue about it, and the block had never dissolved. The room called it the statecraft rows: T'Challa of Wakanda at its center, composed as a treaty; Victor von Doom two seats away, tolerated under the truce that had held since Chapter One; Namor the Sub-Mariner at the aisle, because Namor required an aisle the way fires require oxygen; Magneto behind them with the patience of a man who had buried more nations than most of the row had governed. And at the block's edge, in a chair he'd dragged there himself somewhere around the tyrant chapters, Nick Fury — one eye, no throne, and the professional conviction that anywhere kings gathered, somebody sensible should be counting the exits.

The room needed tonight, and knew it. Two chapters of tyrants — the one spared, the one not — had left the aisles raw, and beneath everything, still, sat the photograph with the child-shaped hole in it, which no one mentioned and no one had stopped thinking about. When the gacha wheel descended and the steel-grey track lit with an unmistakably jaunty flicker, several thousand beings leaned forward with the shameless hunger of mourners smelling comedy.

**GACHA PULL: 1 OF 2,** printed the screen. **"INTERNATIONAL SUMMIT SPEECH" — RARITY: EPIC.**

And then, in smaller type, with what the room had learned to recognize as the machine enjoying itself:

**CONTEXT: THE RED DRAGON EMPEROR ATTENDS A PEACE CONFERENCE. THE MANAGEMENT ACCEPTS NO LIABILITY.**

"Oh no," said Ravel Phenex, from the fiancées' row, in the voice of a manager reading a subpoena. "Oh no, it's *that* one."

The theatre swallowed them.

---

The hall was built on neutral ground that six mythologies had spent a month arguing about, which told the audience everything before a word was spoken. Marble that remembered Olympus. Rafters carved with Yggdrasil's roots as a courtesy to the Norse delegation. Wards layered by Heaven, Grigori, and the Underworld in triplicate, because after the war against the Evil Dragons, nobody trusted anybody's wards alone.

The vision supplied the roster the way the machine always did, cold captions over vast presences: **ZEUS, GOD OF THE SKY.** **ODIN, THE ALLFATHER.** **INDRA, LORD OF THE DEVAS.** **AMATERASU-ŌMIKAMI, BY PROXY OF THE KYOTO DELEGATION.** **THE ARCHANGEL MICHAEL.** **AZAZEL, GOVERNOR GENERAL OF THE GRIGORI.** **SIRZECHS LUCIFER.** Rows of lesser thrones. A gallery of human observers from governments that had only recently been told, gently, that everything was real.

And, seated with the Devil delegation in formal dress he was visibly at war with, one (1) reincarnated High-Class Devil, hero of the recent war, twenty years old at the absolute outside, being briefed in an urgent whisper by a silver-haired Valkyrie.

"—and you address the Allfather as *Allfather*," Rossweisse was hissing, in the vision, "not as—"

"I know, I know."

"Say it back to me."

"Allfather. I've *got* this, Rossweisse."

In the theatre, present-day Rossweisse put her face in her hands with the smoothness of ritual.

The agenda was reparations. The war had cratered three pantheons' territories, and the question of who owed whom had curdled, over six hours of flawless diplomacy, into something with teeth. The vision compressed the six hours mercilessly: Zeus, magnificent and wounded in his pride, itemizing Olympus's dead with thunder banking behind every syllable. Indra, smiling like a knife shop, suggesting — never stating — that certain factions had profited from the chaos, and that the Devas might feel obliged to *rebalance* matters. The Norse pressing claims. Heaven conciliatory and ignored. Azazel cracking exactly one joke, which died on the marble. The human observers going grey as they realized the beings around them were negotiating the way tectonic plates negotiate.

The theatre's statecraft rows watched the mechanics with narrowed, expert eyes.

"There," T'Challa said quietly, at hour four of six. "That clause. Indra has just made war survivable for everyone who wants it. He's built them a door."

"He built it three exchanges ago," Doom said. "Zeus has been walking toward it since."

They were both right. By the time the vision let time run at full speed again, the hall had gone quiet in the specific way rooms go quiet when the next sentence is a declaration, and Zeus had risen, and the thunder was no longer banking.

Which was when Issei Hyoudou — who had been asleep with his eyes open since the appendices, sustained only by Rossweisse's elbow — stood up because his leg had cramped, knocked his water glass onto the table of the Deva delegation, said "*Sorry!* Sorry — crap — was that magic water? Is it ruined? It looks expensive—", and brought the collective attention of six pantheons down onto himself like a dropped chandelier.

Silence. Zeus, mid-declaration, stared at him.

"You have something to add," said the God of the Sky, in the tone that had preceded several floods. "*Boy.*"

The theatre watched Issei Hyoudou look around the hall — at Sirzechs's careful non-expression, at Rossweisse's dawning horror, at a month of diplomacy balanced on a knife — and visibly decide that since he was already standing, he might as well be honest.

"Yeah, okay," he said. "Um. I didn't understand most of today. I'm not smart. Everyone here knows I'm not smart; my own contract literally has a clause about not letting me negotiate." (**IT DOES,** the caption confirmed, unprompted.) "But I understood the last hour fine, because the last hour, you all stopped using long words. You're going to fight. You're dressing it up, but I've been in enough fights to know the face people make right before one, and, no offense, but gods make the same face as high schoolers."

A sound moved through the Norse delegation that might have been a laugh being strangled at birth.

"So I just want to say the thing nobody's said all day." He wasn't loud. That was what the vision made the theatre feel — he was the only person in six hours who hadn't projected. "Everyone in this room is stronger than me. Seriously, everyone; I checked the seating chart. And every single one of your dead — I'm sorry. I mean it. Some of them died next to me. But my best friend was murdered in a park by a war exactly like the one you're about to start — a *technically*, a leftover, a war nobody in the room actually wanted anymore, and she died because a bunch of people way above her pay grade made this face and nobody stood up." He took a breath. "So. If somebody has to bleed for everyone's pride to be okay — take mine. I'm offering. Any of you, one-on-one, whenever, I lose *all the time*, ask literally anyone. But you only get me. Because if it's armies, then it's kids in parks again, and I've done that funeral, and I'd rather do mine."

He sat down. Then stood halfway back up.

"Also, Lord Zeus, sorry about earlier, when I asked if the beard was attached. It's an amazing beard. That was a compliment that came out wrong."

He sat down again. Rossweisse had achieved the color of her own hair.

The hall held still for three full seconds. Then Odin — one-eyed, ancient, delighted — began to laugh, a sound like a longship coming home, and said: "The boy offers what no diplomat has brought me in two thousand years. A true price, from an open hand. Well, Sky-King? Shall we make war against *that*? The skalds would flay us."

And the theatre watched a declaration die. Not defeated — *embarrassed to death*. Zeus looked at the boy who had offered himself like small change, and the thunder went out of the room degree by degree, and the God of the Sky sat down with the heavy grace of a man choosing the better story. Indra's smile stayed exactly where it was, fixed, the smile of a craftsman watching a stranger step on six months of work. **RESOLUTION: RATIFIED,** printed the caption. **METHOD: UNPRECEDENTED. FILED UNDER: DIPLOMACY (DISPUTED).**

---

The theatre came back to itself in stages, like a crowd surfacing from cold water, and the statecraft rows came up arguing.

"*No.*" Namor was on his feet, which surprised no one, and pointing at the screen, which surprised no one either. "No. I have declared war for less than that water glass. I have *drowned navies* over protocol a tenth as violated. He interrupted a sovereign mid-declaration. He confessed weakness before assembled kings — he *inventoried* it. He apologized twice in one speech. Every law of statecraft I have ever—" He stopped. The room watched Namor the Sub-Mariner wrestle with something, and lose. "—and I would have accepted his offer, and then I would have made him an admiral. This is what enrages me."

"It should," said Doom.

The row turned. Doom had not risen. Doom was watching the frozen frame of the boy and the beard apology with the stillness he usually reserved for Strange's better spells.

"Understand what you witnessed," Doom said. "Doom has attended such summits. Doom has *engineered* such summits. Six hours of that hall was a machine for converting grievance into war while allowing every party to claim they resisted — Indra built it beautifully; Doom would have hired him. And that boy walked into the machine and jammed it with the one substance it cannot process." A pause. "Sincerity. Do you grasp the horror? It cannot be counterfeited past a god's perception, it cannot be purchased, it cannot be trained. Doom commands every instrument of statecraft ever forged, and Doom could stand in that hall for a century and not do what the idiot did by *knocking over a glass*. He has no diplomacy. He has only terms. It merely happens that the terms are true." The mask turned slightly toward T'Challa. "Say it, then. You have been composing it for a minute."

"I have," T'Challa admitted, and there was a smile in it. "Only this: we call honesty a virtue and treat it as a handicap. Every court on my world assumes a hidden position — that assumption *is* diplomacy; the entire game is priced on it. But a man whose position is fully visible cannot be outmaneuvered, because there is nothing behind him to reach. The gods could verify every word he said. His offer required no trust, no verification clause, no hostages — his transparency *was* the collateral. My father told me a king's word must be a wall. That boy's word is a wall with the interior lit and the door open, and it turns out that is stronger." He inclined his head at the screen. "Polished diplomacy would have failed in that room. It had already failed. It failed for six hours in perfect grammar."

"Speaking as a professional liar," said Nick Fury, from his exit-counting chair, "this is the worst thing I've ever seen." He said it with something dangerously close to reverence. "Everything I have ever built runs on one assumption: everybody lies, so you price the lie. That kid breaks the pricing model. You can't flip him, because he'd tell you he'd been flipped. You can't feed him disinformation, because he'd announce he didn't understand it. You can't even *recruit* him, because the moment you make him an asset, he stops being one — the whole value is that nobody owns him and everybody can check." He shook his head slowly. "I've spent forty years learning every dialect of deception, and the most dangerous negotiator I've ever watched work is a kid whose entire toolkit is *meaning it.*"

Behind them, unhurried, two men were writing.

Loki's notes, those nearest him swore afterward, were genuinely on paper, in a hand like frost: *A man who cannot lie is the perfect instrument — everyone believes him. Corollary: never let him know the plan; simply arrange for him to describe it. Second corollary, worse: he cannot be used, because being used is a lie he'd say out loud. Conclusion: the only way to profit from him is to be genuinely on his side. Suspect this is deliberate on the universe's part. Insulted.*

And Magneto, who wrote nothing down and missed nothing: "Charles spent fifty years learning to speak to frightened people so that the fear could hear him past the words," he said, mostly to Xavier, who was already listening. "This boy skips the learning. There is nothing between his intention and his mouth for fear to hide in. If the mutant cause had possessed one voice that provably could not lie—" He let it stand unfinished. "Take your notes, trickster. I am taking mine."

"He'd be *impossible* to write speeches for," Shuri said, delighted. "Zero message discipline. A communications catastrophe. I want to study him."

"I want to *guard* him," muttered Okoye, staring at the frozen hall full of gods. "One principal, six pantheons, and he spills water on the Devas. My spear hand itches."

**GACHA PULL: 2 OF 2,** said the screen, and the steel-grey track flickered into something the theatre had never seen it do — a rimshot.

**"ACCIDENTAL INSULT TO A GOD" — RARITY: COMEDY-CLASS. FORMAT: SUPERCUT.**

"No," said Rossweisse, in the front row, to the ceiling, to fate. "*No.*"

Yes. Item: Issei Hyoudou meeting Odin, Allfather, wisest of the Aesir, and greeting him — warmly, respectfully, from the heart — with "You're an even bigger pervert than me, gramps. That's actually amazing. I have so many questions" — while his bride, formerly the Allfather's own bodyguard, died standing up in dress uniform. Odin's recorded verdict: "I like this one." Item: Issei assuring Zeus, at their first meeting, that "honestly, some of the rumors about you make *me* look responsible," which the caption scored **INSULT: ACCURATE**, and which Zeus had — after a pause the length of a weather system — declined to hear. Item: Issei thanking the Shinto delegation for "the good sun" during a battle and adding, to the Archangel Michael, *in front of Amaterasu's proxy*, that Heaven's light "kind of stings, no offense" — one sentence, two pantheons, and Michael laughing before his aides could gasp. Item: Issei telling Indra, flatly, publicly, "I don't trust you. You smile like Azazel when he's about to ruin my week" — captioned **INSULT: DOUBLE. BOTH PARTIES CONFIRMED FLATTERED. ONE OF THEM WAS LYING.**

The theatre was helpless. Even the statecraft rows. Even — briefly, awfully, a sound like a bass string — Doom.

Only at the supercut's end did the laughter thin, because the machine appended one final frame without comedy scoring: a throne of bone, a god shaped like a grave, and Issei Hyoudou looking at Hades with no joke anywhere in him at all. **SOME GODS,** the screen noted, **HE DOES NOT TEASE.** The frame lasted two seconds and closed like a door being marked for later.

"Ah," said Loki softly, pen stopping. "So the honest man keeps one ledger of his own."

The wheel rose. The steel-grey band glowed on, and beneath it the screen printed its hook into the dark with the smugness of a machine that knew exactly what dragons kept besides promises:

**NEXT: EVERY KING HAS A TREASURY. YOU HAVE MET THE DRAGON. YOU HAVE NOT SEEN THE HOARD.**

In the villains' rows, entirely without meaning to, Rocket Raccoon — visiting from the Ravager block, allegedly to heckle — made a small, involuntary, deeply sincere noise.

"Oh no," said Ravel Phenex again, to no one, to history.
