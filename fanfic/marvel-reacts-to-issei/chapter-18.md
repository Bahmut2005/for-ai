# Chapter Eighteen: The Price of Boosting

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Bruce Banner • The Sentry • Cosmic Mentors (Eternals, the Watcher)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Boost over Limit Consequences" — **Rare**
2. "Exhaustion Collapse" — **Common**

---

The theatre had learned to read its own weather, and tonight the weather was wrong.

Not storm-grey, which meant death. Not gold, which meant Thor pouring. Tonight the lights came up white — flat, sourceless, even white, the white of a corridor outside an operating room at three in the morning. Nobody was drinking. Several people who had been talking stopped without knowing why, the way conversations die in hospitals.

Ravel Phenex's ledger had done that. The previous chapter had ended with a small girl in curls itemizing a boy's injuries in immaculate script, and the theatre had gone home — insofar as prisoners go home — with the uneasy sense that they had been shown the index of a book they had not yet been made to read.

Tonight the book opened.

The gacha wheel descended without ceremony for once, rings turning slow and deliberate, like a surgeon laying out instruments. It clicked. It stopped. The token fell into the crystal bowl with a sound less like a wineglass and more like a coin dropped on a steel tray.

**PULL ONE: BOOST OVER LIMIT CONSEQUENCES — RARE.**

"Consequences," said Bruce Banner, very quietly, from the seat he always chose — aisle-adjacent, sight lines to two exits that did not exist. He had a paper cup of tea the theatre kept refilling whether he asked or not. He set it down. "Okay. Okay. There it is."

Three rows back and forty seats to the left — because he always sat forty seats from everyone, by unspoken mutual arrangement — Robert Reynolds went very still, golden and luminous and trying not to be, like a lighthouse attempting to slouch.

---

The screen did not open on a battle. It opened on a chalkboard.

That was the gacha's little joke, and nobody laughed. A schoolroom in Kuoh, empty, after hours, and Issei Hyoudou at the board with Ddraig's gem glowing on his manifested gauntlet, writing numbers with the squeaky, effortful penmanship of a boy for whom mathematics had historically been an enemy nation.

"Okay," on-screen Issei said. "So. Boost doubles me. Every ten seconds. So after ten seconds I'm two-me. Then four-me. Then eight-me—"

**"Correct so far,"** said Ddraig, in the tone of a tutor whose student has surprised him by showing up.

"—and there's no cap. You said no cap. So if I just stand there boosting for, like, five minutes, I'm—" squeak, squeak, scratch — "—a *billion*-me? That can't be right. Ddraig, am I secretly a billion-me?"

**"The Gear has no ceiling, partner,"** the dragon said, and there was something in the old voice that the theatre, which had grown fluent in Ddraig, recognized at once and did not like. Care. Care of the kind that precedes bad news. **"You have a ceiling. The Gear will double whatever you are, as many times as you ask. It will never once ask whether the vessel can hold it. That question falls to your bones. Your blood. Your heart, which is a fist-sized muscle and not a metaphor. The previous wielders—"**

"The previous wielders what?"

A pause, on-screen and in the theatre.

**"Ask me again when you are older,"** said Ddraig, and the memory folded, and the real pull began.

---

It was a fight the theatre had not seen before — a night engagement, a churned and cratered field under a shattered barrier, the Gremory peerage scattered and bleeding across it, an enemy at the far end whom the gacha kept deliberately out of focus, because the enemy was not the point. The point was the boy in the middle distance, on his knees, one arm sheathed in red.

The point was the number over his head.

The gacha had never done this before: a diagnostic overlay, cold green, hovering above the vision like the readout of a machine that had no opinion about what it measured.

**[BOOST COUNT: 9. OUTPUT: 512× BASELINE. VESSEL INTEGRITY: 71%.]**

**[BOOST.]**

**[BOOST COUNT: 10. OUTPUT: 1,024× BASELINE. VESSEL INTEGRITY: 64%.]**

"Oh," said Beast, softly, into the white light. "Oh, it's showing us the *invoice.*"

On-screen, Issei got up. That was all — he got up, which the theatre had watched him do in twelve chapters the way other heroes threw punches, and this time the overlay made them feel every degree of it. At 1,024 times himself, the power came off him in visible pressure, tearing the crater rim, and the capillaries in his left eye let go all at once, flooding the white with red.

**[BOOST.]**

**[VESSEL INTEGRITY: 55%. ADVISORY: SAFE THRESHOLD EXCEEDED.]**

**"Partner."** Ddraig's voice, low, urgent, meant for one person and heard by a multiverse. **"Your body is answering questions it was not built to be asked. Two more and your forearm fractures under its own aura. Four more and I cannot promise your heart."**

"Asia's still behind me," Issei said. His voice was wet. "Rias is still behind me. So."

**[BOOST.]**

**[VESSEL INTEGRITY: 46%.]**

"So," he said again, and smiled with red teeth, and the theatre — gods, monsters, kings, and killers — sat in a silence with texture to it.

**[BOOST.]**

**[VESSEL INTEGRITY: 38%. ADVISORY REPEATS. ADVISORY REPEATS.]**

And then he did the thing that broke the room, which was: he didn't use it. Four thousand and ninety-six times himself, arm hairline-cracking, one eye blind with blood — and the boy turned, found Rias Gremory across the field, and gave it all away.

**[TRANSFER.]**

The power left him like a tide leaving a harbor, and the screen split its attention with terrible economy: on one side, crimson annihilation arriving downrange, the out-of-focus enemy simply ending; on the other side, the invoice coming due. Issei Hyoudou folded at the knees. Vomited blood into the churned dirt, once, twice, with the horrible businesslike rhythm of someone who has done this before. Tried to catch himself on the left arm and found the left arm no longer accepting instructions. Went down face-first, and did not move, and the overlay printed its last line without emphasis, without cruelty, without anything at all:

**[VESSEL INTEGRITY: 31%. WIELDER NONRESPONSIVE. BOOST RESET. DURATION OF INCAPACITY: 4 DAYS, 7 HOURS.]**

The vision held on him for a long moment — small, face-down, steam rising off the gauntlet — and the last sound before the lights came up was Ddraig, alone in a boy's unconscious dark, saying, to no one who could hear him:

**"You counted past ten again, partner. You always count past ten."**

---

The theatre lights rose on a room that did not want to talk, which had never once stopped it.

"Somebody check the math," said Sam Wilson, finally, from the Avengers block. "Somebody who isn't me. Because I've been assuming — I think we've all been assuming — that the doubling thing was, y'know. The cheat code. The 'give him ten minutes and he beats anybody' button."

"The math checks," said Bruce Banner.

He hadn't stood up. Banner never stood up if he could help it; standing up was how things started. But the theatre had learned that when the quiet man with the tea spoke, you let him, because there was an eleven-foot appendix to everything he said.

"I've been running it since chapter one," he went on, in the mild, apologetic voice of a man defusing himself as he spoke. "It's a clean exponential. Doubling every ten seconds is six doublings a minute — that's sixty-four-fold in the first minute. Two minutes, four thousand and ninety-six. Three minutes, a quarter million. Five minutes—" he turned his cup a quarter-turn on the armrest, "—five minutes is a billion, the kid had it right on the chalkboard, give or take. There is no fighter in this room, there is no *being* in this room, you couldn't theoretically out-scale with five uninterrupted minutes and a stopwatch. That's what the Gear is. That's what everyone here has been quietly afraid of since the tutorial."

"I detect an 'and,'" said Doctor Strange, from the mystic tiers.

"And you just saw the denominator," Banner said. "The Gear multiplies. It doesn't *carry*. Every doubling is written to a sixteen-year-old body — human-baseline until recently, low-tier devil now — with a sixteen-year-old's blood vessels and a sixteen-year-old's heart. The power has no ceiling, so the ceiling has to come from somewhere, and you all watched where it comes from. It comes from him hitting the ground." He looked at the dark screen, and his voice stayed exactly as level, which was its own kind of alarming. "It's not a cheat code. It's a loan structure. He can borrow any amount — literally any amount, that's the horror of it — and the collateral is always the same. It's him. The interest rate is *meat.*"

"Then he requires discipline," Doom said flatly, from his aisle. "A regimen. Limits imposed from without, since the Gear imposes none. Doom sees an engineering problem."

"Yeah, see, I want to agree with you," Banner said. "I'd love this to be an engineering problem. But you saw the trigger. Everybody saw the trigger." He counted it off, gently, on his fingers. "*Asia's still behind me. Rias is still behind me. So.* His output isn't indexed to training, Victor. It's indexed to how much he *loves* the people in the blast radius. The power scales with feeling. The cost lands on the body. The decision runs through willpower. That's the whole machine."

He stopped. The theatre waited, because everyone — everyone — knew what was coming, and knew he knew, and there was a kind of courtesy in letting him choose to say it.

"I know that machine," Bruce Banner said. "I've been living in that machine since the gamma range. Power that answers emotion. You never get to be strong on schedule; you get to be strong when it *hurts*, because hurting is the ignition. The difference — " and here, for one second, the mildness flickered, not toward green, just toward tired — "the difference is that my curse protects the body it rides in. Whatever else the other guy is, I wake up whole. It costs me everything except tissue. That kid's version costs *tissue first.* He's got my throttle and none of my armor. He is doing, voluntarily, over and over, with a smile, the thing I have spent my entire adult life building cages to prevent." He picked his tea back up. "So no. Not a cheat code. From where I'm sitting, it's the most expensive power in the building."

The white light hummed. And then a voice said, "It's not, though."

Heads turned. Forty seats from anyone, gold against the velvet, Robert Reynolds had spoken without standing, both hands flat on his knees like a man keeping them where he could see them.

"Sorry," Bob said. "Sorry. It's — Bruce, it's not the most expensive power in the building. Mine is. And that's — that's actually what I need everyone to understand about what we just watched, because you're all looking at the blood, and the blood is not the scary part."

Nobody heckled. When the Sentry was frightened, the theatre had discovered, fear observed a food chain.

"The power of a million exploding suns," Bob said, "runs on the same fuel his does. Will. Feeling. Belief. That's the tank. And everyone thinks the danger of a power like that is *running out* — that the fragile psyche fails, and the power sputters, and down you go. That's backwards. That's so backwards." His hands pressed flatter on his knees. "The danger is that the power *learns the route.* It learns that fear opens the tap. It learns that grief opens it wider. It learns which of your feelings are load-bearing, and then — and I need you to hear this — then it starts *having opinions about your feelings.* It wants the tap open. It will arrange for the tap to be open. And a boy whose strength doubles when the people he loves are in danger is a boy whose strength has a *vested interest in his loved ones being in danger*, and he will never see it coming, because it will feel exactly like love."

Silence. Banner had turned all the way around in his seat.

"His body says no at thirty-one percent," Bob said. "Okay. Bodies are honest. But what happens on the day the power finds a way around the body? A power with no ceiling, wired to a heart with no off switch — that doesn't stay a boy breaking himself forever. One day that's a door. And things come through doors." He looked up at the dead screen, and asked it, directly, the way one asks a thing one suspects is listening: "What's behind his?"

And the screen — which had been dark, between pulls, dormant — flickered.

One line. White text. A tenth of a second too long to be an accident:

**[QUERY ACKNOWLEDGED. RELATED FILE LOCATED: ██████████ DRIVE. CLASSIFICATION: FORBIDDEN. THIS PULL IS NOT YET AVAILABLE.]**

The theatre did not gasp. The theatre had, by chapter eighteen, exhausted its gasps. What moved through the tiers instead was worse: the low, many-throated sound of ten thousand apex predators updating a threat model at the same time.

"It heard him," said Faiza Hussain, from the MI-13 rows. "It's still doing the thing where it hears us."

"RED WEATHER file," said Pete Wisdom, hollowly, producing the folder. "Reopening. New annotation: *storm warning.*"

Bob Reynolds put his face in his hands, gold light leaking between the fingers, and Bruce Banner did something he had done for no other being in the theatre: he got up, crossed forty seats' worth of no-man's-land, and sat down next to him. He didn't say anything. Two men who understood the machine sat side by side, and the wheel, tactful as a headsman, began to spin again.

**PULL TWO: EXHAUSTION COLLAPSE — COMMON.**

"Common," said Hank McCoy, and his voice came out strange. "Colleagues — apologies, one moment — the taxonomy matters here. The gacha grades by *frequency.* Mythic happened once. Rare happens seldom." He took off his glasses. "It is telling us that what we are about to see is *ordinary.* That in the life of this child, this is weather."

---

It was a montage, and it was worse than the battle.

Issei face-down on the Gremory garden grass after the ten-day training, hauled up the stairs by Koneko with the neutral efficiency of a girl carrying firewood. Issei collapsing mid-sentence at a victory party, mid-*laugh*, blood at his nose, caught by Kiba before his head hit the table, and the terrible detail was that nobody at the party screamed — they had a *procedure.* Cushions appeared. Asia's hands were already glowing. Someone kept the music on so his parents downstairs wouldn't worry.

Issei after a stray-devil hunt, paying the invoice in an alley, one arm braced on a wall, waving off the concern of his own shadow. Issei transferring power to Koneko's fist, to Akeno's lightning, to Rias's annihilation, and every Transfer ending the same way — the tide leaving the harbor, the knees folding, the small businesslike apology as he went down: "sorry — gimme ten — I'm good—"

He was never good in ten.

The gacha cut to the infirmary at Kuoh Academy, and then to the Hyoudou household, and then to a page — a single, immaculate page in handwriting the theatre had learned the previous chapter, curls of gilt ink, Phenex letterhead:

*Collapse log, current term. Post-Transfer syncope: 9. Over-boost hemorrhage: 4. Days unconscious (cumulative): 22. Note: subject describes this as "a normal amount." Note appended: it is not a normal amount. — R.P.*

And then the last vision, and the gacha let it linger, because it had learned the room and knew exactly where the nerve was: night, Issei's own bedroom, the boy unconscious for the third day, grey and small under the blankets. Asia Argento asleep in the chair beside him, healing rings still faintly circling her hands even in sleep, because she had trained herself to heal him unconscious. And in the dark, the gem in the half-manifest gauntlet glowing like a banked ember — Ddraig keeping the watch he always kept.

**"They think you do not hear me when you are like this,"** the dragon said quietly. **"Perhaps you do not. It is easier to say if you do not. Every wielder I have had, partner — every one, for two thousand years — spent himself like this, in the beginning. Boosted past ten. Paid in blood, and laughed, and paid again. And every one of them, one day, found the place where the body stops saying no."** The ember dimmed. **"I have decided you will be the first I talk out of it. I have not yet decided how. Sleep, boy. Count to ten. Stop there. For an old dragon's sake — one day, stop there."**

The screen went dark.

---

They came down from the cosmic tiers slowly, the way glaciers arrive: the Eternals, who had watched eighteen chapters mostly in the high seats, in the manner of people auditing a course they'd taught for centuries.

"We should say the quiet thing," said Sersi, "since no one else is equipped to. Every being in this room with real power falls into one of two designs. Either the cost is externalized" — she nodded, delicately, up toward the mezzanine where the Silver Surfer hung above his board — "paid by a star, a patron, a planet, a Machine. Or the vessel is engineered to the load. We were *built* to our output. Thor's frame was forged for Thor's lightning. The design principle of the entire cosmos is: *the container is rated for the contents.*" She looked at the screen. "That child is the first being I have ever seen whose design principle is *the container will simply agree to break.* His ceiling is not physics. His ceiling is however much self-destruction he will consent to. And you all watched his consent. It's *bottomless.*"

"Then the analysis is short," said Ikaris, arms folded. "It doesn't sustain. Nothing built on that curve sustains. Doubling output against a linear body — he is a mortal running arbitrage against his own lifespan. Five years. I am being generous because the little healer is skilled."

"You are being *arithmetical,*" said Thena, and there was an edge under the silk of it. "I have spent ten thousand years among warriors who ration themselves, who husband their strength, who survive. Do you know what they never do? *They never win the last war.* The ones who stand at the end are always, always the ones with the flaw in the ledger — the ones who will pay what sane beings won't. I watched a boy hand away four thousand times his own strength because his people were behind him, and you saw an actuarial problem. I saw the only kind of soldier who has ever mattered."

"He will still be dead in five years."

"Then he will have been *magnificent* for five years, and you and I will still be here, being rated for our contents." Thena sat back. "I find I know which I envy."

Ajak had said nothing, watching the dark screen with her hands folded, and when she spoke it was half to herself. "We keep asking whether it sustains. Perhaps the wrong question. The Celestials made us fixed — the same forever, safe forever, finished forever. That boy is *unfinished* in a way nothing we serve has ever permitted. A being whose limit is renegotiated every ten seconds by love and stubbornness." She smiled, faintly, troubled. "I cannot decide whether I am watching a tragedy or the early drafts of a god. I notice the theatre has odds on both."

High above them all, in the tier where no one else sat because nothing else fit, Uatu the Watcher held his silence, as was his oath and his habit. But he had watched a billion timelines, and in every single one, power indexed to self-destruction resolved in one of exactly two ways — the grave, or the monster — and he had never, not once, recorded a third ending.

*He is in no timeline I hold,* Uatu thought, and the thought had been unsettling for seventeen chapters and tonight, for the first time, it curved toward something almost like hope. *Which means his ending is not yet on file. Perhaps that is why we were all brought here. To watch a third ending get written.*

And far below, in the villains' dark, Mephisto laughed softly into his wine, alone among the horrified.

"Oh, you're all reading it upside down," he murmured, to nobody, to everybody. "You've just been shown the *provenance.* Eight Pawns for a boy who pays for power in blood and never misses a payment? Nothing priceless is free, children. The price is the proof."

The lights began their end-of-chapter dim. The last thing the screen showed was small: the collapse-log page again, Ravel's gilt ink — but the gacha had appended one line of its own beneath her tally, in cold system-white, and it let the theatre read it twice before dark:

**[SUBJECT WILL COUNT PAST TEN AGAIN IN: 2 CHAPTERS.]**

Banner exhaled. Bob Reynolds did not.
