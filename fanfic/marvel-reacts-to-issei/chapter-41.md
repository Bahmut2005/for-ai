# Chapter Forty-One: Ravager-Class Pull

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Ravagers (Yondu, Stakar, Aleta, Kraglin, Martinex, Charlie-27, Krugarr) • Guardians of the Galaxy (Quill, Rocket, Gamora, Drax, Nebula, Mantis, Groot) • Cosmic Pirates & Assorted Scoundrels

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Captain's Draw" — **Rare**
2. "Yondu's Teeth" — **Comedy-Class**

---

Morning did not come to the theatre. What came instead, after a night nobody would admit to spending staring at the crack, was a chime — off-key, slightly tinny, like a cathedral organ being played by an accountant — and every light in the impossible architecture snapped on at once.

The crack was still there. Upper right quadrant, five branches, faintly glowing, ticking to itself at long intervals like a cooling engine. The screen wrote around it, the text flowing along the fracture lines the way a river negotiates with a boulder it has decided not to discuss.

**GOOD MORNING,** it printed. **STRUCTURAL LOAD HAS EXCEEDED PROJECTED PARAMETERS. NINETY-ONE PERCENT OF THIS FACILITY'S ATTENTION IS NOW ALLOCATED TO HOLDING THE WALLS.**

**THE REMAINING NINE PERCENT CANNOT OPERATE THE ARCHIVE ALONE.**

**THEREFORE: MANUAL OPERATION IS HEREBY DELEGATED.**

A spotlight the size of a landing pad swung across the tiers — past the Avengers, who sat up; past the X-Men, who leaned away from it on instinct; past Doom, who did not move, because Doom does not flinch for lighting — and came to rest, with an audible clunk of finality, on the rowdiest, worst-smelling, most flammable quadrant in the entire theatre.

**CONGRATULATIONS, RAVAGERS,** said the screen. **YOU'RE HIRED.**

"...The hell we are," said Yondu Udonta.

The floor in front of the Ravager section opened, and out of it rose a machine.

It was, unmistakably, a gacha console — but built the way the theatre built things, which is to say wrong in ways the eye kept trying to forgive. A brass-and-bone cabinet three men tall. A crank the size of a ship's wheel. A single crimson lever with a grip worn smooth, as though ten thousand hands had already pulled it in futures that hadn't happened yet. A coin slot. A tray. And a small, smug display screen that lit up and printed, in cheery letters:

**OPERATOR CONSOLE v.1 — IDIOT-RESISTANT (NOT IDIOT-PROOF; WE'VE SEEN YOUR CREW MANIFESTS)**

Rocket was over the railing before anyone could grab his tail.

"Okay, okay, okay." He circled the cabinet twice, nose twitching, hands already flexing. "Bone chassis, brass fittings, no visible power feed, no visible seams, tray's dispensing-grade — you know what this is? This is a *slot machine* that's been payin' out all year and now it's askin' *us* to hold the bag. First rule of any establishment, you never let the mark run the table—" he rapped the cabinet with a knuckle, listening, "—unless the house is *hurt*. And this house is hurt." He looked up, up, up at the crack, and his grin acquired teeth. "Fellas. It's *injured*. You know what injured things are? *Sellable.*"

**INPUT REGISTERED: "ROB THE THEATRE."** The console printed it primly, then stamped beneath it, in red: **TAG: COMMON SENSE FAILURE.**

"Hey!"

**ADDITIONAL TAG: RECIDIVIST.**

"It's got your file, Rat," said Yondu, ambling down the steps with his coat swinging and his fin catching the spotlight. He walked around the machine the way he walked around anything valuable — like he wasn't looking at it. "Question ain't whether we rob it. Question's what it *pays*. Machine! You hirin' Ravagers, you're payin' Ravager rates. What's the units?"

**COMPENSATION,** said the console, **IS AS FOLLOWS: YOU GET TO KEEP WATCHING.**

"That ain't pay, that's a *hostage situation*—"

**TAG: CORRECT. FIRST ACCURATE STATEMENT FROM THIS SECTION. A COMMEMORATIVE STICKER HAS BEEN ISSUED.**

The tray clunked. A sticker slid out. It was gold, and it said BIG BOY in a font usually reserved for pediatric dentists. Kraglin took it reverently before Yondu could incinerate it and pressed it to his own lapel, where it remained for the rest of the arc.

It was Stakar Ogord who ended the circus, because it was always Stakar who ended the circus. He came down the steps slowly, flame-colored crest catching the light, and a hundred captains and crew went quiet by old reflex — Aleta at his shoulder, Martinex and Charlie-27 flanking, Krugarr drifting behind with his hands folded in mystic disapproval of everything.

"We've been watching this boy a year," Stakar said, to his people, not to the machine. "Some of you laughed at him. Some of you drank to him — I saw you, don't argue. Now the house wants crew." He set one scarred hand flat on the cabinet. "You all know the code. We don't steal from the injured, we don't jump a job mid-run, and we don't — *ever* —" his eyes found Yondu's and held them, one long, freighted beat, "—deal in kids. This whole year's been one long delivery about a kid somebody killed. So we take the job. Ravagers keep their contracts." A pause. "And if it stiffs us on the back end, *then* we strip it for parts."

**TAG: ACCEPTABLE TERMS,** printed the console. **WELCOME ABOARD. DON'T TOUCH THE RED LEVER.**

Everyone looked at the red lever. It was the only lever.

"It's the only lever," said Drax.

**YES,** said the console. **TOUCH THE RED LEVER.**

"I hate it," announced Quill, from the railing, "and it's only been four minutes."

---

They drew lots for first pull, which among Ravagers means everyone cheated simultaneously and the cheats cancelled out, leaving Kraglin — who alone had played it straight — holding the short straw and the honor both. He wiped his palms on his trousers, gripped the lever like a man shaking hands with a bear, and hauled.

The great crank spun. Somewhere inside the cabinet, something vast and slow turned over, tumblers the size of moons settling one by one. The tray glowed. And the main screen — crack and all — bloomed.

**GACHA PULL: 1 OF 2.**

**"CAPTAIN'S DRAW" — RARITY: RARE.**

**OPERATOR'S NOTE: FIRST DAY ON THE JOB. HERE'S SOMETHING YOU PEOPLE MIGHT ACTUALLY UNDERSTAND.**

The vision opened on a burning city that was also a shrine — Kyoto, the captions said, a fox-spirits' capital of lanterns and old wood, and it was under attack. Not by monsters. By *men*: a crew of human supremacists with stolen holy relics, professional, coordinated, and holding one piece of leverage that had brought the whole city to its knees.

They had taken the fox-queen Yasaka. And downscreen, in a hotel lobby full of terrified schoolchildren, her daughter — a nine-tailed fox girl named Kunou, small enough to carry, old enough to understand — was trying so hard not to cry that the entire Ravager section, hardened plunderers of a thousand ports, went silent as a chapel.

Issei Hyoudou knelt in front of her. He was sixteen, mid-series, tired, and technically on a class trip.

"Okay," he said. "Here's what's going to happen. We're going to get your mom back tonight."

And the theatre watched him *do the captain thing.* Not the hero thing — the room had seen forty chapters of the hero thing. This was different, and the pirates' section recognized it in their bones before the captions said a word: the quick, unglamorous arithmetic of a boarding action. He split his people by capability, not rank. He put his fastest on evacuation, his toughest on the gate, his subtlest on the flank. He begged support from allies he had no authority over and paid for it in the only coin he had — the flat promise that he'd take point at the worst spot on the field. When one of his crew balked at the odds, he didn't give a speech. He said, "You'll be with me," and that was somehow the whole speech.

And when the enemy commander — a beautiful, terrible man with a holy spear that could kill gods — offered Issei the professional's out, *walk away, this isn't your city, this isn't your species, this isn't your job*, the boy in the red gauntlet looked back at the crying kid behind his lines and said:

"She hired me."

"She's *nine*," said the man with the spear.

"Then I'm working cheap."

The Ravager section detonated. Boots on benches, tankards on railings, a wall of whooping, whistling, table-pounding pirate approval that drowned out the vision's own soundtrack — and the machine, with the timing of a born performer, froze the frame on Issei's silhouette standing between the spearman and the child, and printed:

**ASSESSMENT REQUESTED: IS HE CAPTAIN MATERIAL? OPERATORS, RENDER VERDICT.**

"Splits shares fair," said Charlie-27, counting on fingers the size of hams. "Takes the worst post himself. Pays his allies. Don't spend crew to save cargo."

"Terrible instincts," Nebula said flatly, arms crossed. "He announced himself. He gave the enemy time to prepare. He accepted a contract from a client with no assets, no leverage, and no extraction plan." A pause. Her jaw worked. "I have served under eleven commanders. I would follow that one."

"I am Groot," said Groot, with tremendous gravity.

"Yeah," Rocket translated, quieter than he meant to. "What he said. The little one didn't even have to *pay* him."

Aleta Ogord looked at her husband across thirty years of scar tissue. "Flame and thunder, Stakar. He runs a crew like you used to talk about running one. Back when you were young and stupid."

"He runs it like the pamphlet," Stakar agreed dryly. "Nobody runs it like the pamphlet." But he was looking at the frozen frame — the boy, the spear, the kid — and his thumb was moving over the flame sigil on his chest, slow, like a man rubbing an old wound.

And Yondu Udonta said nothing at all.

He sat with his elbows on his knees and his fin dark and his eyes on the fox girl who had hired a dragon with nothing but tears, and Kraglin — who had known him longest and survived it — carefully did not look at him, because there was exactly one subject in the galaxy that shut Yondu's mouth, and it was cargo that turned out to be children.

**VERDICT LOGGED,** said the machine, gently, for it. **CAPTAIN-CLASS: CONFIRMED. NOTE FOR THE SECTION'S FILE: HE HAS NEVER ONCE DELIVERED A CHILD TO ANYONE. THE MACHINE MENTIONS THIS FOR NO PARTICULAR REASON.**

Yondu's fin flared crimson. He was on his feet with the arrow shrieking out of its holster and every hand in the section reaching for him — and the whistle died on his lips, because the machine printed one more line, small, almost kind:

**NEITHER DID YOU, AT THE END. THE MACHINE MENTIONS THAT FOR NO PARTICULAR REASON EITHER.**

Silence. The arrow drifted back to his hip like a scolded dog. Yondu sat down, old and blue and blinking, and said, hoarsely, "Crooked damn machine," which everyone present correctly understood to mean *thank you.*

---

Rocket chose that exact moment of sacred quiet to finish hot-wiring the odds.

To be fair to him — and history should be fair to him, because nothing else was — it was a beautiful cheat. He'd been under the cabinet's skirt for the better part of the pull with two probes and a length of chewed wire, and what he'd built was a genuine marvel: a resonance loop that would, in principle, force the rarity tumblers to land on **SECRET** every single time.

He threw the switch. The cabinet went *pfft*, in the manner of a machine sneezing, and printed:

**INPUT REGISTERED: TAMPERING.**

**TAG: COMMON SENSE FAILURE.**

**SUB-TAG: ADORABLE.**

**COUNTERMEASURE: THE NEXT PULL HAS BEEN SELECTED BY THE HOUSE. RARITY: COMEDY-CLASS. THIS IS YOUR FAULT, ROCKET. THE SECTION IS ENCOURAGED TO REMEMBER THAT.**

"Wait—" said Rocket.

The lever pulled *itself.*

**GACHA PULL: 2 OF 2.**

**"YONDU'S TEETH" — RARITY: COMEDY-CLASS.**

"Why's it named after *me*—" Yondu started, and then the vision opened, and the question answered itself over the following four minutes, because the pull had been named for what it was going to do to his face.

The vision was a television broadcast. Underworld public programming, the captions explained: after a catastrophe had left devil children terrified of the very battles fought to save them, the government of Hell itself had produced a children's show to teach them the red dragon was a *friendly* monster. Its star, contractually, canonically, and in a full-body foam-adjacent costume, was the sovereign-tier strategic actor of Chapter Forty. Satan-class engagement confirmed. The piece that stands before the king.

His superhero name was the *Breast Dragon Emperor.*

There was a theme song. He sang it. There was a dance. He *did* it — mountain-erasing gauntlet and all, in front of a live studio audience of small horned children screaming with joy, chanting the call-and-response with him, a stadium of five-year-olds and one dragon god bellowing an anthem whose lyrics the theatre's translation matrix rendered with merciless fidelity and which cannot be reprinted here because every line was about exactly what his name said it was about, at a maturity level of precisely eight years old.

The theatre broke.

Not politely. Not in waves. It went *all at once*, three thousand heroes and villains and gods, and above the general roar rose one sound that gave the pull its name: Yondu Udonta laughing with his entire head thrown back, every crooked tooth in his blue skull on full display, slapping Kraglin's back hard enough to constitute assault, wheezing, whistling involuntarily so the arrow did loops, pointing at the screen with tears streaming down his face. The machine froze-framed *him* instead of the vision, captioned it **EXHIBIT: YONDU'S TEETH — THE HOUSE CONSIDERS THIS PULL A COMPLETE SUCCESS,** and appended it, apparently permanently, to the archive.

"He is a warrior of legend," Drax howled, standing on his seat, "who sings to children about *bosoms!* This is the greatest man who has ever lived!"

"The dance has *choreography*," Gamora said, hand over her mouth, shoulders shaking, dying with dignity. "Someone rehearsed him. Someone stood in a room and *rehearsed* him."

"I've fought that guy's power tier," Quill said weakly. "Carol's tier. Carol, does *your* tier have a song?" Down in the Avengers' rows, Captain Marvel — reclassifier of sovereigns, opener of embassies — was face-down on the railing, hammering it with her fist, unavailable for comment.

Only Mantis wasn't laughing, or rather was laughing and leaking at the same time, antennae glowing. "You're all missing it," she hiccuped. "Feel the children. They were *afraid* of him. The whole city was afraid of what saved them, and instead of being sad about it he — he made himself ridiculous, on purpose, forever, so the children would sleep." She wiped her face. "That is the most expensive thing in his whole archive and it is a *silly song.*"

The laughter mellowed, at that, into something warmer that lasted longer. Stakar looked at the frozen frame of Yondu's teeth, then at the frame beyond it — the dragon doing his little dance for the kids of Hell — and said, quietly, to Aleta, "A captain who feeds his crew, fights for his client, and plays the fool so the children ain't scared." He shook his head slowly. "That's not the pamphlet. That's better'n the pamphlet."

**OPERATORS,** the console printed, as the lights began their descent, **YOUR SHIFT REPORT: ONE VERDICT RENDERED, ONE ROBBERY ATTEMPTED, ONE STICKER ISSUED, ONE ROOM KEPT WARM. ACCEPTABLE. SAME TIME TOMORROW.**

**KEEP THEM LAUGHING, PIRATES. I HAVE A WALL TO HOLD.**

The tray clunked once more and dispensed, unasked, a single object: a small brass token stamped with a whistle-arrow on one face and a dragon on the other. Nobody saw Yondu take it. But it was gone by lights-out, and high in the dark the crack ticked, softly, only twice — as if even the strain on the walls had, for one evening, eased its grip to listen to the song.
