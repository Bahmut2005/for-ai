# Chapter Forty-Five: The Treasure Planet Incident

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Guardians of the Galaxy (Star-Lord, Gamora, Rocket, Groot, Drax, Nebula, Mantis) • Ravagers (Yondu Udonta, Kraglin, Stakar Ogord, Martinex) • The Collectors' Row (Taneleer Tivan, the Grandmaster) • Cosmic Archaeologists (the Rigellian Recorder, Shi'ar Academy scholars, Uatu observing)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Planet-Size Vault Raid" — **Legendary**
2. "Sealed Entity Within" — **Mythic**

---

Five nights into Arc Five, the theatre had adjusted to its new management style, which was to say: the gacha wheel now had a lever, the lever had a skull-and-flame decal that nobody had authorized, and the machine — which had survived Doom's demands and the Hulk's fists without blinking — had apparently decided that being operated by pirates was funny.

High in the upper right quadrant, the crack ticked once, softly, the way it had ticked every night since the red sky. The room had learned to live around it the way one lives around a relative's cough. Nobody had learned to ignore it.

"House rules," announced Kraglin, who had been promoted to wheel-boy by a process no one could reconstruct, spitting on his palm and gripping the lever. "Fifth pull of the Ravager Rolls. After the rat's heist went nowhere and the Cap'n's — " he glanced at Yondu, chose life, " — *conversation*, machine says tonight's theme is *acquisitions*."

"Assets," corrected the Collector, from his roped-off luxury row, in the voice of a man who had been waiting five nights for exactly this word. Taneleer Tivan had dressed for the occasion. Taneleer Tivan had dressed for the occasion *every night*, on the theory that eventually the occasion would arrive.

Kraglin pulled the lever.

**GACHA PULL: 1 OF 2.**

**"PLANET-SIZE VAULT RAID" — RARITY: LEGENDARY.**

"Oh," said Rocket Raccoon, sitting up so fast his tools scattered. "Oh, it's a *heist* episode."

---

The vision opened on a dead system in the far dark of Issei's cosmology — past the Underworld's mapped territories, past anything with a postal address — and on a world that was not a world.

It had been one, once. Now it was a **vault**. The captions delivered the specifications with the machine's usual brutal economy: a planetary crust reworked, over centuries, into a single continuous strongroom. Continental plates hinged like cabinet doors. Mountain ranges that were, on closer inspection, *wards* — Norse chains the width of rivers, sutra-script scorched into deserts, angelic geometry orbiting in rings, devil contract-circles inlaid in gold across the entire southern hemisphere. Every pantheon Issei's world contained had contributed a lock, which meant every pantheon had agreed on something, which — as the room had learned across forty-four chapters — never, ever happened.

"Joint custody," murmured Gamora, eyes narrowing. "Gods don't share. Not treasure. Something made them share."

The appraisal figures began scrolling anyway, because the machine knew its audience. Estimated contents in tonnage of gold. In relics. In *named* relics. The numbers went up and kept going up until they stopped being numbers and became weather.

The Collector made a sound the theatre had never heard from him. Beside him, the Grandmaster — who had drifted in three nights ago "just for the vibes" and never left — began, delightedly, taking bets.

And then the raid started, and the room understood why this pull was rated for combat.

A consortium came out of the dark — rogue devils, stray gods, a mercenary column of treasure-hunters with siege-craft and world-cracking munitions, the kind of professional acquisitions fleet that Ravagers watch the way art students watch masters. And rising off the vault-world's surface to meet them, crimson against the starfield, wyverns wheeling around him like a solar system that had unionized: Issei Hyoudou, Cardinal Crimson, standing in front of the largest pile of unclaimed wealth in his universe like a man guarding a bus stop.

"Okay, hold on, hold up." Peter Quill was on his feet. "He's the *security*? They put the horny dragon kid in charge of Fort Knox: The Planet? Who does the vetting in that universe?"

"**The pay is terrible,**" Ddraig's voice offered dryly over the vision, in the tone of a dragon settling in to enjoy a misunderstanding. "**He does it anyway. Watch.**"

They watched. It was a beautiful fight and a strange one, and the strangeness took the professionals in the room about ninety seconds to diagnose. Issei did not defend the vault the way one defends *property* — no perimeter, no interdiction of the loot approaches, none of the geometry of a man protecting a claim. Rocket saw it first, because Rocket had planned a hundred robberies against this exact kid a few nights ago and lost every one.

"He's not guarding the doors," Rocket said slowly, telescoping his scope down. "Look at his positioning. He's ignoring three open cargo lanes — you could back a freighter into the eastern hemisphere right now. He's only contesting hits on the *seals*. He's not stopping them from taking anything out." A pause, whiskers flat. "He's stopping them from letting something— "

The captions cut him off, because the machine liked its own timing:

**THE RAIDERS WANT WHAT IS INSIDE. SO DID EVERYONE WHO BUILT THE LOCKS. THAT IS WHY THERE ARE LOCKS.**

On-screen, a siege-lance the size of a city block came down on the largest contract-circle, and Issei — who had spent the fight conserving, deflecting, herding — stopped conserving. The Crimson Blaster took the lance out of the sky in three seconds of shrieking accumulation, and the shockwave of it rang the vault-world's wards like a bell the size of a moon, and the vision, for a single frame, showed the entire mercenary fleet hesitate at the *sound the planet made in response*.

Not a bell-tone. A heartbeat.

The screen froze there — crimson figure, ringing world — and printed, with what the theatre could only interpret as showmanship:

**INTERMISSION. THE MANAGEMENT INVITES INTERESTED PARTIES TO CONFER.**

---

The intermission lasted one hour, and it was the most profitable hour of larceny-planning in the theatre's history, conducted entirely in the open, because there was nowhere to go.

The Ravagers colonized the frozen frame like it was a tavern table. Stakar Ogord ran the assessment with the gravity of an admiral; Martinex modeled the ward-lattice in refracted light off his own facets; Kraglin annotated approach vectors directly onto the screen with a grease pencil until it shocked him, twice, on principle. The Collector's people arrived with contracts. The Grandmaster arrived with a whiteboard of odds. Rocket — Rocket, who had sworn off Issei-related heists after the simulation disaster, publicly, with witnesses — was somehow at the center of it, sketching, muttering, *alive*, because a planet-sized lock is not a temptation, it is a love letter.

"Sixty percent to the fleet, fifteen to Tivan for financing—"

"Twenty."

"Fifteen, you undead magpie, and the anomalous items go to open auction—"

"The anomalous items," said the Collector, in a voice gone silk-thin, "come to *me*."

Only the Guardians' row stayed out of it. Gamora had not moved since the heartbeat. Nebula stood behind her sister's seat, arms folded, watching the planning circus with the expression of a woman who had grown up in a house where things were collected. And Drax, arms crossed, staring at the frozen crimson figure, said the quiet part at full volume, as was his gift:

"Why does the boy fight like the treasure is behind *him*?"

Yondu Udonta heard it. The room saw him hear it. He had been on the planning circle's edge all hour, arrow drifting lazy figure-eights over his shoulder, uncommitted — and at Drax's question the whistling stopped, and Yondu looked at the screen the way he'd looked at it during his own chapter, one night and a lifetime ago.

"'Cause it ain't treasure," Yondu said. "Boy's standing like a man in front of a door he don't want opened. Seen that stance before." A beat. "Wore it."

The machine, which had been waiting for exactly that, dimmed the lights.

**GACHA PULL: 2 OF 2.**

**"SEALED ENTITY WITHIN" — RARITY: MYTHIC.**

The Grandmaster's whiteboard, very quietly, erased itself.

---

The vision took them inside.

Down through the hinged continents, through vault-halls the size of seas — and yes, there was gold, gold in ranges, gold in geological strata, relics racked like wine, the appraisal-weather from earlier turning out to have been *conservative* — and the machine let the Collector's row drink it in for exactly eleven seconds before the perspective kept going. Past the hoard. Beneath it. To what the hoard was *for*.

The treasure was arranged in a spiral, all of it, every ingot and crown and god-forged blade, radiating inward around a chamber at the planet's heart. And in the chamber, wound in seal-script so old the captions declined to translate it, something slept.

The vision would not render it whole — the same courtesy, the room realized with a chill, that it had extended to the Dragon Blaster and the Longinus Smasher: some things it refused to show at full resolution. They got scale, and scale was enough. A curve of flank like a horizon. Breathing that moved the gold in tides. One eyelid, continental, veined with the same script as the wards, closed.

**MANIFEST,** printed the screen. **CONTENTS OF VAULT: ONE (1) OCCUPANT.**

**THE GOLD IS NOT THE TREASURE. THE GOLD IS THE BEDDING. OLD POWERS SLEEP QUIETER ON A HOARD. THE PANTHEONS DID NOT BUILD A BANK. THEY BUILT A CRADLE, AND AGREED NEVER TO SAY SO, BECAUSE CRADLES INVITE PITY AND VAULTS INVITE ONLY THIEVES.**

**THEY BUDGETED FOR THIEVES.**

The silence in the theatre had texture. Into it, the vision played its last movement: the raid broken, the fleet gone, and Issei Hyoudou — armor dismissed, a teenager again, small as a coin on the vault-world's skin — kneeling at the great southern contract-circle with his gauntleted hand flat against the seal.

**[BOOST.]** said the gear, gently. **[BOOST.] [BOOST.] [TRANSFER.]**

And the theatre watched the accumulated power of the entire battle — every doubling, every stolen watt the wyverns had levied off the raiders — pour not into the boy but *out* of him, down through his palm, into the hinges of the lock. Feeding the seal. Paying into the prison's upkeep like a man paying his grandmother's heating bill.

He talked while he did it. Of course he talked. The room had four arcs of evidence that Issei Hyoudou had never met a silence he respected. He told the sleeping thing about the fight it had missed. He apologized for the noise. He said — and the caption transcribed it without comment, which was somehow worse — *"If you ever wake up and you wanna talk instead of, y'know, ending everything, first meal's on me. I know a place. They do a really good melon bread."*

"**He does this every visit,**" said Ddraig, and the dragon's voice had no dryness left in it. "**He has never been paid. He was never assigned. He heard what was down here, and he decided the door should have a friend on both sides.**"

---

The debate, when it came, did not sound like the theatre's usual debates. It sounded like a crew meeting.

"Okay." Quill stood, hands out, addressing the planning circle he had refused to join. "Everybody who was drawing heist diagrams an hour ago, look at me. We *did* this one. The orb. You crack open the shiny thing and surprise, it's not loot, it's a *scream* with a market value. We lost — " his voice caught the way it always caught, and Mantis's antennae dipped, " — we lost people to a guy who thought the universe was his to unwrap. You don't open a box just because the not-knowing itches."

"Spoken," said the Collector, "like a man who has never owned anything worth guarding." But it came out wrong — thin — because Taneleer Tivan had spent the last ten minutes discovering something about himself in public: the manifest had not cured his wanting. It had *refined* it. A god in a cage was no longer merchandise; it was the exhibit of exhibits, the piece his collection would be remembered for, and the entire theatre could see him fail to stop doing the math. The Grandmaster — the Grandmaster, whose moral floor was located in the sub-basement of a casino — edged one seat away from his brother, delicately, like a man distancing himself from a bad smell at a party.

"I will take that bet now," En Dwi Gast announced to the room, cheerful and shrill. "What's *in* the vault, best odds in the house, come on, someone — no one? The breathing planet has killed the betting? It has. Ugh. Sealed entities are *terrible* for business."

"It is not a treasure and it is not an exhibit," said Drax. "It is a prisoner who has not escaped. Where I am from, we do not dig up a buried blade to ask if it is still sharp."

"Where you're from got *invaded*," Nebula said flatly. Then, quieter, arms still folded, eyes on the seal-script: "My father collected daughters and called it rescue. Every collector calls it something. Preservation. Curation. *Curiosity.*" Her gaze flicked, once, lethal, to Tivan's row. "The only honest word is the one the boy uses. It's a door. Doors have a right side."

Gamora finally spoke. "The pantheons built it together and told no one. Think about what that means. Every god in that world looked at what's sleeping down there and chose *shame* over *glory* — chose to be forgotten as jailers rather than famous as conquerors. Gods don't do that." A breath. "Unless the alternative frightened them into being, briefly, wise."

It was Rocket, in the end, who closed the heist. He did it without ceremony: climbed up on the planning table, in front of Stakar and Martinex and the annotated screen and sixty hours of the best acquisition planning of his life, and tore the schematic in half.

"Job's off," he said. To the objection forming on a Ravager captain's face: "Job's *off*. You know what I heard down there? I heard breathing in a locked room. I *been* the breathing in the locked room, okay? Subject 89P13 knows exactly what it sounds like from the inside when clever guys with tools decide the lock is the interesting part." His voice didn't crack, because he would have shot anyone who said it did. "The treasure's a *guy*. You don't fence a guy. Groot, back me up."

"I am Groot," said Groot, with enormous finality — and up on the screen, unprompted, un-cued, the vision's audio rendered a single low rumble from the sleeping dark beneath the gold, as if something in Issei's universe had briefly answered him — and Ddraig went silent, mid-breath, for the first time in forty-five chapters. The mystics' rows would argue about that until dawn. Chapter Forty-Six was already loading.

Stakar Ogord stood. He looked at the torn schematic, at the rat who had torn it, at the frozen frame of a boy paying a prison's heating bill with his own doubled heartbeat.

"New line in the code," Stakar said. "Ravagers don't deal in kids. And Ravagers don't open cages to sell what's inside." He glanced at Yondu — old grief, old amends, the arc's whole cargo passing between them in a look. "All in favor keep breathing."

The clans kept breathing.

From the scholars' tier, the Rigellian Recorder spoke its finding into the hush, toneless and terrible: "Recording paradox. The vault is the most valuable object yet assessed in the subject's universe, and its value is conditional on its never being collected. The perfect treasure is the one that must be left where it lies. Filed under: *lessons this audience resists.*"

And the screen, which had been quiet a long time, printed its verdict small, without a banner:

**YOU HAVE SPENT FIVE NIGHTS ASKING WHETHER HE IS A PIRATE, A CAPTAIN, OR A THREAT. TONIGHT HE WAS A LOCK. LEARN THIS SHAPE. HIS WORLD HAS OLDER DOORS THAN THIS ONE, AND WORSE SLEEPERS, AND THE NIGHT IS COMING WHEN YOU WILL NEED TO KNOW THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN A GUARD AND A JAILER.**

Ddraig said nothing. That was the loudest part.

The lights held a moment longer than usual, and in the pause, several thousand beings had the same thought at the same time and hated it together. It was Mantis, of all of them, who said it aloud — softly, antennae glowing, looking not at the screen but at the theatre's indestructible walls, and up at the hairline crack ticking patiently through its frost-flower branches:

"We are also inside something no one can open. With something outside, testing the locks." Her voice was very small. "Does anyone know which side of the door we are on?"

Tick, said the crack.

The lights went out.
