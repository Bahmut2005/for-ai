# Chapter Ten: The Red Dragon Emperor

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Cosmic Entities • Avengers • Magicians

---

The intermission ended the way a held breath ends.

For the whole of the dark between chapters, the Potential Threat Index had gone on ticking — soft, patient, arrhythmic, the sound of a meter measuring something that refused to stop growing even when no one was watching it. Ten thousand beings had sat in the dimness listening to it the way you listen to a faucet dripping in a house you have just been told is haunted. Conversations had started and died. Hercules had tried to collect early on his wager with Ares and been rebuffed. Somewhere in the villains' tier, someone had begun humming along with the meter and been made, by consensus, to stop.

Then the lights rose a single degree, and the wheel assembled itself, and above it, in letters that seemed larger than usual — or perhaps only heavier — the theatre printed:

**GACHA PULL: 1 OF 2.**

The wheel spun. It did not tick down through its colors with its usual coy roulette patience. It accelerated, blurred, and slammed to a stop on a color no one had yet seen it land on: a deep, arterial crimson shot through with gold, the exact shade of a certain gauntlet, and the theatre flooded with that color to its highest tier as the screen stamped:

**"BALANCE BREAKER: SCALE MAIL" — RARITY: LEGENDARY.**

"New tier," Kraglin said, into the hush, with the doomed helpfulness of a man who had appointed himself the room's rarity commentator nine chapters ago and could not now resign. "That's — yeah. That's above Epic. Legendary. We ain't had a Legendary."

"We've had Mythics," Sam Wilson said. "Mythic's higher, right? Dragon introduction, roar at Heaven — those were Mythic."

"Mythics were *about* the dragon," said Shuri, quietly, eyes already on the screen with the light of it pooling in them. "Look at the naming convention. This one is about the *boy*."

**[WELSH DRAGON BALANCE BREAKER,]** said the screen, in the vast gravel voice that was not the system's voice at all, and the theatre took a breath, and swallowed them.

---

A valley in the mountains of northern Japan, after midnight, under a sky that was wrong.

The wrongness was professional: a barrier, the audience understood at once, the knowledge arriving the way knowledge did here. A pocket of sealed space folded out of the human world, ten kilometers of evacuated dark — pine slopes, a dead ski town, a river like a strip of foil — requisitioned as a battlefield so that nothing that happened here would happen to anyone. Devils did this, the vision supplied. Standard practice. War with the noise removed.

The war in question stood on the air above the ruined town, and it had eight wings.

He had been beautiful once, in the way of things made before continents. Now the fallen angel who called himself **Absalom of the Scattered Host** was beautiful the way a burnt cathedral is: tall, gaunt, robed in the grey of old ash, his eight wings the color of paper that has been through a fire and kept, unaccountably, its shape. Beneath him, arrayed through the streets of the empty town, moved his Host — stray devils gone feral, rogue exorcists with stolen light-tech, a congregation of everything three factions had discarded, gathered up by a commander who had deserted Heaven's fallen when even *they* signed a peace. In his hand, held loosely, point down, was a lance of light so old and so saturated with use that it had stopped being white. The vision named it the way a museum names an exhibit: *Last Psalm. Forged in the Great War. Casualties: no longer counted.*

"Mythic-tier," murmured Doctor Strange in the theatre dark, reading resonances off the vision like a man reading fine print. "Not the foot soldiers — *him*. That's not a Raynare. That's not even a squad of Raynares. That's a general officer of a war older than their Heaven's silence."

"He is a relic that refuses to be one," Doom said. "I have employed such men. They are only ever cured of themselves by force."

At the southern edge of the town, the force arrived.

The audience knew the peerage by now — nine chapters had made the silhouettes familiar. Crimson-haired Rias with annihilation gathering at her fingertips like rot made obedient; Akeno already laughing thunder; the little Rook, Koneko, cracking her knuckles with a sound like a quarry starting work; Kiba a polite blur; Asia safe at the rear behind three layered wards, hands clasped, lips moving. They fanned out to hold the perimeter, to keep the Host off the barrier's walls.

They were not there to fight Absalom.

They were there to give the boy room.

Issei Hyoudou walked up the center of the dead main street alone, and the theatre noticed everything at once: that he was taller than the boy from the fountain, though not by much; that he walked with his weight settled the way Shang-Chi had spent three chapters complaining he didn't; that he was afraid, visibly, honestly afraid, his heart audible to a blind man in the cheap seats — and that the fear was not slowing him down by so much as a step.

"Absalom of the Scattered Host!" he called up, and his voice cracked in the middle of the name, and he plowed on anyway. "You were offered terms! Everyone down here was offered terms! Last chance — stand your people down, and nobody has to—"

Light answered him. Not a spear — a *sky* of spears, a hundred lances condensing above the street in one breath, the signature move of the thing that had murdered him rewritten as a weather system. The audience felt the whole room flinch on the boy's behalf, ten thousand memories of a fountain and a hole below a sternum.

Issei did not flinch. He raised his left arm, and the green gem caught the spear-light and turned it the color of deep summer, and he said — quietly, like a man saying it to himself, like a key going into a lock he had finally, finally earned —

"Balance Break."

**[WELSH DRAGON BALANCE BREAKER.]**

What happened next took less than a second, and the vision, in its mercilessness, spent a long time on it. Crimson erupted from the gauntlet and *unfolded* — not conjured plating, not a suit arriving, but something more like a truth being told at last about what had been walking around in a school uniform. Scale locked over scale, up the arm, across the chest, down the spine; golden spikes rose at the joints; a helm closed over the terrified brown eyes and replaced them with slits of burning green; great jointed pinions of red membrane and blade snapped out from the shoulder blades and caught the night air with a crack like a mainsail; clawed gauntlets flexed, and a long segmented tail lashed once, insolently, behind. On the breastplate, a second green gem kindled. In the corner of the vision, small and clinical, a counter appeared:

**[ SCALE MAIL — SUSTAINED OPERATION. ]**

The rain of spears came down, and the Red Dragon Emperor went *up*, through it, into it, wings shedding turn after impossible turn, and the fight began.

It was not the fight the theatre expected. The theatre had been braced — after mountains erased and oaths roared at Heaven — for a bomb going off. What it got was worse and better: it got *craft*. Issei fought the spear-storm the way Steve Rogers fights a room, the way Carol Danvers flies a curtain of flak — economically, structurally, ten seconds at a time.

**[BOOST.]**

He took a lance across the pauldron and let the armor eat it, rolled off the impact, and put a claw through the casting-matrix of the exorcist battery that had fired it, and pulled the claw out gently, and the rogue exorcist inside the wreckage was alive, and furious about it.

**[BOOST.]**

He hit the stray-devil vanguard at the river like a red comet, and the audience waited for the crater, and instead he decelerated at the last instant — *wings as brakes, look at that,* Sam Wilson breathed, *he's flying it like he was born in it* — and went through them low and precise, tail sweeping legs, claws shearing weapons and not wrists, each **[BOOST.]** metered out and spent like a man on a budget.

**[BOOST.] [BOOST.] [BOOST.]**

"He's *banking*," Tony Stark said, on his feet in the theatre, hands sketching furiously in empty air. "Look at the intervals — he's not detonating the doubling the second he gets it, he's carrying compound load *inside the armor*, sustained, that's — okay, quick show of hands, who remembers chapter three? Who remembers when this form lasted *seconds* and nearly tore him apart? That counter's been running four minutes."

"Six," said Vision, mildly.

And then Absalom stopped watching, and came down, and the sky hit the boy.

*Last Psalm* against dragon scale sounded like the Great War being quoted. The first exchange threw Issei through the ski town's church — the vision lingered, without comment, on eight burnt wings framed by a broken steeple — and the second exchange he *took on the claws*, caught, held, the ancient lance grinding against gauntlets while the fallen commander looked into the burning green slits at close range and said, in a voice like a hymn left in the rain:

"They sent a *child* in a *toy*. I broke a hundred of your Emperor's hosts before your species learned bronze, little dragon. Take off the armor and I will make it quick, the way I did for—"

"**Partner,**" rumbled the vast voice out of both gems at once, bored, ancient, and unmistakably fond, "**he is monologuing at you. This is the oldest gift an enemy gives. Say thank you.**"

"Thanks," said Issei Hyoudou, and boosted point-blank.

**[EXPLOSION.]**

The shockwave unzipped the street. Absalom went up and back, wings hammering for control, and got it, because Mythic-tier gets it — and rewrote the rules, dissolving *Last Psalm* into a lattice, a cage of light closing on the valley itself, spears by the thousand orienting on the peerage, on the perimeter, on the wards where Asia stood. The oldest move in the commander's book: the boy is the shield, so aim at what he shields.

The theatre heard Issei's breathing change inside the helm. It heard him say, small and cold, "Okay. You aimed at her. Ddraig—"

"**Already counting, partner.**"

**[BOOST.] [BOOST.] [BOOST.] [BOOST.] [BOOST.] [BOOST.] [BOOST.] [BOOST.] [BOOST.] [BOOST.]**

The ten seconds between each doubling had never felt so much like a countdown in a silo. The Red Dragon Emperor rose above the caged valley, wings wide, both palms coming together, and crimson gathered between his claws — the mountain-eraser, the Dragon Shot the room had seen level a horizon in chapter three, now fed by a compounding that made Bruce Banner start doing arithmetic aloud and then, at ten to the fourth baseline and climbing, deliberately stop.

Absalom laughed at it. "You cannot fire that here, boy. Everything you love is under this sky."

"Yeah," Issei said. "That's why you're gonna be over it."

The claw closed on ash-grey robes faster than eight wings could beat — *Penetrate*, the audience felt, the boy's grip slipping through a defense older than his species like the defense had been a rumor — and the wings hammered, and the Red Dragon Emperor hauled a screaming Mythic-tier general straight up, out of the lattice, through his own cage of spears, past the barrier's dark ceiling into the sealed sky's empty upper vault where there was nothing left to shield and nothing left to lose, and fired.

The vision was kind to the audience in exactly one way: it showed them the light from below, the way the peerage saw it. A second sunrise, vertical. A column of crimson that erased the cloud deck of a pocket universe. When it faded, the lattice of spears was gone the way a spiderweb goes when the spider does, and something with the remains of eight wings was drifting down the long dark like a burnt page, alive — deliberately, surgically alive — to be caught, at the bottom, by a boy in red armor who set the ancient horror down on the riverbank almost gently, and crouched beside him, helm retracting from a scraped, exhausted, absurdly young face, and said:

"So — terms. Same ones as before. They don't get worse just 'cause you lost."

The counter in the corner read **[ SCALE MAIL — SUSTAINED OPERATION: 21:07 ]** and was still, patiently, counting.

The vision released them.

---

For three full seconds, the theatre made no sound at all.

Then it made all of them.

"Run the intercept back," Carol Danvers was saying, over the roar, to no one and everyone, with the specific indignation of a pilot watching someone fly her airspace better than expected. "The vertical extraction. He identified the hostage geometry, inverted it, and turned a city-buster into a *safe* shot by changing the battlefield's *axis*, mid-fight, at Mach — someone give me a number—"

"You're missing it," Sam said. "You're all missing it. Chapter one, that kid couldn't dodge a *thrown spear*. That's the same kid. That's *ten chapters* of the same kid."

"That," said Steve Rogers, "is the entire point of the exercise." He was on his feet, arms folded, and he was not smiling, but there was something in his face that his oldest friends recognized from bond drives and beach landings — the look he wore when the skinny kid theory of the universe received new supporting evidence. "Everything we've been shown, in order. The murder. The failures. The training. The losses to Sairaorg. The oath. And now the same boy metering his strength like a veteran and offering terms to the thing that aimed at his family. You don't get this from talent." He looked around the tiers. "You get this from *character* plus *time*. Somebody built him the slow way. It's the only way that holds."

"The armor," said Thor, and the room quieted for him, because his voice had the old-story weight in it again. "You are all admiring the boy, as is right. Admire also what he *wore*. That was no suit, Stark, no forged thing. Balance Breaker — did you feel the word's shape? It is a *covenant broken open*. The dragon does not lend him that armor." He shook his head slowly, with the reverence he reserved, in all the Nine Realms, for perhaps four beings. "The dragon *becomes* it. Ddraig wrapped his own body around a mortal child for twenty-one minutes and called it partnership. I have seen gods refuse far less to far worthier."

"It is more interesting than that," Doom said, and the theatre performed its now-traditional maneuver of pretending not to lean toward the truce corner where Doom and Strange had, without ever agreeing to, begun sitting a mere aisle apart. "The sorcerers in this room will have noted what the soldiers did not. Balance Breaker is described in their cosmology as a *flaw* — a bug, Stark, in your dialect. The system that made Sacred Gears did not intend this state. It is reached by desire exceeding design." The mask tilted. "Consider what we watched, then. A god builds a weapon with a governor bolted to it, and the governor's override condition is *wanting something more than the weapon was built to allow*. That boy overrode divine engineering with the strength of his *feelings*. Doom finds this architecture… instructive."

"Doom finds it *replicable*, he means," Strange said dryly, but his heart wasn't in the sparring, and everyone could hear it. He was quiet a moment. When he went on, it was slower, and it was addressed to the room. "Ten chapters ago I told you we were inside a briefing. Here's my updated read. We've now seen the full shape of a weapons system that starts at *nothing* — genuinely nothing, the boy was the least remarkable soul in his zip code — and compounds. No cap. Uncapped doubling, in armor that lets him hold the charge, wielded by someone whose response to absolute power is to *offer terms twice*." He looked up at the dark above the dark. "If you were preparing a multiverse to meet something, that's precisely the resume you'd make them read. Power without appetite. I've been doing this a long time. It's the rarest thing on any menu."

Wanda Maximoff, who had said little for nine chapters, said quietly, from the Midnight Suns' block: "The armor answered grief." Heads turned. She didn't elaborate at first; she was watching the frozen last frame, the boy crouched by his broken enemy. "Everyone's talking about the engineering. The override wasn't ambition. Watch it again. Every threshold he's crossed since chapter one — the gauntlet, the roar, this — it triggers when someone he loves is *aimed at*. That's the ignition system." Her voice stayed level, and was somehow worse for it. "Be very careful, all of you, about ever letting anything happen to that girl in the wards."

"Seconded," said Mephisto pleasantly, "notarized, and filed." The demon lord had produced, from nowhere, something that looked disturbingly like an actual ledger. "Meanwhile: chapter one, this room watched a princess spend eight Pawns on a corpse and I told you she'd seen priceless goods. Today the corpse held off and humanely defeated a Great War cadre commander at age seventeen, in a form his own God never authorized. I invite the room to appreciate my track record, and note that I am no longer the only buyer paying attention." He smiled his appalling smile up at the highest tiers. "Am I."

He was not. The cosmic tier had gone very still. The Silver Surfer hung above the mezzanine, and when he finally spoke it was with the flat vast gentleness that had unsettled the room since chapter one. "I was made a herald by a bargain," he said. "My power was given in a moment, whole. I have wondered, ten chapters now, why I cannot look away from this child. I understand it tonight." He looked at the frozen frame — the crouch, the offered terms. "He is being given power the way sediment is given to a river. Slowly enough to remain himself the entire way up. I was not. Nothing that rises fast is." A pause. "I would like to see what a kind being looks like at altitudes I know. I have never once been shown."

And above them all, Uatu the Watcher, who had spent an intermission unable to take his attention off the boy, said nothing at all — but those nearest him noticed that his great hands, folded for nine chapters, had come quietly apart, and that one of them now rested on the rail.

The screen chimed.

**GACHA PULL: 2 OF 2.**

The wheel spun and landed, almost apologetically, on plain silver-blue.

**"ILLEGAL MOVE TRIAINA TEASER" — RARITY: RARE.**

"Rare," Rocket said flatly. "*Rare.* We just did a whole new tier of red for the tin suit and the thing called 'ILLEGAL MOVE' is *Rare*?"

"The *teaser* is Rare," said Loki softly, from wherever Loki was sitting this chapter, which was never twice the same seat. His eyes had narrowed to green slits with arithmetic behind them. "Mark the noun, rodent. The theatre prices honestly — that has been its one courtesy. A glimpse is cheap." He steepled his fingers. "It is the *thing glimpsed* that will cost us."

The theatre did not swallow them whole this time. That was the first strangeness. Instead the screen itself changed — pulled back, letterboxed, dimmed at the edges like a memory of a memory — and across the top, in the small clinical font it used when it was talking to itself, it printed:

**[ PREVIEW. AUTHORIZATION: PARTIAL. THE FOLLOWING HAS NOT YET OCCURRED. ]**

"'Not yet,'" murmured Natasha Romanoff. "It shows futures now. Noted."

What followed lasted perhaps forty seconds, and the room would spend far longer than that arguing about it.

A flash: the Scale Mail, familiar now, crimson and complete — and then *changing*, plates flying off it, discarding mass, the armor slimming to something knife-blue at the joints and jet-swift, and the boy inside it moving in a way that made Quicksilver's brother-in-arms across the mutant block stand up involuntarily, because the frame did not blur so much as *concede* — a dozen afterimages of red-blue shredding a battlefield's worth of enemies between two beats of the audience's collective heart, and under it a system voice they knew, saying a word they didn't:

**[CHANGE: WELSH SONIC BOOST KNIGHT.]**

A flash: the opposite. The armor *gaining*, forearms swelling into something industrial, gauntlets become foundry blocks, and the boy planting his feet as something vast came down on him — and the vast thing losing, caught and crushed against knuckles that struck with a sound the theatre's own indestructible floor seemed to take personally:

**[CHANGE: WELSH DRAGONIC ROOK.]**

And a third flash, longest of the three. The armor opened. Back-mounted housings unfolded into a battery of cannons around a core of gathering light — and the light was wrong, the sorcerers were all on their feet before it fired, Strange and Doom and Wanda and Loki upright in the same instant like a string section — and when it fired, the beam did not travel through the intervening space so much as *renegotiate* it. The vision showed, with clinical relish, the fabric of the preview's unnamed battlefield bending around the shot: distance folding, the horizon flinching, a mountain that had been comfortably remote suddenly participating.

**[CHANGE: WELSH BLASTER BISHOP.]**
**[DRAGON BLASTER: STANDBY.]**

Then, stamped across all three frozen silhouettes at once — the Knight, the Rook, the Bishop, three futures of one boy —

**[ ILLEGAL MOVE: TRIAINA. ]**
**[ A PAWN MAY PROMOTE. THIS PAWN DOES NOT WAIT FOR THE BACK RANK. ]**

The screen went politely grey.

"No," said Hank Pym's voice from somewhere, "hold on, go back—"

"It warped space," T'Challa said, quietly, to Shuri, and Shuri, for the first time in ten chapters, did not have a follow-up question ready.

"It's the chess," Tony said. His voice had gone odd — flat, quick, the tone he used when the math had stopped being fun. "Everyone see it? Their whole reincarnation system runs on chess. Pawn, Knight, Rook, Bishop, legal moves, promotion rules. It's a *governed* system — Strange said it in chapter one, weaponized divinity, standardized. Rules everywhere." He pointed at the grey screen. "And they just told us that this kid's next trick is called *Illegal Move*. He's not going to climb their ladder. He's going to *cheat at the game their entire afterlife is built on*, one piece at a time, and the piece with the *cannons* bends spacetime. As a *preview*." He sat down, slowly. "The Legendary pull was the tutorial boss. The Rare pull was the syllabus."

"An apt word," said Doom, and something in his voice made even Stark look over. "Doom breaks rules that deserve breaking; it is the founding act of every sovereign. But mark the theatre's phrasing. *This pawn does not wait for the back rank.* Promotion, in chess, is earned by crossing the entire board under fire." A pause. "The board is what we have been watching for ten chapters. It is telling us he crosses it."

Thor laughed — the true laugh, the one with weather in it. "Three shapes! Speed, and the mountain-fist, and the lance that folds the world! Father kept a hall of tapestries of warriors with one such gift apiece." He shook his head, delighted and, underneath the delight, not. "And the dragon lets him *choose between them*. Loki. Brother. Tell me honestly what you would have been at seventeen with a menu of apotheoses."

"Insufferable," said Loki, "briefly," and did not take his eyes off the grey screen.

It was into that noise — the arguments cross-hatching the tiers, Danvers and Rhodes already gaming intercepts against a Sonic Boost Knight, Wong and Strange sketching containment geometry for a beam that renegotiated distance, Mephisto annotating his ledger with a pen that squirmed — it was into all of that, at the exact moment the room's voice reached its crest, that the screen came back on.

Everything else on it went away.

The rarity banners. The gacha wheel, mid-reassembly. The scrolling gold history of ten chapters of pulls. The status displays. Even the Potential Threat Index — and the room had time to register that its ticking had stopped, and to find the silence worse — all of it wiped, without ceremony, to black. Ten thousand beings quieted, tier by tier, the way a stadium quiets when the injury on the field stops looking routine.

And in the center of that perfect black, alone, in plain white letters with nothing to soften them, the theatre printed:

**TUTORIAL COMPLETE.**

No chime. No fanfare. The words simply sat there, patient as a headstone, while their meaning went through the theatre like cold water finding the gaps in a wall.

Peter Parker said it first, because someone had to, and it was always going to be him. His voice came out small in the enormous dark.

"Tutorial. That was the — guys. The murder. The dragon. The mountain. The oath at Heaven. The war general. All of that was the *tutorial*."

"The part of the game," said Miles, hollowly, "before it starts keeping score."

"The part they let you *lose*," said Gwen.

Fury's voice came out of the dark, low and even, the sound of a man updating a threat file he now understood to have been mislabeled at the cabinet level. "Ten chapters. Ten chapters of exponential growth, divine oaths, and a Great War commander going down humanely — and the system that built this room rates all of it as *onboarding*." A beat. "Recalibrate, people. Everything we've argued about, every list we've moved him on and off — we've been reviewing the cover of the book."

"Then attend," said the Silver Surfer softly, from above them all, "to how thick the book must be."

Hela was heard, from the villains' tier, to begin — very quietly — laughing. It was not her connoisseur's laugh. Ares, who had wagered on the boy dying, was not laughing at all; nor was anyone accepting his attempts to renegotiate the stakes with Hercules, whose grin had gone slightly fixed. Fin Fang Foom's scorn had assumed the specific density of scorn that is protecting something underneath. Doom stood motionless in his aisle, and the aisle had never seemed more built for him, and behind the mask Doom re-derived, alone, the implication he had already stated aloud: *the board is what we have been watching. It is telling us he crosses it.*

And up in the highest tier, Uatu the Watcher looked at the two words in the dark and did something he had not done in longer than most universes had existed.

He settled in.

The white words held one moment more. Then, beneath them, smaller, in the font the theatre used when it was talking to itself — the font it had used, ten chapters ago, for a line about the things they could not stand before — it added, gently, like a door opening onto a very long hallway:

**ARC ONE OF TEN.**

**NEXT: THE MAKING OF A DRAGON.**

The wheel began, softly, to tick.
