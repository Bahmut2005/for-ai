# Chapter Thirteen: The Welsh Dragon

**Primary Reaction Groups:** British heroes (Captain Britain, MI-13 — Pete Wisdom, Union Jack, Faiza Hussain), Asgardians, Atlanteans (Namor)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. Confirmation of Welsh Red Dragon Heritage — Mythic
2. Nationalistic Misunderstandings — Comedy-Class

---

The theatre had learned, over twelve chapters of an unasked-for existence, to read its own weather.

When the great screen dimmed to storm-grey, empires braced. When it went gold, Thor started pouring. And when the lights came up in a soft, buttery warmth — the exact colour of a pub at four in the afternoon — everyone understood, on some animal level, that tonight nobody was going to die, and that somebody was going to be humiliated instead.

The gacha wheel descended from the impossible ceiling in its usual state of smug ceremony, rings within rings, sigils rotating like a slot machine designed by a god with a gambling problem. The last chapter had wrung them all out — Issei bleeding through another lost duel, winning nothing but the grudging nod of Sairaorg Bael and a slow, private smile from Victor von Doom, which several observers agreed was somehow worse than the bleeding.

Tonight the wheel spun almost lazily. It clattered, slowed, and dropped its first token into the crystal bowl with a chime like a struck wineglass.

**PULL ONE: CONFIRMATION OF WELSH RED DRAGON HERITAGE — MYTHIC.**

"Heritage," said Pete Wisdom, from the smoking section that the theatre refused to let him smoke in. He'd lit four cigarettes so far this evening. The theatre had extinguished all four with tiny, judgmental gusts of nowhere-wind. "Dragons have heritage now. Wonderful. Next it'll want a passport."

He would remember saying that.

---

The screen opened on night. Kuoh Town, after training — the canon world's version of after training, which meant Issei Hyoudou face-down in the Gremory garden making the noise of a deflating air mattress while his muscles filed formal complaints.

"Ddraig," he mumbled into the grass. "Tell me something."

"That is a broad request, partner."

"Something about you. You know everything about me. You literally live in my arm. You've seen my browser history from the *inside.* But I don't know anything about you except you're old and you sigh a lot."

"I sigh a proportionate amount," said the deep voice from the gauntlet, and the theatre's Ddraig-appreciators — Thor foremost among them — chuckled in fond recognition. "Very well. Sit up. A dragon's history is not told to a boy lying in a flowerbed."

On-screen Issei sat up. The Boosted Gear shimmered into being along his forearm, green gem glowing in the dark like a patient emerald eye, and Ddraig began to speak — and the gacha, sensing Mythic material, did the thing it did for its highest rarities: it *illustrated.* The screen bloomed outward into memory.

Two dragons over a young world. One red as an open wound in the sky, one white as the inside of lightning. They fought across continents that had not yet finished deciding their shapes; their war did not respect the war between God and the devil kings, and so God and the devil kings, in a rare moment of managerial consensus, stopped fighting each other long enough to destroy them both. Flesh unmade. Souls sealed. Two gauntlets, two Sacred Gears, passed hand to mortal hand down the centuries like heirlooms nobody could refuse.

"The Two Heavenly Dragons," Ddraig said. "Myself — and Albion. The White Dragon Emperor. My rival, my opposite, my oldest argument. Where I boost, he divides. Where I am, eventually, he will be. Every generation, our wielders find each other. It is not optional."

The theatre took collective note of that — *another gauntlet, another wielder, somewhere in that world* — and filed it under Ominous, For Later.

"But you asked of *me*," Ddraig continued, and the memory-light shifted. A green island in a cold sea. Hill forts. Rain with personality. "After my sealing, my legend outlived my body, as legends do. There was an isle in the world's west where men had seen me in the old days — truly seen me, red across their sky — and they did not forget. They painted me on their war-banners. They told of me under a hill called Dinas Emrys, where a boy prophet showed a king two dragons warring beneath his tower's foundations — the red and the white — and named the red dragon victorious. They put me, in the end, upon their flag. They fly me still."

On-screen, Issei was already fumbling for his phone. The theatre watched, riveted, as a lecherous Japanese schoolboy typed *red dragon flag* into a search engine, and the screen helpfully enlarged the result:

A green and white field. A red dragon passant, claw raised, tongue out, looking frankly magnificent.

The gacha stamped the image with a rotating Mythic seal, and letters of fire wrote themselves across the bottom of the screen:

**CONFIRMED: Y DDRAIG GOCH. THE RED DRAGON OF WALES. HERALDIC. ANCESTRAL. NATIONALLY SIGNIFICANT.**

"Ddraig," Issei whispered, with the reverence he usually reserved for other subjects entirely. "You're a *flag.* You're on a *flag.* That's so much cooler than being a country."

"It is considerably cooler than being a country," Ddraig agreed.

The screen paused itself, as it did between pulls, and the theatre lights came up on the audience.

Which is where the trouble started.

---

Captain Britain was standing.

Brian Braddock was a large man made larger by conviction, and at this moment he wore the expression of someone who has just been informed that the Crown Jewels are being kept in a teenager's sock drawer in Osaka. His cape did something stirring despite the total absence of wind.

"That," he announced, in the voice he used for addressing Parliament and interdimensional invasions, "is a British national treasure."

"Here we go," said Wisdom.

"That is *the* red dragon. Y Ddraig Goch. The oldest heraldic symbol in continuous use in the United Kingdom. That dragon is on our *flag*—"

"*A* flag," said Union Jack, who was wearing the other one. "Not to be pedantic, Brian, but he's not on mine. Have you ever actually looked at the Union Jack? Three crosses, no dragon. Wales didn't even make the flag. This is literally the founding grievance."

"The point *stands.* An entity of foundational significance to the British Isles is currently bound to the arm of a fifteen-year-old boy in *Japan*, and I would like to know what anyone intends to do about it."

"Do about it," repeated Faiza Hussain, delighted. She had Excalibur across her knees, and — several rows had noticed — the sword had begun humming very faintly the moment Ddraig started speaking, like an old dog hearing a familiar car in the drive. "What's the proposal, exactly? Extradition? He's a metaphysical dragon soul sealed in a divine artifact. He doesn't have *residency.*"

"He has *heritage,*" said Braddock. "You heard the gacha. Nationally significant."

Pete Wisdom had produced, from somewhere inside his terrible black suit, an actual manila folder. He licked his thumb and opened it with the grim relish of a career civil servant scenting jurisdiction.

"MI-13 charter," he said. "Paragraph one. *All superhuman, mystical, and otherwise weird matters arising within, or pertaining to, the British Isles fall under MI-13's operational purview.* Pertaining to. *Pertaining to,* Brian. That dragon pertains SO hard. He's on the flag. He's in the Mabinogion-adjacent material. He fought under Dinas Emrys, which is in Gwynedd, which — I've checked — remains in Wales. I'm designating him a national asset. Provisional codename: DRAGON, RED, ONE OF."

"You cannot *designate* a Heavenly Dragon," said Faiza.

"Watch me. I've got a form."

"He predates forms."

"Nothing predates forms. Forms are eternal. Civilization is a thin crust over an infinite ocean of forms." Wisdom clicked a pen. "I'm also opening a repatriation file. If a museum can spend eighty years arguing about marbles, MI-13 can put in a claim on a dragon."

"Repatriation to *where?*" Union Jack demanded. "He's not IN anything. He's a soul in a gauntlet. What are you going to do, ask the boy to post his arm to Cardiff?"

"We nationalized coal," said Wisdom. "We nationalized steel. We nationalized the railways. Twice, arguably. The machinery exists."

"The railways," said Union Jack, "are not a compelling precedent, Pete. I've *ridden* the railways."

And it was at this point — with Captain Britain drawing breath to invoke, God help everyone, *Merlin* — that the gacha wheel, which had been sitting quietly overhead this entire time, began to spin again *entirely of its own accord.*

The theatre went quiet. The wheel had never done that before. It clattered with what could only be described as glee, and dropped its second token.

**PULL TWO: NATIONALISTIC MISUNDERSTANDINGS — COMEDY-CLASS.**

"Oh no," said Faiza, grinning. "It heard you. It *heard* you, and it's got material."

---

The screen resumed — but not in the present. The gacha served up a montage, and the montage was, unmistakably, aimed at specific people in the audience.

*Item the first:* a memory older than the sealing, dressed in the gacha's illustrative fire. A chieftain of the old Britons standing on a hill, arms spread, formally offering the actual, living, mountain-sized Ddraig the position of "guardian beast of our people, to fight at our fore and take a fourth part of our cattle."

The actual, living, mountain-sized Ddraig looked at him for a long moment and then flew away without saying anything at all. The chieftain told everyone the dragon had accepted.

*Item the second:* Dinas Emrys. Two dragons warring beneath the earth — and modern-day Ddraig's voice-over, dry as slate: "That was not a prophecy, partner. That was *memory.* An echo of my war with Albion, soaked into the stones. The boy Myrddin was clever enough to hear it, and human enough to immediately turn it into politics. Red dragon defeats white dragon, therefore Britons defeat Saxons, therefore support this specific king. I have been a campaign advertisement for fifteen centuries."

*Item the third:* Bosworth Field, 1485. Henry Tudor marching under the red dragon banner. Ddraig's voice-over: "I was not consulted."

*Item the fourth:* 1959. A committee formally standardizing the Welsh flag. A room of men in grey suits debating the correct posture of Ddraig's tail. Ddraig's voice-over, now audibly pained: "I was *not consulted.* And the tail is wrong."

*Item the fifth* — and here the theatre realized the gacha was no longer showing the past, because the screen now showed the Gremory garden again, present-canon, and Issei asking the exact question everyone in the fourth row had been shouting for ten minutes:

"So wait — does that make you, like… British? Are you a British dragon? Should I be paying taxes on you?"

The theatre leaned in. Wisdom actually raised his pen.

And Ddraig said:

"Boy. Attend."

It was not louder than his usual voice. It was *older.* The gem on the gauntlet flared, and even filtered through a screen, across a dimensional gulf, into a cosmic theatre full of gods and monarchs and men in flags — something in that voice reached out and put its hand flat on the room.

"I watched that island rise from the sea. I flew above it when its mountains were young and rude, before it had a name, before *names* had names. I fought Albion through skies no map has ever held, and where we fell, the world was changed, and small things crept into the changed places and, in time, called themselves kingdoms. The nations of men are the footprints of my war. They grew in the marks we left." A pause; the ember-glow of the gem banked low and steady. "The men of that western land did not give me my image. They *took* it — as the small have always taken the names of the vast, hoping to be mistaken for them in the dark. I hold no grudge for it. It is the most human thing I know, and it kept my memory green when God Himself wished me forgotten. I am fond of them, as one is fond of a village built at the foot of one's mountain. But hear this once, and hear it whole: I am not Welsh. I am not British. I am not property, treasure, asset, or heritage. Dragons do not belong to nations. If anything under heaven is true, it is nearer the reverse."

Silence.

Real silence — the theatre's first in some time. Pete Wisdom's pen was still raised, and stayed raised, and then descended very slowly, and did not write anything. Captain Britain sat down without appearing to decide to. Faiza's sword had stopped humming and gone quite still, the way a cathedral is still. Even Namor, who had been assembling a devastating remark for the better part of a minute, held it.

Everyone in that room commanded, defended, or personally embodied a nation. And the thing on the screen had just, gently and without malice, reminded them that nations were *recent.*

On-screen, Issei broke the silence in his world the way only Issei could.

"…So can I tell people my arm is a flag?"

"You may tell people your arm is a flag."

"BEST. ARM. EVER."

The theatre exhaled into laughter, grateful for it, and the lights came up for open floor.

---

Thor was on his feet before the laughter finished, drinking horn raised so vigorously that two Asgardians in the splash zone activated defensive measures.

"WELL SPOKEN, ELDER WYRM!" he thundered. "Aye — thus it has ever been! A dragon is a sovereign of one, a nation of himself, a kingdom with wings! In Asgard we do not say a dragon *lives in* the mountain. We say the mountain has been *annexed.* You cannot own a dragon. You can only be, for a time, *tolerated* by one." He turned, beaming, on the British section. "You wish to claim him? Then do as honor demands — challenge him to single combat for the flag rights!"

"We are not," said Union Jack, "challenging the primordial dragon to single combat for flag rights."

"Then the flag rights are forfeit! This is basic law!"

Loki, folded elegantly into his seat like a knife into a napkin, examined his nails. "I confess myself moved by the noble Captain's patriotism," he said, in the tone of a man setting out caltrops. "Truly. There is something exquisite about watching *Englishmen* form a committee to claim the dragon of *Wales.* Tell me, Captain — when the red dragon was left off your Union Flag, was Wales consulted? I only ask because 'was Wales consulted' appears to be a theme."

"That's a fifteenth-century administrative technicality—" Braddock began.

"And here is my favorite part," Loki continued serenely, "which I notice you have all been too traumatized to say aloud. The red dragon's eternal rival — the *white* dragon, the enemy, the adversary he is prophesied to defeat — is named…" He let it hang. He was a professional. "*Albion.*"

A noise went through MI-13's rows like a structural failure.

"Oh no," said Faiza.

"The oldest name for Britain," Loki said, savoring each word like a boiled sweet. "Your island's own true name is worn by the *villain* of your flag. The prophecy your kings marched under is 'Britain defeats Albion.' You have been flying a divorce settlement for fifteen hundred years and calling it patriotism."

"I'm reclassifying the file," said Wisdom, into his hands.

"To what?"

"From ASSET to WEATHER. You don't own weather. You don't repatriate weather. You just — check the forecast and dress appropriately." He looked up, hollowed. "I'm designating him RED WEATHER. That's my final act on this matter."

"That's the sanest thing MI-13 has produced in a decade," said Union Jack.

Loki's gaze, all the audience nearest him noted, drifted back to the screen — to the gauntlet, glowing green on a boy's arm — and stayed there a moment too long, covetous as ever. If Ddraig could not be owned by a nation, the look said, that still left several categories unexplored.

---

Namor had waited. Namor always waited; it made the entrance better. He rose from the Atlantean section with the unhurried arrogance of a man who considered gravity a surface-dweller custom, ankle-wings flicking, and spread his arms toward the screen.

"At *last,*" he declared. "Thirteen chapters, and this theatre finally produces a figure of adequate rank. Red-scaled. Ancient beyond your squabbling little map-lines. Openly contemptuous of surface nations and their paperwork." He pressed a hand to his own chest. "He is practically Atlantean. I have never felt so seen."

"You are not," said Wisdom, "claiming our dragon."

"YOUR dragon—" Namor's laugh was a beautiful, terrible thing. "Little smoking man, you cannot even claim your own *lighter* in this room. No. Namor claims nothing. Namor *recognizes.* There is a difference that royalty understands and committees never will. We red-scaled monarchs" — and here he smirked at his own trunks, which were, indeed, scaled and red, and several people realized with horror that the joke had been loaded since chapter one — "are a rare fellowship. He rules the sky's memory; I rule the greater seven-tenths of the actual planet. I shall extend him honorary citizenship of Atlantis, monarch to monarch, as a courtesy."

"He *just said,*" said Faiza, "at length, movingly, that he doesn't do citizenships."

"He said he does not do *nations,*" Namor corrected, ascending two smug inches. "Atlantis is not a nation. Atlantis is an inevitability with a throne. Also — and I say this with love — imagine declining. Imagine choosing, instead, what he currently has: a landlocked adolescence inside the arm of a boy whose life's ambition the previous chapters have made *distressingly* clear. From that, even Wales looks good. From that, *Pete Wisdom's folder* looks good."

"Leave the folder out of this."

"Imperius Rex," said Namor, which was not a rebuttal but had never needed to be, and sat back down to scattered applause from himself.

From the high dark of the monster galleries, where he coiled alone because no row of seats had survived him, Fin Fang Foom spoke for the first time that evening.

"The red one is correct," he rumbled, and the theatre braced, because Fin Fang Foom agreeing with anyone was historically a prelude. "A dragon owned by a nation is no dragon. A dragon on a flag is no dragon. Foom would burn any banner bearing his image, and then the loom, and then the concept of textiles." A pause, and then, with bottomless contempt: "Of course, the red one lets a mammal *wear* him, so his opinions on dignity arrive somewhat pre-owned."

"He CHOSE the boy, wretched worm of Kakaranthara!" Thor bellowed up at the gallery. "That is sovereignty in action!"

"He was *sealed* into the boy's ancestors' species by a dead god's plumbing decisions. That is not sovereignty. That is *tenancy.*"

They were still going when the lights began their end-of-chapter dim.

The last image the screen offered before dark was small, and the gacha let it linger: Issei asleep in the garden where he'd dropped, and the Boosted Gear still faintly manifest on his arm — Ddraig keeping watch, an old red ember in the dark, older than every flag in the theatre and gentler than he would ever admit to any of them.

Somewhere in the audience, Captain Britain stood — quietly this time, without the cape doing anything — and saluted the screen. Just once. Not the flag. The dragon.

Wisdom pretended very hard not to have seen it, and wrote *RED WEATHER: FORECAST FAVOURABLE* on the folder, and closed it.

## Theatre Ledger

- **New Pulls This Chapter:** *Confirmation of Welsh Red Dragon Heritage* — Mythic; *Nationalistic Misunderstandings* — Comedy-Class. Notably, the second pull triggered itself in direct response to the audience's argument — the first confirmed instance of the gacha reacting to the theatre rather than the canon.
- **Secrets Revealed:** Ddraig is confirmed as Y Ddraig Goch, the red dragon of Welsh myth and the Welsh flag. His eternal rival is Albion, the White Dragon Emperor, sealed in a matching gauntlet with a wielder of his own — and the two wielders are fated to meet. The Dinas Emrys prophecy was no prophecy at all, but an echo of the Heavenly Dragons' ancient war soaked into the land.
- **Alliance / Opinion Shifts:** MI-13 formally downgrades Ddraig from prospective "national asset" to designation RED WEATHER ("forecast favourable"); Captain Britain's territorial bluster ends in a genuine salute. Faiza Hussain notes Excalibur resonates with Ddraig's voice. Thor's reverence deepens; Fin Fang Foom agrees with Ddraig on principle while insulting him on tenancy. Namor extends "honorary Atlantean citizenship, monarch to monarch" — unanswered, undeterred.
- **Unresolved Hooks:** Who wears Albion's gauntlet in the canon world — and when do the fated rivals collide? Why does Excalibur hum for Ddraig? Loki's covetous gaze has shifted from owning the dragon to "several categories unexplored." Doom, conspicuously silent all chapter, was observed taking notes under the heading *Latveria possesses no dragon; oversight.* And the gacha is listening to the audience now. It has never done that before.
