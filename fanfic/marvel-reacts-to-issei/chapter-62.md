## Part One: The Divine Chamber

The theatre had transformed. Gone was the familiar seating arrangement. Now the auditorium had become a cathedral of light—a vast chamber with marble floors and towering columns, the ceiling lost in divine radiance. The Avengers found themselves arranged alongside beings they'd spent lifetimes fighting or fearing: Asgardian gods in formal regalia, Olympian deities draped in ancient authority, and higher powers whose names existed only in whispered prayers.

Thor sat forward, massive and golden, his hammer across his knees. Beside him, Odin watched in silence, his single eye reflecting depths of understanding. Zeus occupied a throne of carved thunder, his posture rigid with the weight of endless supremacy. Strange stood with his hands folded, already sensing the magic woven through this place—not Marvel's cosmic power, but something older, more fundamentally opposed to mortal intervention.

In the pit, the gacha machine hummed.

Behind the glass of the vision-screen, Issei stood in what appeared to be a council chamber—somewhere between heavenly court and throneroom. He was smaller here, surrounded by divine authority, but his stance was alert, protective. Behind him, barely visible, were silhouettes: Rias, Akeno, Koneko, and others. His family.

Before them all stood a Seraph.

The angel was beautiful in the way that terrible things are beautiful—perfect geometry of light and gold, eyes that had witnessed creation itself, wings that seemed to contain entire universes. Cassiel, the divine records whispered. A voice of God. A carrier of divine will.

And this voice of God had just made a pronouncement that would reshape the entire theatre's understanding of Issei Hyoudou.

"The draconic bloodline taints the mortal world," Cassiel announced, his voice carrying the weight of absolute doctrine. "The children of Hyoudou, born of dragon essence and human flesh—they are abominations against the natural order. Heaven demands their purification. Your wives will be brought to the Celestial Pyre, Issei Hyoudou. They will burn away the draconic corruption—either to be reborn pure, or to be consumed entirely. It matters not which they choose. Divine law supersedes your petty draconic traditions."

The angel had smiled. That was what would stay with everyone watching.

He had smiled while condemning Issei's entire family to a pyre.

## GACHA PULL ONE: Punishing a Divine Figure for Harming His Family

The vision swam into perfect clarity.

Issei's reaction was not loud. It was not the explosion that might be expected from a shounen protagonist. There was no scream, no burst of power, no righteous declaration. Instead, there was a silence that somehow contained infinite pressure.

He walked forward.

It was the walk of someone who had decided something fundamentally. Not in anger—anger would be too small for this. It was in the cold certainty of consequence. Each step was measured. His hand rose.

Cassiel began to speak again—"You dare—"—but the words cut off as Issei's hand closed around the Seraph's collar. Not the ethereal robes. Not the symbolic vestments of divine office. The *collar itself*, grabbed with the grip of someone who had trained to manipulate power itself.

And then Issei *pulled*.

The Seraph had expected defiance. Perhaps combat. Divine combat, measured and formal within the laws of the cosmos. What the angel had not expected was to be *dragged* like a prisoner, like a criminal seized at the scene of a crime, hauled backwards through the divine chamber as if his rank and power and eternal status meant *nothing*.

The shock rippled through the council. Other divine beings rose from their seats, but Issei was already moving, his stride eating distance. The Seraph's protests became frantic, his power flaring—golden light and divine authority exploding outward—but Issei did not slow. His own energy rose to meet it, and the theatre gasped.

It was not the dragon power they had seen before. This was something else. Cardinal Crimson crashed against the Seraph's light like two suns colliding. Where it had been rainbow iridescence before, it now burned scarlet and gold, the Pseudo-Dragon Deification flaring to full manifestation. The very air howled with the pressure differential.

Issei took the Seraph through the chamber wall.

The impact shattered marble and celestial light both. They disappeared into a corridor beyond, and for a moment there was nothing but the echo of power. Then the throne room fell silent—a silence so absolute that the collective breath of every observer seemed to freeze.

The divine beings looked to one another. The Marvel heroes leaned forward. Villains and pragmatists began calculating.

Then Issei returned, alone, his breathing controlled but heavy. There was no blood on his hands. His power had retreated. But something hung in the air behind him—a seal, shimmering and complex, binding the Seraph somewhere beyond sight.

He walked to the center of the chamber. His voice, when he spoke, carried no waver:

"Any god or cosmic entity who threatens my family will face what they have earned. Not death—I choose to be better than that. But consequence. Justice. Binding. Exile. Whatever I judge fit. That is not negotiable. That is not debatable. That is *law* in the draconic tradition—family is sovereign. You do not touch a dragon's nest without accepting the price."

He turned back to where his family's silhouettes waited, his protective stance crystal clear.

"This is not a request for your understanding. It is a statement of fact."

Then he sat down, and the vision faded.

## Part Two: Reactions Cascade

The theatre erupted.

"By Odin's beard," Thor breathed, and there was something like respect in his voice. "He treated a Seraph like a child caught stealing. Magnificent."

"He violated divine sanctuary," a lesser god protested. "Assaulted a being of pure celestial light. There must be consequences—"

"Why?" The question came from Hercules, who had risen from his seat. "He protected his family. I have fathered hundreds of children—do you think I would have acted differently? Divinity does not grant the right to harm what another holds sacred."

Loki's face had split into an expression of pure delight. "Oh, this is *exquisite*. Do you see what he's done? He's created a legal precedent. He's established that the laws of his world supersede yours. Just wait—you won't be able to touch him through celestial courts anymore."

"That's a problem," the Living Tribunal said quietly, and even the other cosmic beings seemed to pull back at the note of genuine concern in those words. "The cosmic frameworks are not meant to overlap this way. If draconic law becomes coequal with divine law—"

"Then there must be a judgment," Zeus declared, his voice crackling with authority. "A tribunal. The question before us is simple: Did the boy act within the bounds of cosmic law, or did he commit transgression?"

Rias stood from the devil's section. "Before any judgment is rendered, I speak for the underworld. Issei's actions fall squarely within the traditions of the draconic clans. In our law, the family is inviolable. What he did was not just permitted—it was required. No dragon would accept a threat to their bloodline without response."

Strange nodded slowly, understanding the implications. "So we have a collision of legal systems. Different cosmologies with different moral frameworks."

"Exactly," Rias said. "And unless Marvel's divine hierarchy wants to declare war on an entire dimension's legal tradition, they'll have to accept the precedent."

## GACHA PULL TWO: Trial in Front of Divine Council

The gacha machine glowed again, and the theatre transformed once more.

Now they were in a cosmic courtroom, the chambers of the gods arranged in concentric rings. At the center stood Issei, no longer in the council chamber but in neutral space—a place where the rules of all realities touched and could be weighed equally. He did not look cowed. If anything, he appeared more solid here, as if the simple act of defending his family had anchored him more deeply in reality itself.

Before him, the divine tribunal was convened.

The charges were read by a neutral arbiter, voice echoing across dimensions:

"Charge One: Violation of Divine Sanctuary. The defendant entered a hallowed chamber and violated the peace of the gods.

"Charge Two: Assault Upon a Celestial Being. The defendant physically laid hands upon and transported a Seraph against its will.

"Charge Three: Presumption of Moral Authority Over Divinity. The defendant declared himself competent to judge and punish a being of eternal standing.

"How does the defendant answer?"

Issei's voice was steady. "Not guilty. The Seraph made a direct threat to my family's existence. In every moral and legal framework I operate under, I was obligated to respond. I did so without killing, without permanent harm. I showed restraint. I acted within the law of my own cosmology."

The trial that followed was unlike anything the theatre had ever witnessed.

Hercules spoke first, his voice rich with the weight of experience: "I have fathered many children across the ages. Some with mortals, some with divine beings, some with nymphs and spirits. And I have protected each of them fiercely when they were threatened. The defendant's action honors the sacred duty of fatherhood. Whatever else he may be, he is a father protecting his brood. I do not call this a crime."

Thor nodded, his expression grave and approving. "The boy invoked something older than the written laws of heaven or council. He invoked the warrior's code: family first. That is a law that exists in every cosmos where strength and honor matter. I find no transgression here."

Zeus rose, his voice crackling with barely restrained fury. "If we permit this precedent, what is to stop every mortal in every realm from seizing their gods when displeased? Divinity depends upon hierarchy. Order depends upon respect for rank. The boy has shattered both."

"No," came a new voice. Odin had stood. His single eye surveyed the chamber, and even the other gods seemed to hold their breath.

"No," he repeated. "He has not shattered order. He has revealed that your order is not universal."

The All-Father's voice carried the weight of a thousand years of hard-won wisdom. "I have broken laws to protect my children. I have sacrificed my eye, my arm, my dignity—all for the survival of those I love. I cannot condemn Hyoudou for doing the same. If you call this transgression, you call my own choices into question. And I have lived too long to be judged by those who have never had to choose between their principles and their family."

Odin paused, his gaze steady.

"The boy violated no law I recognize. A parent protecting offspring is not a crime—it is a duty. If you condemn him for this, you condemn yourselves."

The chamber fell silent.

The angels and Seraphs demanded punishment—imprisonment, seal-breaking, exile. They spoke of divine law and cosmic order. But their voices seemed smaller now, less absolute. The lower-ranking gods were divided. Some supported Issei's position; others remained cautious. The Olympians were noncommittal, though Hercules's support had clearly influenced their thinking.

Finally, the neutral arbiter spoke:

"The tribunal cannot reach consensus. The charges rest upon a fundamental disagreement about what law applies. The defendant operates under a cosmological framework—draconic law—that your tribunal does not have jurisdiction to judge. To punish him would be to impose celestial law upon a being who answers to a different set of rules."

The arbiter paused, and when it spoke again, its voice carried an official finality that seemed to reshape the very fabric of the theatre:

"**SUBJECT'S ACTIONS CLASSIFIED: LAWFUL UNDER DRACONIC COSMOLOGY. TRANSGRESSION UNDER CELESTIAL LAW. JURISDICTION UNRESOLVED. BOTH SYSTEMS WILL COEXIST.**

"The tribunal hereby establishes that Issei Hyoudou operates under a legal framework distinct from Marvel's divine hierarchies. His actions cannot be judged solely by celestial standards, nor can celestial law be unilaterally applied to him without violation of his own cosmological sovereignty."

It was a landmark ruling. A cosmic precedent.

The implications rippled through the theatre like an earthquake.

## Part Three: The Lasting Shock

The vision faded, and the theatre returned to normal. But nothing felt normal anymore.

"That's..." Strange began, then stopped. "That's a formal acknowledgment that he's not subject to divine law."

"It's an admission that the cosmos is more fragmented than they want to admit," Loki said, practically purring. "If different legal systems can exist simultaneously without hierarchy—if there's no single ultimate law—then none of them can claim absolute authority."

"This means Mephisto can't claim his soul through celestial contract," a demon lord muttered, realizing the implication. "If draconic law supersedes contracts made under celestial framework—"

"Exactly," Rias confirmed. "The entire system of cosmic agreements is now destabilized. You cannot force someone to honor a law they don't recognize as binding."

Thor leaned back in his seat, a grim smile crossing his face. "The boy just made it impossible for any single faction to control him through external law. He answered only to his own code, and he proved it. And the tribunal had to accept it."

Odin remained silent, but there was something like recognition in his expression. He had seen this before—not in this form, perhaps, but in essence. Someone willing to break every external rule to protect what mattered most. The All-Father understood that kind of defiance. He respected it.

The Living Tribunal's agitation was palpable now. "This will cascade. Other beings will claim similar cosmological sovereignty. The unified cosmic order—"

"Was never as unified as you pretended," Odin said softly. It was the only comment he made, but it seemed to close the discussion.

## Part Four: The Hook

In the devil's section, a demon whispered urgently to another: "If draconic law is now established as coequal to celestial law, then any contract Mephisto tries to enforce—"

Rias met their gaze and smiled. "Then we'll see what happens when a demon lord tries to claim something that runs counter to draconic tradition. My guess? He'll find that the supernatural theatre won't protect him the way it protected the angels."

Loki was already thinking several moves ahead. "This is the precedent. This is the crack in their authority. I wonder what happens when someone tries to destroy him through celestial means now."

The Avengers exchanged looks. They had come to understand Issei as powerful, yes—but also as bound by the expectations and rules that governed all beings. Now they understood something else: he had carved out a space where *different* rules applied. Where his choices were sovereign.

Thor stood, his expression thoughtful. "The next matter will test whether this precedent holds."
