## The Theatre Holds Its Breath

The Theatre had never gone quiet like this.

Not for the Avengers' defeats. Not for the revelation of Thanos's conquest. Not even when the Infinity Gauntlet had been wielded before them. But now, as the stage dimmed and the gacha wheel slowed, the cosmos itself seemed to draw breath.

The Eternals occupied the left block, seated in their marble-hewn section. Phastos had his forearms on his thighs, his fingers splayed. Ajak sat rigid, her seven thousand years of life compressed into the tension of her shoulders. Thena's hand rested on the pommel of a sword that could cleave mountains. Sersi and Gilgamesh flanked them—watching. Always watching. The Celestials had chosen them to be eternal. To witness. To remember.

To their right, the Celestials themselves occupied a tier that seemed to exist slightly outside normal space. Arishem the Judge sat foremost, their form vast and angular, composed of gold and void. Around them: Oneg the Prober, Exitar the Executioner, and others whose names few in the theatre could pronounce. They were the architects. The seed-bearers. The divine engineers who had shaped every world in this universe, including Earth.

When the gacha wheel began to slow, they leaned forward.

Phastos felt it first—the disturbance in the cosmic background radiation. The other Eternals followed his gaze upward, toward the stage.

The wheel resolved.

## Gacha Pull 1: "Summoning a Celestial Component from Issei's Archive"

The gacha didn't manifest an image. It did something far worse.

It *opened*.

A seam appeared in the air above the stage, and from it stepped forward a figure wreathed in light—not Issei himself, but his manifestation in the Theatre. His armory. His archive. The vault where he kept not weapons, but *reliquaries*. The places where the sacred dwelt.

From within the light, something emerged.

It was small enough to rest on two human palms. But it radiated with the resonance of creation itself.

Celestial armor.

Not a full suit—a component. A pauldron, perhaps, or a segment of a chestplate. The surface was composed of a metal that shouldn't exist in any periodic table, golden-white and shot through with veins of cosmic energy that pulsed like a heartbeat. It was flawless. Unblemished. Perfectly preserved across eons. The craftsmanship was beyond mortal comprehension—every curve and edge spoke of mathematics that predated human understanding, of intent woven into matter at the quantum level.

It hung in the Theatre's light, rotating slowly.

And the room *broke*.

Phastos's hands clenched so hard his knuckles turned white. The sound that escaped his throat was something between a gasp and a prayer. Ajak's eyes closed. Not in dismissal, but in the way one receives a wound—eyes shut against the inevitability of pain. Thena rose halfway from her seat before Sersi's hand caught her wrist. Around the cosmic tier, the Celestials began to whisper. Not words. Something older. A keening that resonated in frequencies no human ear could hear.

"No," Ajak breathed.

"That's impossible," Sersi whispered.

Reed Richards leaned forward in the Marvel contingent, his eyes narrowing behind his glasses. Forge—who had built weapons before, who understood the cost of engineering marvels—stood. His cybernetic eye whirred, refocusing. Adjusting. Trying to see if what he was seeing was real.

The cosmic scientists in the Theatre went very, very still.

"What am I looking at?" Black Panther asked quietly. No one answered. Because anyone who understood what that piece was couldn't speak. The words wouldn't come. They were too heavy.

Phastos finally found his voice. It emerged raw, cracked like ancient stone splitting.

"Celestial armor. A component. That's—" He stood abruptly, his hands shaking. "That armor is a Celestial's birthright. It's woven into their form at creation. You can't simply *remove* it. You can't—" His voice fractured. "You can't take it from a dead Celestial unless..."

"Unless the Celestial is destroyed beyond regeneration," Ajak finished, her voice hollow. "Unless every atom of their essence is unmade."

Arishem the Judge rose to their full height, a figure vast as a mountain, regarding the armor piece with an intensity that made stars dim.

"One of ours fell," Arishem said. Not a question. A truth being spoken into existence. "When?"

The Eternals looked at each other. Seven thousand years of life, and none of them knew. The Celestials had always been distant, occasionally communicating through the Eternals, but they kept their own counsel. If one of them had died, it would have been—

It *would have been noticed*.

Unless it had been kept secret. Unless there was a reason.

"Phastos," Ajak said quietly. "How old would this armor be?"

Phastos approached the stage, careful, reverential, as if one wrong step might shatter the very laws of physics. His hand trembled as he reached toward the piece. He didn't touch it—that felt like blasphemy—but he examined it. His senses, honed by thirty-five centuries of craft and creation, read the architecture of the metal. The signature of its forging. The density of cosmic radiation embedded in every lattice.

"Millions of years," he whispered. "At minimum. The radiation signatures are... they're old. Old like the universe is old. But the preservation is perfect. Absolute zero wouldn't have kept it this way. You'd need..." He turned to look at Issei's manifestation. "You'd need to keep it in stasis. In a pocket of stopped time."

"He kept it safe," Sersi said softly. "Not as a trophy. As a relic."

"Why?" Thena's voice cut through the Theatre. "Why would a Celestial allow a mortal to carry their armor?"

The gacha wheel hummed. It was time for the second pull. The Theatre held perfectly still.

## Gacha Pull 2: "Context of the Kill"

The pull manifested not as a vision, but as memory—projected from Issei's archive into the Theatre's collective consciousness. Everyone present experienced it simultaneously. This was the gacha at its most powerful: not mere information, but *experience*. The sensation of being there.

The scene opened on a world wreathed in cosmic storms. Not Earth. Somewhere else. Somewhere ancient. Issei stood alone on a plane of black glass, looking up at the sky.

And the Celestial descended.

It came not in apocalypse, but in grace. Its form was different from Arishem's—more fluid, less geometrically perfect. A researcher. A scout. One of the Celestials who went *into* universes rather than overseeing them from beyond. Its armor caught the light of a dying star nearby.

When it spoke, the Theatre heard it in their bones.

*"You are the one called Issei Hyoudou. I have traveled far to find you. Your presence resonates across dimensional boundaries. You are known even to us."*

Issei's expression was calm. He had faced gods before. Dragons. Primordial entities. But the sight of a Celestial still struck something ancient in him—a recognition that here stood something that had been shaping worlds since before his timeline had even begun.

*"I have a warning,"* the Celestial said. *"And a plea."*

What followed was a recounting that made even the Eternals in the Theatre listen with the intensity of prayer. The Celestial had been investigating anomalies across universes. Realities were being *consumed*. Not conquered. Not destroyed through war. Unmade. Erased. As if they had never existed. Entire timeline branches were vanishing from the cosmic tree.

"The Devourer," the Theatre heard the Celestial say, though that was not the creature's true name—only the closest mortal approximation. "It exists between universes. It is not of any single reality. It feeds on existence itself. My brethren dismiss it as myth. As a phenomenon, not an entity. But I have seen the wreckage. I have witnessed universes going *dark*. And it is coming to your timeline. It is *hungry* for your reality."

*"What do you want from me?"* Issei asked.

*"To survive,"* the Celestial answered simply. *"I cannot face it alone. My power is vast, but I am finite. It is not. But if we bind our forces—if we create a collective barrier—we might delay it. We might buy your timeline centuries. Millennia, if we are fortunate."*

Issei did not hesitate. Heroes do not.

The battle that followed was rendered in impressions rather than clear images—the Theatre's consciousness could not process the full power exchange. But fragments were clear:

The Devourer's manifestation. A shape of hunger and negation. Not quite physical. Not quite energy. Something that unmade reality in its path.

Issei unleashing power he had been saving. Power that burned through him like sacred fire. His draconic nature flaring. His demonic heritage rising. His dragon god power achieving synthesis that humans had no words for.

The Celestial engaging with the patience of immortality, armor glowing, cosmic forces flowing through it like blood.

They held the line.

But the Devourer was *vast*. It was patient. It was eternal in ways even Celestials were not.

The Celestial's armor began to crack.

Not from the impact, but from the *unmaking*. Piece by piece, its structure was being unmade, returned to potential. The Celestial was being erased. Not killed—killed implied a simpler end. This was dissolution. Cessation of existence.

It spoke to Issei one final time.

*"I am unmade. But the seal holds. You have bought time. When it returns—and it will return—you must be ready. This armor piece. Take it. It is all that remains of me now. It exists outside normal causality. Show it to those who doubt you. Let them see what it costs to fight what is coming."*

Issei caught the falling piece as the Celestial dissolved.

The memory ended.

## The Theatre Absorbs the Truth

Silence fell. Absolute. Complete. The kind of silence that only follows revelation.

Phastos's face was wet with tears. Not the tears of sadness, but of profound, religious awe. "A Celestial," he whispered. "A Celestial chose to sacrifice itself. For a mortal. For *him*."

Ajak's hand moved to cover her heart. "Not a kill," she said softly. "A martyrdom. A Celestial gave its life as a message. As a beacon."

Thena gripped the arm of her seat so hard her knuckles matched Phastos's. "It trusted him," she said. "One of the architects of worlds looked at a human and chose to trust him with existence itself."

Around the cosmic tier, the Celestials rose as one. Arishem's form seemed to diminish slightly—not in power, but in certainty. In the knowledge that one of their own had fallen. That the universe was less than it had been.

"The Devourer is real," Oneg the Prober said. "The Celestial would not sacrifice unless the threat was absolute."

"We knew," Exitar said grimly. "We have always known. But to hear it confirmed..."

Reed Richards stood, his scientific mind racing. "If a Celestial died to a threat," he said carefully, "then the question becomes: what do we contribute? What role does humanity play in a conflict between cosmic architects and something that *unmakes* them?"

"We endure," Forge answered quietly. "That's our superpower. Not cosmic energy. Not immortality. The ability to keep going even when facing the impossible."

But it was Galactus who spoke the final truth.

The Devourer of Worlds had been silent, watching from the cosmic margins. Now the entity shifted, drawing every eye. When Galactus speaks, universes listen.

"I told you of the destroyed reality," Galactus intoned. "The universe that was unmade. Twelve billion years of life, erased. I alone survived, preserved through the exile of my hunger. The Celestial your companion hunted was correct. The threat *returns*. It samples new universes now. Testing their defenses. Your timeline will be among them."

The Theatre trembled.

"How long?" Ajak asked.

"Uncertain," Galactus replied. "But the seals weaken. The Celestial bought time, nothing more. The Theatre gathers you all—heroes and villains alike—because what comes will require every power that can be mustered."

Phastos looked at the Celestial armor, still rotating gently in the Theatre's light. "This is a message," he said. "It's not a trophy. It's a *call*. Issei has been carrying this alone, knowing what's coming. Knowing that something killed a god."

"And he said nothing," Sersi added, understanding dawning in her eyes. "For how long? How long has he carried this burden?"

The Theatre had no answer. Issei's manifestation had faded with the memory. Only the armor remained, a golden-white fragment of sacrifice, rotating in the light.

## Theatre Ledger

**Cosmic Significance Record:**
The Theatre had recorded many things over its infinite watching, but the appearance of Celestial armor—an object that by all laws of existence should not exist in mortal hands—marked a new threshold. A gacha pull that shifted the fundamental understanding of what threats awaited. The Eternals recognized the armor for what it was: proof. Not of conquest, but of alliance. Not of victory, but of *chosen sacrifice*.

**Celestial Acknowledgment Registry:**
With Arishem's confirmation that one of the Celestials had fallen, the cosmic order faced a reckoning. The Eternals, who had served as intermediaries between Celestials and humanity, now understood that the architects themselves were vulnerable. The hierarchy had changed. The universe was no longer a place where Celestials were assured of eternity. They too could be unmade. They too could bleed.

**Issei's Burden Quantified:**
The gacha pull revealed what no sensor could measure: the spiritual weight Issei had carried. A Celestial's final gift became his final responsibility. He alone had known the full scope of the threat. He alone had stood beside a god at the moment of its sacrifice. Every choice he had made afterward was informed by this knowledge—the knowledge that existence itself could end, and that it would take everything he had, and still might not be enough.

**The Devourer's Confirmation:**
By Galactus's own testimony, the Devourer was not myth or isolated phenomenon. It was a returning threat. A force that operated on a scale where even Celestials became finite. The Theatre's gathering of heroes and villains across the centuries was not random preparation—it was triage. A collection of every possible advantage, because when the Devourer arrived in full, there would be no second chances. There would only be those who had prepared, and those who had already been unmade.
