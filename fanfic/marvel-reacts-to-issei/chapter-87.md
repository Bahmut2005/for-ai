**ENTRY 1: THE QUESTION UNASKED FOR YEARS**

Yondu Udonta had learned early in life that the galaxy didn't care much about legitimacy. You either found your place or you didn't. You either belonged to someone or you found people who needed you badly enough to belong.

He'd walked into the observation chamber during a quiet moment—one of the rare ones where Issei wasn't surrounded by heroes wanting to analyze his power or villains wanting to exploit it. The dragon emperor was simply sitting, looking out at something far away, his mind elsewhere.

"Question for you, kid," Yondu said, pulling up a chair without asking permission. It was his way. "And I want a real answer, not the diplomatic thing you give to Captain Goodness or T'Challa the Political."

Issei turned, recognizing Yondu's tone as one that demanded honesty. "Alright."

"What makes someone a father?" Yondu leaned back, his red skin catching the light. "Not biologically. Not the genetics thing. What *makes* someone a father? Because I figure a kid who goes around adopting half the multiverse has thought about this more than most."

The theatre went very still.

Yondu had no idea he'd just activated one of the most significant gacha pulls of the session. The system recognized the question's weight and locked in, creating an observation frame that would capture this moment in absolute clarity.

Issei took a long breath. He wasn't the type to rush through important questions.

"I think..." he began slowly, "a father is someone who chooses to carry weight that isn't theirs to carry. Someone who looks at a kid—or a situation, or a person—and decides that their life needs to include that. Not because they have to. Not because it benefits them. Because they decide that kid's future matters more than their convenience."

Yondu nodded slowly, like he'd expected that but was listening for something more.

"But that's everyone who helps someone," Yondu pressed. "That's just being decent. There's more to it than that."

"There is," Issei agreed. "A father is someone who doesn't just help once. Who keeps showing up. Who gets frustrated and tired and wants to quit, but doesn't. A father is someone who teaches you not just what to do but *why* to do it. Who lets you make mistakes and helps you fix them. Who knows you're going to fail and *doesn't judge you for it*."

He paused, looking at Yondu directly.

"And a father is someone who doesn't need blood to make it real. Who can look at a kid who isn't theirs and decide: *this is mine now*. I'm claiming this one. Not to control them. But to protect them. To give them a place where they belong."

In the observation chamber, Logan Howlett leaned back in his chair, eyes closed. He knew what Issei was describing. He'd lived it. Had been abandoned, then chosen by someone who decided his broken pieces still mattered.

Nick Fury was quiet, his one eye tracking the conversation. He'd stolen children his entire career, making them into weapons. But he was beginning to understand the difference between taking and choosing, between ownership and responsibility.

T'Challa watched with the contemplation of a king who'd inherited his role rather than chosen it. He was beginning to see the value in Issei's approach to leadership and family.

"I had a father," Yondu said quietly. "Sort of. Before Stakar found me, before the Ravagers, there was... someone. He wasn't good. He wasn't kind. But he was there sometimes, and when he was, I was his kid. And when he wasn't, I was just another mouth the village had to feed."

He looked away.

"Then Stakar came along and I thought, okay, this is a captain, not a father. This is a job. But Stakar..." Yondu's jaw tightened. "He showed up. Every time. Even when I was stupid. Even when I almost got his crew killed. He kept showing up."

"Then he was your father," Issei said simply.

Yondu took that in like a physical blow. He'd never quite let himself think of it that way. Stakar wasn't *that* kind of figure. Stakar was a captain. A mentor. A boss.

But yeah. A father.

**ENTRY 2: THE WEIGHT OF CHOOSING**

The conversation expanded as word spread through the theatre. Logan joined them, uninvited but not unwelcome. Fury appeared in the back. Magneto, surprisingly, took a seat and listened without commenting. Even Rocket came back from the workshop, sensing something significant happening.

"Here's what I never understood," Logan said, his voice rough with years and scars. "Why anyone would choose to carry that weight. It's not like you get rewards. You don't get gratitude. Kids grow up and leave. They make mistakes that hurt you. They become their own people and sometimes you don't even recognize who they become."

"That's true," Issei acknowledged. "But you still do it."

"Why?" Logan leaned forward. "Because it matters. Because the person you're raising—they're going to do things. They're going to affect people. They're going to change the world. And if you're there, helping them become *good* at changing the world, then you're responsible for all of that too."

Rocket whistled low. "That's heavy, Logan. That's philosophy."

"It's parenting," Logan corrected. "All parenting is philosophy. You're making bets about what kind of person someone will become and trying to stack the deck in favor of them being decent instead of terrible."

Magneto spoke for the first time: "This assumes that fatherhood is about teaching someone to be good. But some fathers teach their children to be strong. To dominate. To take what they want."

"Those aren't fathers," Issei said quietly. "Those are men who had children. A father is someone who wants their kid to be better than they are. To do better than they did. To not repeat the mistakes. That's the actual definition."

"A father is someone who cares more about the kid's future than their own legacy," T'Challa added, and there was something in his voice that suggested he'd been thinking about this since the throne came to him. "Someone who would sacrifice their own vision for their child's freedom to make their own choices."

"Sometimes you sacrifice a lot," Fury said quietly. "Sometimes you sacrifice everything. And you never tell them what you gave up, because that's not their responsibility to carry."

Issei looked at Yondu. "Is this about Peter? About Peter being your son?"

"Peter is my son," Yondu said with absolute certainty. "Not because he was born that way. Because I chose him. Because I kept showing up. Because I made mistakes and he forgave me and we figured things out together." He paused. "And at the end, I did the thing you said—I sacrificed something so he could be free. Sacrificed everything, actually."

"And?" Issei prompted.

"And he became the person I wanted him to be," Yondu said, and his voice cracked slightly. "Better than I was. More decent than I deserved. That's the reward. That's what you get for being a father. Not gratitude. Not recognition. You get to see the kid become something good and know you had something to do with that."

The theatre held the silence like a cupped hand holding something fragile.

---

**ENTRY 3: THE MULTIPLE FATHERS**

"I was wondering," Rocket said slowly, breaking the quiet, "about your eight wives. You said they're not all biological mothers to your kids in canon, right? Some are, some aren't. But are they all fathers and mothers to the children in the household? Does it matter who the biological parent is?"

"No," Issei said firmly. "Every single one of them is a parent. Some by blood. Some by choice. Some by adoption. It doesn't matter which. They all show up. They all teach. They all sacrifice. That makes them parents."

"That's a lot of parents," Rocket said, and he didn't sound judgmental, just... curious.

"It is," Issei agreed. "But a kid having multiple parents isn't a bad thing. Different perspectives. Different strengths. Different people to fail you in different ways and then help you figure out how to keep going. I had Gorou Hyoudou—my biological father. Good man. Solid. But I also had teachers, mentors, other fathers in all but name. And I needed all of them."

Magneto stood abruptly and left the chamber. No one commented on it, but they all recognized it as a gesture of something—not acceptance, but acknowledgment. Recognition that fatherhood was a responsibility Magneto had always treated as interchangeable with power, when perhaps there was more complexity there.

Fury remained, watching Issei with the careful attention of someone who'd made different choices and was beginning to wonder if different was better.

"What you're describing," Fury said quietly, "it's not just fatherhood. It's community. It's the opposite of what most of us have built."

"Most soldiers learned to build walls," Issei replied. "To keep people out so they couldn't hurt you. But I learned early that the walls don't protect. They isolate. And isolation is slower death than any bomb could manage."

"So you did the opposite," T'Challa said thoughtfully. "You built a family so large and so connected that an attack on one is an attack on all."

"Is that wrong?" Issei asked.

"No," T'Challa said slowly. "It's just... different. Wakanda protects through isolation. You protect through connection. I'm beginning to think your way might actually be stronger."

---

**ENTRY 4: THE ANSWER TO AN UNASKED QUESTION**

As the chamber emptied, Yondu approached Issei one more time.

"Thank you," he said, and it seemed to cost him something to say it.

"For what?" Issei asked.

"For answering the question I was afraid to ask. For letting me know that what I did with Peter—that it counts. That it matters. That he's really my son even though he wasn't born that way."

Issei put a hand on Yondu's shoulder. "He's your son because you chose him and kept choosing him. That's more real than blood."

Yondu nodded, but didn't move away immediately. They sat in the quiet together—a dragon emperor and a Ravager captain—both understanding at last that fatherhood was about the choices you made and the burdens you carried because someone else's future mattered more than your comfort.

"For what it's worth," Yondu said eventually, "I think you're going to be a good father. You're already making the right choices."

"I hope so," Issei said softly. "I'm going to need people like you teaching me though. People who've already figured out what matters."

"That's what family does," Yondu replied. "They teach each other. Again and again until it sticks."
