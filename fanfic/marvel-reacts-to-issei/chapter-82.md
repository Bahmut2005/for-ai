### SECTION I: THE MAGNITUDE OF VIOLATION

**[HUD Status: Subject Emotional State — ESCALATING | Multiversal Count — CALCULATING | Consent Violation Level — STAGGERING]**

Issei's question hung in the Theatre like a curse.

"How many?" he asked quietly, turning to Theatre Keeper with the deliberate focus of someone whose fury had cycled into something far more dangerous than rage. Fury could be satisfied. Wrath could be channeled. But the cold precision in his voice now—that was the tone of someone conducting final accounting before judgment.

"How many consciousnesses have witnessed my private moments?"

Theatre Keeper, ancient and experienced in matters of observation and knowledge, did not look away. "Approximately four thousand, seven hundred and sixty-three observers across accessible multiversal boundaries. Not all simultaneously, but sequentially through archive access over the temporal span—"

"Stop," Issei interrupted. His hand rose, and the draconic magic swirling around his fingers began to take on colors that none of the heroes in the Theatre had seen before. Not the reds and golds of standard dragon fire. These were colors that seemed to exist at the edges of perception—the hue of betrayal, the shade of violation, the light of sacred things broken.

"Four thousand," he repeated softly. "You allowed four thousand beings to watch me love my wives. To observe moments that belonged to *them*. To steal memories that were never yours to show."

Rias had reached the platform now. She gripped his arm—not to restrain, but to anchor. To remind him that he was not alone in this violation.

"Issei," she said, and her voice contained the weight of her devil heritage, the authority of someone who understood power and its misuse. "They need to understand."

"They do understand," Akeno added, descending more slowly, her traditional smile replaced by the cold assessment of a lightning-class warrior. "They're beginning to understand exactly what they've done."

The Avengers watched. The X-Men watched. Doom, with that terrible awareness of his own, watched. And they began to comprehend the true nature of the violation.

It wasn't simply that Issei had been observed. It was that every intimate moment—every vulnerable second where he had lowered his defenses with the women he loved—had been transformed into *data*. Into *content*. Into something that could be archived and discussed and analyzed without consent or consideration.

They had taken the sacred and made it spectacle.

"In my world," Issei said, still quiet, still controlled, "violation of a woman's privacy in intimate moments is a crime. A violation of her dignity and autonomy that demands recompense. And what you've done is far, far worse, because you've violated *four women*. Four people who deserve protection. Four souls whose trust was sacred."

He turned to look at each of his wives in turn, and the anguish in his eyes was profound.

"I failed to protect you," he said, his voice breaking slightly. "I placed you in a Theatre I didn't know existed and allowed this to happen."

"No," Rias said sharply. "No, Issei. This isn't your failure. This is *their* violation." She pointed toward the amphitheatre, toward every spectator who had watched and listened and learned. "They chose this. They permitted this. They're responsible."

"Thousands of them," Issei whispered, and suddenly the Theatre seemed smaller around him. His draconic presence expanded—not in physical size, but in awareness. Every being in the chamber felt the weight of his attention, the heat of his shame and fury and violated trust.

"Show me," he commanded Theatre Keeper. "Show me every consciousness that watched. I want names. I want species. I want to understand the scope of what was stolen."

Theatre Keeper hesitated—a gesture that suggested even the ancient being understood it was standing at a precipice. "The revelation would take considerable time. The archive contains detailed logs of—"

"Do it," Issei said flatly. "I'm not leaving this Theatre until I understand every single being who was granted access to my wives' intimate moments."

Some faces familiar to the Avengers. Some utterly alien. Some that seemed to exist in registers of reality that human perception struggled to accommodate.

Four thousand, seven hundred and sixty-three beings had watched. And now, as Issei's draconic fury rolled through the Theatre like a wave, those same beings—if they were still present in adjacent viewing spaces—began to feel the weight of their violation made manifest.

"Do you understand what you've taken?" Issei asked the assembled crowd, his voice carrying now with the harmonic undertones of draconic authority. "You've taken moments that were meant for four people. You've taken vulnerability and transformed it into resource. You've violated the consent of four women who had no say in whether their most intimate moments would be broadcast across multiversal boundaries."

The cosmic beings in the Theatre—those who dealt in abstract concepts and reality-shaping—began to feel genuinely *frightened*.

There was something in Issei's fury that resonated at frequencies they understood. The betrayal of trust. The violation of sacred boundaries. The transformation of love into spectacle. These were not concepts that could be explained away with academic theory.

"In my world," Issei continued, "there are contracts. Agreements that establish consent. Oaths that mean something because we've built them on the foundation of trust. What you did here violated every single principle that makes such agreements meaningful."

He looked at Carol Danvers, who had stood and begun to approach the platform—not as a threat, but as a captain trying to understand a crisis she had witnessed but not created.

"You came to observe," he said. "But you came without permission. You witnessed without consent. You took without asking. That makes you—" He paused, searching for the English word. "—complicit in violation."

"We are," Carol said quietly, and her tone carried the weight of genuine understanding. "You're right. We violated your privacy and your wife's. We need to establish consent-based frameworks before continuing any observation."

"There will be no continuing," Issei said flatly. "There will be reckoning. There will be new rules. And there will be consequences if those rules are broken."

The wives had formed a protective circle around him now—Rias, Akeno, Xenovia, and Rossweisse. Not because he needed protection, but because they understood the magnitude of what had been violated and they wanted him to know that he was not bearing this alone.

Magneto rose from his seat, and his presence carried weight. The master of magnetism understood violations of autonomy better than most beings in existence.

"The dragon-king speaks truth," Magneto said, his voice resonating through the Theatre. "Observation without consent is a tool of oppression. You've used it here, likely without even recognizing it as such."

Charles Xavier, still seated but with his psychic presence expanded to brush against every consciousness present, spoke with the authority of centuries of wisdom: "The archive must be restructured. Consent protocols must be established. And compensation must be considered for the violation of privacy that has already occurred."

The Tea Theatre, which had been convened to witness spectacle and wonder, now faced the reality that spectacle built on violation is merely abuse dressed up in academic language.

## THEATRE LEDGER — SECTION III: THE NEW REQUIREMENTS

**[HUD: Consensus Emerging — REFORM NECESSARY | Subject Satisfaction Level — PENDING COMMITMENT | Wives' Anger — STRATEGIC]**

Issei's demands, when they came, were delivered with the precision of someone who understood power and how to wield it against those who had misused it.

"First," he said, "the archive contains moments involving my wives. Those moments are to be removed in full. All four thousand copies, all seven hundred and sixty-three observers' access, completely purged. If I discover even a fragment remaining, there will be consequences."

Sue Storm, her scientific mind still running calculations, raised a hand. "That eliminates the primary archive. Do you understand what you're asking?"

"Yes," Issei said coldly. "I'm asking you to delete the evidence of your violation. I'm asking you to treat my wives' privacy as sacred again. I'm asking you to restore dignity that you stole."

"There is a way," Theatre Keeper began carefully, "to establish consent-based observation. The subject and his partner-units could approve which moments were observational, under what conditions, with what access permissions—"

"No," Rias said sharply. "No observers. None. The Theatre shows us. We're not putting our lives on display for anyone."

"But the archive would be invaluable for—" one of the cosmic beings began.

"For what?" Akeno's smile was all teeth. "For your curiosity? For your research? We're not specimens. We're not subjects. We're people who have already been violated. We're establishing new boundaries now."

Xenovia's hand had come to rest on her sword—a reminder that some boundaries would be enforced with more than words.

"Second," Issei continued, "any future observation of my world, my people, my family requires explicit, ongoing consent from every individual observed. Not consent from their government. Not consent from their dimension. Consent from *them*. As people. As individuals with autonomy and dignity."

"The logistics would be—" Theatre Keeper began.

"I don't care," Issei interrupted. "If observation requires consent and consent is difficult to obtain, then perhaps observation is not your right. Perhaps some stories exist to be lived, not archived."

Rossweisse, the warrior-scholar of the group, added her voice: "You've created a precedent here. You've demonstrated that privacy can be violated if the violation is conducted by sufficiently powerful entities. You've shown that consent doesn't matter if you're advanced enough. That's a precedent you need to undo."

"Third," Issei said, and now his voice carried the weight of formal declaration—the tone of a king establishing law, "you will install consent protections in your Theatre. Not suggestions. Not guidelines. Protections that prevent observation without explicit agreement. And those protections will be auditable by external parties—neutral observers who can verify that you're actually following your new rules."

Tony Stark, who had been quiet for some time, suddenly stood. "He's right," the genius billionaire announced. "And I want to volunteer my company to conduct those audits. If someone's built a multiversal observation system, and they've already failed the consent test once, they need external oversight."

Doctor Strange nodded slowly. "The Mystic Arts community would support this as well. We can provide verification frameworks. Ensure you're not repeating this violation."

"The Avengers endorse consent-based observation protocols," Captain America said formally. "And we can help enforce them."

The room was shifting. The spectators were beginning to understand that this wasn't a negotiation. This was the establishment of new law, backed by power and legitimacy and the terrible weight of violated trust.
