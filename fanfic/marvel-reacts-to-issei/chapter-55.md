# Chapter 55: Fury's Contingency

**Primary Reaction Groups:**
- S.H.I.E.L.D. command and tactical staff
- S.W.O.R.D. observers
- Intelligence operatives and spies
- Hydra watchers (in the back, very quiet)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Nick Fury's Contingency Plans" — **Secret**
2. "Theatre Grading of Each Plan" — **Comedy-Class**

---

The theatre dimmed with the particular weight that came before revelation—not the rush of action sequences or the intimate focus of personal drama, but the slower, more methodical darkness that preceded a bureaucrat's reckoning.

Nick Fury stood.

His black eye-patch caught the ambient light from the screen as he straightened his jacket. The theatre fell silent. Even those who'd been whispering—and there had been plenty of whispers during Issei's latest rampage—shut their mouths. Fury had that effect. Decades of S.H.I.E.L.D. command, of walking through nuclear accidents and alien invasions with the same measured stride, had worn his presence into something heavier than mere authority.

Behind him, rows of S.H.I.E.L.D. operatives shifted. Natasha Romanoff leaned back in her seat, arms crossed, a small smile playing at her mouth. Tony Stark had actually put down his phone. Captain America sat with his jaw set, waiting. Strange watched with the vague amusement of someone who knew that mortal contingency plans rarely accounted for the supernatural. The Avengers, the X-Men, Wakanda's intelligence network, the intelligence officers of a dozen nations—all of them felt the shift.

Fury's eye fixed on the screen.

"Before we continue," he said, his voice steady and cold as November fog, "the Council requires a technical assessment. Contingency framework."

The screen flickered. White letters materialized against a black background:

**GACHA PULL — NICK FURY'S CONTINGENCY PLANS**  
**Rarity: SECRET**

A tactical document materialized on the screen—and it was *comprehensive*. Red folders, stamped with classification marks, overlapping in a digital cascade. The theatre's systems seemed to respect the documents enough to render them with bureaucratic precision.

Fury's single eye never wavered. "Five operational frameworks," he said, as if he were briefing the Security Council itself. "Designed to contain, disable, or strategically neutralize a threat matching the Issei Hyoudou profile."

The word *neutralize* hung in the air like a threat.

Fury gestured, and the screen shifted.

**PLAN ALPHA: ADAMANTIUM CONTAINMENT MATRIX**

A blueprint materialized—a chamber with walls reinforced with vibranium and adamantium alloys, layered with electromagnetic shielding and magical null-fields. The design was *obsessive*, every inch calculated to prevent escape by conventional or unconventional means.

"Containment in a sealed environment," Fury stated flatly. "No teleportation. No dimensional rifts. Magical seals provided by Strange's cooperation. Dimensional anchor via Stark's tech. Duration: indefinite."

The theatre's grading system materialized beneath the plan in glowing red letters:

**RATING: IMPOSSIBLE**

**Commentary:** *"Vibranium walls won't stop a man whose power scales infinitely. Null-fields require constant feeding of energy he can absorb. Also, what are you going to *feed* him? The logistics of keeping a sentient nuclear reactor contained for 'indefinite' duration would bankrupt your organization in six months. Skip this one, Nick."*

Fury's jaw tightened minutely.

**PLAN BETA: TELEPORTATION DAMPENING MATRIX**

The screen shifted to a second blueprint—a globe-spanning network of satellites and ground stations, designed to project a field that would lock out any form of dimensional travel or teleportation.

"Coordinated effort with Thaddeus Ross's military infrastructure and Wong's magical monitoring," Fury said. "Active surveillance of known power signatures. Any spike in dimensional energy gets automated response."

**RATING: UNLIKELY**

**Commentary:** *"He doesn't need to teleport, Nick. Last we checked, he can FLY. Also, your 'automated response' is humans with guns. That never works. Rating: Unlikely because it might accidentally stop him for approximately four seconds."*

A ripple of dark laughter moved through the theatre. Even Cap had to hide a smile.

Fury's hand clenched briefly, then relaxed. He moved on.

**PLAN GAMMA: STRATEGIC DETERRENT**

A third document materialized. This one made the room go very still. It showed a targeting solution for a thermonuclear warhead, with Issei's last known coordinates circled in red. Backup protocols for follow-up strikes. Fallback positions in case the first didn't work.

"Nuclear option," Fury said, his voice not changing. "Immediate deployment if Issei demonstrates hostile movement toward civilian populations in violation of established perimeter agreements. Paired with Stark's defense grid for coordination."

**RATING: COMMON SENSE FAILURE**

**Commentary:** *"Oh Nick. Beautiful, terrible Nick. Did you show this to Tony? He's literally made multiple suits of armor to prevent exactly this scenario. Also, your new friend here—the demon king with wives and a seven-digit regeneration factor—is not going to politely wait for a nuke to arrive. Funny plan though. Very 1970s."*

Tony made a noise somewhere between a snort and a laugh. "I was wondering when that was coming," he called out. "You know I have a JARVIS-level defense specifically to shoot down nukes, right?"

Fury didn't acknowledge him. He pressed forward.

**PLAN DELTA: COOPERATIVE MAGICAL SEAL**

The fourth blueprint was *complex*—a mandala-like arrangement of magical circles, requiring coordination between Strange, Wong, Doom (if he could be convinced), Nico Minoru, and a network of other practitioners.

"Joint effort with the mystical community," Fury said. "Issei's power has a magical component. Coordinated seal disrupts the fundamental architecture of his draconic engine and regenerative capacity. Requires his cooperation to enter the seal space."

**RATING: UNLIKELY**

**Commentary:** *"Depends entirely on getting Issei to cooperate with a plan to disable him. Let's see ... why would a being with infinite upside potential and a family he's willing to die for agree to that? Oh, right. He wouldn't. Lovely theoretical framework though. Shelf it."*

Strange tilted his head slightly, acknowledging the logic.

Then came the fifth.

**PLAN EPSILON: LEVERAGE CONTINGENCY**

The screen displayed something different this time. Not a blueprint or technical schematic. Instead, it showed a simple list:

*Rias Gremory. Issei's primary emotional anchor.*  
*His human family (parents, extended relatives).*  
*His other wives and bonded companions.*  
*His dreams for their collective future.*

Below it, a single operational note:

*Strategy: Convince Issei that the safety and future security of his family depends on maintaining strategic alliance and cooperative terms with S.H.I.E.L.D./Avengers. Implicit threat: "Step out of line, and we ensure their protection becomes impossible."*

**RATING: POSSIBLE**

**Commentary:** *"Finally, Nick. A plan that might actually work. Not because it's clever—it's not, it's boringly obvious—but because it's TRUE. His family IS his only leverage. His only vulnerability. His only priority. So yes, you could probably force cooperation by threatening what he loves, if you were willing to forfeit every ounce of his trust in the process. This plan works right up until he decides you're a threat they need to be protected FROM. Then you have a very large problem with nowhere to run. Probability of success: Possible. Probability of survival post-success: Do the math yourself."*

The theatre erupted.

Not in cheers—in a kind of stunned, complex silence that immediately fractured into heated discussion. The S.H.I.E.L.D. officers in the rows behind Fury leaned forward. The spies and intelligence operatives exchanged glances. Natasha's smile had vanished entirely, replaced by something thoughtful and dangerous. Captain America looked like someone had just shown him a door he didn't want to walk through.

Tony stood up.

"Okay, so we're doing the hostage strategy," he said flatly. "Great. Love that for us. Really instills confidence in our position as the 'good guys.'"

"It's not a hostage strategy," Fury said, still looking at the screen. "It's a strategic reality assessment."

"It's a hostage strategy," Natasha said quietly. She'd sat forward now, her expression unreadable. "You're telling Issei that cooperation is the only way to keep his family safe from external threats. That's a hostage situation where the hostage doesn't know they're a hostage."

"And the moment he realizes we're using his family as leverage," Strange added, his voice carrying that strange, multilayered quality that suggested he was speaking from experience, "we've transformed ourselves from potential allies into his exact list of threats he already knows to neutralize."

Fury's eye remained fixed on the screen. In the harsh theatre lighting, his face looked carved from granite.

"Do you have a better option?" His voice was quiet.

The silence that followed was answer enough.

Cap cleared his throat. "So what are you saying, Director? That the only way forward is... trust?"

"I'm saying," Fury replied, his voice dropping to something almost conversational, "that every technological solution, every tactical framework, every contingency plan that requires force or deception is already rated impossible to unlikely. We can build better containment units. We can network more satellites. We can stack more seals. And none of it works, because he will eventually refuse to cooperate, and then we're in active conflict with someone whose power ceiling we haven't even found yet."

He paused, letting that sink in.

"The only plan with a 'Possible' rating," Fury continued, "requires no contingency at all. It requires the opposite of contingency. It requires alliance. Genuine alliance. Based on—" he seemed to have to physically force the word out "—mutual respect."

Hydra's watchers in the back of the theatre had gone very still. One of them was actually writing something down.

"Essentially," Fury finished, turning away from the screen, "the theatre is telling us that the only way to manage an existential threat is to stop trying to manage it. To stop preparing for the moment he betrays us and instead prepare for the moment we have to prove we won't betray *him*."

"That's a massive strategic vulnerability," someone called from the S.W.O.R.D. section.

"It's also," Natasha said quietly, "the only strategy that works."

The theatre seemed to settle at that—not with comfort, but with a kind of resigned acknowledgment. The screen flickered, preparing to move on, when Fury held up his hand.

"One more thing," he said.

The screen shifted back to Plan Epsilon. The operational note remained visible:

*Strategy: Convince Issei that the safety and future security of his family depends on maintaining strategic alliance and cooperative terms with S.H.I.E.L.D./Avengers.*

"The theatre graded this plan 'Possible,'" Fury said, his voice absolutely devoid of inflection. "But there's a contingency within that contingency that it didn't explicitly state. The moment Issei is *certain* that we would never use his family as leverage—the moment he has proof that we'll protect them regardless of whether he cooperates or not—*that's* when the genuine alliance becomes unbreakable."

He let that hang.

"Because then we're not threatening his family's safety. We're guaranteeing it. We're saying, 'We will protect what you love not because you owe us, but because we're choosing to.' And a being who will sacrifice anything for his family? He becomes the most reliable ally in existence. Not from fear. From gratitude."

The theatre was completely silent now.

Strange was nodding slowly, that small, knowing smile on his face.

Cap looked like someone had just given him permission to hope.

Tony... Tony was actually looking at Fury with something like respect.

"So," Fury concluded, settling back into his seat, "we move forward with alliance. Real alliance. Not contingency. Because contingency assumes you're planning for betrayal, and betrayal is a self-fulfilling prophecy."

The screen faded to black.

A new title card began to materialize, and the theatre lurched forward into Chapter 56—but Fury's words hung in the air like a challenge and a confession all at once.

In the rows below, Natasha caught Cap's eye and raised an eyebrow. He shook his head slightly, a small motion that somehow contained an entire conversation: *He knows. He's always known. This was just him admitting it out loud.*
