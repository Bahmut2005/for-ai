# Chapter Fifty-One: The Avengers Tribunal

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Avengers • Illuminati • S.H.I.E.L.D. • X-Men

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Footage of Issei's Battles Against Grendel and Euclid" — **Epic**
2. "Avengers Debate" — **Secret**

---

The Avengers' section had been quiet for forty-eight hours. Not the quiet of sleep or contentment — the quiet of a room where the arguments are too big to spend in open air. The theatre had just watched the Ravagers vote, had just heard the machine announce that its second half would be *what he is for*, and the crack had grown, and Ddraig had spoken, and everyone was still integrating what the dragon had meant by *siege*.

But the Avengers had other work. They had been briefed since before the Ravager arc even started — their liaise to the room (some combination of Fury's ghost and the machine's administrative charity) had clarified it weeks ago: once the pirates finished their judgment, the matter came to the formal chambers. Nations. Accords. Global security frameworks. The things that actually governed whether a seventeen-year-old could dock a multiversal battleship in Earth orbit without being shot at.

Steve Rogers had not slept. Tony Stark had not stopped moving. Thor had cleaned his armor three times. Natasha Romanoff had acquired a thermos of something she would not explain and was protecting it with the focus usually reserved for infinity stones. T'Challa, King of Wakanda, had simply sat in his corner with his fingers steepled, watching the Ravager block in the way he watched markets — reading what they valued, what they feared, what they would defend.

"All right," Steve said, at the start of the fifty-first night. "We need to hear the footage. We need to make a recommendation. We need to do that before the room gets ahead of us, because the machine is going to let the room get ahead of us, that is literally what we have learned." He looked at the rest of the Avengers block — at Wanda, at Sam, at Bruce, at Rhodey, and up at the screen where the lever hung waiting. "Tony?"

"I'll pull," Tony said flatly. "Let's rip the band-aid."

He crossed the room and took the lever — crisp, businesslike, no ceremony — and yanked it hard.

The wheel spun, gold-bright and efficient, and the theatre held its breath the way it always did when rarity was in question. Epic was not as rare as Secret, not quite, but Epic meant the veil was coming off. Epic meant direct power assessment. Epic meant Marvel was about to stop theorizing and start watching the math play out in real-time.

The wheel landed on the gold-bright band. Not shimmer-gold, not performance gold. The deep, weaponized gold of something that could take the measure of a city.

**GACHA PULL: 1 OF 2.**

**"FOOTAGE OF ISSEI'S BATTLES AGAINST GRENDEL AND EUCLID" — RARITY: EPIC.**

"Oh. Okay," said Tony. "Okay, so the machine is being *specific*. It's showing us threat assessments. It's not showing us cute wins, it's showing us — Grendel. Euclid. Both of those are high-tier. Both of those are *designed* problems. So we're watching baseline capability against enemies that don't break easy, and we're watching it in armor, so we're watching his ceiling, not his floor, and—" He stopped. The vision was already taking them.

---

The vision opened in a dark that had architecture — columns of black glass, a cavern designed by something with a sense of aesthetic cruelty, and standing in the middle of it, a thing that Marvel's categorization system immediately labelled *Grendel* because the screen insisted and because the creature's own grandmother probably called it by that name out of resignation.

Grendel was not a shape so much as a refusal. It was a mass that ate light and kept eating. Claws like architectural failures, hide that had never heard of surrender, and eyes that held the flat conviction of something that had only ever been hungry and correct. The captions supplied the context with no warmth: **Humanoid Killer (High-Class Devil Tier). Ancient. Adaptive. Noted for feeding on warrior-blood as sustenance and power. Immune to conventional blade-strike due to dense magical armoring. Subject engaged at age nineteen, Cardinal Crimson Promotion stage.**

And there — against that thing's bulk, blood-red armor with the full reach of its wings folded tight, a boy in armor that had clearly been built in a day when someone had asked *what would a dragon wear if it wanted to look like it had already won?* — Issei Hyoudou in Cardinal Crimson, the True Queen form, the integrated Triaina, the full red and gold regalia that the Marvel scientists had not yet been allowed to dissect.

The arena shook as Grendel moved.

It was fast. Genuinely, objectively fast. The kind of fast that should not have been available to something built out of meat and malice. It closed distance in what might have been three seconds, claws raking out at head-height, and the Avengers section leaned forward because they knew speed and this was *speed*.

Issei did not dodge.

He stepped *into* the strike instead, took the rake across his armor's chest guard — and in the moment Grendel's claws met Cardinal Crimson, the theatre heard a sound like breaking glass mixed with something deeper, something that the captions described as **Solid Impact Booster: Active**. The force rippled outward. Grendel skidded backward fifteen feet, and Issei was *there*, not pursuing — flowing. The Boosted Gear's voice crackled once, system-bright:

**[BOOST.]**

Doubled. Everything Issei was, doubled. The second punch that came in was not faster so much as it was more, more real, more present, more *consequence*. It was aimed at the beast's core, and the captions tracked the analysis: **Penetrate function engaged. Misdirection shielding bypassed. Primary armor integrity compromised seven percent.**

"He's using Penetrate on the opening move," Natasha said, low. "Why? He's not fighting for time. He's fighting for efficiency."

"Because Grendel adapts," T'Challa said quietly. He was watching the beast's reaction, the way its hide had already begun to thicken where the strike had landed. "Each second you waste, its defenses harden. He's solving for that math. Fast and hard and clean."

The fight — and it was a fight, not a slaughter — lasted seven minutes. The vision refused to compress it. Grendel was indeed adaptive; with each exchange, its bulk refined itself, learned angles, countered the patterns it was seeing. And Issei met every evolution of it with a different piece of his arsenal: Knight-form speed bursts, Rook-form concussive impacts, the Bishop-form channeling of pure energy that the machine's captions labeled **Dragon Blaster: Partial Output, Environmental Consideration Protocol Active**. What that meant, translated to Avengers-standard, was *he's fighting in a cavern that could collapse on civilians if he punches hard enough, so he's chosen to win slower instead of faster*.

The theatre watched him _choose_ to take more hits so that the ceiling would not fall.

When Penetrate finally found Grendel's core, the beast went down — not instantly, not in defeat, but in the kind of capitulation that only came when a living thing realized it had been outmaneuvered by someone working off a completely different game board. The captions read: **Grendel (High-Class) defeated after 349 exchanges. Estimated energy expenditure: 23% of Cardinal Crimson output ceiling. Collateral damage: zero (environmental shielding maintained).**

The vision cut.

Not to black, not to a break — directly to the next arena, and the next thing, and this one made three-quarters of the Avengers section make a collective sound that could have been a gasp or a prayer.

Euclid.

If Grendel was hunger, Euclid was order. Where Grendel was all reflex and feed, Euclid was architecture made hostile. It looked like nothing Marvel had ever built, and yet unmistakably like a *weapon* — layered, modular, with the clean lines of something designed by a committee of engineers who had decided suffering should be efficient. **Killer-Class Devil. Specialized in geometric defense and probabilistic warfare. Adapts by calculation rather than instinct. Opponent of ancient lineage in Issei's world. Known for defeating experienced fighters through systematic dismantlement of their capabilities. Subject engaged at age twenty, Cardinal Crimson Promotion stage.**

Issei versus a thing that had looked at the problem of fighting him and designed a solution set.

This fight was different from the first. There was no rushing. There was no Boost-and-close. The moment Euclid activated, the arena became a grid, and the captions showed Issei's armor lighting up with counter-logic, defensive geometry of its own, as if someone had said *yes, you think faster than a seventeen-year-old boy? Let me let you meet the seventeen-year-old boy's dragon.*

What followed was a strategic engagement that would have made military tacticians weep. Issei did not rely on speed here; he relied on *coverage*. He flooded the grid with Transfer-channeled power, distributed across fields and counterfields, used the Rook-form's Blaster in a way that choked Euclid's probabilistic calculations by flooding them with variables it had not anticipated. He was playing a different war than the one Euclid had designed.

Three minutes in, Euclid attempted what the captions labeled **Recursive Logic Cascade** — a feedback loop designed to break systems that could not think faster than it could calculate. And Issei, in response, did something the theatre had not seen him do before.

He *stopped* thinking.

The Boosted Gear's voice crackled again, but this time different, rougher, like someone had taken the machine-sound and made it sound like a prayer:

**[DRAGON'S BREATH. SURGE.**]**

And something changed. The armor went from managed to *burning*. It was like watching a chess match suddenly become music — the calculations still there, but underneath and beside them, not above. A rhythm. An instinct. The kind of fighting that could only come from a dragon and a boy who had spent enough time together that the line between partnership and fusion had gotten very, very thin.

Euclid lasted four more minutes. When Penetrate finally found the core and broke the recursive engine, Euclid shut down with the finality of a machine that had lost power, and the captions read: **Euclid (Killer-Class) defeated after 417 exchanges. Estimated energy expenditure: 31% of Cardinal Crimson output ceiling. Collateral damage: zero (geometric barriers maintained). Analysis: Opponent countered optimization with integration.**

The vision released them.

---

Nobody spoke first. The Avengers' section just sat in the aftermath of what it had just watched — which was not a teenager playing at being strong, but a teenager *in the actual act of* being strong, and choosing, in each moment, to spend that strength in ways that did not obliterate the world around him.

"Environmental shielding," Tony said finally, into the dark. "He's taking longer fights so the building doesn't fall on his own people. That's not something you program into a weapon. That's something you choose. Every. Single. Second."

"Nineteen and twenty years old," Steve said quietly. "And he's already that aware of collateral damage. Most soldiers take twenty years of training and combat experience to learn that arithmetic, if they ever do at all."

"The machine is showing us something," Wanda said. Her red magic was flickering faintly around her fingers — a tell that she was tracking what she had felt when the vision took them. "It's not just showing us his power, it's showing us his *discipline*. The way he uses it. The way he... stops using it, even when he could use more." She looked at Thor. "You have an opinion."

"I have several," Thor said. His armor was immaculate, had been immaculate at the start of the night, and he had not bothered to move since the vision released. "First: he is a warrior. The dragons I have known do not accept unworthy partners; the fact that Ddraig tolerates him is a statement, not a question. Second: he has mastered four distinct combat forms within his primary armor configuration — the Triaina specializations are not toys. They are disciplines, the way a master swordsman does not simply swing; he *thinks* with the blade."

"But does that make him an ally?" Natasha cut in. Her question had the sharp edge of someone who had been waiting for an opening. "Because yes, fine, he doesn't destroy the building. He also doesn't *need* to. His baseline power is so far above the threats in his own world that he can afford to be careful. On Earth, against Omega-level mutants or cosmic-tier beings, does the same calculation hold? Or does the calculus change when you actually *have* to use everything?"

"You're asking if restraint is a feature or a necessity," Bruce said. He'd been quiet, but his voice came with the weight of someone thinking through probability. "Right? If he fought someone that actually matched him, would the building still be standing?"

T'Challa raised his hand slightly, and the room quieted — not because he commanded it, but because he had not spoken yet, and everyone had learned to wait for the King of Wakanda's timing.

"The machine is not showing us his worst case," T'Challa said. "It is showing us his deliberate case. His chosen case. That is the only arena we can assess from. Speculation about scenarios where he must abandon restraint is not analysis; it is fear dressed as caution." He let that sit. "My question is different: Why is the Avengers' Tribunal being called at all? We have watched a boy hold environmental geometry in his armor while fighting ancient killers. We have watched Ravagers vote him *kin*. What does Marvel's formal judgment add, except delay?"

"Alliance status," Fury's voice said — not Fury in person, because Fury was dead and Marvel had voted him dead and was respecting the vote — but Fury's delegate, a woman named Maria Rambeau who had taken the liaison brief and wore it like a problem she'd already solved somewhere else. "The machine's rules. Arc Six is named 'Marvel Judges the Dragon.' That's not poetry. That's structure. The theatre's design says we get to *vote*. The question is what we're voting on — and that's where it gets complicated."

"Explain," Steve said.

"Ally means: we cooperate, we trust his judgment on major decisions, we accept that his authority is legitimate in his own sphere. Sovereign means: we acknowledge he is not accountable to our frameworks and will not try to make him so. Threat means: we initiate containment protocols and prepare defense positions." Rambeau met each of their eyes. "The vote in fifty minutes will ask the room to choose one. The Avengers need to show up unified, or we look fractured, and a fractured recommendation means the room takes longer to decide, and longer means more factions get air time, and more air time means the political side of this grinds into everything."

"So you're asking us to choose," Natasha said flatly.

"I'm asking you to decide what you actually believe," Rambeau corrected. "Before the vote is called. Tony, you're going to argue for monitoring. Steve, you're going to argue for trust. Thor will say he's a worthy warrior and deserve the chance to prove it. Black Widow will note the risk. Panther will say we need diplomacy before we settle anything. That's your real problem — you're going to splinter across four different answers, and the theatre doesn't care about nuance."

Steve stood. Paced. This was what he did when the calculus was impossible — he moved, as if his body might find an answer his mind could not. "You're asking me to pick one position and defend it against my own team."

"No," T'Challa said quietly. "I am asking why we are pretending this is our decision to make. He is not asking to be judged. The machine is deciding we get to judge. The question is whether we fulfill that function or refuse it."

"We can't refuse it," Tony said. "The machine's been clear — pull the lever or the lever pulls itself. We vote or we don't vote and the room votes without us. At least if we're in the room, we're not starting from behind."

"All right," Steve said. He stopped pacing. "We vote. We state our actual positions. And we accept that we might not agree, because four different questions got asked tonight by four different people, and they don't have one answer."

The screen chimed.

**INTERMISSION TIMER BEGINS: 47 MINUTES.**

**GACHA PULL: 2 OF 2.**

**"AVENGERS DEBATE" — RARITY: SECRET.**

"No," Tony breathed. "No, you don't get to pull our argument, that's — you can't record that, that's not — "

**THE MACHINE WILL RECORD WHAT THE MACHINE WILL RECORD,** said the screen, with the tone of something that had just been insulted by a particularly stupid fish. **THE SECOND GACHA CATEGORY IS CONSCIOUSNESS AND DECISION. THIS ROOM'S CONSCIOUSNESS REGARDING ISSEI HYOUDOU IS REQUIRED DATA. THE DEBATE WILL BE RECORDED AND WILL PERMIT THE THEATRE TO ASSESS YOUR REASONING. THIS IS NOT OPTIONAL. COMMENCE WHEN READY.**

"Well," Natasha said, and actually smiled, which was never a good sign. "I guess we're doing this live."

---

What followed took forty-three minutes and spiraled through every permutation of what it meant to trust something powerful.

Tony started. **"He's dangerous, and I don't care if he's also disciplined — dangerous plus disciplined is still dangerous, just dangerous in a direction you predict. We monitor. We build protocols. We do *not* give him blanket sovereign status because he made nice with pirates and can hit things accurately."**

Steve's response was immediate. **"He's seventeen. He's held absolute power and used it to build things instead of destroy them. He could have taken over his own world and he didn't. That tells me something about his actual goals, not just his power level."**

Thor cut in, his voice carrying the weight of actual experience. **"I have walked alongside dragons for eight centuries. I have seen them burn cities and hoard gold and covet power with the totality of their nature. This one — Ddraig chose to elevate a boy. A *boy*. Not a warrior-king, not a born noble, not someone who was already mighty. A schoolboy who died on a first date. That choice is the only thing I need to know about the dragon's judgment. The boy is secondary to what the dragon has become by binding to him."**

Natasha, because she had to be the one to push on the unexamined assumption, asked: **"And if Ddraig's judgment is wrong? Dragons are arrogant, Thor. They have been for a thousand years. They overestimate their partners. What if this looks good from the inside and we don't see the crack until it breaks?"**

T'Challa, finally, stood. **"Wakanda will not vote to contain him based on speculation. Wakanda will not vote to trust him based on sentiment. Wakanda votes to engage. We offer him the framework of diplomacy, the courtesy of actual negotiation, the respect of formal alliance or formal non-alliance — his choice, clearly stated. We do not guess. We do not assume. We ask. That is the Wakandan position."**

"And if he refuses?" Sam Wilson, the actual heart of the Avengers, asked quietly. **"If he says he doesn't want alliance with Marvel?"**

**"Then,"** T'Challa said, **"we respect that. We are not owed his allegiance. We are owed only his honesty about his intentions. If he provides that, the debate is settled. If he does not — if he obfuscates or equivocates — then we have our answer about whether to trust."**

The theatre sat with that. The Avengers sat with it. And above them, unremarked, the crack in the screen's upper quadrant — which had been still and silent since the machine announced the Secret pull — ticked forward one centimeter, one hairline branch, reaching further into the center of the screen.

Ddraig's voice came out of the darkness, rough and warm and entirely amused:

**"You debate whether to judge the thing that will stand before what you cannot. The walls argue about their own purpose. How cosmically thorough."**
