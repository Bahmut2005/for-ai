# Chapter Fifteen: Monster Hunter

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Blade • Elsa Bloodstone • Midnight Suns (Robbie Reyes, Magik, Nico Minoru) • Punisher

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Hunting Stray Devils" — **Rare**
2. "Killing a Dragon to Save Children" — **Super Rare**

---

The theatre had sections the way a city has neighborhoods, and by the fifteenth chapter everyone knew the map. The Avengers held the bright middle rows. The gods sat high and argued in weather. The villains had colonized the mezzanine, where the sightlines were good and the moral lighting was flattering.

And down at the very edge of the floor, nearest the screen, where the glow off the canon world lit faces from below like a campfire that showed things instead of warming them, sat the hunters.

They had never organized. They had simply *accreted*, the way people who work nights end up at the same diner. Blade at the aisle, sword across his knees like a seatbelt. Elsa Bloodstone one row back with her boots on the chair in front of her, which the theatre had stopped fighting her about somewhere around chapter six. The Midnight Suns scattered loosely behind — Robbie Reyes with his jacket collar up, Magik with one armored boot swinging, Nico Minoru taking notes in a book that took its own notes. And at the far end, in the seat with clear lines to two exits that did not exist, Frank Castle.

The previous evening had belonged to the Ravagers — a tiny boat with a dragon's destiny, and Rocket asking a question about riding Issei that half the theatre was still recovering from. Tonight the wheel came down without ceremony, spun once, hard, and dropped its token like a shell casing.

**PULL ONE: HUNTING STRAY DEVILS — RARE.**

"Rare," said Elsa Bloodstone, to no one. "Fifteen chapters of dead schoolboys and national dragons, and the job listing is *Rare.* Story of the industry, really. The work's never the headline."

"Quiet," said Blade, not unkindly. "It's a work night."

The screen opened on darkness that smelled — the theatre did this now, another new trick nobody had authorized — of rust and old rain.

---

An abandoned building on the edge of Kuoh Town. The kind of place every city keeps without meaning to: a factory or a hospital or both, windows like knocked-out teeth, a chain-link fence that had given up. On the cracked lot outside stood Rias Gremory's peerage, and among them, hands jammed in his pockets, trying to look like he did this all the time, Issei Hyoudou.

"A Devil who kills or abandons their master and runs," Rias was saying, in the tone of a teacher who is also a landlord and also, in a very real sense, a knife, "is called a Stray. The power we're given doesn't hold its shape without the contract that granted it. Cut the leash and the power keeps growing anyway — sideways. It eats the person who was holding it. What's left stops rationing itself. It hunts humans, because humans are easy, and it starts with the easy ones."

"Starts?" said Issei.

"Children," said Koneko, flatly, from his elbow. "It's been eating children."

The theatre's floor sections went very quiet, very fast.

"We're sanctioned to destroy it," Rias said. "Ordered to, actually — the Archduke posts them like bounties. Strays are the one job every faction agrees on. We protect the town, we get paid, and my family's name is worth a little more in the right rooms. Tonight you watch, Issei. Learn what the pieces mean."

What followed, the hunters at the front recognized within about nine seconds, was a *field certification exercise* — a senior team running a live hunt slow enough for the new hire to see the shapes of it.

The thing that came down the stairwell had been a woman once. The gacha, mercifully, did not linger on what it had become — it filmed in glimpses and shadows, the way a smart horror movie does: a torso where a torso should not attach, limbs with extra elbows, a mouth in the wrong hemisphere entirely, and a voice that still remembered how to sound like a person, which was the worst part, because it used that voice to talk about what it ate.

"Kiba," said Rias, and a blond boy became a line of moonlight. "My Knight. Speed, and blades." Two of the wrong limbs hit the floor.

"Koneko. My Rook." The small girl caught a descending claw the size of a door on one raised forearm, looked briefly bored, and hit the Stray so hard it changed rooms. "Strength, and defense that doesn't move."

"Akeno." The shrine maiden rose into the air with her palms crackling and an expression the theatre's teen-rated lighting handled by cutting quickly back to Issei's appalled face. "My Queen. All of it at once. She'll be a moment. She… enjoys her work."

"That one," said Elsa Bloodstone approvingly, over the sound of thunder, "would pass the Bloodstone interview at the handshake stage."

On-screen, when it was done — when the Stray lay broken in the wreck of the lobby, still hissing about hunger through its wrong mouth — Rias walked to it without hurry. And here the theatre saw the thing several of them would argue about for the rest of the night.

She asked it a question first.

"Any last words?"

Not for show. Not gloating. It was a door held open for exactly one second — long enough for anything left inside to walk through. What answered her was appetite in a stolen voice, and Rias nodded once, like a magistrate closing a file, and unmade it in a single sheet of crimson light. No trophy. No speech. She stood a moment over the scorch mark with her eyes shut — the theatre would have missed it if the gacha hadn't held the frame — and then it was over, and she was telling Issei about invoicing.

The screen shifted into montage: weeks, then months. Hunts by railway sidings and drowned basements and one memorably terrible parking structure. Issei getting better — first bait ("why is it ALWAYS me luring it out," he howled, sprinting from something with too many wings, and Elsa barked a laugh and said "because you're loud and expendable, it's a *classic* apprenticeship"), then flanker, then the one who put the Boost into Koneko's fists at the perfect moment. Civilians who never knew, walking out of memory-smoothed evenings. A small ledger of payments; Issei spending his first hunt money on dinner for his parents and lying about a part-time job.

And threaded through the montage, twice, the shape of the line.

The first time: a Stray that had barely turned, days out from its crime, still wearing most of its human face. It wept. It begged. It said *I didn't mean to* in a voice that cracked in the right places, and Issei — sixteen, soft-hearted, murdered once himself by someone who smiled at him — lowered the gauntlet and took a step forward, and the thing's weeping mouth unhinged into a bear trap and it nearly took his head off. Koneko's fist arrived where his skull had been. Afterward, shaking, he didn't say *I'll never hesitate again.* He said, "Next time I'll check from further away."

The second time: a hunt where the Stray's terrified human familiar — an actual person, a contracted man who'd fed it victims out of fear — stood between the peerage and the door with a kitchen knife. The theatre watched Issei, who had that same week punched through a reinforced barrier, disarm a middle-aged accountant with agonizing gentleness, sit on him, and yell for Rias to *please* handle the memory thing because the man wouldn't stop screaming. The man went home. He remembered nothing. His file, the gacha noted primly, was marked VICTIM, COERCED.

The screen paused between pulls, and the theatre lights came up on the hunters' section, where every professional killer of monsters on the floor was already leaning forward.

---

"Okay," said Blade. He stood, because Blade standing was how that section called itself to order. "Somebody upstairs finally pulled a chapter about the *job.*"

"The job," agreed Elsa. "Not the destiny, not the flag, not the tragic girlfriend. Contract, briefing, target, exits, invoice. I could weep. I shan't, but I could."

"Walk it through," said Robbie Reyes, quietly. He had his arms crossed, and the thing under his skin was paying attention too; you could tell by the way the shadows near him idled like an engine. "For the rows that don't do this. Why does it look like that? Why the — " he gestured — "the last words thing?"

"Because of what a Stray is," Blade said. He addressed the theatre generally, which he almost never did. "I've worked vampires my whole life, so let me translate. The person is *gone.* That's rule one and it's the whole rulebook. What's left is hunger wearing their memories, and it will use every one of those memories as a lure, because the lure is all they're for now. You heard the girl — the power eats the person, then it goes shopping. That thing tonight talked like a woman because talking like a woman *works.*" He turned and looked at the screen. "And the kid nearly died learning it, which is the only way anyone ever learns it. The mistake he made? Everybody makes it once. The ones who make it twice, we bury. He adjusted his *distance*, not his *heart.* That's — " Blade paused, choosing the word with care, because from him it was currency — "correct."

"The last words," said Elsa, boots coming off the chair, "are the part I'd frame and hang in the family manor, except my family manor is full of my father's taxidermy and his opinions, so possibly not. Listen — my father raised me hunting these things, and Ulysses Bloodstone's standing rule was *never talk to it, monsters lie, mouths are for trophies.* Efficient. Also corrosive. Do it his way for thirty years and you stop being able to tell your own species from the prey list, and I'd know, I've read his journals, it's why I drink." She nodded at the screen. "The Gremory girl asks the question knowing the answer will be teeth. She asks *anyway.* It costs her one second and it keeps her — and the boy watching her — on the right side of the line every single time, because the day something answers the question like a person is the day they'll stop, and they both know it. It's not sentiment. It's *calibration.* You test the blade every night so you notice the night it's wrong."

"It's a ritual," said Nico Minoru, pen stopped over her notebook. "That's literally witchcraft, what she's doing. A rule you perform so the rule stays real."

"Every hunter tradition has one," said Magik. Her voice came out with the faint echo it carried when Limbo leaned close to listen. "In Limbo, mine is that I remember being the thing I'm killing. Keeps the sword honest." She smiled, thin as her blade. "The devil children hunt tidily. I approve."

Through all of it, Frank Castle said nothing.

He had watched the montage the way he watched everything — like it was surveillance footage and someone he loved was in the background of it. Now, in the pause, Daredevil's voice carried from the street-level rows, gently baiting: "Nothing, Frank? A sixteen-year-old with a body count. I'd have bet money."

"Body count's monsters," said Frank.

"They were people once. Devils. Some of them were *humans* once, before the pieces. He kills them without a trial."

"No," said Frank, and stood up, because he was going to use his hands to point at the screen and that required standing. "Pay attention. Everybody watched the fights. I watched the *sort.*" He held up fingers, cataloguing. "Thing in the factory: gone, confirmed hostile, eating kids — killed clean, no show, no speech. Crying one: he checked. Almost died checking, so he changed procedure — he still *checks*, he just checks smart. The man with the knife — armed, obstructing, dangerous — and the kid takes a shoulder strike he didn't have to take, to bring him in soft. Man goes home to his family." Frank lowered his hand. "That's not a berserker and it's not a bleeding heart. That's target discrimination. He's running a cleaner ROE at sixteen than most departments I've seen run with lawyers in the room."

"High praise," said Daredevil.

"It's not praise yet." Frank sat back down, eyes on the screen. "Anybody can sort easy targets. Monster that looks like a monster — that's a gift, that's the job doing your thinking for you. I want to see what he does when the categories fight back. When it's got a face he likes." He settled into stillness. "Roll the next one."

The gacha, which since chapter thirteen had been listening — everyone knew it now, nobody liked it — obliged him almost instantly.

Which was when Deadpool arrived at the hunters' section carrying a duffel bag.

---

He'd had the duffel bag since chapter two. No one knew what was in it. He dropped it in the aisle next to Blade with the confidence of a man joining a carpool he has definitely been invited to.

"Team's all here! Okay. Pitch meeting. The kid hunts monsters for money, I hunt *everything* for money, this is *synergy*, this is the crossover the streaming service ordered. I've prepared a résumé." He had. It was laminated. Under SKILLS it said *unkillable, hilarious, owns own swords* and under REFERENCES it said *don't.* "All I need is for the magic bingo cage to beam me into Japan, or beam him out here, or — hear me out — beam us BOTH into a bottle episode—"

"No," said Blade.

"You're not the boss of the cage. Cage! CAGE." Deadpool addressed the gacha wheel directly, arms spread. "Buddy. Sweetie. You react to the audience now, everyone's talking about it. React to THIS: one (1) application for the position of Stray Devil Hunting Associate, submitted with love, by me."

The theatre had learned to dread the gacha's new responsiveness. This was the moment the dread paid rent. The wheel lit. The screen split, politely bookmarking the canon world, and displayed, in the cold system font it used for verdicts:

**APPLICATION RECEIVED: WILSON, W.**
**FORWARDING TO SUBJECT SIMULATION FOR ASSESSMENT.**

"See?" said Deadpool. "Process. I respect process."

The screen showed a rendering — clearly labeled SIMULATION, in the manner of chapter forty-things to come — of Issei Hyoudou being shown Deadpool's file. All of it. The theatre watched the boy read, in fast-forward, with his face doing a journey. At the end, simulated Issei looked up and said, with the specific firmness he otherwise reserved for defending Asia:

"No."

"He didn't even give a reason!" Deadpool howled.

**SUBJECT'S STATED REASON,** printed the screen, **QUOTE: "HE MAKES KILLING LOOK FUN. IT'S NOT SUPPOSED TO LOOK FUN. THE LITTLE ONES COPY WHAT LOOKS FUN." END QUOTE.**

The hunters' section made a low, collective sound, the sound an audience makes at a dart hitting a board dead center. Deadpool stood very still for a moment.

"…Okay, wow," he said, at reduced volume. "The teenager just performance-reviewed my whole bibliography."

**APPEAL?** offered the screen, with what everyone present chose to believe was not hope.

"No," said Deadpool, picking up his duffel bag with dignity. "No, he's — ugh — he's *right*, and I'm going to go sit with the villains, where being seen clearly is considered rude." He paused at the aisle's end and pointed back at the screen. "I'm keeping the laminate. And I'm starting a new list. He's on it."

"What list?" said Nico, unwisely.

"People Who Get It," said Deadpool, and went.

The wheel was already spinning again. It spun long, this time — long enough for the whole floor to go quiet, because they had learned that too. The token dropped like something falling into a well.

**PULL TWO: KILLING A DRAGON TO SAVE CHILDREN — SUPER RARE.**

Down at the front, Blade's hand found the grip of his sword — not drawing, just touching it, the way you touch a doorframe in a shaking house. In the gallery high above, Fin Fang Foom uncoiled by one degree. And the screen opened on rain.

---

The briefing came fast and wrong. The theatre could tell it was wrong because Rias's voice, on-screen, had lost its landlord calm.

A Stray, three towns over — outside Gremory territory, but the local jurisdiction had begged. This one was old, as Strays went. It had kept itself fed and quiet, and its power, growing sideways all that time, had grown *toward* something. Somewhere in its wandering it had found the barrow of a lesser dragon dead a thousand years, and it had eaten what it found there, heart and hoard and all — and the shape a Stray takes is the shape of what it eats.

"Partner," said Ddraig, and the gauntlet's voice was careful. "You should hear this from me before you see it. It will look like kin. It is not. It is a thief wearing a grave. But your blood will not know the difference at first sight, and I will not lie to you — neither, entirely, will mine."

It had taken three children from a school camping trip at the reservoir. Taken, not eaten. Dragons hoard, said Ddraig, low. Even stolen dragonhood hoards. It has put them somewhere it considers treasure, and it will keep them alive exactly as long as they are treasure, and no longer.

The nest was in a flooded quarry, in the drowned mouth of a maintenance tunnel, and the thing coiled around three small sleeping shapes was, the theatre had to admit, *beautiful.* The gacha did not glimpse-and-shadow this one. It showed it whole: red-black scales with the deep ember shine of a banked fire, wings folded like cathedral architecture, a long head with old, old eyes. After fourteen chapters, the audience knew what they were looking at. It looked like the memory-visions of Ddraig himself, rendered small and wrong — a tribute band of a god.

The peerage plan was artillery: Akeno from above, Rias from the ridge, overwhelm it away from the nest. The dragon-thing made the plan obsolete by scenting the air once and going *directly back to its hoard* — coiling tighter around the children, wings mantling over them like a vault door. Any barrage would go through it into them. Kiba's speed meant nothing in a flooded tunnel. Koneko couldn't hit what used children as chest armor.

And then it raised its head, and looked across the black water directly at Issei, and made a sound none of them had ever heard a Stray make.

It crooned.

"It smells me," said Issei. His voice came out strange. "Ddraig — it smells *you.*"

"Yes," said Ddraig.

"It's not attacking me."

"No. To its stolen blood, you are the Red Dragon Emperor. You are the closest thing to an elder it will ever meet. It is *presenting its hoard to you.*" A silence, on-screen and off, as the theatre understood one second before Issei said it aloud. "Partner. It will let you approach."

The theatre watched Issei Hyoudou walk on the surface tension of devil-magic across black water toward a dragon that was singing to him, and every hunter in the front row stopped breathing, because they all saw the shape of the only plan that saved three children, and they all knew what it was going to cost the person executing it.

**[BOOST.]**

Soft, the gem barely glowing. **[BOOST.]** Ten seconds. **[BOOST.]** The thing lowered its great head as he came, pupils going wide and warm, and nudged its wing aside a fraction — *look*, the gesture said, ancient and terrible and almost innocent, *look what I kept safe* — and showed him three children, alive, unhurt, curled in a bed of stolen coats and bicycle parts and one traffic mirror it must have liked the shine of.

"Hey," Issei whispered to it. His hand was on its snout. His voice was wrecked. "Yeah. You did good. You kept them safe."

**[BOOST.]**

"I'm sorry."

The children never woke. The theatre barely saw the shot — a single point-blank Dragon Shot angled *up and away* through the one line that touched nothing but the Stray, the angle he'd been walking three careful degrees at a time since the water's edge. The light filled the tunnel and was gone, and the thing that had been singing came apart into ember and ash without ever once knowing it had been hunted, and the last expression in its old eyes, the gacha held long enough for everyone to carry away with them, was trust.

Issei caught the biggest child before she touched the water.

---

He got them out. He was steady the whole time — steady handing them up the quarry wall to Kiba, steady while Rias smoothed the night out of their memories into a story about getting lost, steady right up until the peerage was gone ahead with the kids and he was alone at the waterline with a tunnel full of ash.

"Partner," said Ddraig.

"Don't," said Issei. "Not yet. Just — was any of it real? The dragon part. Or was I just — a really good lure. Like the crying one, like Blade-guy's rule, mouths are all lures now—" He stopped, sat down hard on the stone, and put his face in his hands, gauntlet and all.

The old voice took its time.

"Attend," said Ddraig at last, gently, in the tone the theatre had last heard address an island. "It was not a dragon. It was a hungry ghost in a dragon's stolen skin, and if you had hesitated it would have kept those children as treasure until it forgot why, and treasure that is forgotten gets *spent.* You did not betray kin tonight." A pause. "But you mourn as kin. That is not a flaw in you, partner. That is the tax of our blood, and I will tell you now, because you have earned the knowing: I have killed dragons. True ones, mad ones, friends gone wrong across ten thousand years. It has never once cost nothing. The day it costs nothing, the Red Dragon Emperor is just a very large Stray."

"…What do I do with it, then. The — " he gestured at his own chest — "this."

"What is done for any dragon. Fire is not left in the dark. Burn it skyward."

The theatre watched a sixteen-year-old boy stand in a flooded quarry at three in the morning and, following the murmured instructions of the oldest thing that loved him, send a column of red flame up out of the tunnel mouth into the rain — a funeral rite older than every nation whose representatives were watching — and stand at attention until it went out. He cried through the whole thing. He did not let it change the flame.

"It never even had a name," Issei said.

"Dragons name themselves by their fire, and it never got the chance to make its own. So remember the fire *you* gave it. That will do. That has always done."

The screen dimmed. The lights came up. And for the second chapter running, the theatre was silent.

Frank Castle stood.

He walked down the aisle to the front — the hunters made room; even the theatre made room, the floor-lights tracking him — and he pointed at the dark screen, and delivered, unasked, the after-action review.

"Sequence," he said. "Kids first — every choice bent around the kids from the first minute; the whole approach was one long firing solution that didn't exist until the kids were covered. Threat second — one shot, no showboat, no hesitation *at the moment*, and the moment's the only place hesitation kills. Himself third — he walked point-blank into a dragon's mouth on the theory it liked his smell. Feelings *last.* He locked it down till the kids were clear and the civilians were handled, and then he paid the bill in private, in full, with nobody watching." He looked around the theatre — at the moralists, at the mezzanine, at the gods. "That's the order. That is the *whole* order. I've spent my life watching people get it wrong in every direction — grieve first and bury kids, or skip the bill and turn into the thing in the tunnel. The kid's ledger balances." He turned to go, and stopped, because he was a fair man about exactly one thing, and added the asterisk. "He kills monsters that used to be people and spares people acting like monsters, and so far the world's let him have that line clean. It won't forever. One day the thing eating kids will have a pulse and a face and paperwork, and I'll want to see this chapter again. Till then—" and Frank Castle said the sentence the front row would quote for the rest of the run — "the kid hunts like the children are his and the monsters aren't. Approved."

He sat down. Blade raised two fingers off his sword hilt in salute — from him, a parade. Elsa Bloodstone said, hoarsely, "Field certification, Bloodstone standard, and my father can rotate in his shrine," and pulled her boots back up on the chair so nobody would look at her face. Robbie Reyes said quietly, to no one, "He used what he is to get close, and stayed what he is after. That's the trick. That's the whole trick," and the engine-shadow around him idled soft, almost peaceable. Magik proposed, seriously, that the boy would survive Limbo, which from her was a marriage offer of sorts; Nico wrote *the rule you perform so the rule stays real* and underlined it twice.

High above, Fin Fang Foom spoke once into the quiet: "The burning was done correctly." Nothing else. From him, a eulogy.

And in the mezzanine, Mephisto — who had laughed at the boy's death and delighted in his price — was not smiling at all, because incorruptibility is a rumor until you watch someone pay grief full price on time and in secret, and then it is a *problem.*

The lights had begun their end-of-chapter dim when the screen flickered once more — the gacha's now-familiar parting gift, next door's coming attraction. It showed a forge. Holy fire. And taking shape on the anvil, a long, pale blade whose purpose the system font spelled out over the embers with what could only be called timing:

**NEXT: THE FORGING OF ASCALON — A SWORD MADE FOR ONE PURPOSE: THE SLAYING OF DRAGONS.**

"Oh, that's *cruel,*" said Nico, into the dark.

The theatre went to black on the hunters' row, all of them still seated, none of them leaving first.

## Theatre Ledger

- **New Pulls This Chapter:** *Hunting Stray Devils* — Rare; *Killing a Dragon to Save Children* — Super Rare. The gacha also processed its first audience "application" (Wilson, W.) via subject simulation — establishing that the theatre can render simulated Issei responses to audience requests, a mechanism with obvious future implications.
- **Secrets Revealed:** Stray Devils — Devils who kill or abandon their masters — mutate into monsters as their unleashed power devours them, and hunting them is sanctioned, posted work (Archduke bounties) that every faction endorses; it is how Rias's peerage protects Kuoh, earns income, and builds the Gremory name. Issei's kill-line was mapped in operation: monsters that prey on innocents die fast and unceremonied; anything with a person left in it — coerced familiars, the barely-turned — gets checked for, at personal risk ("Next time I'll check from further away"). A dragon-shaped Stray (a devil that devoured a dead lesser dragon's heart and hoard) recognized Issei's blood, trusted him, and he used that trust for a point-blank kill to save three hoarded children — then performed his first dragon funeral rite ("burn it skyward") under Ddraig's instruction. Ddraig confirmed he has killed true dragons across his ages and that grief is "the tax of our blood."
- **Alliance / Opinion Shifts:** The hunters' bloc formally approves: Blade salutes his technique and his distance-not-heart correction; Elsa Bloodstone grants "field certification, Bloodstone standard" and pointedly contrasts Rias's "any last words" calibration ritual against her father's corrosive method; Magik and Robbie Reyes claim kinship with killing what shares your nature; Punisher delivers a full after-action approval of Issei's priority order (kids, threat, self, feelings — "the kid's ledger balances") while filing an explicit asterisk for the day the monster has a human face. Deadpool's application to join the hunts was refused by simulated Issei ("He makes killing look fun… the little ones copy what looks fun") — and Deadpool, stung into sincerity, conceded the point and added Issei to a new list: "People Who Get It." Mephisto has stopped smiling; incorruptibility is becoming a problem for him rather than a delight.
- **Unresolved Hooks:** Punisher's standing challenge — what happens when the thing eating children has a pulse, a face, and paperwork? (Arc Four will answer.) The gacha's simulation mechanism now takes audience requests — who applies next, and for what? The nameless dragon-Stray's funeral fire is remembered; Issei's kinship with dragons is now a wound as well as a strength. And the next chapter's tease lands like a knife: the boy who just mourned a dragon is about to be handed Ascalon — a holy sword forged for exactly one purpose.
