# Chapter Thirty-Nine: Who Owns a Miracle?

**Primary Reaction Groups:** S.H.I.E.L.D. • S.W.O.R.D. • X-Men • Doom • Wakanda • Eternals

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Debate Over Sharing Dragon Technology" — **Epic**
2. "Issei's Answer" — **Legendary**

---

The argument had started before the lights came up.

That was new. For thirty-eight chapters the theatre had waited on the machine — bickered, bet, grieved, converted, but always in response, always downstream of the wheel. Tonight, in the grey wash of the Arc Four banner, the machine descended into a room already shouting, because last night it had shown several thousand of the most acquisitive minds in a multiverse the inside of a dragon's treasure vault, and then turned the lights off.

"—not talking about the *swords*," Nick Fury was saying, on his feet in the S.H.I.E.L.D. block, coat thrown back, the flat carrying voice of a man who had run this exact meeting a hundred times in rooms with worse upholstery. "Keep the swords. I'm talking about the medicine. There was a *shelf* in that vault, Stark's scans caught it, rows of vials with a Sanskrit label, and the archive index called it a *panacea*. If that's real, it's not treasure. It's public health."

"It's an invasive species," said Abigail Brand, from the S.W.O.R.D. tier above him, boots on the rail, green hair like a biohazard flag. "You're welcome, by the way, for the only accurate sentence anyone's said in ten minutes."

"It's *his*," said Kitty Pryde, from the X-Men rows, with an edge the room had learned to respect.

"Everything is somebody's, right up until it matters," Fury said. "Then it's policy."

And the screen, which had been listening — the theatre was certain by now that it listened — printed, with what could only be described as relish:

**MOTION HEARD. THE HOUSE WILL NOW REVIEW PRECEDENT.**

**GACHA PULL: 1 OF 2.**

The wheel spun through its colors and stopped on deep violet.

**"DEBATE OVER SHARING DRAGON TECHNOLOGY" — RARITY: EPIC.**

"It's showing us a *committee meeting*," Rocket said, betrayed, from the Ravager block. "Last night, infinity swords. Tonight, C-SPAN."

The theatre took a breath, and swallowed them.

---

A chamber like the inside of a struck bell: the Grand Conclave of the Alliance of Mythologies, the same body the theatre had watched Issei accidentally insult into peace two nights ago. Tiered seats of devil nobility, Grigori researchers, Heaven's seraph-delegates, Norse and Hindu and Yokai envoys, and — newer, stiffer, visibly overwhelmed — human observers in ordinary suits.

On the floor of the chamber, on an evidence table, sat three items. A single crimson-lacquered vial. A sheaf of schematics stamped with the seal of Ajuka Beelzebub's research division. And a sword that hummed like a struck tuning fork even in its case.

The vision gave the theatre the docket the way it gave them names, directly into the mind: *Item one: simplified Amrita, a stabilized nectar-derivative healing draught developed from divine pharmacology and dragon-refined by the Sekiryuutei's household; in field trials it has closed wounds no phoenix tear could touch and arrested two plagues. Item two: partial schematics toward externalizing the Boosted Gear's Transfer function — power-sharing as infrastructure. Item three: a dragon-forged blade, one of many, from the vault.*

*The motion before the Conclave: whether such assets may remain in private hands.*

And then the debate — and the theatre, braced for boredom, found itself leaning in, because the machine had chosen the speeches with a curator's cruelty.

A Yokai envoy from a plague-struck northern territory, voice cracking: "I am not here to philosophize. I buried forty children last winter. This spring the dragon's wife walked into our sick-house with a crate of those vials and asked for nothing, and I buried none. I am told I should be *concerned* about the precedent. I am concerned about *April*."

An old-guard devil noble, dry as a ledger: "And when the boy dies? Or turns? Or simply *tires*? Charity is weather. Institutions are climate. The Conclave must hold the formula, or the formula holds us."

A seraph, gently, terribly: "We held miracles centrally once. The system worked flawlessly until the administrator died, and then we lied about it for two thousand years rather than share the keys. I speak for the faction with the most experience hoarding grace, and I am telling this body: the vault does not keep the miracle safe. The vault *becomes* the miracle. Then men fight for the vault."

A human observer, standing, hands flat on the table like a man steadying himself on a cliff: "Six months ago my government did not know any of you existed. Now I learn there is a schoolboy — forgive me, a *dragon* — holding a cure for everything, distribution decided by whether he *likes you*. You are all debating theology. I am asking a smaller question. What do I tell the mother in the hospital corridor about why her hospital isn't on his list?"

Silence in the chamber. Silence in the theatre.

The security hard-liner spoke last — a devil in Conclave grey, reasonable, weary, the most dangerous voice in any such room. "Then the motion is simple. Registration of all dragon-forged assets. Licensed replication of the draught. Compulsory escrow of the Transfer schematics with this body. Not punishment — *stewardship*. No private citizen may hold a stockpile of miracles. Draft the requisition order. Send it to the Hyoudou residence."

The vision held on the word *requisition*, and let it curdle.

---

The theatre came back to itself already divided, and the division did not run along any line it had drawn before.

"Draft the requisition order," Emma Frost repeated, softly, from the X-Men block, and the temperature around her dropped in a way that had nothing to do with diamond. "Do you know, I've heard that sentence before. Nearly word for word. It had *mutant* in it instead of *miracle*, and it was also drafted by tired, reasonable men, and it was also called stewardship." She crossed her legs. "I've been the asset on the evidence table, darlings. Let me save everyone some time: the greater good has an accounting problem. It is always calculated by the people doing the taking."

"And I've stood in the hospital corridor," Fury said, without heat, which made it worse. "Frost, I hear you. I ran the registration files; I know exactly what I sound like. But you're arguing about who he is, and I'm arguing about what happens at scale. Trust is a beautiful distribution system for one village. It does not *scale*. He can't personally vet eight billion people, so somewhere a line gets drawn around who he happens to know, and everyone outside that line is dying of geography. You don't have to be a tyrant to find that unacceptable. You just have to be the one holding the corridor's phone number."

"Then the failure is the line, not the boy," T'Challa said.

Heads turned. The king of Wakanda had been quiet all night, and beside him Shuri had gone uncharacteristically still, because the Wakandan block was watching a mirror and knew it.

"For centuries my country held the vibranium mound and every miracle we grew from it," T'Challa said. "We told ourselves withholding was wisdom. From inside, it *was* wisdom — every projection said the world would weaponize what we shared, and the projections were correct. They are still correct. We opened our doors anyway, because a man came to us and showed us the cost of the alternative in his own body." He looked at the frozen chamber on the screen. "Both of my ancestors' arguments are in that room. I notice the boy's answer is in neither. He does not withhold, and he does not surrender. He *gives*, one hand to another hand. It is the slowest possible method. It is also the only one that transmits the miracle *and* the trust, and my family learned at terrible cost which of the two runs out first."

"It's pretty, cousin," Shuri murmured, "but the corridor is still full."

From the villains' dark, Doom had risen — not to the floor, never to the floor, but to his full height in the aisle that was always somehow his.

"Doom will state what the sentimental cannot." The voice came level and metallic and certain. "The draught is not the issue. The *schematics* are the issue. Transfer — power itself made a grid, strength redistributed like water — that is not medicine, that is the architecture of a new civilization, and the question of who holds it is the only political question that has ever existed. This Conclave of theirs is correct that it cannot remain in private hands. They are merely wrong about whose hands." A pause, entirely unembarrassed. "In the boy's grip it is a lottery ticket. In Doom's it would be *policy*: no famine, no plague, no war Doom did not permit. You recoil. Yet every state in this theatre is built on exactly one act — the confiscation of dangerous power from private men — and you call it *law* when it is old enough."

"He's not wrong," Brand said, and raised a hand at the noise. "Stow it — I said *not wrong*, I didn't say *right*. My whole job is quarantine. Something world-changing crosses a border, I don't ask if it's good, I ask what it does to an ecosystem that didn't evolve with it. Amrita loose on one world means lifespans double in a decade — pension systems die, then economies, then somebody fights a war over the supply chain of *mercy*. That's not a reason to bury it. It's a reason nobody in this room gets to be smug, because sharing it is an extinction-level event for the world that exists, and so is hoarding it, just slower."

And then a voice from the Eternals' row, which had watched ten thousand years of this argument and rarely bothered to join it.

"I built them a plow once," Phastos said.

He said it conversationally, and the theatre quieted anyway, because there was a weight under it like a hand on the back of the neck.

"A plow. A nudge. I loved them — I love them still — and I couldn't stand watching them dig with sticks, so I gave. Freely. With trust. Exactly the way the boy's defenders are recommending." He looked at his own hands. "The plow became the wheel became the engine became a light over Hiroshima brighter than anything I ever showed them, and I stood in the ash and swore off giving. So take it from the one being here who has actually run the experiment: *freely given* doesn't sanctify anything. The gift forgets the giver in two generations. Whatever you decide about his miracles, you are not deciding for him, or for you. You're deciding for whoever is standing next to them in seventy years." He sat back. "I still build, by the way. I married, I have a son, I fixed the neighbor's mower last spring. I'm not telling you the answer is *never give*. I'm telling you there is no version of this where anyone's hands stay clean, so pick whose they are on purpose."

Into the silence after that, the screen chimed, almost politely.

**PRECEDENT REVIEWED. THE HOUSE WILL NOW HEAR FROM THE ASSET.**

**GACHA PULL: 2 OF 2.**

Gold flooded the wheel.

**"ISSEI'S ANSWER" — RARITY: LEGENDARY.**

---

The Conclave chamber again, days later. The requisition order lay on the evidence table where the vial had been, and Issei Hyoudou stood over it in his school blazer — he had come straight from class; the theatre could see the eraser dust on his sleeve — reading it with his lips moving slightly, at the speed of a boy for whom paperwork had always been enemy terrain.

**[Partner,]** rumbled the gauntlet, low, for his ears and several thousand eavesdroppers, **[the word you are stuck on is 'compulsory.']**

"Yeah," Issei said. "That's the one I thought it was."

He put the paper down. He looked around the bell-shaped room at the tiered faces — the weary hard-liner, the ledger-dry noble, the human observer with the corridor behind his eyes — and the theatre braced for the blunt-force speech they'd watched him give at the summit, and did not get it. He spoke quietly. It was worse.

"Everything I have, someone gave me," he said. "My power came from a dragon who got stuffed in my arm without anyone asking *him* — and he stayed anyway, and now it's ours, not mine. My life came from a girl who spent everything in her hand on a dead pervert she barely knew, and nobody made her, and that's the only reason there's anybody standing here for you to requisition. My sword was a gift. My house was a gift. I know exactly what I am, and what I am is *given*." He tapped the crimson vial's empty spot on the table. "So ask me. That's the whole procedure. The northern territory asked, and their crates go out on the first of the month, forever, free, and if the shipment's ever late it's because I'm dead. Azazel asked for the Transfer research and he's had it for a year, because I'd trust that shady old crow with my life and have. Ask me, and mean it, and it's yours — and here." He slid a folder across the table to the human observer, who stared at it. "Amrita production protocol. For the corridor lady. No price. You asked the room a good question and nobody answered you, so — yeah."

Then he looked at the hard-liner, and the boyishness went out of his face like a tide.

"But nobody *takes*," Issei said. "Not because it's my property. I don't actually care about property; you've seen my vault, half of it's junk I keep because a friend touched it. Because the day you can take a miracle with a form, every miracle after that one arrives with soldiers, and everyone who might have asked learns to hide instead, and then you're not distributing trust anymore, you're mining it, and it doesn't grow back." He picked up the requisition order, and the theatre heard the paper's edges char faintly between his fingers. "A miracle you take stops working. Oh, the *magic* still works. The part that made it worth having doesn't. I got handed my first miracle by somebody who wanted something from me, once. On a first date. I know the difference in my chest, where the spear went." He set the paper down, singed but whole, deliberate. "Motion denied. Lunch is on me, though. I mean it. All of you. There's a place near the station."

**[The Conclave,]** Ddraig noted, to the theatre at large, with ancient satisfaction, **[went to lunch.]**

---

The vision released them, and for once no faction erupted, because every faction had won a piece and lost a piece and knew it.

"He gave the human the folder," Fury said finally. "Unprompted. Which means his line isn't where I said it was." A long breath through his nose. "Doesn't scale. Still doesn't scale. But I've run assets my whole life, and I'll say this into the record: I have never once gotten out of a man voluntarily what that room almost took by force and lost."

"Motion denied," Emma said, savoring it like a vintage. "The miracle votes."

"The miracle is *seventeen*," Brand said. "Ask me again in seventy years, when the folder's grandchildren are pointed at each other."

"So we are agreed," Doom said, into the dark, "that no one in this theatre would have his answer — and that no one has a better one." No one, notably including Doom, disputed it.

Above them all the wheel rose, ticking — and as it went, one segment that had sat greyed-out and dead since the arc began, a segment the color of dried blood, flickered. Warmed. Went the deep, breathing crimson of a crown.

**QUERY LOGGED: WHO OWNS A MIRACLE,** printed the screen, small, as the lights went down. **INSUFFICIENT DATA. RE-ASK WHEN YOU HAVE SEEN WHAT ONE COSTS.**

**NEXT BANNER: CRIMSON.**
