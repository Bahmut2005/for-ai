# Chapter Thirty-Eight: The Dragon's Treasury

**Primary Reaction Groups:** The Collector • The Grandmaster • The Ravagers • Tony Stark • Rocket

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Archive of Dragon-Forged Weapons" — **Legendary**
2. "Ryuuteimaru's Armory" — **Epic**

---

By the middle of the steel-grey arc, the theatre had developed an economy, and like all economies it had a bad neighborhood.

It occupied the right-hand gallery, three tiers up, and it had assembled itself the way mold assembles: nobody had decided anything, and yet there it was. Taneleer Tivan, the Collector, sat at its center in a seat that had somehow become a booth, upholstered in a leather no one asked about, surrounded by floating catalogue-slates on which he had spent thirty-seven chapters appraising everything the screen showed him. Two seats over lounged En Dwi Gast, the Grandmaster, gold-robed and delighted by absolutely everything, keeping odds. Below them, in rows that smelled faithfully of engine grease and bad decisions, sat the Ravagers — Stakar Ogord upright and disapproving, Yondu Udonta with his boots on the seat back, Kraglin between them like a comma — and, at the end of the row, vibrating faintly, Rocket.

Rocket had been vibrating since the slave-market chapter. The raid had earned Issei the Ravagers' rarest currency — an unamused nod from Stakar — but it had also shown the boy casually snapping a slaver's vault off its hinges, and Rocket had looked at that vault the way other beings look at sunsets. He had a list now. The list was titled, in Rocket's own handwriting, *STUFF THE DRAGON KID DEFINITELY DOESN'T NEED ALL OF.*

Tony Stark did not sit in the bad neighborhood. Tony Stark sat one aisle below it, at the precise distance Doom had pioneered — *I am not with these people; I can hear every word these people say* — because after the summit chapter he had questions about logistics. A boy who armed villages, refit fleets, and out-shouted pantheons had to keep his materiel *somewhere*, and thirty-eight chapters had taught Stark that the machine heard wanting.

The machine heard wanting.

The house lights sank to a color the theatre had never seen it wear: not forge-orange, not storm-grey, but the deep, felted, climate-controlled gold of a bank the public never enters. The gacha wheel descended through it like a chandelier with opinions, spun, and chimed — and the chime, tonight, was the sound of a very large key in a very old lock.

**PULL ONE: "ARCHIVE OF DRAGON-FORGED WEAPONS" — LEGENDARY.**

The Collector rose from his booth so smoothly it looked rehearsed.

"At last," said Taneleer Tivan, "*inventory*."

---

The vision opened on the Hyoudou residence — and went down.

Past the kitchen with its treaty-length table. Past the six known basement floors, which the theatre had toured in the family arc. Past a door of ordinary wood bearing a hand-lettered sign — *ISSEI'S STUFF. KNOCK FIRST. YES, THIS MEANS YOU, KUROKA* — and then through a fold in space that made Stark's jaw tighten, because the door was mundane and what it opened onto was not.

A pocket dimension, warm as an attic in summer. And in it, rank upon rank into the golden distance: the treasury.

It was not a hoard. The theatre understood that within seconds, and the understanding rearranged the whole room, because everyone had been braced for dragon clichés — coins, gems, a mountain of glitter with a lizard on it. Instead they got *shelves*. Racks and cases and plinths, labeled in handwriting that started diligent and collapsed into scrawl, arranged not by value but by — the aisles took a moment to parse — by *story*.

A rack of holy swords, and at its head, home between wars, Ascalon — and beside it a second blade of the same lineage, leaner, newer, labeled *ASCALON II*, and beyond that, further down the rack, more sword-shaped silhouettes greyed out into static, their labels unreadable, the gacha's cold font stamped across them: **NOT YET PULLED.**

"The *First* Ascalon," breathed Shuri, from the Workshop Quarter, twenty-two chapters of suspicion paying off at once. "I *said* the title was load-bearing."

A sealed reliquary case holding shards of broken holy steel, light still moving inside them like fish, labeled: *EXCALIBUR FRAGMENTS — RECOVERED FROM KOKABIEL'S THEFT. HELD IN TRUST. TECHNICALLY IRINA'S. DO NOT TOUCH. SERIOUSLY. SHE COUNTS THEM.*

An empty plinth, lovingly dusted, bearing only a card: *REPLICA MJOLNIR — RETURNED WITH THANKS. GOOD HAMMER.* (In the high tiers, Thor made a sound like a cathedral being happy.)

And then the working aisles. Dragon-forged gear in ranks: blades quenched in Boosted aura, armor plates grown from shed Scale Mail and hammered flat, spears and gauntlets and shield-bosses that Eitri, rising slowly from his three seats, identified one by one in a voice that kept losing altitude. Weapons Issei had forged for his peerage. Weapons forged for allied villages, pattern-marked for farmers' hands. Weapons taken from dead evil dragons and *retired* — racked with their edges sheathed in blessed wax, labeled with the name of the dragon and one line each: *Fought well. Chose wrong.* Trophies he had refused after wars, mailed to him anyway by grateful pantheons, stacked unopened in a corner like awkward birthday gifts.

**"Understand what you are seeing,"** said Ddraig's voice over the vision, dry and old and unmistakably proud. **"Dragons hoard. It is the oldest law of us. Gold, grudges, territory — a dragon's hoard is a dragon's self-portrait. Twenty thousand years I waited to see what my partner would pile up."** A pause with a shrug in it. **"He hoards receipts. Every object in this vault is a promise somebody kept. I have given up being embarrassed. It turns out this is what a hoard looks like when the dragon is rich in the only thing he ever wanted more of."**

The vision drifted down the last aisle — and, at the very back, panned past a small case, child-height, its contents unreadable, its edges shivering with a static the theatre knew now, and hated. The vision did not slow. The vision, if anything, sped up, and the front row of eight women leaned forward as one and got nothing.

The frame settled instead, wide and golden, on the whole treasury at once, and the gacha stamped its rotating Legendary seal and editorialized in letters of vault-light:

**ASSESSED VALUE: INCALCULABLE. INSURED VALUE: ZERO.**

**NOTHING HERE WAS TAKEN. THEREFORE NOTHING HERE CAN BE.**

---

The lights came up on pandemonium, and the pandemonium had factions.

"Item the first." The Collector was already in the open floor, catalogue-slates orbiting him like moons, addressing the front row with the courtly hunger of a man who had bought planets before breakfast. "I am prepared to offer, for the reliquary of Excalibur shards *alone*: a Kree Soul-Forge, functional; the last egg of a species I am assured was magnificent; two minor Infinity artifacts, provenance negotiable; and my personal assurance of climate-controlled display conditions superior to — forgive me — a *suburban basement*—"

"The basement," said Rias Gremory, without turning around, "is warded by a Norse rune-master, a nekoshou sage, and the Crimson-Haired Ruin Princess, and it has never once tried to put my husband in a display case. Sit down, merchant."

"Everything is for sale, madam. The seller merely hasn't met the price—"

"I have destroyed prices," said Rias, and let one coil of crimson-black Power of Destruction lift off her shoulder and eat, with a sound like the end of an argument, a decorative flourish off the nearest catalogue-slate. The Collector looked at the smoking slate. He revised, visibly, several appraisals, one of which was of Rias.

"Or — or — hear me out, hear me out." The Grandmaster had flowed down beside Tivan, hands sketching arenas in the air, blue jaw bright with delight. "Nobody *sells* anything, that's so — Taneleer, honestly, it's so *transactional*, it's *beneath* the moment. We make it a *game*. A tournament! Open brackets, all-comers, and the *prize* — " he framed the frozen treasury on the screen between his thumbs — "is one item of the winner's choice from the dragon's little gift shop. I've hosted four hundred thousand years of games, trust me, wars have started for *worse* prizes—"

"The last man who treated my husband's swords as prizes," said Xenovia Quarta, and she did turn around, with the serene, unblinking directness that had frightened the theatre since the crow story, "was a fallen angel war criminal named Kokabiel, and my husband beat him unconscious, and then a man with white wings put him in a place where he will never be bored again. I carry Durandal. Durandal is not in that vault, because Durandal does not rest. If a bracket forms, I will enter the bracket, I will win the bracket, and as my prize I will select *you*." A pause. "I am told I lack a sense of humor, so to be clear: that was not a joke. I checked."

The Grandmaster's hands lowered slowly, framing nothing. "I like her," he announced, to the room, in a slightly higher register than usual. "Terrifying. Great energy. No tournament."

And below all of it, in the Ravager rows, Rocket Raccoon had gone somewhere else entirely.

He wasn't heckling. That was the alarming part; Kraglin kept glancing over the way you check a pot that's stopped bubbling. Rocket sat hunched, eyes glassy with love, fingers twitching through invisible schematics, narrating at a volume he genuinely believed was internal: "—okay, okay, pocket dimension, fine, doors are a *suggestion*, you go in through the *fold*, you'd need a phase-discriminator and a really stupid amount of nerve, gravity's assist not enemy, wards read *intent* so you send in someone with no intent, hi, hello, that's a raccoon with a lobotomy setting, I *have* one — the fragments are portable, the second sword's portable, the wax dragon-swords are basically *pre-wrapped*—"

"Rat." Yondu had not moved his boots off the seat back, but his voice came down like a wrench dropped from orbit. "You seein' the same show I'm seein'? Kid ripped a slave fortress open like a ration pack. His wife over there just ate a *price*. And your plan is *rob his house*."

"It's not robbing, it's — *redistributing*, he's got *duplicates*, Yondu, he has literal spare holy swords, that's not a hoard, that's a *sample sale*—"

"Boy keeps his war trophies in *wax*," said Stakar Ogord, and his tone shut both of them up, because Stakar had spent the arc grading Issei against the Code and this was the sound of a final mark going in a book. "Labels the dead. Refuses the tribute and keeps it *sealed* so no one else takes it either. That ain't a treasure room, gentlemen. That's the tidiest weapons decommissioning program I ever saw run by a teenager. Hundred Ravager crews couldn't hold discipline like that over one payday." He tipped his head back toward the screen. "You don't steal from that, Rocket. You *salute* it. Or you find out."

"I can hold *both* positions," muttered Rocket, still sketching.

Stark had come down to the rail, arms folded, uncharacteristically quiet, and when he finally spoke it was to Tivan, almost gently, the way you break news. "You're miscataloguing it, by the way. Your slates are pricing a collection. It's not a collection." He nodded at the frozen gold. "Collections are things you take *out* of the world so you can have them. Every rack in there points back *into* the world — who it was for, who it came from, what it cost, who counts the fragments. He's not collecting weapons." A beat, and Stark, who had built and buried an armory of his own once, heard his own voice go strange. "He's keeping *minutes*. It's not a vault. It's the world's most heavily armed scrapbook."

The Collector stood very still among his orbiting slates. "Everything I own," he said at last, with the first unguarded sentence of his captivity, "screams. Beautifully, but it screams. His inventory *purrs*. I confess I did not know that was a feature one could acquire." He returned to his booth and began, quietly, re-appraising nothing at all.

---

The wheel spun again while Rocket was still mid-schematic, and the second chime was not a key in a lock. It was a boat bell.

**PULL TWO: "RYUUTEIMARU'S ARMORY" — EPIC.**

"Ryuu-tei-maru," sounded out Kraglin. "That a sword?"

The vision answered: a forest of fog and familiar-light, years ago, and a boy on his knees at the edge of a pond, being introduced by a top-hatted familiar-master to the least impressive legendary creature in creation — a boat. A tiny one. A palm-sized, slightly dented, faintly glowing boat, paddling in a circle.

**LEGENDARY-CLASS FAMILIAR: SKÍÐBLAÐNIR-LINE LIVING SHIP,** captioned the screen, and the entire Asgardian section stood up at the name while the rest of the theatre was still deciding whether to laugh. **GROWS BY FEEDING ON ITS MASTER'S AURA. GROWTH CEILING: UNKNOWN.**

And the vision showed the feeding. Years of it, compressed: Issei, after every battle, every training collapse, every triumph, sitting exhausted on a dock in the familiar forest with the little boat in his lap like a cat — **[BOOST.]** **[BOOST.]** **[TRANSFER.]** — pouring surplus dragon-power into the hull, chattering at it about his day. The boat drinking. The boat *growing*. Palm-sized to canoe. Canoe to cutter. Cutter to a sleek dragon-prowed ship the size of a house, then a hall, then a hillside — and inside it, the pull's namesake: the armory, which no shipwright had installed, because the ship had *grown* it. Ballista-spines budded from the keel like teeth coming in. Blaster-mounts flowered along the gunwales. A hangar deck folded itself into existence the way a lung inflates. The ship built weapons the way a body builds bone: out of love, from the inside, to protect what fed it.

"It's not a vehicle," said Stark, at the rail, very quietly. "It's a *pet* that decided to become an aircraft carrier."

**"He talks to it,"** Ddraig confirmed, resigned and fond. **"Every night, for years. It has never once answered. It is the best listener in his life, and given his life, the competition was fierce."**

The vision ended on the ship at full glory, sails of light, waiting in fold-space like a hound at a door — and then the gacha did its museum trick. Light stabbed down into the open floor, and out of it rose not a plinth but a *pool*, silver and shallow, and on the pool, paddling in a proud small circle: an echo of Ryuuteimaru. Palm-sized. Slightly dented. The bell on its prow went *ting*.

**EPIC BONUS: EXHIBIT IS AN ECHO. ECHO IS FAITHFUL. DO NOT FEED THE EXHIBIT.**

**THE MANAGEMENT KNOWS EXACTLY WHO NEEDS TO HEAR THAT.**

"Why's it looking at *me*," said Rocket.

But someone was already moving. Groot unfolded from the Guardians' row and came down the tiers with the unhurried patience of a season changing, and knelt at the pool's edge, vast and mossy, and the tiny boat stopped circling and came about to face him.

The theatre went quiet without being asked.

"I am Groot," said Groot.

The boat's light rippled. It bumped, very gently, against the shore of the pool, against the bark of his hand — once, twice — the way a cat decides about a stranger. Two beings who had spent their whole lives being translated by other people regarded one another across the silver water. One had a vocabulary of three words. One had a bell.

*Ting,* said Ryuuteimaru.

"I am Groot," Groot agreed.

No caption arrived. For once the machine, which itemized everything, offered no translation, and the room understood that this was the translation: that some conversations are between a tree that grew into a family and a boat that grew into a fortress, and both of them had done it the same way — by being fed, faithfully, by someone who talked to them at night — and it was nobody else's business. Groot touched one finger to the tiny prow, and a sprig of green unfurled from his fingertip and rooted there, a leaf no bigger than a coin, and the boat's glow took on the faintest ring of spring.

Then it sailed one last circle — listing slightly to port, where Rocket sat — and stopped, and pointed its prow at him, and simply *looked*, in the way that only a boat with no eyes can look.

"Okay," said Rocket, ears flat, to a dinghy, in front of the entire multiverse. "Okay! Fine. Message received. It was a *thought experiment*." A pause. "Mostly."

"I am Groot," said Groot, standing, which everyone in earshot correctly understood to mean *he had four hundred contingencies and I will be telling the ship all of them.*

---

The pool sank. The lights began their end-of-chapter dim, and the screen offered its parting shot over the frozen image of the golden aisles — the racks, the receipts, the greyed-out swords stamped **NOT YET PULLED**, the small static-edged case at the back that no one had been allowed to see.

**YOU HAVE NOW SEEN WHAT THE DRAGON KEEPS.**

**ONE OF YOU PRICED IT. ONE OF YOU GAMED IT. ONE OF YOU PLANNED FOR IT.** — and here, to a roar of laughter and one string of Ravager profanity, a copy of Rocket's own handwriting appeared on screen, annotated in the machine's cold font: **PLAN RECEIVED. FILED UNDER: SIMULATION — PENDING.**

**NONE OF YOU ASKED THE ONLY QUESTION THAT MATTERS.**

The letters held, then rearranged.

**NEXT: WHO OWNS A MIRACLE?**

In the booth, the Collector steepled his fingers over his darkened slates and, for the first time in thirty-eight chapters, wanted something he could not name a price for. In the front row, Xenovia asked Rias, in a whisper the whole theatre heard, whether "I will select you" had been too subtle. And at the end of the Ravager row, Rocket Raccoon looked up at the words *SIMULATION — PENDING* with his heart in his throat and his list in his pocket, torn precisely between terror and hope, which — as Yondu observed to Kraglin, boots still up, grinning like a knife — was the most Ravager the little idiot had ever looked.
