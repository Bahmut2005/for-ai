## THEATRE LEDGER — SESSION 75

**ENTRY 1: THE WEIGHT AFTER THE WEIGHT**

The theatre had watched Rias talk him down. It had not yet watched what came after.

**[GACHA SYSTEM: PULL 1 — "THE DAYS AFTER" — RARE — ACTIVATING]**

The vision opened three days later. Issei hadn't left the Occult Research Club's back room since the safehouse. He wasn't in danger—Rias had made certain of that—but he also wasn't entirely present, either, moving through routines like a man checking that his own hands still belonged to him.

Asia, healed and stubborn, refused to leave his side, though she'd stopped trying to talk him through it. She'd learned, apparently, that some silences needed sitting in rather than filling.

Akeno brought tea he didn't drink. Xenovia sparred with him at half-speed, letting him win points he hadn't earned, watching his reactions more than his form. Irina prayed, unsubtly, in the doorway where he could hear her if he wanted to and ignore her if he didn't. Koneko simply sat against his side for an hour at a stretch, saying nothing, her presence a small unmovable fact.

"He doesn't need us to fix it," Rias said quietly, to Akeno, in the hallway outside. "He needs to know it didn't cost him us."

In the theatre, Natasha Romanoff watched this with an expression the room had learned to associate with her rawest observations. "This is the part nobody trains you for," she said. "Not the rescue. The unglamorous weeks after, where someone has to just keep showing up with tea nobody drinks."

Wolverine grunted agreement. "Anybody can pull a man back from the edge once. Question's whether they're still there in week three, when he's not screaming anymore, just quiet in a way that's worse."

Steve Rogers watched Asia settle beside Issei without a word, for the fourth time that hour by the theatre's count. "That's not weakness," he said. "That's discipline. Choosing to stay is a decision, made over and over, long after the crisis makes headlines."

**ENTRY 2: WHAT KONEKO SAID**

On the fourth day, Koneko finally spoke, in the middle of one of their silences, without preamble.

"You're allowed to be scared of what you did," she said. "But you don't get to disappear from us because you're scared of it. That's not fair to the people who stayed."

Issei's hands, wrapped around a cup of tea he still wasn't drinking, tightened.

"I didn't ask you to stay," he said.

"No," Koneko agreed. "We chose to. That's different from you earning it. You don't have to earn people staying, Issei. You just have to let them."

Rias, watching from the doorway, felt something in her chest that the theatre's HUD—when it flickered briefly to life—rendered as a single quiet line:

**[STATUS: HOUSEHOLD COHESION — NOT A TACTICAL FORMATION. A CHOICE, REPEATED DAILY.]**

Black Panther, watching, offered the room something close to a confession. "I ruled Wakanda believing isolation was strength—that a king carries weight alone so his people don't have to. This shows something I resisted learning for a long time: that allowing others to carry weight *with* you is not abdication. It is the harder discipline."

"Every spy I trained was taught personal attachment was a liability," Natasha said. "I believed that for the better part of two decades. Watching this, I'm less sure the doctrine was ever really about safety. I think it was about control—about how much easier a person is to command when they have nothing to go home to."

Fury, arms crossed, said nothing for a long moment. Then: "Adjusting the file. He's not a weapon with a hair-trigger. He's a person with people who show up. Those aren't the same threat profile."

**ENTRY 3: A FUTURE NOT YET EARNED**

**[GACHA SYSTEM: PULL 2 — "A FUTURE NOT YET EARNED" — FAMILY-CLASS — EX-CONTINUITY FLAG DETECTED — ACTIVATING]**

The theatre's tone changed before the image did—a shift in the light that longtime viewers of the gacha stream had learned meant *this is not a memory.*

**"NOTICE,"** the system said, plainly, refusing to let the vision play unlabeled. **"THIS PULL IS NOT DRAWN FROM THE SUBJECT'S PRESENT TIMELINE. IT ORIGINATES FROM A POSSIBLE FUTURE APPROXIMATELY THREE DECADES FORWARD, CONTINGENT ON CHOICES NOT YET MADE. TREAT ACCORDINGLY."**

The vision that followed was brief, gauzy at the edges the way distant futures always were in the archive's grammar—a warm room, older versions of familiar faces, and children who existed only as possibility given form: small hands pulling at an older Issei's sleeve, insisting he was needed for something trivial and precious, an older man setting down whatever weighed on him because a much smaller problem had just become more urgent.

It lasted less than a minute. The system closed it deliberately, gently, like drawing a curtain rather than ending a scene.

**"THIS FUTURE IS NOT GUARANTEED,"** the system said. **"IT IS SHOWN ONLY SO THE ROOM UNDERSTANDS WHAT THE PRESENT STRUGGLE IS FOR. NOTHING SEEN HERE IS PRESENT-DAY FACT."**

Wanda Maximoff exhaled something between a laugh and a sob. "That's cruel and kind at the same time. Showing him a reason to survive the version of himself that scares him most."

"It's honest, though," Rias said—present-timeline Rias, watching alongside the rest of the theatre's guests. "That's not a promise. It's a maybe. But I'll take a maybe."

**ENTRY 4: THE THEATRE'S REASSESSMENT**

Tony Stark, ever the one to reach for the practical conclusion, broke the quiet that followed. "So the threat model isn't 'ticking bomb.' It's 'a man with real failsafes, and the failsafes are people who keep choosing to show up, on days that don't make for good television.'"

"Because they're people he cares about," Pepper said. "Not devices. Not protocols. People, who could leave, and don't."

Steve Rogers looked at the frozen image of Koneko leaning against Issei's side, saying nothing, simply present. "In every strategic assessment we've run on him, we treated his attachments as variables—complications to route around. I think we had it backwards. They're not complications. They're the whole structure holding the rest of it up."
