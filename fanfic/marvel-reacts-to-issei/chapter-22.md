# Chapter Twenty-Two: Seventeen Wives?

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Cultural analysts (T'Challa, Magneto, Doom, Namor) • The Ravagers & Guardians (Yondu, Star-Lord, Gamora, Rocket, Drax, Nebula, Mantis, Kraglin) • Corporate strategists (Pepper Potts, Maria Hill) • Cosmic observers (Uatu, Silver Surfer, Fin Fang Foom)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Girls' Club of Hell" — **Comedy-Class**
2. "Marriage Ledger" — **Secret**

---

The theatre had spent one whole chapter marinating in the Family-Class banner's warm domestic gold, and it had not gotten used to it. Twenty chapters of war-red and grief-grey had trained the audience for a certain register, and now the impossible ceiling glowed like a kitchen window at dinnertime, and the gacha wheel descended with its new doorbell chime, and half the room's most dangerous people sat there feeling ambushed by coziness.

The previous night's pull had introduced the eight — Rias, Akeno, Xenovia, Irina, Asia, Koneko, Kuroka, Rossweisse — as living, arguing, laughing fixtures of the boy's life rather than names in a caption, and the theatre was still recalibrating. Tonight, before anyone had settled, the screen did something administrative.

It posted a dossier.

**SUBJECT: ISSEI HYOUDOU. AGE: SEVENTEEN. OCCUPATION: HIGH SCHOOL STUDENT; PAWN OF THE GREMORY PEERAGE; RED DRAGON EMPEROR.**

**WIVES: SEVENTEEN (17).**

The theatre made a sound like a cathedral organ sitting on a whoopee cushion.

"SEVENTEEN?" Johnny Storm was on his feet. "He's seventeen and he has seventeen — that's a wife per *year of being alive*—"

"The math is not the problem, Johnny," said Peter Parker faintly.

Down in the Guardians' crash-landed quadrant of seating, Rocket Raccoon stood up on his chair, cupped his little paws around his muzzle, and bellowed at the screen with the pure joy of a creature discovering a new lever to pull.

"HEY. HEY, SCREEN. Serious question. How many love interests does one guy actually *need*? Asking for inventory purposes. Is there a bulk discount? Does he get a punch card? Ten wives, eleventh wife free—"

Gamora smacked him. Open palm, back of the head, the crisp economical stroke of a woman who had performed this exact maintenance operation ten thousand times across four galaxies. Rocket's ears went flat.

"Seventeen," he muttered, rubbing his head, "and not one of 'em would'a stopped that."

"All seventeen of them would have stopped that," said Gamora.

But up in the analysts' tier, T'Challa had not laughed. The king of Wakanda was frowning at the dossier with the specific expression of a man who has read many intelligence briefings and can smell a bad one through the paper.

"Note the sourcing," he said quietly, and the screen — which had developed, over twenty-one chapters, an unsettling responsiveness to good questions — obligingly enlarged the citation line at the bottom of the dossier.

**SOURCE: "MIDNIGHT UNDERWORLD," DEVIL-SOCIETY WEEKLY GOSSIP PERIODICAL. CIRCULATION: 40 MILLION. ACCURACY RATING: [CALCULATING…]**

"A tabloid," said T'Challa. "The machine is citing a tabloid."

"The machine," said Doctor Strange slowly, from across the aisle, "has never cited *anything* before. It has simply been right."

And that — more than the number seventeen — was what made a certain kind of person in that theatre sit forward. Uatu the Watcher turned his vast pale head a full degree. The machine that had shown them twenty-one chapters of unimpeachable truth had just repeated a rumor, and either it had made its first error, or it was about to teach them something about the difference between what is said and what is written down.

The wheel spun. The token dropped — a chattering, kaleidoscopic thing that giggled audibly on impact — and the bowl chimed.

**PULL ONE: "GIRLS' CLUB OF HELL" — COMEDY-CLASS.**

**LET THE RECORD BE CONTESTED.**

---

The vision opened on a house.

The theatre knew the Hyoudou residence by now — knew that it had begun the story as a perfectly ordinary suburban home and had since been rebuilt by devil contractors into something with six stories underground, a ballroom, a training floor, and a bath large enough to have weather. What the theatre had not seen was a full ordinary day inside it, and the gacha, in a mood, gave them one at gently accelerated speed.

Morning: a fourteen-person breakfast assembled with military precision, Rias directing, Akeno humming over a pan, Asia carrying a stack of plates taller than her own composure. Koneko ate four servings in silence and then, without ceremony, climbed onto Issei's lap to finish her fifth, and the boy's soul visibly left his body and returned. Kuroka draped herself across the back of his chair like a shawl with opinions until Rossweisse — silver-haired, furious, holding a household budget spreadsheet — pried her off by the collar while lecturing about *per-capita grocery outlays.* Xenovia announced her training schedule at parade volume. Irina said grace at a table of devils, cheerfully, and nobody stopped her.

School. Club. Homework — Issei's, supervised in shifts, because his brides had a rota for it. Evening: Ravel Phenex arrived with a leather folio and ran the entire household's next week like a chief of staff born to it; Kunou — visiting from Kyoto, having ditched her diplomatic escort — orbited Issei at knee height demanding to be carried; Le Fay Pendragon blushed through a library wall whenever his voice carried; Elmenhilde Karnstein critiqued everyone's tea and stayed four hours past the visit she had described as "brief and reluctant." Ingvild Leviathan sang, half to herself, at a window, and the house went quiet to listen without being asked.

And through it all moved the boy — loud, embarrassing, transparently overjoyed — carrying, fetching, apologizing, blushing, working. The comedic register was exactly what the theatre expected from a Comedy-Class pull: a bath-schedule dispute resolved by rock-paper-scissors tournament bracket (the screen tastefully cut to the hallway, where Issei sat guarding the door with the haunted discipline of a man defusing a bomb); a two-front pillow war; Koneko dead-panning "pervert" at him as both verdict and endearment.

"Fourteen," counted Mantis, delighted, her antennae glowing. "There are fourteen ladies and every single one is *happy.* This house feels like—" she groped for it, hands fluttering, "—like a hug that argues."

Then, on-screen, catastrophe arrived by mail slot.

A magazine. Glossy. *MIDNIGHT UNDERWORLD*, banner headline in flaming infernal type: **RED DRAGON EMPEROR'S SEVENTEEN BRIDES — THE HAREM THAT ATE THE GREMORY FORTUNE — EXCLUSIVE!!**

The house went still the way a fault line goes still.

"Seventeen," said Rias Gremory, in the tone her brother probably used to end wars.

"Seventeen?" said Akeno, sweetly, with thunder in it. "My, my. Ambitious of them."

Ravel Phenex snapped her folio open on the table like a magistrate opening court. "They have counted *wrong,*" she said, with the outrage of a professional confronting amateur arithmetic, "and I can tell you exactly how, because I have read their correction history and they always count wrong the same way. They have included Grayfia-sama — a *married woman* and the strongest queen in the underworld, they will die for that — they have included Lady Miki, who is his *mother* — and they have included Kiba-san. *Again.*"

The theatre detonated.

On-screen, Yuuto Kiba — the peerage's knight, golden-haired, tragically beautiful — put his face in his hands. "It's the third time," he said, muffled. "I am *visibly* holding a sword in the photograph."

In the theatre, Star-Lord was wheezing. Yondu was grinning his whole crooked steel grin. Even Nebula's mouth moved a millimeter, which among her people constituted hysterics.

And then Rossweisse — Valkyrie, former bodyguard of Odin, current civil servant and the newest of the eight — stood, produced from thin air an actual document case with actual stamped seals, and addressed the tabloid as though it were a defendant.

"The legal record," she said, with Nordic bureaucratic fury, "is *public.* Formal engagements, registered and sealed, in order of recognition: Rias Gremory. Akeno Himejima. Xenovia Quarta. Irina Shidou. Asia Argento. Koneko Toujou. Kuroka. And—" the tiniest hitch, a blush fighting a filing system, "—myself. *Eight.* Eight brides. Fourteen members of this — this *association*—" she gestured at the breakfast-table chaos with the despair of a woman who did that household's taxes, "—fourteen who love him, and only some of us have completed the paperwork, and paperwork *matters*—"

"The Girls' Club of Hell," said Kuroka lazily, tail curling, entirely pleased with the name. "Fourteen seats, nya. Eight with rings."

"*Not yet rings,*" said Ravel, strangled, staring at her own folio, at a particular drafted-but-unsigned page in her own immaculate handwriting, and going the color of her family's firebird crest.

Rias picked up the magazine, held it at arm's length, and let a small, polite sphere of the Power of Destruction reduce it to a smell.

**CORRECTION,** said the theatre's screen — and the word arrived in a register the machine had never used, stamped diagonally across its own earlier dossier in glowing red, the way a clerk voids a check.

**FORMAL ENGAGEMENTS: EIGHT (8). ADDITIONAL BONDED HEARTS, UNREGISTERED: SIX (6) — RAVEL PHENEX, KUNOU, LE FAY PENDRAGON, ELMENHILDE KARNSTEIN, INGVILD LEVIATHAN, YASAKA.**

**"SEVENTEEN" IS FANON. THE LEDGER IS CANON.**

**THE SYSTEM REGRETS THE ERROR. THE SYSTEM DOES NOT REGRET WHAT THE ERROR TAUGHT YOU ABOUT YOURSELVES.**

The theatre sat with that for a moment.

"It baited us," said Maria Hill flatly. "The omniscient doom theatre just ran a *media literacy exercise.*"

"And every one of us engaged with the headline before checking the source," said T'Challa, and had the grace to include himself with a small, rueful gesture. "Every nation in this room has been governed worse than that house is. Continue."

---

The reactions came in waves, and the fascinating thing — the thing the theatre itself seemed to be orchestrating — was that no two factions were reacting to the same marriage.

The Ravagers went first, because the Ravagers had no concept of waiting a turn.

"I'll tell you what I'm seein'," said Yondu Udonta, toothpick wandering, "and it ain't wives. Y'all are hung up on the word." He jabbed the toothpick at the frozen frame of the breakfast table. "Look at the *table.* Fourteen mouths, everybody fed, everybody's got a job, everybody'd take a hit for the little idiot at the head of it and he'd take one for any of them — first, and *twice.* That ain't a harem, that's a *crew.* Boy's flyin' a fourteen-hand ship and don't even know he's captain." He leaned back. "You don't count a crew in wives. You count it in who shows up when the hull breaks."

"On my planet," said Drax, with the enormous gravity he reserved for his gentlest memories, "a man took one wife. Mine was named Ovette. She filled my entire heart, and my heart is very large, as is the rest of me." A pause; the theatre held still for him. "If this boy's heart truly holds fourteen without cheating any of them, it is his *capacity* that impresses. I will watch for cheating. I do not expect to find it."

"There's no cheating," said Mantis softly, hands pressed together. "I felt the room. When he looks at each one, the feeling is not *divided.* It is not a pie. It is — fourteen whole feelings. I did not know that was allowed."

"It's not allowed," said Rocket. "It's *rigged.* Nobody gets fourteen— OW. I was AGREEING with the bug lady—"

"You were not," said Gamora.

Then the corporate wing weighed in, and the register changed from crew to conglomerate.

"Set the romance aside for one minute," said Pepper Potts, in her board-meeting voice, and the analysts' tier actually turned to listen, because Pepper Potts's board-meeting voice had ended more empires than most armies. "Look at the structure. Rias Gremory — founding partner, controlling interest, and don't miss that she *spent her entire acquisition budget on him in chapter one*; eight Pawns, all-in, everyone said she overpaid. Akeno, senior partner. Church faction onboarded — twice, Xenovia *and* Irina, that's a cross-denominational merger nobody thought survivable. Asia is the entire wellness division. Koneko and Kuroka reconcile a family dispute that predates the company. Rossweisse is Asgardic talent poached at below-market — that's the hire that tells you the culture's real." She ticked the last one on her thumb. "And Ravel Phenex is the chief operating officer of the whole household, running it flawlessly, *without a contract,* because she is choosing her own timing. This isn't a harem, Maria. This is the most successful alliance rollup in that cosmology, and every single stakeholder retained full voting rights."

"I ran it as a security file," said Maria Hill. "Fourteen principals, six factions of origin — devils, fallen-adjacent, two churches, youkai royalty, Asgard, and whatever the vampire is — all under one roof, zero leaks, zero defections, zero interfaction incidents beyond pillow warfare." She shook her head slowly. "SHIELD couldn't keep three agencies in one building without a stabbing. His retention secret is apparently *sincerity.* I can't file that. There's no form for it."

And then, from the high cold tier where the crowned heads sat, the conversation the outline of history always produces: three men who had each married, or refused to marry, for nations.

"Doom will state the obvious, since no one else appears equipped," said Doom. "Marriage is statecraft. It has always been statecraft; the songs are decoration. What Doom observes in this ledger is a boy who has contracted alliances with the Gremory dukedom, the Phenex estate, the Church, the youkai court of Kyoto, Asgard's civil service, and the blood of the original Leviathan—" a pause of genuine, grudging perplexity, "—and has extracted *nothing.* No territory. No titles. No dowries. The single most valuable bachelor in his civilization is giving it away for *affection.* Doom finds this either sublime or imbecilic, and is irritated to report the data favors sublime."

"Atlantis has sealed wars with weddings," said Namor, arms crossed, regarding the screen with imperial detachment. "I was nearly such a seal myself, more than once. The custom is older than your surface nations and more honest than your treaties — a marriage is a hostage exchange in which the hostages are permitted to be happy." His eyes narrowed, not unkindly. "What is *rare* — what I have seen perhaps twice in a century of thrones — is the political marriage in which the politics are incidental. Those women gain standing by him, yes. They would keep him if he were still nothing. That was true when he was a Pawn who could barely fly. Imperius Rex; even I can read a court."

Magneto had been silent through all of it.

Erik Lehnsherr sat very still in the analysts' tier, and when he finally spoke, the comedy drained out of the room's edges, because his voice had the sound of a man handling something personal with gloves.

"You are all analyzing the wrong institution," he said. "You see a harem, or a merger, or a court. Look *underneath.* The devils of that world fought a three-way war that killed their God and gutted their own aristocracy — seventy-two great houses, half extinct or barren, a species that reproduces slowly, rebuilding on the edge of demographic collapse. Their Evil Pieces, their peerages, and yes, the plural marriages of their great houses — all of it is one thing: a *people refusing to disappear.*" His folded hands tightened slightly. "I have watched a people count its survivors. I have sat in the rooms where the arithmetic of continuation is done, and there is no colder mathematics anywhere. And into that arithmetic walks this ridiculous, warm-hearted boy — and performs the coldest institution his society possesses *for love*, lawfully, gently, with the enthusiastic consent of every party and the blessing of their houses." He sat back. "Mock the number if you must. I look at that ledger and I see a graveyard civilization planting a garden. I will not be laughing."

The silence after that lasted long enough that Rocket, mercifully, did not fill it.

T'Challa inclined his head, slow and formal, the acknowledgment of one king's framework by another. "Then let us name what has happened here tonight," he said, "because it is the theatre's real lesson, and it is a good one. Every faction in this room looked at one household and saw a different institution — Yondu saw a crew. Ms. Potts saw an alliance. Namor saw statecraft. Erik saw survival. The romantics—" a glance at the Spider-family's row, where several people pretended to be elsewhere, "—saw romance, and they were not wrong either. *All* frameworks returned the same finding: fourteen women, freely choosing, none diminished. When every lens agrees on the dignity of the arrangement, the arrangement is sound, whatever it is called. Wakanda would recognize such a house." A beat, and the king permitted himself one dry note: "Wakanda would also assign it a larger security detail than mine."

---

The wheel had waited politely for the debate to crest. Now it spun again — and the theatre's warm gold dimmed several shades toward candlelight, and the second token fell without any chime at all. Secret-class pulls never chimed. The room had learned to brace when the machine went quiet.

**PULL TWO: "MARRIAGE LEDGER" — SECRET.**

**EVERY RECORD HAS A FIRST PAGE. YOU HAVE NOT SEEN THE FIRST PAGE.**

The vision was not a scene. It was a *document* — the actual registry, bound in Gremory crimson, kept (the caption noted) by Grayfia Lucifuge herself, and the gacha turned it one page at a time in enormous light above the theatre floor.

The eight entries, in order, each with its seals: Gremory. Himejima. Quarta. Shidou. Argento. Toujou. Kuroka's, sealed not with a house crest but a single black cat's paw pressed deliberately off-center, like a signature refusing to pretend it came from nobility. Rossweisse's, notarized in triplicate, obviously by the bride. Beneath the formal eight: six drafted lines, unsigned — one of them, in handwriting the theatre had just learned to recognize as Ravel's, complete down to the date format *except the date* — and beside each entry, a column headed TERMS.

Every single TERMS column read the same way.

**TERMS: NONE. HE ASKED FOR NOTHING.**

"There it is," murmured Doom, to his notebook, which had reopened.

But the gacha turned backward, to the page before page one — the flyleaf, where a first entry had been made and then struck through so hard the pen had scored the vellum. The theatre leaned in, and read, and stopped breathing at roughly the same moment.

**ENTRY ZERO: "YUUMA AMANO." FIRST GIRLFRIEND. FIRST DATE. FALSE NAME, FALSE HEART — RAYNARE OF THE FALLEN. RECORD ANNULLED IN BLOOD, KUOH CITY FOUNTAIN PLAZA.**

**THE FIRST NAME IN THE LEDGER IS A WOUND. EVERY NAME AFTER IT IS A COURAGE.**

**HE HAS NEVER ONCE ASSUMED A YES. THE BOY WHO DREAMS ALOUD OF A HAREM HAS NEVER TAKEN A SINGLE HEART FOR GRANTED, BECAUSE THE FIRST TIME HE OFFERED HIS OWN, IT WAS RETURNED THROUGH HIS CHEST.**

In the street-level rows, nobody spoke. Frank Castle's jaw did something quiet and terrible; Logan's knuckles, resting on his knees, flexed once. The theatre had known since chapter one that those two had privately marked Raynare's name. The ledger had just co-signed it.

"That's the engine," said Matt Murdock softly, sightless face tilted toward a document he could hear the room reading. "That's why he's loud about wanting love and paralyzed about receiving it. The bravado's a splint. Fourteen women figured out the fracture underneath and decided, independently, to be careful with it. *That's* the club. That's what they actually have in common."

And then, for the dragons, the caption changed register one last time — and the theatre heard Ddraig's voice, drawn from some remembered fireside conversation, laid gently over the closing image of the crowded, golden, arguing house:

"**Humans call it a harem and snicker, partner. Dragons have an older word. A dragon does not collect treasure to spend it — a dragon builds a NEST, and the nest is the one place in creation the dragon will die rather than yield. You have built yours out of people. It is the strongest architecture I have ever sheltered in, and I have lived in war for ten thousand years.**"

"**Nest law is one law: everything inside the walls is worth everything outside them.**"

High in the god-tiers, Thor set down his cup entirely, which those who knew him understood to be his highest observance. "Nest law," he repeated, reverent. "Aye. My father's vaults had such a law. His halls did not. The boy has chosen better than a king."

Fin Fang Foom — who had spent twenty-one chapters radiating contempt for a "true" dragon leashed to a human boy — was silent for a long moment, foreclaws flexing on the seatback he had crushed into modern sculpture weeks ago. "The nest is real," he finally rumbled, with the physical difficulty of a dragon conceding anything. "I have hoarded islands. Weapons. Centuries. It occurs to me—" the words ground out like tectonic plates, "—that none of my hoards would have *carried me down a mountain.* The Welsh Dragon has not been diminished by the boy. He has been *housed.* There is a difference. I am still deciding how I feel about knowing it."

The Silver Surfer, chrome and quiet, added the cosmic footnote. "I surrendered a beloved to save a world, and called the solitude that followed by noble names. Heralds travel light; we tell ourselves power requires it." His gaze stayed on the struck-through first entry, and the fourteen names that had answered it. "This boy is disproving my life's arithmetic one chapter at a time. His anchors do not slow him. They are *why he accelerates.* Norrin Radd is taking notes."

Uatu the Watcher said nothing for a long time — and then, in the voice of a being checking an archive that kept failing him, said the thing that became the night's splinter: "Fourteen bonds. I have traced each one across every timeline in my custody, seeking precedent. As with the boy himself: nothing. But the ledger's final page—" and the vision, obediently, showed it: blank lines awaiting names not yet written, futures not yet chosen. And one line, low on the page, that was not blank and not written — a faint *shimmer*, like the ghost of ink, like an entry that had existed and been *removed*. The same wrongness Heimdall had flagged at the edge of the Dragon Blaster's scar. The mark left when something is taken out of what-is.

"Someone has been *erasing* in this book," said Uatu, very quietly, "and I do not know who, and I am the Watcher, and I do not know who."

The gold light banked. The wheel rose. And the screen, cozy font utterly unrepentant, posted its exit line:

**NEXT: EVERY BRIDE COMES FROM A HOUSE. MEET THE HOUSE THAT TAUGHT A DEVIL TO LOVE A PAWN.**

**THE GREMORY HOUSEHOLD.**

"I'm not emotionally prepared for his in-laws," said Peter Parker.

"No one is prepared for in-laws," said Namor, with feeling, and the lights went down.
