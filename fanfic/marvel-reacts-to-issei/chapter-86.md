## THEATRE LEDGER — SESSION 86

**ENTRY 1: THE RACCOON AND THE GAUNTLET**

The problem began, as all good problems do, with Rocket Raccoon deciding that the laws of physics were more of a suggestion than a rule.

"I'm just *touching* it," Rocket said, inching toward where Issei sat in the observation area, the Boosted Gear gleaming crimson on his left arm. "Just a little examination. Non-invasive. I promise I won't break anything."

"That's a lie," Gamora said flatly, not looking up from her datapad. "You broke a Celestial Archive last month."

"That was an *accident*," Rocket protested. "And it was already broken."

"You broke it *more broken*."

Issei watched this exchange with the expression of someone who'd realized several seconds too late that allowing the Guardians of the Galaxy into the theatre had consequences. He was a dragon emperor. He'd fought cosmic horrors. He'd destroyed planets with a thought.

He was completely unprepared for a raccoon with engineering curiosity and zero impulse control.

Shuri appeared at the edge of the observation deck like a phantom, already grinning. "Let him try," she said to Issei. "For science."

"No," Tony Stark's voice came through the comm system. "Rocket, if you even *think* about disassembling—"

Rocket moved with the kind of precision that made it clear he'd been planning this moment for weeks. He ducked under Issei's outstretched hand, slid across the armrest, and made a direct grab for the Boosted Gear's central red jewel.

The HUD flickered to life:

```
[RACCOON-CLASS: ROCKET RACCOON] STATUS: INSPECTING
> Intent: Analysis of Sacred Gear mechanics
> Probability of Device Survival: 32%
> Probability of Raccoon Survival: 94%
> Probability of Awkward Conversation: 97%
```

"Fascinating," Shuri whispered, pulling out a handheld analyzer. "The gauntlet's reading his touch signature. It's not rejecting him. It's *assessing* him."

Rocket's claws brushed the red jewel, and suddenly his eyes went wide. The connection was instantaneous—not painful, not exactly, but *overwhelming*. Every technical specification of the Boosted Gear flooded his mind at once. The way it redistributed energy. The theoretical transfer efficiency. The mathematical elegance of how it redistributed power.

"Oh," Rocket breathed. "Oh, that's—that's *gorgeous*."

For someone who'd once tried to steal a dragon, Rocket had excellent taste in machinery.

"He's accessing the internal schematic," Shuri said, her eyes tracking the data stream. "The gauntlet's letting him interface. This shouldn't be possible without authorization."

"It's Ddraig," Issei said quietly. "The dragon inside. He must be—"

And then Ddraig spoke, not to Issei, but directly to Rocket. The Welsh Dragon's voice manifested as a sensation in Rocket's mind, ancient and deep and absolutely amused:

**"You wish to understand me, small thief? Very well. Hear then the song of multiplication. Hear the power of ten seconds and endless scaling. I do not fear your inquiry, little raccoon. Few mortals have the cleverness to see beyond the armor to the mechanism beneath."**

```
[DRAGON-CLASS: DDRAIG] STATUS: DIRECT COMMUNICATION
> Target: Rocket Raccoon
> Intent: Dragon-to-Engineer Conversation
> Confidence in Raccoon's Discretion: MODERATE
> Amusement Level: VERY HIGH
```

Rocket's hands began moving of their own accord, not really stealing, not exactly, but sort of gently shifting components, understanding their relationships to each other, seeing how they *sang* together. His mind was moving at hyperspeed, pulling apart the mystery of how a simple red glove could double power output every ten seconds without violating the laws of thermodynamics.

"There has to be an external power source," Rocket muttered. "Nothing internal is that efficient. The density calculations don't—" He paused. "Oh. Oh *no*."

"What?" Tony was at the observation window now, along with Reed Richards and Shuri, all three of them trying to simultaneously understand what Rocket was understanding.

"It's not external," Rocket said slowly. "It's *recursive*. The gauntlet doesn't generate power. It *borrows* power from the wearer and feeds it back amplified. And the dragon—Ddraig—he's essentially a cosmic battery that feeds on the feedback loop. The longer the boosts stack, the less unstable it becomes because there's an entity inside actively *managing* the cascade."

Ddraig's laugh rumbled through all their minds:

**"Finally. A human with the intelligence to see that the weapon is not crude multiplication, but elegant parasitism. I devour the power that flows through my host, and in devouring, I strengthen him. We are not master and servant. We are symbiotes."**

Tony stepped back from the window. "That's—if that's accurate, then the gauntlet isn't violating physics. It's *negotiating* with physics."

Shuri was already pulling up redesigns on her screen. "If we could replicate the recursive feedback without the dragon—"

"No," Ddraig's voice cut across the theatre like a blade. "The dragon is essential. Without consciousness to direct the cascade, the gauntlet becomes merely a multiplier. Unstable. Dangerous. A weapon that devours its wielder after sufficient boosts. I am not ornament. I am necessity."

"Defensive much?" Rocket grinned, but there was genuine respect in his eyes now. "Look, I like you, dragon. You're proud in a way that actually makes sense. But here's the real question: what if you had better feedback mechanisms? What if the transfer efficiency wasn't *just* dragon consciousness but also had auxiliary systems that could—"

Rocket's hands moved over the gauntlet, not removing it, but pointing at specific sections. Shuri was three steps ahead, already sketching components. They began to speak in the peculiar language of engineers—equal parts hypothesis, wonder, and competitive problem-solving.

"The transfer limiter here," Rocket pointed. "Holding back about 40% of the potential distribution to your allies. If you had a secondary matrix—"

"We could redirect the overflow into a buffer system," Shuri finished. "Store it across multiple recipients instead of a single transfer point."

Issei watched in fascination as two of the brightest minds in the Marvel multiverse and one ancient dragon began what appeared to be a genuine collaborative design session. Tony was taking notes. Reed was running calculations. Even Doom had materialized at the back of the chamber, watching with the expression of a craftsman observing fellow masters at work.

**"I approve,"** Ddraig rumbled. **"This partnership of brilliant fools. You would strengthen the bond between myself and my host without attempting to sever it. This is honorable. When this endeavor concludes, perhaps we shall discuss the design further."**

"Does that mean you'll let me borrow the gauntlet for a week?" Rocket asked hopefully.

"No," Issei and Ddraig said in perfect unison.

"Worth asking," Rocket shrugged, but he was already sketching modifications, working alongside Shuri and Tony like they'd been collaborating for years. The conversation had shifted into higher mathematics and materials science, with Ddraig offering insights that shouldn't have been possible from a being who predated most of modern physics.

---

**ENTRY 2: DOOM'S OBSERVATION**

In the shadows of the observation deck, Victor Von Doom watched the collaboration with the expression of a connoisseur examining fine art.

He approached Issei slowly, deliberately, the way one might approach a valuable artifact.

"You permit your weapon to be questioned," Doom observed. "Few emperors would tolerate such scrutiny."

Issei glanced over, unsurprised by Doom's arrival. "Ddraig isn't my weapon. He's my partner. If questioning makes him stronger, he'll want to answer."

Doom nodded slowly. "Indeed. I notice he chose to engage in technical discussion rather than establish dominance. The pride of dragons typically prevents such collaboration." He paused. "Unless the dragon recognizes superior minds. Or sees the collaboration as advantageous to his own understanding."

"Both," Issei said simply. "And he enjoys teaching. I think Ddraig spent so long alone inside the Boosted Gear before I came along that having people take genuine interest in how he works... it matters to him."

Doom watched as Rocket made an excited gesture, and Ddraig responded with what could only be described as enthusiasm—a resonance from the gauntlet that made the laboratory equipment vibrate sympathetically.

"I will make an observation," Doom said quietly. "When we first saw your armaments, I recognized them as superior craftsmanship. Ascalon blades forged with technique that bordered on sorcery. Ryuuteimaru demonstrating design principles I have spent decades pursuing. But I dismissed the partnership with your dragon as... ornamental. A narrative flourish."

He turned to face Issei directly.

"I was wrong. The dragon is not ornament. He is architecture. The difference between a weapon that works and a weapon that *thinks*. Few emperors possess such advantages. Fewer still recognize their true value."

"Are you asking me to explain it better?" Issei asked, genuinely curious. "Or are you considering the implications for your own work?"

Doom actually smiled. "Both, perhaps. But primarily, I am observing that a collaboration has begun that will produce technical advancement. When Stark and Richards have completed their documentation, I will require a copy."

It wasn't a request.

"I'll ask them," Issei said. "But I think Rocket might want to keep some modifications proprietary."

"Of course he will," Doom said, returning his attention to the workshop. "And I will eventually reverse-engineer them anyway. But the collaboration itself will have value. When individual geniuses are combined, the resulting work exceeds the sum. Your dragon seems to understand this. I am... appreciative of such pragmatism."

---

**ENTRY 3: THE THEORETICAL TRANSFER ENHANCEMENT**

By the time the session concluded, Rocket had filled four datapads with schematics. Shuri had designed two different auxiliary matrices. Tony had reluctantly admitted that at least one of Rocket's ideas was "actually not completely stupid." And Ddraig had learned more about how modern technology might interface with ancient magic than he'd known in several centuries.

"So we're clear," Rocket said, making absolutely certain, "I can modify my own weapons using these principles, but the gauntlet stays with Issei?"

"**Correct,**" Ddraig responded. "**The gauntlet remains with my host. But the knowledge—the knowledge is freely shared among those who ask with genuine curiosity rather than greedy intent.**"

"I can work with that," Rocket grinned. "Though I'm gonna want a follow-up conversation about this recursive feedback system once we've had time to build a prototype."

"We will speak again, Rocket Raccoon," Ddraig agreed. "You have the mind of a craftsman. Such minds are rare. I do not object to collaboration with rare minds."

As Rocket and Shuri left the chamber, already planning their next conversation, Issei caught something Ddraig had said to him privately through their bond:

**"That small thief sees the beauty in what we are, partner. Not the power, but the elegance. Few humans understand the difference. This one does. This one respects. We have done well to make such an acquaintance."**

Issei smiled. "Rocket said you were his favorite dragon."

**"Then Rocket has excellent judgment,"** Ddraig replied, with the kind of satisfaction that only comes from being genuinely appreciated for one's craftsmanship rather than one's destructive potential.
