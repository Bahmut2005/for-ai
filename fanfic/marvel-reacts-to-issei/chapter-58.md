## Part One: The Proposal

The theatre's great screen had dimmed to a soft grey as the gacha wheel began its familiar spin. The Avengers' section of the theatre—a cordoned block of reinforced seats that had become their unofficial headquarters within the supernatural cinema—was unusually quiet.

Captain America sat rigidly in his chair, a holographic document floating before him. His jaw was set. To his left, Tony Stark leaned back with arms crossed, eyes narrowed at the projection. Natasha Romanova sat perfectly still, hands folded in her lap. Carol Danvers—Captain Marvel—stood, her body tense with the kind of focused anticipation she brought to every conflict.

"The theatre's asking about me," Natasha said softly. "About whether he'd work with us. Whether he'd *stay* with us."

Steve Rogers nodded slowly. "That's the question, isn't it? After everything we've seen—the dragon's power, his loyalty to his family—would the Red Dragon even consider joining an organization like ours?"

"He'd be valuable," Tony said, and there was a calculating edge to his voice. "Raw power. Strategic flexibility. If we could direct that—"

"If," Natasha interrupted, turning to look at him. "That's always been your problem with people like him, Tony. The 'if.' The assumption that power without your direction is power wasted."

The wheel slowed. The theatre held its breath.

Carol said nothing, but her eyes were locked on the screen, tracking the spin with the intensity of a predator watching prey.

## Part Two: Gacha Pull One — Compatibility Report

The wheel stopped. The theatre erupted in low, cascading music—not triumphant, but analytical. Clinical. The screen bloomed with light.

**COMPATIBILITY REPORT: AVENGERS / ISSEI HYOUDOU**

The title hung in the air, and beneath it, a dense report began to scroll. But the theatre was kind—it crystallized the data into a format the assembled heroes could quickly parse.

**SECTION A: THEORETICAL MISSION PARTICIPATION**

*Subject would join Avengers in crisis: YES (Probability: 87%)*

"He'd come," Steve said, reading ahead. "If we needed him, if the world was ending, he'd come. That tracks."

*Subject would obey standing orders: CONDITIONAL*
*— Probability 59% if order aligns with subject's family safety or core ethical framework*
*— Probability 12% if order conflicts with family obligations or creates moral hazard*
*— Probability 4% if order involves violation of personal autonomy without consent*

Tony straightened, his jaw tightening. "Conditional. There it is. Insubordination."

"Or integrity," Natasha said quietly. "Depends on your perspective."

The report continued.

*Subject would remain long-term: UNLIKELY*
*Estimated tenure: 1-3 missions before returning to family/personal obligations*
*Rationale: Subject's priority hierarchy places family, intimate relationships, and personal freedom above coalition loyalty. Organization membership incompatible with this hierarchy.*

Carol's expression had shifted to something like intrigue. She was studying the words with the focus of someone looking for a weakness to exploit—or a worth to respect.

**SECTION B: INTERPERSONAL DYNAMICS**

*Subject would argue with leadership: LIKELY*
*— Probability with Stark: 84% (methodological disagreement, philosophy of power)*
*— Probability with Rogers: 23% (mutual respect for authority, but resistance to top-down imposition)*
*— Probability with Danvers: 61% (competitive respect, divergent tactical philosophies)*

"Twenty-three percent," Steve said. It wasn't quite satisfaction in his voice, but something close. "He respects Steve Rogers."

"Because Rogers doesn't try to own him," Natasha said. "Steve's dangerous to control. He respects that."

Tony made a noise that might have been a laugh. "Eighty-four percent with me. He's not wrong about that."

**SECTION C: SUBJECT RESPECT MATRIX**

The report listed them like a hierarchy:

*Primary respect: Rogers (authority through moral example, not imposition)*
*Secondary respect: Odinson (warrior code, freedom valorized)*
*Tertiary respect: Romanova (pragmatism without dogma, personal agency)*
*Limited respect: Stark (effectiveness acknowledged, methods questioned)*
*Contextual respect: Danvers (professional competence, ideological tension)*
*Resistance: Organizational bureaucracy, power-hoarding, collective decision-making prioritizing control over coalition*

"He respects you, Tony," Steve said. "Just not your methods."

"Then we'd clash," Tony replied flatly. "And that's a problem in a team environment."

**SECTION D: COMPATIBILITY ASSESSMENT**

The final section of the report was longer, more narrative:

*ISSEI HYOUDOU is fundamentally incompatible with permanent Avengers membership. Subject values personal autonomy above team cohesion, family above organizational mission, and mutual aid above hierarchical command. Subject is not insubordinate in crisis—probability of participation remains high at 87%—but cannot be reliably directed toward tasks deemed strategically necessary if those tasks conflict with his priorities.*

*Subject is, however, extraordinarily compatible with coalition-based operations. As a temporary ally in crisis situations, subject demonstrates exceptional capability and trustworthiness. Subject's moral framework, while different from Avengers protocols, is not antithetical—subject values protection of innocents, preservation of life, and honest dealing.*

*RECOMMENDATION: Treat ISSEI HYOUDOU as an allied force, not a member. Coalition-builder, not employee. Approach as peer with different priorities, not subordinate with conflicting orders. Subject responds to respect and autonomy; responds poorly to control and hierarchy.*

*PREDICTION: Subject will aid Avengers in 3-5 crisis situations across next decade. Subject will not accept permanent roster position. Subject will not delegate family safety to organizational mandate. Subject is ally. Not member.*

The report faded. The theatre was silent.

## Part Three: First Reactions

Steve Rogers exhaled slowly. "He's right," he said. "About the report. About Issei. We treat him as an ally. That's what matters."

"An ally with power we could use more effectively if he weren't so obstinate about his little family obligations," Tony countered, his voice sharp. But even he seemed to acknowledge a certain grudging accuracy to the report.

Natasha had read the compatibility matrix three times over. "The respect hierarchy," she said quietly. "It's not about power. It's about autonomy. Steve doesn't try to own him. Thor valorizes freedom. I don't pretend to be something I'm not, and he knows that. That's why he respects us."

"And why he'll argue with me," Tony said. "Because I *do* try to optimize outcomes, and I don't always care how I get there."

"Eighty-four percent," Natasha repeated, with something almost like sympathy.

Carol Danvers had been silent throughout. Now she spoke, her voice thoughtful. "Sixty-one percent with me. Competitive respect." She was almost smiling—not quite, but almost. "Ideological tension. I want to see that. I want to see if I can convince him that my approach works."

"That," Natasha said, turning to face her, "is exactly why you'll clash with him. You assume you can convince him. He assumes his path is the right one. Neither of you yields easily."

"Then it'll be interesting," Carol said. Her eyes were still on the screen, on the faded words of the report. "I want to fly a mission with him. Just to see."

## Part Four: Gacha Pull Two — Hypothetical Mission

The theatre's wheel began to turn again. The music shifted—less analytical, more visceral. The lights dimmed. The screen exploded with motion.

**HYPOTHETICAL SCENARIO: JOINT OPERATION**

The scene materialized in vivid detail.

### The Scenario

A doomsday device had been seized by a rogue faction in Latveria. The device—a cascade-detonation model that would trigger a chain reaction across a densely populated periphery zone, killing an estimated 300,000 civilians if triggered—was armed and counting down.

The team dispatched: Cardinal Crimson (Issei, his armored form gleaming in the simulation), Captain Marvel (Carol Danvers in her blue-and-gold armor), Thor Odinson, and Natasha Romanova. Four warriors. One goal: disable the device. Multiple approaches.

### The Conflict

The device sat in the center of a marketplace. Civilians were being evacuated, but the window was closing. Forty minutes to activation.

Captain Marvel's scan came first. "Precision bombing run. I can target the cascade core without triggering—estimated collateral, 300,000 if we fail; 200,000 if precision breaks. I have a 78% success rate on this profile."

"That's 78% collateral death," Issei said flatly. "No."

Thor laughed—not unkindly, but with the confidence of someone who'd crushed armies. "Overwhelming force. I'll strike with Mjolnir before the thing can cascade. The shockwave will clear the zone. Estimated 500,000 collateral if the cascade triggers, but I'll make sure it doesn't."

"Overwhelming is still overwhelming," Issei said. He closed his eyes. The Red Dragon's power rippled through the simulation, visible as shimmering distortions in the air. "The device's cascade has a sympathetic resonance. Blunt force triggers it. Precision bombing has margin for error."

He moved to the device. It was brilliant to watch—Issei's power unfolded like origami, precise and infinitely layered. His technique was nothing like the blunt force of his teammates. Instead, he threaded his strength through the device's architecture, finding the cascade core not by destroying the surrounding structure but by navigating through it.

"How long?" Carol asked.

"Eighteen minutes," Issei said. "But I can disarm it without triggering cascade. Collateral: approximately 15,000. Not zero—there's proximity risk. But acceptable."

"Eighteen minutes," Carol repeated. She was running calculations. "We don't have eighteen minutes. Cascade window is twelve."

"Then you have twelve," Issei said. "Or you trust my count."

It was, Natasha observed from the scenario, a masterclass in stubbornness versus pragmatism.

Thor spoke: "I trust the Dragon's count. Let him work."

It took sixteen minutes. The cascade window closed with seven seconds to spare. The device powered down. When Natasha's scan completed, the collateral count was 14,823—an improvement of 285,177 lives over Carol's best-case scenario, 485,177 over Thor's worst.

The Issei in the scenario climbed down from the device, power fading. He was breathing hard. But smiling.

### The Aftermath

The mission debrief played out on screen.

Steve Rogers (in the scenario, via comms): "We work well together."

Issei: "I help. I don't obey. Those are different things."

Tony Stark (via Iron Legion feed): "Your method cost us three extra minutes. The cascade nearly activated. We gambled and won this time, but—"

"Three minutes," Issei interrupted. "And 285,177 survivors. Precision over speed. I'll take that trade every time."

Carol Danvers: "He's right. And it's infuriating to argue with someone who's right."

Natasha Romanova: "That's not infuriating. That's reliability."

The scenario faded. The theatre returned to its neutral grey.

## Part Five: Secondary Reactions

The silence that followed was different from the first—less uncertain, more thoughtful.

"We work well together," Steve repeated softly. "That's the key phrase. Not 'he works well for us.' 'We work well together.'"

"Because he's not subordinate," Natasha said. "He's a peer. Different priorities, different methods, but peers."

Carol stood, arms uncrossed now. There was something like hunger in her expression. "Three minutes," she said. "He held position because he believed his method was better. He didn't break formation. He just... refused to hurry past optimal."

"That's who he is," Natasha said. "He'd come to us in crisis. He'd work with us. But he wouldn't subordinate his judgment to organizational mandate. He'd argue. He'd insist on his method. And—" she paused, "—he'd probably be right more often than not."

Tony was quiet. When he spoke, it was without the sharp edge from before. "The collateral math is legitimate. 285,000 lives is... significant. But it's also why he's dangerous to work with. He makes judgment calls in the field. He refuses acceleration. In a crisis, we need rapid response."

"You need rapid *obedience*," Natasha corrected gently. "Rapid response is something else entirely. Issei responds rapidly. He just responds to his own judgment, not your orders."

"Then we can't recruit him," Tony said flatly.

"We shouldn't recruit him," Steve said. His eyes were on the faded screen. "The report was right. We should ally with him. As a peer. When crisis comes, we reach out. When he answers—and he will answer, 87% probability—we listen to his methods and we respect his autonomy."

Carol was still standing. "And if I wanted to fly a mission with him. Just to see. To test his methods against mine in a real scenario—not a simulation."

"Then you'd learn what I learned watching that hypothetical," Natasha said. "That arguing with Issei is only frustrating until you realize he's arguing because he cares about getting it *right*, not getting it *fast*. And once you understand that, you start respecting him. Even when you disagree."

Carol nodded slowly. There was something like satisfaction on her face—the look of someone who'd found a worth opponent. "I want to do that. I want to fly with him. Not as his commanding officer. As his partner."

"Then don't try to command him," Steve said. "That's the only real rule."

## Part Six: The Hook

The theatre's lights shifted, preparing for the exit fade. But before the scene could end entirely, a new prompt appeared on the screen—not a gacha pull, but an announcement:

**NEXT SCENARIO: WOULD HE RULE MARVEL?**

The Avengers' section erupted in murmurs. Steve exchanged a look with Natasha. Tony straightened. Carol's expression shifted from intrigue to something more complex.

"Rule?" Carol said. "That's not—"

"A different question," Natasha finished. "Membership is about obedience. But rule is about power. About whether he'd want to hold authority."

Steve was quiet, thinking. The theatre's music began to fade.

"I don't think," Steve said slowly, "that he'd want to rule anything. But the theatre's going to ask the question anyway."

The lights dimmed further. The Avengers' block settled into silence, each warrior processing what had been revealed, what had been tested in that hypothetical scenario. The simulation had shown them something clear: Issei Hyoudou would be an ally of extraordinary value.

But never an employee.

Never subordinate.

Peer or nothing.
