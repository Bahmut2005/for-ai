# Chapter Twenty: Illegal Move Triaina

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Avengers heavy hitters (Thor, Captain Marvel, Hulk, the Thing, Vision) • Cosmic tacticians (Silver Surfer, Uatu, Gladiator, Karnak, Heimdall) • Villains (Doom, Loki, Mephisto, Taskmaster, Kang, Thanos)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Welsh Sonic Boost Knight" — **Epic**
2. "Welsh Dragonic Rook" — **Epic**
3. "Welsh Blaster Bishop" — **Epic**

---

The theatre had learned to read its own weather, and tonight the weather was wrong.

Not storm-grey, which meant grief. Not pub-gold, which meant somebody British was about to be humiliated. Tonight the impossible ceiling cooled to the colour of a tournament hall an hour before the match — that particular hush of lacquered wood and held breath — and the floor of the theatre, for the first time in twenty chapters, dimmed itself into sixty-four vast alternating squares of light and dark.

"Oh," said Sam Wilson, quietly, looking down at the board they were all suddenly sitting on. "It's a chess night."

They had been waiting for this one. The machine had teased it back at the end of the first arc — a flicker of three armoured silhouettes behind the words ILLEGAL MOVE TRIAINA, gone before anyone could screenshot it with their eyes — and then had spent nine chapters making them earn it. They had watched the boy train until he ruptured things. They had watched Fenrir's teeth go through him and Ravel Phenex log the damage in her terrible, meticulous ledger. They had watched him collapse from over-boosting while Banner took notes with a shaking hand, and they had watched him accept a duel out of pure dragon pride and get scolded for it by eight furious women, which several Avengers privately considered the most dangerous engagement of his career.

Nine chapters of cost. The theatre understood, by now, how this machine did arithmetic. Tonight was the purchase.

The gacha wheel descended — and it had changed. Its rings had locked into concentric ranks, and around its outermost band, tiny pieces revolved in stately procession: pawn, pawn, pawn, knight, rook, bishop. No queen. Everyone noticed there was no queen. The wheel spun once, with none of its usual smug clatter, and dropped three tokens into the crystal bowl at the same instant — a thing it had never done — and the chime they made was not a wineglass. It was a bell.

**ARC FINALE,** said the screen, in letters that did not glow so much as weigh. **THE MAKING OF A DRAGON: FINAL LESSON.**

"Everyone's laughing about the chess theme now," said Carol Danvers, not laughing. "Get it out of your systems."

---

The screen opened on Kyoto at night, and the theatre's street-level rows tensed, because they knew this shape of story: a city full of civilians, a mountain above it, and something terrible arranged on the summit. The Hero Faction — human supremacists with stolen god-tools, the gacha's captions explained with brutal economy — had kidnapped Yasaka, the nine-tailed guardian of the city, away from a small fox-eared daughter who had cried on Issei's shoulder about it. They were using the mother as a battery. The child was waiting at the bottom of the mountain for someone to bring her back.

At the summit, leaning on a spear that made every holy sense in the theatre itch even through the screen, stood a young man with calm eyes. Cao Cao. Human. Entirely, merely, magnificently human — and holding the True Longinus, the spear of the crucifixion, the first and strongest of the Sacred Gears.

"Ah," said Shang-Chi softly, reading his stance. "That one has never needed to be more than human. That's the most dangerous kind."

On-screen, Issei Hyoudou stood in battered Scale Mail with his team scattered and bleeding around him, and the theatre watched him do the thing this arc had taught him to do. He stopped. He *thought.* Nineteen chapters ago he would have charged.

"Ddraig," he said. "The thing we've been working on. The thing that breaks the rules."

"**It will hurt, partner,**" said the dragon-voice, and there was pride banked in it like an ember. "**Each shape will cost you what it gives. You understood that when you built them.**"

"Yeah." A breath, inside the helm. "He's got a spear that kills gods and hostages that need everything I've got. Cheating's all I have. Let's make an illegal move."

The gauntlet's gem flared, and the gacha froze the frame to lecture — it had learned, over twenty chapters, exactly how much this audience loved rules.

**EVIL PIECE SYSTEM — PROMOTION,** the screen wrote, laying out a chessboard of light. **A PAWN WHO ENTERS ENEMY TERRITORY MAY PROMOTE, WITH THE KING'S SANCTION, TO KNIGHT, ROOK, BISHOP, OR QUEEN. THIS IS THE RULE.**

A pawn of light marched up the board and became a queen, politely, legally.

**ISSEI HYOUDOU DOES NOT FOLLOW THE RULE. HE HAS FORGED, WITH THE WELSH DRAGON, A PROMOTION THAT ASKS NO SANCTION, WAITS FOR NO TERRITORY, AND POURS THE FULL OUTPUT OF THE BOOSTED GEAR INTO ONE PIECE'S NATURE AT A TIME. THE PIECE OBJECTS. THE BOARD OBJECTS. THE MOVE PROCEEDS.**

**DESIGNATION: ILLEGAL MOVE TRIAINA.**

**THE TRIDENT. THREE PRONGS. THREE PRICES.**

"He cheats at the game he was resurrected *into,*" murmured T'Challa, and on the far side of the theatre, in the dark where the villains sat, Mephisto's perpetual smile flickered — just once — like a candle noticing a draught.

---

**PULL ONE: WELSH SONIC BOOST KNIGHT — EPIC.**

"Change—" said Issei, on-screen, and the world took a breath. "**STAR SONIC!!**"

**[CHANGE: WELSH SONIC BOOST KNIGHT.]**

The armour *shed itself.* That was the only way to describe it — plates peeling away and dissolving into red light, the heavy dragon-shell thinning to something skeletal, sleek, almost cruelly minimal, all sweeping lines and exposed joints, built by someone who had crossed out the word "survive" and written "arrive" instead. The wings lengthened into blades of light.

Then he moved, and the screen apologized for its own frame rate.

The mountaintop cracked with overlapping sonic booms — not one boom, a *drumroll* of them, a boy breaking the sound barrier the way other people breathe. Cao Cao's spearwork was the finest pure technique the theatre had been shown in twenty chapters, a web of holy light with no gaps in it, and Issei went through the gaps that did not exist. He was behind the spear. He was under it. He was seven places, and three of them landed hits, and the summit's ancient stones ripped from their beds in his wake like the ground was trying to follow him.

In the theatre, Quicksilver stood up so fast his own chair fell over.

"Okay," Pietro said. "Okay. No. That's — hold on. That's not superspeed, superspeed is *mine*, that's a missile that learned footwork. What's his turning radius? Did anyone clock his turning radius? He's cornering at Mach — you can't *corner* at Mach, cornering at Mach is how you become a red smear with opinions—"

"Boost-doubling channelled entirely into velocity," said Vision, whose eyes had gone to instrument-glow. "Compounding every ten seconds. There is no ceiling on this except his skeleton."

And then the screen showed the price. Cao Cao — reading, adjusting, *human* — caught him with the butt of the spear, a grazing hit, barely a touch.

The Knight armour crumpled like foil. The theatre heard ribs go. Issei tumbled a quarter mile through a shrine gate and came up coughing blood inside a helm with a crack across its eye.

"**The Knight buys speed with your shell, partner,**" said Ddraig, steady, unsurprised. "**In this shape, you must never be touched. That is the wager.**"

Up in the Inhuman rows, Karnak had not moved for the entire sequence. Now he spoke, in the flat voice of a man reading a fracture in a load-bearing wall.

"Flaw: the form is a bet that speed is armour. Against anything that reads patterns — a precognitive, an area-denial weapon, a lucky child with a net — the bet fails catastrophically. One touch. That is the whole flaw. One touch."

The screen, in what was becoming a habit, stamped the vision with glowing text at the very moment he finished:

**SUB-FORM ONE: OVERWHELMING SPEED. SACRIFICES: DURABILITY. ONE TOUCH.**

Karnak looked at the screen. The screen did not look away. He sat back, very slightly disturbed to have been agreed with.

---

**PULL TWO: WELSH DRAGONIC ROOK — EPIC.**

On-screen, Issei spat blood, looked at the spear that had touched him once and nearly ended him, and changed his bet.

"Change — **SOLID IMPACT!!**"

**[CHANGE: WELSH DRAGONIC ROOK.]**

Where the Knight had shed armour, the Rook *hoarded* it. Plate stacked on plate; the frame broadened until the silhouette was less "boy in armour" and more "siege engine that had heard about boys"; the forearms swelled into rammed towers of dragon-scale, and when he set his feet, the mountain accepted him as a permanent geographic feature.

Cao Cao put the True Longinus — the spear that unmade gods — square into his chest.

The theatre flinched. The Rook did not. The blessed point skidded, gouging a molten line across the plates, and Issei took the single greatest holy weapon in his cosmology across the sternum the way the Thing takes weather.

Then he hit back.

The gacha slowed the punch out of respect. One blow — no flight, no flourish, a short, ugly, perfectly weighted straight — and the shockwave alone flattened a square kilometre of summit forest, peeled the face off a neighbouring ridge, and rang the city's ten thousand temple bells at once, so that far below, a small fox-eared girl looked up toward the mountain where her mother was.

In the theatre, Ben Grimm rose slowly to his feet, a mountain range standing to salute a new peak.

"THAT'S the stuff," the Thing rumbled, with tears he would deny under oath. "Look at that form. Look at them SHOULDERS. Kid skipped every fancy lesson and went straight to the only sentence that matters." He wiped his eye with a granite thumb. "It's clobberin' time in *Japanese.*"

The Hulk, who had been watching from his customary demolished quadrant with the sulky attention he gave all large strong things that weren't him, made a low sound. "Hulk could take that punch," he said. Pause. "Hulk would *respect* that punch, though." From Hulk, the theatre understood, this was a knighthood.

Thor was already pouring. "The Rook!" he boomed. "The tower that walks! In Asgard we would call this shape *skjaldborg-of-one* — a shield-wall with a temper! Boy-dragon, thou hast built thyself a fortress— oh. Oh, no. Watch."

Because the screen was showing the price. Cao Cao, spear-work fluid, had simply stopped being where the Rook could reach. He circled. The Rook turned like a cathedral trying to waltz — majestic, devastating, four steps behind — and every rotation cost Issei ground, position, tempo, while the human with the spear carved lines into his flanks and never once stood still.

"Flaw," said Karnak, before the screen could beat him to it. "The form is a bet that the enemy will consent to stand in front of you. Mobility is not reduced; it is *forfeited.* Anyone patient enough to walk in circles defeats the strongest defence on that board without ever testing it."

**SUB-FORM TWO: IMMENSE POWER AND DEFENCE. SACRIFICES: MANOEUVRABILITY. THE TOWER DOES NOT DANCE.**

"Stop *doing* that," Karnak told the screen.

Gladiator of the Shi'ar Imperial Guard — who had spent nineteen chapters filing Issei under "local fauna, developing" — spoke for the first time that night, arms folded, mohawk immaculate.

"Imperial assessment revised," he said. "That defence turned the edge of a god-killing weapon. In Shi'ar doctrine, any asset that can anchor a battle-line against theotech is classified at the squadron level, not the individual level." A beat. "He is sixteen."

"He is," said Captain America quietly, from the row where the coaches sat, "and he built all three of these *after* losing. Look at the design philosophy. Every form is an answer to a specific way he's been beaten. Fenrir taught him he could die fast — so he built something faster. Sairaorg taught him he could be out-toughed — so he built the tower." Steve's jaw worked. "This isn't a power-up. This is a kid turning every scar he owns into doctrine. That's the arc we just watched. That's what it was *for.*"

---

**PULL THREE: WELSH BLASTER BISHOP — EPIC.**

On-screen the fight had turned, as fights against patient geniuses do, into a losing ledger. And Issei Hyoudou, bleeding inside his fortress, did the last thing the theatre expected of the boy from chapter one.

He retreated. He gave ground *on purpose*, and as he did, he shouted — not a battle cry, a *request*: "Buy me time! Kiba, Xenovia — I need a minute! Maybe two!"

And his comrades, without one syllable of question, threw themselves between him and the strongest human alive, because they had exactly that much faith in whatever he was about to become.

"He asked for help," said Sam Wilson, sitting forward. "Loudest kid in two universes just used his big moment to ask for help. Somebody's been coaching him." He glanced at Steve. Steve said nothing, but the corner of his mouth committed treason.

"Change—" said Issei, kneeling, gauntlet blazing, voice dropping into something the theatre had never heard from him: *calm.* "**FANG BLAST!!**"

**[CHANGE: WELSH BLASTER BISHOP.]**

The armour rebuilt itself a third way — neither sprinter nor fortress but *emplacement*: the plates ribbing back into a braced, rooted stance, and over each shoulder, with a sound like a cathedral organ clearing its throat, rose a cannon. Two of them. Dragon-mouthed, rune-throated, drinking the Boosted Gear's doubling output not into limbs or plate but into *stockpile.*

**[BOOST.]** said the gear. **[BOOST.]** **[BOOST.]** **[BOOST.]**

The charge took a full minute, and the gacha made them sit through every second of it — the cannons whining up through registers that made Heimdall tilt his head, the air around the kneeling boy beginning to grieve, light bending wrong near the muzzles, Kiba and Xenovia paying for each of those sixty seconds in blood and steel just off-centre of frame.

"Sixty seconds of charge," said Taskmaster, in the villains' dark, to no one. "Sixty seconds of standing still in a fight." He said it like a man reading a price tag and finding it obscene. He would revise this opinion in approximately forty seconds more.

"**DRAGON BLASTERRRR!!**"

The theatre had seen the mountain-erasing blast of basic Scale Mail back in Arc One, and had recalibrated accordingly. This was not that. This was not a bigger version of that. When the twin cannons fired their braided lance of red, the screen's sound cut out entirely — not artistically; the theatre's reality simply declined to render it — and the summit of the mountain, the fortress on it, the army in the fortress, and the *geometry those things had occupied* went away. Not destroyed. *Audited.* And for one held frame, around the wound the beam left in the world, space itself visibly puckered — distances disagreeing with each other, a ripple of wrongness where here and there briefly lost track of which was which — before reality smoothed its coat and pretended nothing had happened.

Cao Cao stood at the edge of the erasure, having survived on pure precognitive genius and the last inch of his spear's power, and the calm in his eyes had finally, finally cracked.

Issei's cannons vented steam and folded away. He swayed. The Triaina armour dissolved entirely, and he went down to his hands and knees in the dirt, empty as a wrung cloth, unable to lift his own head — and grinning anyway, because down the mountain a fox-eared girl was about to get her mother back.

**SUB-FORM THREE: STRATEGIC ARTILLERY. THE DRAGON BLASTER LEVELS BATTLEFIELDS AND BRIEFLY DISTORTS LOCAL SPACE-TIME. SACRIFICES: STAMINA. CHARGE IS SLOW. THE CANNON FIRES TWICE, AND THEN THE BOY IS DONE.**

---

The lights came up, and the theatre discovered it had rearranged itself.

Not physically — although Quicksilver's chair was still on its back — but in some deeper seating chart. Nobody was lounging. The heavy hitters had drifted forward. The tacticians had stopped taking notes on paper and started taking them on each other's faces. And the villains' gallery, which had treated nineteen chapters of this boy as a charming regional soap opera, had gone quiet in the specific way a casino goes quiet when someone starts winning *correctly.*

Carol Danvers spoke first, because someone had to, and she said it like filing paperwork, which for Carol was a form of prayer.

"Reclassification. As of tonight I'm striking 'promising underdog' from every assessment I've got on this kid. New designation: regional-calamity-class, conditional, team-force-multiplier. Knight form outruns most of our fliers. Rook form eats hits that would put half this room in the medbay. And the Bishop—" she paused, and the pause had a beam of light in it. "The Bishop is a strategic weapon with a sixteen-year-old's cardio as its only arms-control treaty. If he showed up in our sky tomorrow, I don't scramble Avengers. I scramble *everyone,* and I pray he's still the kid who asks for help."

"He is the kid who asks for help," said Steve. "That's the entire reason the number doesn't scare me."

"The number scares *me,*" said Banner, mildly, "and I'd like to say why, because I've been the number." He took off his glasses, cleaned them, put them back on. "It's not the output. It's that every one of those forms has a *cost he agreed to in advance.* Speed that can't be touched. Power that can't move. A cannon that empties him. He's not suppressing his monster — he's *invoicing* it. Line items." A small, complicated smile. "I spent twenty years trying to build that ledger. He's sixteen and he's already itemized."

Vision's voice came level as a spirit-gauge. "Additional finding. All three forms presuppose allies. The Knight needs someone to hold what it cannot survive; the Rook needs someone to catch what it cannot chase; the Bishop needs someone to buy its sixty seconds. Statistically, this is not a lone-warrior arsenal. It is the arsenal of someone who has never once planned to fight alone." A pause of exactly the wrong length. "I find I trust that more than any durability figure."

From the cosmic tiers, the Silver Surfer had drifted forward off his customary aloof altitude, board angled toward the dark screen like a compass needle finding a new pole.

"I have levelled battlefields," he said, and the theatre hushed for him, because the Surfer never volunteered his résumé. "I know the weight of a weapon that ends the argument rather than winning it. Heralds carry such weight because a Devourer requires it of them. This child built it *himself*, out of chess and stubbornness — and then chained it to his own exhaustion so it could never become his habit." The Surfer's chrome face did something almost like wonder. "We named him underdog. The word is dead. Let no one here use it again."

No one ever did.

Heimdall, who saw everything and editorialized about almost none of it, added one sentence: "When the great cannon fired, I watched the space around the wound. For a moment, distances *lied.* I have seen that lie before only near the roots of Yggdrasil, where things have been cut away." His golden gaze went, briefly, to the Watcher. "Someone should ask *him* what I mean."

But Uatu the Watcher stood apart, vast and pale, and his unease — the structural unease of a being who had found no timeline anywhere that contained this boy — had acquired a new floor beneath it. "The distortion signature of the Dragon Blaster," he said slowly, as if reading a word in his own archive that he had not written, "resembles the edge of an *excision.* The mark left when something is removed from what-is." He did not elaborate. Watchers never elaborated at the worst possible moment; it was practically the job description.

And across the theatre, in the villains' dark, Kang the Conqueror was on his feet.

He had laughed at the boy in Arc One. Most of that gallery had — Mephisto's indulgent chuckle, Loki's connoisseur smirk, the general professional condescension of men who collected planets toward a teenager who collected rejections. Nobody in that section was laughing now.

"That was time trauma," Kang said, flat and precise, a man identifying his own signature on a document he never signed. "Localized, crude, self-limiting — and produced by a *chest cannon* built out of a chess metaphor. Do you comprehend what I am telling you? Causality has a scar tissue, and I have spent forty centuries learning to cut it *politely,* and that child just bruised it by accident on a Tuesday." He sat back down slowly. "He goes in my equations now. Every version of them. Every century of them."

"Doom concurs," said Doom, and closed his notebook with a sound like a verdict — the notebook he had kept on Issei Hyoudou since chapter one. The theatre had learned to fear the notebook. This was worse. "Doom is finished taking notes on the boy. The boy is now a *constant.* One does not take notes on constants; one designs around them." The mask turned fractionally toward the heroes' section. "Doom advises you all to begin. Any plan for any world that does not account for where the dragon is standing is no longer a plan. It is a wager."

Taskmaster laughed once — short, unhappy, professional. "I watched all three forms frame by frame. You know what I got? Nothing. There's nothing to *copy.* It's not technique — the kid's technique is honestly still mediocre — it's arithmetic wearing armour. You can't photographic-reflex a *budget.*" He pulled his hood down over the skull mask. "I don't do jobs where he might show up. Adding it to the contract boilerplate tonight."

Mephisto had not spoken. Mephisto, who had spent twenty chapters delighted, gleeful, treating Rias Gremory's eight-Pawn expenditure as the insider tip of the eon — sat with his fingers steepled and his smile *gone*, doing math visibly behind his eyes, and when Loki arched a brow at him, the demon said only: "I priced him in chapter five. The market has moved. It has moved out of *my* range, little prince, and I own several afterlives." Which, from Mephisto, was not an expression of loss. It was an expression of *awe*, and everyone near him found it far more frightening than his smile.

Loki himself said nothing at all. But those seated closest observed that the god of stories had stopped looking at the gauntlet the way a thief looks at a jewel, and started looking at the boy the way a chess player looks at a piece that has just done something the rules of the game do not contain. One does not pickpocket weather. One *forecasts* it.

And in the deepest seat of that gallery, where the shadows were less an absence of light than a professional courtesy, Thanos of Titan — who had watched nineteen chapters in a silence so complete that half the theatre had forgotten to be afraid of it — inclined his head one single degree toward the screen, and moved, on whatever board he kept behind those eyes, one piece from one column to another.

No one saw which columns. Everyone understood the gesture.

The laughing was over. That was the threshold, and the whole room felt itself cross it together: heroes and villains, for one strange minute, leaning across the aisle to compare threat assessments like rival scholars sharing a text — the theatre's first joint calculation, made about a boy face-down in the dirt of a mountain he had personally edited, grinning about somebody else's mother.

---

The screen was not quite finished.

It showed Issei carried down the mountain on Kiba's back, too drained to walk, loudly narrating his own victory in a way that cost him the room's solemnity and none of its respect. It showed a fox-eared girl hit him at a dead run and refuse to let go. And then it dimmed to the tournament-hall hush one final time, and laid out its chessboard of light, and on it: three pieces glowing red. Knight. Rook. Bishop.

And behind them, greyed out, silhouetted, locked — a fourth piece.

A queen.

"**Not yet, partner,**" said Ddraig's voice over the dark, gentle as a door left open. "**The trident has three prongs. But you and I both know what the pieces want to become.**"

The silhouette burned faintly crimson at its crown, and the theatre — every faction, every aisle, every column of every ledger — made the same note at the same time.

Then, in letters of quiet fire:

**ARC TWO COMPLETE: THE MAKING OF A DRAGON.**

**THE DRAGON IS MADE. WHAT FOLLOWS IS WHAT DRAGONS GUARD.**

And beneath it, for exactly one frame, the old line — the one that had haunted them since the beginning, now flickering as if the Dragon Blaster's scar had reminded the machine of something:

**HE IS THE ONE WHO WILL STAND BEFORE THE THINGS YOU CANNOT.**

The gacha wheel rose back toward the ceiling. As it went, its rings shifted, and around the outermost band a new track lit up in warm, domestic, utterly ominous gold, with a small chime like a doorbell:

**NEW BANNER UNLOCKED: FAMILY-CLASS.**

"...I don't like that font," said Sam Wilson. "Why is that font *cozy?*"

Nobody answered. The lights went down on a theatre that had arrived, twenty chapters ago, to watch a stranger — and sat now, in the dark, planning around a constant.
