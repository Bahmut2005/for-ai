# Chapter Fifty-Two: The Mutant Question

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Krakoa's Quiet Council • Arakko • X-Men • Magneto

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Issei Defending Mutants from Devils" — **Rare**
2. "Issei Versus Apocalypse and Rizevim" — **Epic**

---

The theatre held its breath between chapters the way a city holds its breath between sirens.

Tony's argument still echoed in the ceiling: *need to monitor, need contingency, need containment.* Steve's counter-argument hung in the air like smoke, resolute and unshakeable: *he's been given time and he's used it to become good.* The Avengers had filed back to their tier unresolved, which meant the theatre as a whole remained unresolved, which meant the next faction to face the gacha pulls would do so under the specific weight of uncertainty.

That faction was waiting, and it was mutant.

Krakoa's Quiet Council occupied the row directly parallel to the Avengers' box—symmetrically positioned, as if the theatre itself was holding court between two sovereignties. Emma Frost, immaculate in white, sat forward with her hands folded and her telepathy bristling at the edges of containment; Magneto occupied the corner seat, silver-haired and composed, though the small rattle of metal dust in the air suggested his control was a habit he maintained through effort; Exodus stood between them, black-robed, watching. Mystique was there, shape-shifter's eyes sharp and calculating. Presently Xavier wheeled himself forward to join them, accompanied by a contingent of the X-Men: Cyclops at his shoulder, Storm beside him, Wolverine in the back row flanking the space where Arakko's war-captains had gathered—Okkult, Juwali, and a dozen others, Arakko survivors who came to the theatre not as subjects of Krakoa but as witnessed powers in their own right.

The gacha wheel assembled itself with a soft tick-tick-tick, and the screen printed:

**GACHA PULL: 1 OF 2.**

The wheel spun fast, deliberately—this was not a rarity that would pause for drama. It struck red, warm red, the color of mercy rather than blood, and the theatre filled with that color as the screen stamped:

**"ISSEI DEFENDING MUTANTS FROM DEVILS" — RARITY: RARE.**

Wolverine made a low sound. Emma Frost's hands tightened. Magneto did not move.

**[MUTANT CHILDREN,]** said the screen, and the vision took them.

---

A basement in Prague, converted church, consecrated ground and then *deconsecrated* by the presence of devils—three of them, minor-rank, the vision supplied with casual knowledge. They were in the process of moving cages of children, mutant children, identified by the faint shimmer of power around each small body. Kidnapping with a market in mind: the devil hierarchy paid for mutant genetics, for the potential locked into mutant flesh. Standard predation.

The cages were old iron, spelled with something that muted the mutant flame inside them. The children were silent, the particular silence of small things learning that screaming does not help.

Then the wall *opened*.

Not broke through—opened, the red shell of something vast splitting the brick and mortar like it was curtseying aside. For a moment the audience saw him in silhouette: six-foot-three, maybe six-four, a Japanese boy in a school uniform against a cathedral's broken edge, and then the gacha pulled back and filled in the details at leisure.

Issei Hyoudou's eyes were burning green.

"Okay," he said to the three devils, and his voice was steady in the way voices get when they've already made the surrender decision and are just now notifying the body. "Last chance. Three, two, one—"

They ran.

Issei did not chase them; he *let* them go, because the children were priority, and priority was always priority, and he understood that down to the cellular level in a way that no other power explained. Instead he walked to the nearest cage and gripped the lock—not with his hand, but with a soft red glow that built around the mechanism, not force, something *gentler*, something that was asking the lock to *please let go*—and it did.

The first child stepped out, mutant-young, maybe eight years old, eyes still full of the silence.

"Hey," Issei said, and his tone shifted—the low, safe voice of someone who had practiced being gentle with small, terrified things. "It's okay. It's going to be okay. Can you do me a favor? Can you take the other kids and get out of the basement? There's a street up those stairs. There's a café two blocks north on Nerudova Street. Go there, tell them an Asian guy sent you, and wait for me. Can you do that?"

The child nodded, too shocked to disbelieve.

One by one, Issei opened the cages. One by one, the mutant children filed out, following the eldest, and he did not leave his position between them and the devils' escape route until the last child was up the stairs and gone from the vision's view. Only then did he turn to where the three devils were waiting, regrouping, preparing themselves for the fight that was now inevitable.

And he walked toward them without armor, without power, without Boost, just a boy in a red shirt who had committed himself to not letting *anyone* hurt children ever again, and the gacha held that image—ordinary boy, burning green eyes, the simplicity of absolute certainty—and then:

**[BOOST.]**

The screen released them.

---

The theatre was very quiet.

"That's not—" Cyclops started, and stopped. "That's not him fighting. That's him choosing."

"Which is the fight," Professor Xavier said, softly. "Choosing to stand between them and harm. Always that choice first."

Emma Frost's expression had not changed, but the whiteboard in front of her—she was writing something, very fast, words appearing in telepath-script: *Not a threat to mutantkind if values align. Question: do they?*

Magneto had stood, slowly, his posture changed from the comfortable power of minutes ago into something older, something that remembered fighting for his own small ones. He was looking at where the vision had been, at the empty air that had shown him a child stepping out of a cage.

"I have seen boys like this before," he said to the room, and his voice was level, dangerous with control. "The ones who believe they can carry the weight themselves. They break."

"He's still carrying it," Wolverine said. There was something in his tone that wasn't argument, more like acknowledgment. "Question's whether he breaks or doubles down."

Storm spoke up from her seat: "The gacha shows mercy. The system doesn't show mercy. What's the second pull?"

The wheel was already assembling.

**GACHA PULL: 2 OF 2.**

This spin was different—faster, harder, the colors a blur of urgency. It struck Epic-purple, deep and absolute, and the colour filled the theatre like an warning. The screen printed:

**"ISSEI VERSUS APOCALYPSE AND RIZEVIM" — RARITY: EPIC.**

**[SIMULATION. HYPOTHETICAL. NOT OBSERVED.]** the screen added, and that made the whole room sit up straighter—this was the theatre showing them *what could be*, not what *was*.

**[EXPERIMENTAL PARAMETERS: ISSEI AT CARDINAL CRIMSON PROMOTION. MAGNETO AT COMPOSITE POWER. APOCALYPSE AT BASELINE.]**

Then the vision opened like a fist.

---

A ruined city, featureless, built for war-testing.

Issei was already armored—Cardinal Crimson Promotion, the gacha supplied, the integrated form of his three Triaina specializations fused into one. Red armor, ornate, with gold trim and the suggestion of wings. On his wrist, the Boosted Gear glowed faintly. In the air around him, the sense of gathered power, of Boost stacked and banked inside the armor like coins in a purse.

Across from him stood two figures.

One was Magneto—or a composite of him, the theatre explained silently. All the power he'd ever wielded, at the peak. Magnetism sharp enough to renegotiate atomic structure. The weight of thousands of tons of metal suspended in the air around him like a crown.

The other was Apocalypse.

He was massive, four-armed, the blue of his skin so dark it was nearly black. Power radiated off him in waves that made the gacha's simulation shimmer with the effort of containing it. Ancient. Omega-level. The kind of being that had walked through apocalypses and called them Tuesday.

"Come," Apocalypse said, and his voice was amused, the amusement of something that has won so many fights it has stopped learning from them.

Issei boosted.

**[BOOST.]**

He moved like a meteor, armored form cutting across the empty space, and the air *broke* around him—not from his speed alone, but from the power he was carrying, the Solid Impact Booster firing on acceleration. The force of his punch would have leveled mountains.

It hit Magneto's magnetic shield and *stopped*.

Not deflected—stopped. The energy of the blow was caught by the field and redistributed harmlessly into the earth. Magneto had barely moved.

**[BOOST. BOOST. BOOST.]**

Issei came again and again, adapting, using the Crimson Blaster to strafe, trying to come in from angles. The magnetic field flowed like water, absorbing every direction. Magneto was smiling, not cruelly, just with the satisfaction of mastery. Metal—there was so much metal in a city, and he could *sing* with it.

Then Apocalypse moved.

His arm came up, and it was not a punch—it was a gesture, casual, the way you might wave away smoke. Reality around Issei *bent*. Not broken, not shattered, but bent out of true, the laws that had let him fly suddenly negotiating their existence.

Issei fell.

He caught himself, armor smoking, and the gacha supplied the damage: Apocalypse-level hax didn't crack his armor, but it renegotiated *how* the armor was meant to function. For a few seconds, gravity had been holding different opinions about which direction was down.

**[PENETRATE!]** Ddraig's voice roared out of the gauntlet, and Issei nodded, accepting the offer. He charged again, this time with the power-piercing gift activated—no more bouncing off shields, but striking through them, hitting the core of the problem.

He got close to Apocalypse. He actually struck *through* the old mutant's defense and—

—and Apocalypse casually grew his arm four times larger and *backhanded* him across the simulation-city.

Issei impacted a building hard enough to indent his shape into it. When he pulled himself out, his armor was cracked.

The simulation showed thirty seconds of the fight continuing, each exchange teaching Issei something and teaching him too late to use it. Magneto's magnetism was *perfect*—it could counter any approach, any direction, any metal in his armor that the boy tried to leverage. Apocalypse was Apocalypse: old, adapting, healing, powered by mutant genetics that had thousands of years to refine themselves.

The simulation showed Issei landing a good hit. It showed him barely escaping a killing blow. It showed him boosting up a Longinus Smasher charge that would have obliterated Magneto, and Magneto simply ripping the metal out of his armor, out of the air around him, and using it to block.

And then the simulation showed the moment where Issei, breathing hard, realized he was going to *lose*.

Not die—the simulation implied he might escape, or stalemate into exhaustion. But lose. Face an opponent he couldn't overcome, not because he wasn't powerful, but because he was fighting *two* Omega-level threats at once and one of them was basically immortal.

The vision shut down abruptly, the screen printing:

**[SIMULATION RESULT: INCONCLUSIVE. STALEMATE IF EXTENDED PAST 15 MINUTES. APOCALYPSE TOO ADAPTIVE. MAGNETO CONTAINMENT CANNOT BE OVERCOME BY FORCE. RECOMMENDATION: DO NOT ENGAGE WITH BOTH SIMULTANEOUSLY.]**

The theatre exhaled, slowly.

---

"Well," Storm said into the silence, "that answers one question."

"He's not invulnerable," Cyclops said. There was something like relief in his voice, but also something like concern—as if he'd been braced for the opposite answer and wasn't sure which one he preferred. "Magneto alone could keep him contained if they fought. Apocalypse alone, Issei might win, depending on the day."

"Together, he's *limited*," Emma Frost said, still writing. "That's what matters. Not whether he can beat us, but whether he can be beaten. There's a difference."

Magneto had sat back down, slowly. When he spoke, his voice was different—still measured, but carrying the weight of older thoughts. "I watched a boy choose to open cages. Then I watched a simulation where he faced down two Omega-level mutants rather than yield ground." He paused. "He did not call for help. He did not surrender the city. He fought alone, with full knowledge he was losing, and he fought *anyway*."

"That's stupidity," Exodus said flatly.

"No," Magneto said. "That's principle. There is a difference."

Xavier wheeled himself slightly forward. "The question before us is not whether Issei is a threat. The gacha has established that multiple times. The question is whether he is a *directed* threat. Whether he would use his power to stand against us, or beside us, or ignore us entirely." He paused. "Mutant-kind has enemies enough without gaining another. But we also have enemies enough to benefit from another standing against them."

"You want to recruit him," Mystique said. It wasn't a question.

"I want to know him," Xavier corrected. "And I want to ensure he is not turned against us. The simulation shows he can be matched—two Omega-level mutants can contain him, though at cost. If we know that, and he does not, we have leverage."

Wolverine stood up from the back row, his claws sliding out and back in—a gesture of frustration. "You're all talking like he's a pawn to move. From what I'm seeing? Kid opens cages. Kid fights two Omegas knowing he'll lose and does it anyway. That's not someone you *contain*, that's someone you *trust* or you get out of his way."

"There is no third option," Magneto said.

"Sure there is," Wolverine said. "You could *listen* to him."

The Arakko contingent had not spoken during any of this, but now one of them—Juwali, a war-captain with visible scars—inclined her head slightly.

"We will watch," she said. "Krakoa will watch. When the Dragon flies over our island, we will see which way his wings turn. Then we decide."

Professor Xavier nodded. "A fair assessment."

The screen had not left—it was waiting, patient, for the theatre to settle. When it did, when the argument quieted to a murmur, it printed:

**NEXT: DOOM'S INVITATION.**

And somewhere in the villain's tier, someone was opening a letter written in very careful script, addressed to a Dragon Emperor he had not yet met, signed with a single word: *Equal*.

- **Cardinal Crimson Promotion** combines all three Triaina specializations into one integrated form: Solid Impact Booster, Crimson Blaster, enhanced armor with visible cracking point under Omega-level assault.
- **Magneto's composite Omega power** can counter Issei's momentum-based attacks entirely through magnetic field manipulation and metal renegotiation. Penetrate bypasses normal defenses but cannot overcome Magneto's strategic containment.
- **Apocalypse** can renegotiate reality-physics on a local scale (e.g., changing gravitational direction mid-fight), heal faster than Issei can damage him, and match adaptive strategy point-for-point. Two Omega-level threats simultaneously exceed Issei's capacity to *overcome* (though stalemate/escape remains viable).

**Alliance / Opinion Shifts:**
- **Magneto**: deeply affected by the cage-opening scene; recognizes the pattern from his own protective history; explicitly notes the difference between "stupidity" and "principle"; pivots from pure threat assessment to recognition of *aligned values* (protection of vulnerable beings).
