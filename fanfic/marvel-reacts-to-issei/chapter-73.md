The wheel had barely finished turning before the theatre understood this pull was different. Trauma-Class pulls always played the same way: the vision would show something, and then it would show the *cost* of that something, unflinching, uncut.

The vision opened on a converted temple compound outside Kyoto, one of the Khaos Brigade's smaller cells, the kind that didn't make headlines because it hadn't yet done anything headline-worthy. Twelve captives. Stray exorcists, a pair of low-ranking Church agents, and Asia Argento, who'd been lured out under the pretense of a wounded child needing a healer and hadn't come home.

Rias's peerage was assembling for the extraction. Issei stood over the projection table, running numbers with a focus that made Akeno's teasing die in her throat before it started.

"Guard rotation changes at 0230," Issei said. "Three-minute gap on the east wall. After that we're looking at full coverage for six hours."

"So we go at 0230," Kiba said.

"Then we're pulling twelve hostages during a rising alert cascade," Issei said. His voice had gone flat, the particular flatness of someone calculating rather than feeling. "Trigger the breach, alarms go up, twenty minutes to full lockdown. We're threading a needle with the whole compound waking up around us."

Xenovia's eyes narrowed. "You've already picked an order."

He looked up. "Asia's held separately. Inner chamber, past the second checkpoint. If I take her out first, personally, before the rest of the extraction starts drawing attention, I can have her clear before the alarm even has a reason to trigger."

"That's not the plan we built," Rias said carefully.

"I know."

"You'd be deviating from formation. Alone. In the one section of the compound with the least support."

"I know that too."

Nobody in the room needed him to explain why. Everyone remembered the light spear. Everyone remembered the courtyard. Nobody was going to be the one to tell him no.

---

The mission went exactly as planned for ninety seconds, and then it didn't.

Issei reached Asia's cell with time to spare, cut the ward binding her wrists, had her moving toward the outer corridor before the compound's secondary wards—wards nobody had flagged in the reconnaissance, wards keyed to *devil* energy signatures specifically, not exorcist or human—detected his presence and screamed the alert early.

He got Asia out. That part of the plan worked perfectly.

But the diversion he'd promised the rest of the team—the forty-five seconds of drawn attention that was supposed to buy Kiba and Xenovia a clean run through the eastern hall—never materialized, because the alert triggered from Issei's position instead of theirs, and the compound's response converged on the wrong corridor entirely.

Two of the Church agents, trapped in a support structure that had been quietly compromised by the earlier fighting, didn't make it out before the ceiling came down.

Issei learned about it back at the rendezvous point, Asia safe and shaking against his side, when Kiba delivered the count in a voice carefully scrubbed of judgment.

"They were in my sector," Issei said.

"You weren't in your sector," Rias said. Not unkind. Certain. "You were saving her. That's what you chose."

"I chose wrong."

"No," Xenovia said. "You chose *her*. That's not the same thing as wrong. It's just not free."

---

In the theatre, the reaction split along a line the audience had been dreading since the pull's rarity flashed onscreen.

Steve Rogers absorbed it the way he absorbed all of it—quietly, with the specific grief of a man who had made this exact trade himself more than once and never once gotten to feel good about it. "Impossible choices are the weight of command," he said. "He made the human one. That doesn't mean it was free."

Frank Castle didn't share the room's patience.

"He made a choice," Castle said, flat as a blade. "Two people are dead because he chose the girl he loves over the mission he agreed to. I've put men in the ground for less discipline than that."

"He'd have gone through hell for those two Church agents just the same, if he'd known where they'd end up," Matt Murdock said, quiet, sensing the room the way he sensed everything—by weight and rhythm rather than sight. "That's the part that should scare you, Frank. Not that he chose wrong. That he'd make the same choice again, every time, and he *knows* it, and he's still going to live with what it costs."

"That's the moral calculus nobody prepares you for," Murdock continued. "Not whether the choice was right. Whether you could have made the other one. And for him—for a boy who watched a light spear go through the girl he's protecting—the answer is no. That's not a flaw. That's just what loving someone costs when you also have the power to choose who lives."

Natasha's voice was quieter than either of theirs. "The two who died don't come back. Not in a rewrite, not in a retcon. They stay dead. That's the part that should scare *all* of us, watching this like it's entertainment. Somewhere in that boy's world, two families are grieving people he'll carry for the rest of his life and never be able to apologize to properly."

The cosmic observer section had said nothing throughout, and it was the Living Tribunal's triple gaze that finally broke the silence, its voice landing on the chamber like verdict rather than commentary.

**"THIS THEATRE DOES NOT OFFER ABSOLUTION. IT OFFERS ONLY THE RECORD. THE RECORD SHOWS TWO DEATHS THAT WILL NOT BE UNDONE. THE RECORD SHOWS A CHOICE THAT WILL BE MADE AGAIN. BOTH THINGS ARE TRUE. NEITHER CANCELS THE OTHER."**
