## The Silence Before the Reckoning

The theatre had fallen into a different kind of quiet than before—not the awed hush that followed cosmic revelations, but the suffocating silence of impending judgment. The stage shifted as the remaining chapters drew near the critical juncture of this arc. The lights dimmed to deep indigo, and the rows of assembled heroes, villains, and cosmic beings felt the weight of what was coming.

This was the hour of the architects themselves.

In the balcony box reserved for the most dangerous neutrals, Nick Fury's single eye tracked the stage with the calculation of a man who had spent decades in rooms where the fate of worlds was decided without bloodshed—mostly. Beside him, Doctor Doom's gloved fingers steepled before his mask with an expression of barely concealed amusement. Neither man spoke. They didn't need to. Both understood that what was about to be revealed would shatter the last pretense of the Illuminati's unified vision.

The gacha machine on stage hummed with intent.

## First Pull: The Secret Meeting of the Illuminati

The holographic display erupted with gold light and celestial chimes. **RARE**.

> **"Secret Meeting of the Illuminati"**

The stage transformed into a circular chamber buried deep beneath the Baxter Building in Manhattan. The walls were lined with sigils and quantum-shielded panels that pulsed with defensive magic and cutting-edge tech intertwined. No windows. No external feeds. This was a room designed so that not even omniscient deities could perceive what transpired within.

Six figures materialized around an obsidian table.

Doctor Strange sat at the head, his red cloak still faintly smoking from the residual magic of the Issei visions. His hands were steepled, and his face bore the exhaustion of a man who had sacrificed too much to leave anything to chance. The Eye of Agamotto hung beneath his collar, dark and vigilant.

"We need to speak plainly," Strange began, his voice steady as stone. "The projections are clear. Issei Hyoudou is on a trajectory toward Apocalypse AnswerArms—a form capable of planetary destruction. The question before us is not whether he *will* reach it. The question is what we do *before* that occurs."

Reed Richards leaned back in his chair, fingers moving through holographic equations only he could see. His brow was furrowed with the particular intensity of a man running catastrophe models. "Containment through conventional means is theoretically impossible. His power scales are non-linear. Even accounting for the wives as force-multipliers—"

"Which is the critical variable," interrupted Tony Stark, rising from his seat. His expression carried the blend of confidence and barely restrained aggression that had defined his approach to existential threats for years. "They are his anchor to this world. They are his *weakness*. I'm not talking about harming them. I'm talking about a surgical strike. Isolate him. Immobilize him before the AxA form reaches critical resonance. We've neutralized threats at smaller scales. This is a controlled, precise operation."

The arc reactor glowed faintly in his chest as he leaned forward. "I vote we strike. Not in anger. In prevention."

Professor Charles Xavier's expression remained calm, but his eyes—those piercing, perceptive eyes—held something that looked almost like pity. When he spoke, his voice was soft, which made it somehow more cutting.

"You're thinking like a man with a hammer, Tony. You see a problem that requires violence because violence is the language you've learned to speak. But what you're proposing isn't precision. It's an act of war." He paused, letting his gaze sweep to each member of the council. "You would alert every ally he possesses—and the canon has shown us, quite clearly, that his coalition is global in scope. His wives are not merely companions. They are bound to him through power itself. A strike against him is a strike against all of them. And you would be declaring, to the entire known universe, that the Earth—that *we*—will kill to maintain dominance."

Reed nodded slowly. "Charles is right about the cascade effects. If we move against him, we don't just face Issei. We face Rias Gremory's demonic networks, the dragon clans, the Angel Faction, the fallen angels. We face a coalition that has already proven it can operate across dimensional boundaries. A preemptive strike doesn't eliminate the threat. It unifies it."

T'Challa, the Black Panther, had been silent until now, his dark eyes reflecting the holographic displays with careful consideration. When he spoke, his voice carried the weight of a leader who had guided his nation through threats both mythological and mundane.

"Wakanda has survived by making alliances, not enemies. If we strike first, we tell every emerging power in this universe that we fear them enough to attack unprovoked. That is not the message a truly strong nation sends." He looked directly at Tony. "The greatest weapon is not the one you use. It is the one your enemy fears you might use while you are negotiating with open hands."

Namor, seated across from T'Challa with the air of a king more comfortable with dominion than compromise, simply shook his head. "Atlantis will not participate in preemptive war against a dragon who has not threatened us. We have observed his canon. He does not seek conquest of the seas. His focus is narrow, fierce, territorial to his partnerships—but not to our domain. A strike against him serves no Atlantean interest."

Strange raised a hand, and the holographic displays shifted. New equations bloomed in the air—ritual sequences, binding matrices, containment arrays.

"There is another approach," the Sorcerer Supreme said quietly. "Containment through treaty. Through magical accord. We could offer him terms. Legitimate recognition of his status. Boundaries for his expansion in exchange for non-interference in our operations."

"And he refuses?" Tony asked sharply.

"Then we fight with eyes open, knowing we tried diplomacy first."

The tension in the chamber was now visible—a palpable weight pressing down on each decision-maker. Tony's jaw clenched. Reed's equations accelerated, running faster, searching for variables that might justify intervention. Professor X remained still, reading the room with his considerable gifts, carefully not pushing his influence into any minds.

"This council will need to vote," Strange said at last. "But first, we should see what happens if we choose the path of force."

He gestured, and the holographic displays warped again.

## Second Pull: The Simulation of Preemptive Strike

The gacha machine roared, and the chimes that followed were discordant, wrong. **FORBIDDEN**.

> **"Simulation: Pre‑emptive Strike on Issei"**

The stage itself seemed to hold its breath.

What manifested was not a simple vision. It was a full sensory experience—time-dilated, immediate, and horrifying. The theatre audience gasped. Several beings shifted uncomfortably in their seats.

The simulation opened on Issei's dormitory room in Japan. He was asleep—actually asleep, alone for once, his form tucked into a bed in a place he considered safe. The canon had shown them this vulnerability, this moment when even the Dragon King could rest without his wives beside him.

Strike team phantoms phased through the walls. They moved with precision, with practiced coordination. Teleportation wards fell away—a significant expenditure of magical resources, but the Illuminati had many. The team was elite: operatives enhanced with magic, technology, and superhuman capability.

A binding circle activated beneath Issei's sleeping form.

And in that instant, everything catastrophically, irrevocably broke.

Issei's eyes snapped open—not with fear, but with the ancient, primal fury of a dragon recognizing a threat. The Boosted Gear activated on his arm, its roar echoing across multiple dimensional frequencies. The binding circle couldn't suppress it; nothing could suppress it on reflex.

But worse—the moment he woke, his wives knew.

Four separate locations triggered emergency protocols simultaneously. Rias Gremory's demonic powers flared, her pieces mobilizing across Kuoh. Akeno Himejima's fallen angel protocols activated, drawing power from realms the Illuminati didn't fully control. Asia Argento's healing and light magic synchronized with her companions. And Koneko Toujou—seemingly small, seemingly weak—triggered a Nekomata transformation that would have terrified most warrior collectives.

The simulation accelerated. What had been a careful, controlled operation collapsed into chaos in 2.3 seconds.

Issei didn't retreat. He *advanced*.

His form shifted mid-transformation, caught between human and dragon. The Boosted Gear was now amplifying not just his power, but his *intent*. In the simulation, his hands literally glowed with the accumulation of force. The strike team, for all their training, was caught in the devastating backwash of his emergence.

Three operatives fell in the first exchange.

Then the dragons came. Ddraig manifested in full form—colossal, red-scaled, and absolutely furious. The ancient dragon's roar shattered the simulation's sound barriers. From other dimensions, other dragon allies responded. The Dimensional Gap itself seemed to rupture with their arrival.

Issei's wives converged from four separate locations, their combined forces moving with the synchronization of beings bound by something far deeper than mere alliance. They weren't coordinating—they were *unified*, acting as extensions of a single tactical will.

The simulation showed the battle not in real-time but in devastating snapshots:

—Rias's demonic armies clashing with the strike team's reinforcements.

—Akeno's lightning and Issei's dragon fire colliding in the sky, the shockwaves registering on richter scales across the Pacific.

—Asia's healing networks bringing down strike team members by *removing their ability to heal*.

—Koneko's true form moving with speed that split the ground in her wake.

The collateral damage began accruing immediately.

Buildings collapsed. Entire city blocks destabilized. The simulation calculated civilian casualties in real-time: 40,000. 400,000. 4 million.

And the battle was spreading.

The counter-strike, once initiated, didn't remain localized. Issei's coalition had prepared contingencies for exactly this kind of assault. The strike team's teleportation vectors were traced and disrupted. The Illuminati's agents found themselves cut off, surrounded, outmatched.

Within the simulation, Doctor Strange—the simulated version of him—tried to open a portal for retreat. The dimensional barriers held, reinforced by Issei's demonic allies. There was no escape.

The simulation jumped forward. Issei in full dragon form. His wives flying beside him, their combined aura visible even in the ghostly holographic display. The strike team annihilated. The Illuminati forces in the field wiped out.

Then came the worst part.

The simulation showed what came next: retaliation.

Issei's coalition didn't stop at defense. They moved against the Illuminati itself—against their bases, their holdings, their resources. The Baxter Building burned in the simulation. Latverian borders flickered as Doom's forces were consulted. SHIELD's helicarrier went dark.

The simulation ended with a blood-red screen and text that hung in the air like a curse:

> **OUTCOME: EARTH CASUALTY ESTIMATE 40 MILLION — 1.2 BILLION. VICTOR: ISSEI AND ALLIED COALITION. ILLUMINATI STATUS: DEFUNCT.**

Silence crashed down like a hammer.

## The Theatre's Reckoning

Not a single member of the assembled heroes or villains made a sound. Nick Fury's jaw was a line of steel. Doctor Doom's posture suggested something between admiration and cold calculation. Even those who had been staunchest supporters of preemptive action felt the weight of seeing the outcome laid bare.

In the theatre section reserved for the Illuminati themselves, Tony Stark looked away first. Not defeated, but... reconsidering. Reed Richards' expression was unreadable, but his fingers had gone very still. Professor Xavier's face held no triumph, only a deep, almost melancholic understanding. Namor met the stage with the serenity of a king vindicated in his caution. T'Challa nodded once, as though confirming something he had already calculated. Strange simply closed his eyes for a long moment, as if absorbing the full weight of what had been avoided.

"The vote," Strange said, his voice carrying across the theatre with sorcerous resonance. "Stands recorded. Illuminati vote on preemptive strike against Issei Hyoudou:"

He paused, and in that pause, the theatre seemed to hold its breath.

"Tony Stark: *Yes*, strike recommended."

"Reed Richards: *Abstain*. The mathematics do not support intervention."

"Doctor Stephen Strange: *No*, contain through treaty and diplomatic accord."

"Professor Charles Xavier: *No*, he is an ally of Earth, not an enemy."

"Namor: *No*, Atlantis remains neutral."

"T'Challa: *No*, Wakanda favors alliance over assassination."

Strange's eye swept the assembled crowd. "Result: Five No. One Yes. Zero Abstain. Consensus denied. Preemptive strike rejected. Illuminati remains... fractured on policy."

The language was careful. Fractured. Not unified. Not in agreement. The Illuminati had decided—barely, and only under duress—not to strike. But the fracture remained. Tony's single vote of yes hung in the air like a warning.

In the villain's section, Doom's laughter was silent but visible in the set of his shoulders. Fury simply made a note in a small black book—a book everyone present recognized as the record of secrets, alliances, and leverage.

---

## The Hook Forward

As the holographic displays faded, Strange turned to the theatre audience one final time. His words were layered with knowledge born from the Eye of Agamotto.

"The theatre has shown us truth," he said quietly. "And truth, once revealed in a place like this, cannot be hidden again. The Illuminati's deliberations were meant to remain secret. But the universe is watching now. Issei Hyoudou is watching. His wives are watching." A meaningful pause. "And they will know that at least one member of Earth's hidden leadership voted to strike them first."

The implication landed like a stone in still water.

"Next," came the formal announcement from the gacha system, "the Villains' Roundtable convenes. Chapter 57 awaits."
