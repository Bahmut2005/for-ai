# Chapter Two: Common-Rarity Pervert

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Spider-Family • Defenders • Street-Level Heroes • Sinister Six (Villain Block) • Deadpool (Uninvited)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Dragon Shot Misfire" — **Common**
2. "Dress Break Prototype" — **Uncommon**

---

The theatre had learned, in the way crowds learn things, that time between pulls was not time off. It was time to argue.

Somewhere in the third tier, Doctor Doom and Stephen Strange were still dissecting the Evil Piece system in low, hostile, fascinated voices, like two surgeons fighting over the same cadaver. Two rows down, Loki had drawn a diagram of the Boosted Gear on the back of a program that had not existed until he needed one. Thor kept confiscating the diagram. Loki kept having another one.

And in the great middle bowl of the room, where the street-level heroes had clumped together out of some unspoken herd instinct — capes with the capes, hoodies with the hoodies — Peter Parker was doing the thing he did when he was upset, which was talk.

"I'm just saying, eight Pawns," he said, for perhaps the ninth time. "That's the whole inventory. That's showing up to a poker game and shoving your house keys into the pot on the first hand."

"You've said that," said Jessica Jones, who was slouched so far down in the red velvet that she was functionally horizontal. "Nine times."

"It bears repeating."

"It really doesn't."

Miles Morales sat between them like a boy between arguing relatives on a long car ride. Gwen Stacy — the one from the universe where the spider had bitten her instead — had her boots up on the seat back in front of her, which belonged to the Rhino, who had complained once and then thought better of it.

"He died," Miles said quietly, and the row went still the way it always did when someone said it out loud. "Like — we watched a kid get murdered on his first date. And I keep waiting to feel normal about the fact that the screen just… kept going."

"You won't," said Matt Murdock, three seats over. He wasn't looking at the screen. He never was, exactly, though everyone had privately concluded that the theatre was doing something for him, or to him — feeding him the pulls in whatever language his senses spoke — because his reactions landed at the same moments as everyone else's, sometimes a half-second early. "You don't get to feel normal about it. That's the point of watching."

The wheel in the corner of the great screen began to tick.

It was a sound the entire theatre had learned in a single evening, the way you learn a fire alarm. Conversations died in expanding rings. Thousands of heads came up — Avengers, X-Men, gods, Ravagers, the cosmic tier with its terrible patience, the villains' section with its folded arms.

The wheel spun. Slowed. Landed.

**PULL: "DRAGON SHOT MISFIRE."**

**RARITY: COMMON.**

The word sat there in flat white letters, and the theatre — which had cut its teeth on an SSR murder and an SR dragon — did not know what to do with it.

The villains figured it out first.

"*Common,*" said the Vulture, and his laugh was an old man's laugh, dry and delighted and mean. He was sitting in the block the seating chart had assembled for them — Adrian Toomes, Otto Octavius, Flint Marko, Herman Schultz, Max Dillon, and Quentin Beck, six mid-tier careers arranged in two rows like a felony class photo, and someone in the theatre's design department had a sense of humor because the placard on the railing read RESERVED: SINISTER. "The cosmic slot machine looked at this boy's whole life and filed part of it under *common*."

"The dragon was the interesting part," said Doc Ock, whose actuators had been confiscated by the theatre but whose posture still somehow implied them. "The boy is the delivery mechanism. One does not review the syringe."

"Booooo," said a voice from everywhere and nowhere, which resolved into Deadpool climbing over the back row of the villain block with two buckets of popcorn he had no visible means of having acquired. "Boo this pod of hater walruses. That syringe has a *name*, Otto, and it's Issei Hyoudou, and he's my favorite character." He dropped uninvited into the one empty seat, between Shocker and Sandman, both of whom edged away. "Common pulls are where they hide the lore. Everybody knows that. Shut up, it's starting."

The screen bloomed with light.

---

It did not, at first, show the Dragon Shot. The theatre would come to understand this about the gacha: a pull was a destination, and the screen liked the scenic route.

The scenic route opened on Kuoh Academy at lunch hour, and on three boys crouched behind the kendo club's storage shed with their ears pressed to a hole in the wall.

"Oh no," said Peter Parker.

"Oh *no*," agreed Gwen.

The screen introduced them with floating gacha-style nameplates, as if they were characters worth introducing: MATSUDA — FORMER SPORTSMAN, CURRENT DISGRACE. MOTOHAMA — GLASSES DO A THING, DON'T ASK. And between them, radiating the doomed confidence of a man walking into a propeller: ISSEI HYOUDOU — SUBJECT.

What followed was a montage, and it was merciless.

It showed the peeping — always attempted, essentially never successful, the hole in the wall discovered within ninety seconds by girls who had clearly discovered it a hundred times before. It showed the kendo club emerging in full gear with bamboo swords already drawn, and it showed the beating, which was choreographed by the universe itself for maximum comedy: Issei taking a shinai to the crown of the head with a sound like a woodblock, Matsuda and Motohama abandoning him at flank speed, Issei alone in a cloud of dust absorbing what could technically be called swordsmanship.

It showed the PE class where he finished the fifteen-hundred meters last, behind a boy with a doctor's note. It showed the female student body's loathing rendered as an actual weather system, a cold front of contempt that moved through hallways ahead of him. It showed his grades, briefly, and Amadeus Cho made an involuntary noise like he'd been shown a crime scene.

And it showed the speeches.

There were, the theatre learned, a lot of speeches. Issei Hyoudou delivered them to sunsets, mostly, standing on the riverbank with his fist clenched against the orange sky, announcing to the water and the weeds and his two weeping friends that one day — ONE DAY — he would become the Harem King, and every beautiful woman in the world would — the screen mercifully compressed the rest into a montage-within-the-montage, because there were forty minutes of this material and it was all the same speech.

The theatre's reaction was not uniform.

Peter Parker had slid so far down in his seat that only his eyes cleared the seat back, watching through laced fingers with the specific agony of a man seeing his own high school years performed by an understudy with less shame. "I can't. Miles. Miles, I can't. It's like watching someone else's cringe compilation and it's *yours.*"

"You were never like this," Miles said, and paused. "Right?"

"I was a different genre of loser. I was a *dignified* loser. There were books involved."

"He memorized three girls' class schedules and can't pass biology," said Gwen, flat. "The priorities alone should be studied."

Daredevil had his head tilted back and his jaw set, enduring the montage the way he endured dental work. "The worst part," he said, to nobody, "is that he means all of it. There's no act. No self-awareness to hide behind. Every word of that harem speech comes out of him like a confession." He exhaled. "It's the most honest voice I've heard since we got here, and I want it to stop."

The villain block, meanwhile, was having a wonderful time.

"THIS?" Electro was pointing at the screen with both hands. "This is the guy? The cosmic theatre kidnapped Galactus to watch THIS guy get hit with kendo sticks?"

"He is beneath notice," Doc Ock pronounced, settling back with the satisfaction of a man whose worldview has been confirmed. "A hormone in shoes. Whatever the screen believes it is foreshadowing, I decline to be shadowed by it."

"I dunno," said Sandman, slowly, because Flint Marko had been a nobody from Queens once too and something about the kid's stupid, hopeful face was bothering him. "He gets beat up a lot and keeps showing up. That's — I mean, it's dumb. But it's a kind of dumb I've seen turn into somethin'."

"It's a kind of dumb I've seen turn into a body," said Jessica Jones from across the aisle, and took a long pull from a flask the theatre kept refilling with something that was definitely not what she'd asked for. "Kid's got the survival instincts of a mayfly with a crush."

"BOOOO," Deadpool contributed, throwing popcorn at the entire villain block in a broad, egalitarian arc. "You're all watching the tutorial level and complaining about the graphics. He's got a dragon in his arm rated 'doubles every ten seconds, no cap,' and you're grading his cardio. Shocker gets it. Shocker, tell them you get it."

"Don't put me in this," said Shocker.

And then the montage ended, the light on the screen changed — dusk now, a forest clearing in the mountains behind Kuoh — and the actual pull began.

---

The clearing had been prepared as a training ground, which was to say someone had dragged a boulder into the middle of it and painted a target on the boulder. Issei stood thirty meters away in a tracksuit, left arm sheathed to the elbow in the red gauntlet, and the theatre felt the temperature of its own attention change, because the Boosted Gear was on screen again, and everyone remembered what lived in it.

**"Boost,"** said the deep voice from the green gem, and the gauntlet chimed.

"Okay," Issei told the boulder, bouncing on his toes like a boxer who had seen boxing described once. "Okay okay okay. Buchou said gather it, compress it, release it. Gather. Compress. Release. Simple. I'm basically a cannon. I'm a sexy cannon—"

"Do not narrate," said the dragon, wearily, in the tone of an ancient being discovering what it had signed up for.

**"Boost."** Chime. **"Boost."** Chime.

Around the clearing's edge, the screen showed his audience: Rias Gremory with her arms crossed and an expression of genuinely maternal patience; the small white-haired girl, Koneko, eating a bun with the emotional investment of a security camera; the pretty-boy Knight, Kiba, smiling encouragingly from what the theatre noted was a carefully chosen position not directly behind, beside, or within a wide cone in front of Issei.

"He's pre-scouted the blast radius," Danny Rand observed. "The sword kid. Look at where he's standing. That's a man who has watched this before."

"That's a man who has watched this *several* times," said Luke Cage.

On screen, red light was pooling in Issei's palm now, and here the theatre's mockery began, very quietly, to curdle into something else — because the light was wrong. Not wrong like weak. Wrong like a garden hose attached to a fire main. It sputtered and flared and doubled with every chime, and the grass around Issei's feet flattened outward in a slow circle, and his arm had started to shake.

"Compress it," Rias called, with the calm of a woman coaching someone through defusing themselves. "Issei, compress, don't feed it more—"

"I've GOT it," said Issei, feeding it more.

"Kid," Peter said to the screen, sitting up, spider-sense itching at footage of another universe, which should not have been possible and was happening anyway. "Kid, no—"

**"Boost."**

"ISSEI, DO NOT—"

He released.

What came off his palm was not a shot. A shot implies aim. What came off his palm was a red-black comet of draconic power that left his hand at a forty-degree angle to anywhere he was looking, because at the moment of release the recoil folded him backward like a lawn chair. Issei went one way — up, and then into a tree, and then through the tree, and then into a second, sturdier tree that stood its ground — and the Dragon Shot went the other way, over the boulder, over the treeline, and into the shoulder of the mountain behind the clearing.

The mountain lost the exchange.

The screen gave the theatre the courtesy of a wide shot: the flash, the delayed boom rolling across the valley, and the notch — a clean, cauterized, fifty-meter bite taken out of a ridgeline, glowing at the edges, rock running like candle wax. Birds departed the prefecture. Somewhere below, a dog began barking and did not stop for the remainder of the pull.

In the clearing, upside down against the base of the second tree, smoking gently, his hair blown into a shape that defied several laws, Issei raised one trembling fist.

"...Did I hit the boulder?"

The boulder, untouched, sat in the middle of the clearing with its painted target, radiating smugness.

"No," said Koneko, and took another bite of her bun.

---

The theatre made two sounds at once, and the sounds did not agree with each other.

The first sound was laughter — huge, rolling, tension-shattering laughter, the first real laughter this room had produced, because after the light spear and the funeral grief of Pull One, watching a teenager get folded by his own attack and put through a tree was medicine. Deadpool was on the floor of the villain block. Miles had his whole face in his hands, shoulders heaving. Even Jessica Jones made a single sharp sound that she would deny under oath.

The second sound was silence, and it came from the people who could do math.

"That was a Common," said Peter Parker, not laughing anymore, doing the thing where his voice went up half an octave and sped up. "Guys. The rarity tag is grading the *event*, not the power. The misfire is common. Him missing is common. The part where an untrained sixteen-year-old with no stance, no aim, and four boosts casually deleted a hillside — the gacha thinks that part is *unremarkable.* That's baseline. That's the tutorial number."

Down in RESERVED: SINISTER, Otto Octavius had stopped smiling somewhere around the wide shot, and was now watching the glowing notch in the ridgeline with the expression of a man revising a paper.

"Four amplifications," he said. "Doubling. So the observed yield represents a base multiplied sixteenfold — meaning his true resting output is one-sixteenth of a mountainside." He was quiet a moment. "And the instrument has, per the previous pull, no ceiling."

"So he gets less funny," said the Vulture, slowly, "every ten seconds."

"He gets less funny every ten seconds," Octavius agreed.

Mysterio, who had said nothing through the entire montage, said nothing now either, but he had taken out something that functioned as a notebook.

"Still a punk kid who can't aim," Electro insisted, but his heart had gone out of it, because Max Dillon had also once been a punchline with too much power and no control, and he knew better than most where that story could go.

And in the middle bowl, Luke Cage was leaning forward with his forearms on his knees, and he had noticed the thing the screen had wanted someone to notice.

"Run it back," he said quietly, to Danny. "Not the boom. Before. Look at his hands."

Because the screen, montage-style, was showing the aftermath now: and the aftermath was not Issei quitting. The aftermath was dawn — a gray, miserable, drizzling dawn — and Issei in the clearing alone, palms wrapped in bandage tape gone brown at the knuckles, doing it again. Smaller. Failing again, a fizzle this time, a shot that went eight feet and died. Doing it again. The screen time-lapsed through days: the boulder acquiring scorch marks in a slowly tightening pattern, like a dartboard learning where the darts were going to be. Issei asleep face-down in the grass with the gauntlet still on. Issei running the riverbank before school, wheezing, last again in PE that afternoon, but four seconds less last. Issei getting kendo-sworded on Tuesday and drilling stance corrections on Wednesday using, God help him, what the beating had taught him about footwork.

"There it is," Luke said.

"There what is," said Jessica.

"That's a kid with no talent," Luke said, "who has decided that's not going to be the thing that stops him. I've watched a lot of gifted people coast. Watch him instead. He gets humiliated at nine a.m. and he's back at the boulder by five. Every day. Nobody's making him." He sat back. "Powers are luck. That? That's the part you can't give somebody."

Jessica looked at the screen for a long moment. The time-lapse boulder now had a scorch dead center, one single hit, and on screen the idiot was celebrating it by screaming his harem speech at the mountain he had previously maimed.

"He's still a disgusting little creep," she said.

"Yep," said Luke.

"...Kid can take a hit, though," she said, into her flask, and that was as far as Jessica Jones went out loud for anybody.

The wheel began to tick again.

---

**PULL: "DRESS BREAK PROTOTYPE."**

**RARITY: UNCOMMON.**

"Prototype of what?" Miles asked.

"'Dress Break,'" Gwen read off the screen, and her eyes narrowed with the premonitory dread of every woman in the theatre simultaneously. "I already hate it. I don't know what it is and I hate it."

The screen opened on the Occult Research Club's training ground, and on Issei explaining his new original technique to his assembled peerage with the pride of a scientist unveiling cold fusion. The theatre was not shown the technique. The theatre was shown Rias's face *hearing* the technique, which passed through polite interest, comprehension, horror, and a long, long pinch of the bridge of her nose.

"A touch-activated spell," the screen captioned clinically, in HUD text, "that unravels the clothing of its target. Instantly. Completely. Developed by the subject through days of dedicated, obsessive magical research — the first spell he has ever successfully created."

The theatre absorbed this.

"His first original magic," said Doctor Strange, in the third tier, with the hollow voice of a Sorcerer Supreme watching a toddler use the Book of Vishanti as a booster seat, "is targeted matter disintegration. Selective. Material-specific. Touch-ranged. And he invented it. For *that.*"

"The discipline required to unbind one class of matter while leaving flesh untouched," Doom said, and it was the closest thing to respect Doom had produced all evening, which made it worse, "is not trivial. The boy has genuine arcane aptitude. He has aimed it at the single stupidest target in creation."

"That's the most terrifying sentence anyone's said in this theatre," said Peter, "and a purple guy with a glove is *in* this theatre."

On screen, the demonstration commenced. The Occult Research Club had prepared for science: a row of training dummies stood in the yard, dressed in secondhand uniforms, and the peerage watched from a distance the screen lovingly established was Kiba's Blast Cone Protocol, now adopted club-wide.

Issei snapped his fingers at dummy number one.

Nothing happened.

He snapped again. Nothing. He touched the dummy and snapped. Nothing. He hit the dummy, harangued the dummy, delivered a short motivational address to the dummy. The dummy remained fully and insolently dressed. Koneko, off to the side, wrote something on a clipboard. The screen zoomed on the clipboard. It said: *"0 for 11."*

"Oh thank God, it doesn't work," said Gwen.

The screen, which had a sense of timing, cut to: LATER THAT NIGHT.

A contract mission. A genuine fight — the theatre sat up — some minor stray devil in an abandoned warehouse, all teeth and bad intentions, and Issei actually holding his own now, footwork recognizably kendo-taught, gauntlet chiming, and the theatre felt the strange lurch of watching the joke start to look like a fighter. The stray lunged. Issei planted, caught its wrist to redirect it — textbook, Kiba-drilled, genuinely well-executed —

— and his palm flashed pink.

"Uh oh," said the entire theatre, as one organism.

The spell, eleven-times-failed, chose that moment to work perfectly. It just didn't work on the stray devil, whose hide, the HUD helpfully noted, *did not classify as clothing.* The pink flash ricocheted off the redirect, ran up Issei's own arm, and —

The screen cut, with the crisp mercy of a sitcom, to reaction shots.

To the stray devil, frozen mid-lunge, its monstrous face performing the universal expression of a creature that no longer wishes to be part of this encounter.

To Kiba, slowly turning to face a wall, shoulders vibrating with the effort of chivalry.

To Koneko, writing on her clipboard. The screen zoomed. *"1 for 12. Note: friendly fire."*

To Rias, arriving through a teleport circle at the worst conceivable moment, taking in the tableau, and removing her jacket and holding it out at arm's length without a single word, with the dead eyes of a woman amortizing eight Pawns over a very long future.

And to Issei's voice, from off-screen, from behind a crate he had achieved at speeds his PE teacher would not have believed: "IT WORKS. BUCHOU, THE SPELL WORKS, I JUST NEED TO FIX THE TARGETING—"

"DO NOT FIX THE TARGETING," Rias said. "DELETE THE SPELL."

"I'M GOING TO FIX THE TARGETING."

The pull ended on the stray devil quietly attempting to leave, and Koneko clotheslining it into the floor without looking up from the clipboard.

---

The theatre took a while to put itself back together.

Deadpool had to be resuscitated by Sandman, a service Sandman performed by gathering him off the floor and pouring him back into his seat like an hourglass. Miles and Gwen had achieved the stage of laughter that is silent and involves hitting the armrest. Even Daredevil was pressing his knuckles against his mouth, and losing.

"I want to be very clear," Gwen said, wiping her eyes, "that the technique is vile, he should be in jail, and the part where it immediately hit *him* is the finest act of cosmic justice I have ever personally witnessed."

"The universe said 'no' in real time," Miles agreed. "The universe has a targeting system and it's better than his."

"Twelve attempts," Matt Murdock said, and there was something underneath the amusement now, the lawyer's tone, the tone of a man reading a record. "He failed that spell eleven times and logged every failure and kept the data and kept going. It's the same pattern as the boulder." He shook his head slowly. "The content of what he wants is garbage. The *engine* under it never stops running. If anyone ever teaches that engine to want something worth having—"

"Then the mountain thing stops being funny," Luke Cage finished.

In RESERVED: SINISTER, the villain block had reached its official verdict, delivered by Doc Ock with a dismissive wave: "A clown. A cosmic clown with a lottery ticket in his arm. Beneath the notice of serious people. Wake me when the dragon does something."

But the screen's cameras — and the theatre had begun to suspect the screen watched its audience as attentively as its audience watched the screen — lingered for one extra beat on Quentin Beck, who was still writing in the thing that functioned as a notebook, and what he had written was: *material-selective disintegration, touch-trigger, self-taught in days, motivated by nonsense. Query: what does he build when properly motivated? Query: who does the motivating?*

The pulls dimmed. The wheel folded itself away. And the screen, as it had once before, flashed a line of text almost too fast to read — so fast that afterward, half the room would argue it had never happened:

**COMMON DOES NOT MEAN WEAK. COMMON MEANS THERE ARE THOUSANDS OF HOURS LIKE THIS ONE.**

**HE SPENT ALL OF THEM.**

Then: **NEXT PULL PENDING**, in patient gray, and the soft idle ticking of the wheel, like a heart at rest.

Jessica Jones stared at where the words had been for a long moment.

"Thousands of hours," she said, to no one. "Little creep actually did the work."

She put the flask away.

**Secrets Revealed:**
- The gacha's rarity tags grade the *event*, not the power level: an untrained Issei vaporizing a fifty-meter notch of mountainside on four Boosts is classified "Common" — i.e., baseline, unremarkable, one of thousands of similar hours. Per Peter Parker, that is the alarming part.
- Issei's raw resting output, back-calculated by Doc Ock from the misfire (sixteenfold amplification at four Boosts), is already substantial — and the Boosted Gear has no ceiling.
- Dress Break is Issei's first original spell: self-taught, material-selective, touch-triggered disintegration. Strange and Doom independently assess the underlying aptitude as genuine and non-trivial — aimed at the stupidest possible application.
- The screen's second cryptic flash: "COMMON DOES NOT MEAN WEAK. COMMON MEANS THERE ARE THOUSANDS OF HOURS LIKE THIS ONE. HE SPENT ALL OF THEM."
- The screen appears to watch its audience back — it lingered deliberately on Mysterio's note-taking.

**Alliance / Opinion Shifts:**
- Spider-family: peak secondhand embarrassment (Peter), but Peter is also the first to decode the rarity-grading implication and is now quietly frightened of the "tutorial numbers."
- Daredevil: pained by the montage but flags the pattern — Issei's honesty is total and his "engine never stops running"; dangerous if it's ever pointed at something worth having.
- Luke Cage: the chapter's designated believer — openly respects Issei's no-talent, all-grit training loop ("Powers are luck. That's the part you can't give somebody").
- Jessica Jones: publicly contemptuous, privately converted by the "thousands of hours" reveal; put the flask away. Will deny laughing.
- Sinister Six official verdict: beneath notice, "a clown with a lottery ticket in his arm" (Octavius). Unofficial cracks: Sandman relates to the nobody-who-keeps-showing-up; Electro sees his own too-much-power-no-control story; Octavius quietly re-ran the math and stopped smiling.
- Mysterio: the sleeper threat — took private notes on Dress Break's disintegration mechanics and posed the chapter's most sinister questions: "What does he build when properly motivated? Who does the motivating?"
- Deadpool: self-appointed Issei hype-man and heckler-in-chief of the villain block; insists "Common pulls are where they hide the lore" (and was right).
- Kiba/Koneko (on-screen): established as long-suffering comedy infrastructure — Kiba's "Blast Cone Protocol" positioning and Koneko's failure-logging clipboard (0 for 11; 1 for 12, friendly fire).

**Unresolved Hooks:**
- If mountain-deletion is "Common," what does this gacha consider Rare — let alone SSR? The rarity scale itself is now a source of dread.
- Issei declared, over Rias's direct order, that he will "fix the targeting" on Dress Break. The theatre awaits this with mixed horror.
- Mysterio's notebook: someone in the villain block is treating the broadcast as R&D, and the screen made sure the audience saw it. Is the theatre curating who learns what?
- Does the screen watch the watchers? Growing evidence that the audience's reactions are part of whatever this is.
- The Chapter One prophecy still stands unexplained — and the "thousands of hours" flash reads like the screen is building a case for it, exhibit by exhibit.
