## SUBSECTION ONE: THE JOKE THAT SAVES

The briefing room erupts in chaos.

Issei stands at the center of a blast radius, armor scorched, hair singed, one arm held at an angle that suggests stitches and possibly a surgical rotation in the very near future. The building behind him still smolders. The building behind *that* is a crater.

Steve Rogers is mid-sentence: "—we're running medical scans, FRI says internal bleeding but—"

Issei raises his good hand.

"Before anyone asks," he says, voice clear, almost musical in its wrongness given the circumstances, "yes, I'm fine. No, I don't need Dr. Banner. And yes, I'm aware that technically I got set on fire *twice* in the same mission, which has to be some kind of record. I should probably add it to my LinkedIn."

The room doesn't laugh. The room *stops*.

Deadpool, lounging in the corner with a energy drink he absolutely should not have, cackles. "Oh, *that's* good. That's real good. The burnt hero joke. Classics are classics for a reason, babe."

"That's not—" Wanda starts.

"It is, though." Natasha's voice cuts through. Quiet. Certain. She's not looking at Issei. She's looking at something past him, through him, into a mirror of herself from fifteen years ago. "He's scared. The jokes mean he's scared."

The room shifts. Turns. Focuses.

Bucky nods slowly. His jaw works like he's grinding through something. "Black Widow Op Nine. Got caught in a building collapse. I was unconscious for eight hours. When I woke up, he cracked jokes for three days straight." He means Steve. The meaning hangs there: *I learned this from him. The coping. The deflection.* "Jokes mean he's processing. Means he hasn't crashed yet."

Logan leans back in his chair, claws retracted but present, leather creaking. He's got a drink—whiskey, straight, no ice, because he's Logan—and he takes a slow pull before speaking. "Kid do anything stupid in the field? Reckless?"

"He ran *into* the explosion," Tony says flatly. "To stop it from expanding into the school next door. Calculated the blast radius, had exactly four seconds before secondary charges, ran through a wall of flame and diffused it manually."

Logan closes his eyes. "There it is."

Jessica Jones, standing in the back like she's got somewhere more important to be (she doesn't), nods once. "Reckless choice. High sacrifice math. He's adding up losses already. The jokes are interest on a debt he thinks he's running."

Issei still has his hand raised. The smile on his face is very, very bright, and it doesn't reach his eyes. "Are we doing the psychology symposium on my coping mechanisms, or—"

"You're not fine," Natasha says. Not a question.

"I'm standing up."

"You're in shock."

"Well, yes," Issei says, and the smile flickers—just once, like a bad transmission—before resetting to its maximum wattage. "But I'm *standing* while I'm in shock, which I think most medical professionals would agree is the optimistic outcome. I'm thinking of it as a glass-half-full situation. The glass is made of flames, and I drank it, but the principle stands."

Wanda's eyes go wet. Tony leaves the room without a word.

Deadpool stands up, still grinning behind his mask. "This is beautiful. This is art. I'm going to treasure this. The kid's falling apart at molecular level and he's doing comedy bits like a professional. If I didn't love you already, babe—" He turns to Issei. "—I'd fall in love with you right now based on this performance alone. This is advanced trauma coping. This is *practiced* trauma coping. Someone trained this kid."

"Nobody trained—" Issei begins.

"High School?" Logan asks.

Silence.

"Before High School?" Bucky asks.

Longer silence.

"Yeah," Jessica says softly. "Yeah, that's it. Something early. Something that taught him this specific mechanism. Make the pain funny, make yourself the target, make them laugh so they don't have time to see you're broken."

Issei's hand comes down slowly.

He looks at his wrist. At the angle. The tremor there that he's been hiding by keeping the arm raised.

"I need to go see Bruce," he says quietly.

"No," Natasha says. She stands. Everyone else follows. "You need to sit. You need to let someone look at you. And then you're going to tell us who taught you to do this, because it's not good work—it's *perfect* work, and perfect trauma coping is a sign that someone you love did this damage."

Issei's jaw tightens.

The joke dies.

## SUBSECTION TWO: THE SHOWER, ALONE

The medical wing cleared out two hours ago. Bruce gave him a clean bill—dislocated shoulder, second-degree burns on 12% of his body, mild lacerations, but the internal bleeding was just bruising. All recoverable. All temporal.

Issei stands under the shower, running water so hot it reads as a threat to basic safety systems.

He doesn't remember the explosion. That's a lie—he remembers it perfectly, frame by frame, the moment he felt the heat signature spike, the calculation of angles, the certainty that if he didn't move *now* there would be seventeen dead children in a building three blocks away.

He remembers running toward the fire.

He remembers *through* the fire.

He doesn't remember the jokes.

They're not his jokes. They're borrowed. Worn. He's been wearing them so long they feel like skin, but that's only because he was trained to think of them that way.

The water hits his shoulders and he folds forward, pressing his forehead against the tile.

His hands shake.

He's used to the shaking. Adrenaline does that. Post-incident, the nervous system needs time to recalibrate. Thirty seconds. Ninety. Two minutes if it was bad.

But it's been two hours.

He presses harder against the tile, trying to feel the cold underneath the heat, and his voice comes out broken. Not loud. Just broken.

He doesn't cry. That's not something he does anymore. He stopped doing that around age seven, when his mother explained that tears meant you were weak, and weakness meant you were useless, and useless people didn't survive. So he doesn't cry.

Instead, he shakes.

Instead, his hands make fists and release, fists and release, like they're trying to punch their way out of his body.

Instead, there's water on his face that isn't tears, it's shower water, it's *shower water*, and—

"I've got you."

Issei spins so fast he nearly loses his footing on wet tile.

Sentry stands in the bathroom doorway, suit still on, still carrying the impossible weight of every mind he's ever absorbed. He's not smiling. Robert Reynolds doesn't smile much anymore, not since he started understanding the full implications of what he contains.

"How did you—"

"I can hear all the things you're not saying," Sentry says simply. He steps inside the bathroom, closes the door, sits down on the bench outside the shower enclosure. "It's a side effect of being able to hear all the things. You're broadcasting, kid. Loud. The fear underneath the jokes, the pressure underneath the performance. I can feel it like a seismic event."

"I'm fine," Issei says automatically.

"No," Sentry agrees. "You're not. And that's okay, because fine is boring, and you're not boring. What you are is hurt. And what you're doing is the thing that people like us do—we hide it, we minimize it, we tell jokes about it until the jokes become the only truth we can stomach."

Issei's hands start shaking again.

"I'm losing control," he whispers. "In the field. I'm making worse choices. I ran into that explosion without calculating the full risk matrix because I just... reacted. I didn't run the numbers. I just *ran*."

"That's growth," Sentry says. "Sounds like you're learning to be human instead of being a weapon. The weapon calculates. The human protects. You protected."

"Seventeen kids if I'm wrong. Seventeen dead kids if my instincts are bad."

"Yeah," Sentry says, and his voice carries the weight of every time he's been wrong, every mind he's absorbed that taught him that he's fundamentally unreliable. "But that's not what happened. What happened is you ran toward the fire and stopped it. And now you're afraid that you're breaking, that the restraint is cracking, that one day you're going to run toward fire and find it's already consumed everything you love."

Issei can't speak. The water cascades down, still too hot, still burning.

"Here's what you need to know," Sentry continues softly. "The bottled trauma? It builds pressure. And pressure builds until it explodes. The jokes you're telling, the coping mechanism you learned—it works until it doesn't. And then what you get is a man who spent thirty years pretending to be fine and then one day isn't fine anymore, and the amount of fine left over isn't enough to clean up the wreckage."

He pauses.

"I know this because I *am* that man. And you're not going to be that man, because you're going to let people help you now. Not later. Not when you break. *Now*."

Issei looks at him. Really looks. Sees the impossible being, the cosmic horror wearing human skin and learning to be careful with it.

"I'm scared," he says.

"Good," Sentry says. "Fear means you still understand what matters. The ones we should worry about are the ones who stop being afraid."

## SUBSECTION THREE: THE SILENCE AFTER

Steve Rogers finds Issei in the common room at 0300.

Not sleeping. Probably hasn't slept. The tea in his hands is cold—he's not drinking it, just holding it like it's an anchor.

Steve doesn't sit. He leans against the counter, crosses his arms, gives the silence room to breathe.

"I was in Berlin," Steve says eventually. "Winter Soldier scenario. We were running extraction, and the building had a structural failure we didn't predict. I was on the third floor. Bucky was on the ground level. The floor gave way."

Issei doesn't look up.

"I could have gone after him. Could have tried to catch the falling concrete, grab him, save him the hard way. That's what I thought about doing. That's what the weapon in me wanted to do." Steve's voice goes soft. "But I didn't have time to calculate the structural integrity of where I was standing. So I jumped down to him instead. Used myself as a cushion. Fractured three ribs, collarbone, and my left arm in two places."

Steve holds up that arm. Still strong. Still useful. Time healed the breaks, but the calcium refraction is visible if you know to look.

"You know what I told him?" Steve asks. "The same thing I'm telling you. 'I'd do it again.'"

Issei's hands tremble. The tea sloshes.

"I'm scared I'm becoming uncontrolled," Issei says quietly.

"You ran into fire to save children," Steve says. "That's not uncontrolled. That's exactly controlled, just in a different direction. That's you choosing to be human instead of being a tool. And the fear? The fear means you understand the weight of power. The ones who stop being afraid are the ones who end up dangerous."

He pauses.

"But you can't carry this alone. The weight of it. The trauma. The choices that can't be unmade. That's the part the world doesn't prepare us for—we talk about being strong enough to carry the load, but we never talk about the fact that the load is designed to crush you. It just takes time. And isolation amplifies it."

Issei finally looks up.

"Natasha knows about the jokes," Steve says. "Logan knows about the borrowing. Bucky knows about the mechanism. Jessica knows about the pattern. And Sentry—Sentry knows about the breakdown." Steve sits down finally, beside him on the couch. "They're not leaving you alone with this. And neither am I."

The tea is spilled now, across Issei's hands, staining his shirt.

"I don't know how to let people in," Issei whispers.

"Nobody does," Steve says. "That's why we practice. That's why we do it together."
