# Chapter Eleven: Ten Days to Become Worthy

**Primary Reaction Groups:** Avengers (Captain America, Thor) • Mutants (Cyclops) • Cosmic Mentors (The Eternals)

**Gacha Pulls:**
1. "Compressed Training Under Pressure" — **Super Rare**
2. "Transfer-Enhanced Rook Punch" — **Rare**

---

The words **TUTORIAL COMPLETE** had hung on the screen through the whole intermission, patient as a tombstone, and the theatre had spent the intermission arguing about them.

"Tutorial," Sam Wilson kept saying, to anyone in range. "That was the *tutorial*. The kid died, got bought, swore war on Heaven, and blew up a mountain, and the system's filing all of it under *teaching him the controls*."

"Every game I have ever played," Kamala Khan said, hugging her knees in her seat, "the tutorial is where they show you the mechanics. Then the real game starts, and the real game is where they start taking things away from you."

The lights dimmed before anyone could tell her she was wrong, which was just as well, because nobody had been planning to.

The screen did something new. Instead of the wheel, it filled edge to edge with slow crimson — not the gold of a pull, not the storm-grey of a death, but the deep, working red of a forge — and printed, in letters that assembled themselves like something being hammered into shape:

**ARC TWO: THE MAKING OF A DRAGON.**

"Ah," said Thor, settling back with the satisfaction of a man whose genre had finally arrived. "Now we are told how the blade was folded."

Beneath the arc title, smaller, the system added a note with its usual total absence of mercy:

**YOU HAVE SEEN WHAT HE BECOMES. YOU WILL NOW SEE WHAT IT COST.**

The wheel descended, spun through its colors, and stopped on silver shading into gold.

**GACHA PULL: 1 OF 2. "COMPRESSED TRAINING UNDER PRESSURE" — RARITY: SUPER RARE.**

And the theatre took its breath, and swallowed them.

---

They knew at once that the screen had jumped backward.

The room had learned the gacha's habits over ten pulls: it did not tell his life in order. It dealt his life like cards. They had already seen the crimson armor that erased a mountain; they had seen him lose, older and harder, to Sairaorg Bael. So when the vision resolved into the Occult Research Club's clubroom and the Issei standing in it was the *scrawny* one — the tutorial boy, weeks dead at most, shoulders like a coat hanger, gauntlet arm still a novelty he flexed when he thought no one was looking — a ripple of understanding went through the dark. This was before. This was the forge.

A magic circle opened on the clubroom floor, and it opened in *fire*.

The man who stepped out of the flames was tall, blond, beautiful, and wore his burgundy blazer half-buttoned the way expensive men do when they have never once been told no. The name arrived in a few thousand minds simultaneously: *Riser Phenex. Third son of the House of Phenex. High-class devil.* He looked around the clubroom the way a landlord looks at a property, and then he looked at Rias Gremory the same way.

"My lovely Rias," he said. "I've come to set a date."

What followed took eleven minutes of vision-time, and by the end of it half the theatre wanted him dead, which — the sharper minds noted — was almost certainly the point of showing it.

The facts assembled themselves. An arranged marriage, contracted between the Houses of Gremory and Phenex when Rias was a child, to shore up pureblood devil lines gutted by an old war. Rias had never consented. Rias had spent years saying so. It did not matter, because the contract did not require her to say yes; it only required her to fail to produce a legal way out. Riser explained all of this while lounging on the clubroom sofa, and then — to make a point to the boy glaring at him from across the room — pulled his own Queen down beside him and made the point at length, smirking at Issei over her shoulder the whole time.

"Oh, I *hate* him," announced Jennifer Walters, with a lawyer's crisp diction, from the row where she had been quietly gavel-fidgeting since the word *contract*. "I hate him and I want his prenup in discovery. A binding marriage compact executed by third parties over a minor's standing objection—"

"Is how half of Asgard's treaties were written," Thor said grimly. "It was vile then also."

On the screen, a woman in a maid's uniform with silver hair and a stillness like a drawn blade — *Grayfia Lucifuge*, the vision supplied, and several magic-sensitives in the audience sat up sharply at the power reading under that stillness — proposed the one legal way out. A Rating Game. A formal ritual war, peerage against peerage, watched by the pillars of devil society. If Rias's household won, the engagement could be challenged. If she lost, she married.

Riser laughed. It was the laugh of a professional invited to play a child.

"She's never played one," he said. "I've won *dozens*, and my pieces are complete. Very well — I'm generous. Ten days. Train them, little Rias. Bring me your best."

And the theatre understood, one beat before the vision cut, what the arc title had meant — because the last shot of the scene was not Riser's smirk and not Rias's white-knuckled fists. It was Issei Hyoudou, weakest piece on the board, dead-last of Kuoh Academy, looking down at the gauntlet on his left arm and understanding, visibly, in real time, that he had ten days to become worthy of the war he'd already sworn to fight.

Then the mountain began.

---

The vision did not montage it. That was the cruelty and the genius of the thing, the audience agreed afterward — a montage is edited by someone merciful, and the gacha was not merciful. It *compressed*. Ten days folded into them like ten days of memory, whole and unabridged and merely fast, and every hero in the theatre lived it from inside the boy's body.

Day one: a mountain trail in the northern forests, and Issei under a backpack the size of a wardrobe, because Rias — walking ahead, unburdened, ruthless — had loaded the entire camp onto her Pawn on the theory that his body was the only part of him she could improve in ten days, and the mountain would improve it or bury it. The audience felt his thighs go first, then his lower back, then the particular white-noise stage beyond burning where the body stops sending complaints because no one is answering. He fell eleven times. He got up eleven times. Nobody carried the pack for him, and — the young heroes noticed, some with outrage — nobody offered.

Day two, day three: swordwork with Kiba in a forest clearing, which meant losing to Kiba, thirty, forty, sixty times a morning, wooden blade spinning away into the ferns while the pretty blond Knight said "again" in a voice that never once condescended. Hand-to-hand with Koneko Toujou, four foot six of white-haired, deadpan Rook, who threw him over her shoulder into the turf with the serene regularity of a metronome. "Too slow," she said, each time, help him up, throw him again. "Too stiff. Again." The theatre felt every impact through his spine.

"She's benching what, exactly?" Luke Cage asked, watching the tiny girl one-arm a boulder off the training field like a stagehand moving furniture.

"Rook piece," said Peter Parker, who had been taking actual notes since chapter one. "Super strength, super defense. She's the smallest one there and she is *conclusively* the scariest, and I think that's on purpose, and I think I love this team."

Day four: magic with Akeno, and the first thing Issei had ever been simply, hopelessly *bad* at with no road forward — hours of guided breathing to produce a mote of demonic light the size of a firefly, while the Queen who could call lightning smiled and said *most devils would have quit by now* in a tone that made it a genuine compliment. Evenings: Rias and a chalkboard, drilling him on Evil Piece doctrine, promotion rules, the geometry of a Rating Game board, and the audience felt the academic dead-last gripping his own skull and *forcing* the material in by pure sustained will, because this part was for her.

And underneath all of it, constant as a heartbeat, the voice out of the gauntlet in the dark of his tent when everything hurt too much to sleep:

**[Your body is a vessel, partner. Small, yes. Laughably small. But the walls are moving. I have worn hosts who trained for decades and widened less than you have in four days. Eat. Sleep. Break again tomorrow. I will be here.]**

Day six, the vision showed them the cost. Asia Argento's healing light, green and gentle, knitting the boy's split knuckles and stress-fractured shins at night while he slept — and lingering, and trembling, because the sweet-faced healer could feel what the audience was now shown in cold system overlay: microfracture maps down both legs, deep-tissue trauma accumulating faster than even devil physiology and nightly healing could fully clear it. He was not getting through the ten days undamaged. He was *spending* something, and the ledger of it was written in his bones.

The vision released them there, on the overlay, holding the skeletal damage-map on screen for three full seconds like an accusation.

---

The lights came up on a divided room.

"Okay — no. Hold on." Miles Morales was on his feet. "He's *seventeen*. That's not a training camp, that's — my coach would be arrested. Someone's supposed to say stop. Someone's supposed to be the adult who says stop."

"They can't." The voice was Scott Summers', and it came out flat and level from the mutant block, and it carried, because Cyclops did not usually volunteer. He sat with his arms crossed, ruby lenses fixed on the frozen damage-map, and his tone was the one he used in the Danger Room when a student wanted the settings lowered. "Run the clock with me. Ten days. Opponent's a veteran with a full roster and a regeneration factor. Every hour they spend protecting that kid from the training is an hour they donate to Riser Phenex. There's no responsible-adult option on the table. There's only the question of whether he breaks before day ten or after it." He paused, and something under the flatness bent, just slightly. "I've had to train children on that math. You do it, and you hate it, and you do it, because the alternative is burying them undertrained. What I want everyone to notice is *him*. Sixty throws a morning from the Rook. He got up sixty times, and by day five he was getting up *angry at himself* instead of at her. That's the rarest thing I ever see in a training room. That kid metabolizes failure into instruction. You cannot teach that."

"No," said Steve Rogers quietly. "You can only find it."

The theatre turned toward him. Captain America was leaning forward, forearms on knees, and his face wore an expression the oldest Avengers recognized: Steve Rogers looking at a scrawny kid who would not stay down, which was Steve Rogers looking into a mirror seventy years long. "I've been through a version of this," he said. "Somebody once compressed me too — one afternoon in a metal coffin in Brooklyn, and I came out the other side with a body that finally matched the fights I kept picking. But Erskine's serum did the work *for* me in ninety seconds. Nobody's doing it for him. He's doing it the way I did it *before* the serum — losing, and getting back up, and losing, and getting back up — except he's doing it with the volume turned up to a level that's breaking his skeleton, and he hasn't complained *once*, because the girl who gave him a second life is going to be sold off if he slows down." He shook his head slowly. "Ten days to become worthy. He decided he was going to be worthy on day one. The ten days are just paperwork."

"The regimen is sound, for what it is." Shang-Chi's voice, measured, from the martial-arts rows. "Overload, recover, overload. The swordsman gives him failure with dignity; the small Rook gives him failure with humility; the Queen gives him a failure he cannot fix, which is the most important kind. My father's courts trained killers in less time and broke nine of ten. This is not that. Watch the girl with the healing light — they are walking the edge of the cliff, but someone is *watching* the edge." He paused. "What concerns me is the arithmetic underneath. Ten days of this closes perhaps a tenth of the distance to the phoenix. They know it. Look at Rias Gremory's face when she thinks no one sees her. She is not training him to win. She is training him to survive losing."

That landed badly, and it was into the uncomfortable silence after it that a new voice spoke — deep, amused, and old in a way that made even Thor's antiquity feel recent.

"You are all reading it as ten days," said Gilgamesh.

Heads turned toward a block of seats up near the cosmic tier that had been, for ten chapters, almost perfectly silent: figures who sat with the unhurried stillness of beings for whom a century was a reasonable length for a conversation. The Eternals of Earth had watched Issei Hyoudou die, rise, swear, and blaze, and had said nothing, because they were still deciding what he was. Now the Forgotten One — who had wrestled heroes into shape under a dozen names across five thousand years — leaned forward with his chin on one enormous fist, and he was grinning.

"I trained men before your Rome," he said. "Before writing, I trained them. And I will tell you the secret every immortal trainer learns and hates: the mayfly learns faster than the mountain. Give me a student with ten thousand years and he will coast for nine thousand of them. Give me a student with ten *days* and a girl he'd die for, and he will pour his whole life through the bottleneck like — well." He gestured at the screen, delighted. "Like that. Compression is not his handicap. Compression is his *element*."

"It is also eating him," said Ajak, beside him, and her voice was gentler and worse. The Eternals' priest and healer had not taken her eyes off the frozen damage-map. "I have mended human bodies since the ice retreated. That overlay is not a training cost, it is a down payment. His resurrection physiology is covering the interest — for now. But I want this room to mark what the machine just showed us, because it showed us deliberately: this boy's answer to every gap between what he is and what he must be is to pay the difference *out of his own body*. Tonight it is shinbones. It will not always be shinbones." Her gaze swept the heroes below. "You have all been admiring his willingness to break himself. I have buried ten thousand years of men whose friends admired that."

"And yet you can't look away either, Ajak," murmured Sersi, who was sitting sideways in her seat with her chin in her hand, entirely charmed. "None of us can. This is the thing about them — the thing I could never make the rest of you *feel*. We watched forests become cities and it moved us less than one furious child carrying a backpack up a hill. Brevity is their fire. He has ten days, so ten days will simply have to be enough. No Eternal has ever once had a thought like that. I think it might be the most beautiful thought there is."

"It's the most *dangerous* thought there is," said Sprite, sharp and small and not looking at anyone. "He gets to spend himself because he's got a self that ends. Lucky him."

Thor's voice rolled up from the Avengers' rows before that bitterness could settle. "In Asgard," he said, "a warrior trains five centuries before his first war, and we called that rigor. I watched this boy compress my five centuries into a mountain week, wept twice, and complained never, while an elder dragon kept the night watch over his sleep like a father over a forge-fire." He looked over at the Eternals, and there was no challenge in it, only an old soldier's professional accord. "Your mayfly wisdom is well said, Forgotten One. But mark the dragon too. Ddraig has worn hosts beyond counting, and he speaks to this one at midnight in the voice my father used on *me*. Dragons do not coddle. If Ddraig tends the fire, it is because the dragon believes the blade is worth folding."

The screen chimed, softly, into the pause.

**GACHA PULL: 2 OF 2.**

The wheel ticked around and stopped on plain silver — a low rarity, and by now the theatre had learned to fear the gacha's cheap-looking cards most of all.

**"TRANSFER-ENHANCED ROOK PUNCH" — RARITY: RARE.**

"Rook," said Peter Parker, sitting up. "That's Koneko. Oh — oh, wait. *Wait.* Transfer. You guys, the spec sheet from chapter one, remember the spec—"

The theatre went under before he could finish, which saved him from being shushed by four separate factions.

---

Day nine, and Issei Hyoudou hit the wall the audience had felt coming since day one.

A mock battle in the high meadow: Rias's whole peerage in motion, a full-tempo Rating Game rehearsal, and the vision put the theatre inside Issei's head as he ran his own arithmetic and arrived — mid-fight, gasping, boosted to the edge of what his half-rebuilt body could carry — at the same conclusion Shang-Chi had reached from the cheap seats. *It isn't enough.* Ten days had made him five times the boy who left town. Riser Phenex would need him to be a hundred times. There was no version of the math where Issei Hyoudou, alone, boosting until his bones sang, arrived at that number in one day more.

He stopped, in the middle of the meadow, chest heaving. And the audience heard him say it — out loud, to the gauntlet, in the small, humiliated voice of a boy surrendering the only dream a weak person is issued at birth, the dream of finally being the strongest one on the field:

"Ddraig. I can't get big enough. Not in time." A breath. "But I don't have to, right? It doesn't have to be *me*."

There was a silence from the gem. When the dragon answered, the theatre would argue for chapters afterward about what was in his voice; the closest anyone came was *pride, ambushed*.

**[...Hoh. There it is. Two hundred hosts, partner. Two hundred, and every one of them clawed for the summit alone, and the summit is where I buried them. You are the first to look at my power and see a *gift*.]** A sound like a mountain range chuckling. **[Then let us give it. LISTEN WELL, BOY — THE GEAR ANSWERS INTENT!]**

The gauntlet blazed. The gem's green light ran liquid along the scales, and the system's voice — the cold one, the spec-sheet one — spoke over a flare of crimson that lit the whole meadow:

**[BOOSTED GEAR: SECOND LIBERATION.]**

**[BOOST.] [BOOST.] [BOOST.] [BOOST.]**

Ten seconds. Ten seconds. Ten seconds. The theatre felt the power stack in his arm like held thunder, doubling and redoubling past what his body could safely spend — and then felt him do the thing no previous Red Dragon Emperor had thought to want. He turned, found the smallest member of the team already moving — because Koneko Toujou had understood his face before he spoke, and was already inside a boulder's shadow, fist cocked — and slapped his gauntlet flat between her shoulder blades.

**[TRANSFER.]**

The vision gave the theatre one full second of what it felt like from inside Koneko: a Rook's monstrous baseline strength suddenly *doubled, and doubled, and doubled, and doubled*, sixteen times her monstrous everything arriving down her arm like a red tide with a dragon's roar inside it.

She punched the granite ridge at the meadow's edge.

The ridge left.

Not cracked — *left*: a wedge of mountainside the size of a cathedral detached, powdered, and rained down the far valley in a rolling grey thunder that went on for eleven seconds, while a four-foot-six girl stood in the settling dust looking at her own small fist with the nearest thing to an expression she had worn all vision.

"...Heavy," said Koneko Toujou. And then, quietly, not looking at him: "...Do it again."

The last thing the vision showed them was the evening's second experiment: Rias Gremory's Power of Destruction — the crimson annihilation the audience had seen unmake lesser threats — with four transferred boosts poured into it, swelling from a river to a *sea*, and Rias turning to stare at her Pawn with exactly the expression she had worn kneeling over his corpse in the park. The jeweler's expression. The one that said: *there* is the fire I paid eight Pawns for.

**DAY NINE: FORCE MULTIPLIER DOCTRINE — ACQUIRED,** said the system, and let them go.

---

"THAT'S the weapon," Tony Stark and Scott Summers said, simultaneously, from opposite sides of the theatre, and then glared at each other for it.

Tony recovered first, already drawing in the air. "Everyone's been doing the math wrong. *I've* been doing the math wrong. I called him a compound-interest bomb in chapter one — single warhead, exponential yield, limited by the chassis. Wrong. The chassis was never the point. He's not a bomb, he's a *bus bar* — he can run the exponential and then dump the accumulated load into any conductor on the field. That tiny Rook just output — conservatively — a two-kiloton punch, and she isn't even the optimal target. The optimal target is whoever's already strongest. Do you understand what that scales to? Point him at Thor. Point him at *Hulk*."

"Point him at an army," said Doctor Doom, from his aisle, and the temperature of the observation silenced the row around him. "Ten thousand soldiers, each doubled, is a power no throne on my world holds. The boy converts *himself* into everyone else's growth. Note it well: this is the first ability we have seen that becomes more dangerous the more he loves the people beside him." A pause, masked head tilting a degree. "He will be very dangerous indeed."

"You're all still doing it wrong," Steve Rogers said, and he stood up to say it, which he rarely did, and the room quieted for him the way rooms did. "You're pricing the output. Price the *decision*. That kid has been the weakest person in every room of his entire life. Nine days ago somebody finally hands him a ladder out — infinite doubling, the strongest-one-on-the-field dream, the exact dream he's been screaming about since the day we met him — and on day nine, with everything on the line, he looks at the summit and gives his ladder away." He looked around the tiers, at gods and monsters. "I've led rosters full of heavy hitters. The rarest thing on any team I ever fielded wasn't strength. It was the guy who'd rather make five people strong than be strong in front of five people. Armies follow that guy off cliffs. Riser Phenex is about to fight a boy who spent ten days learning the one thing money, blood, and immortality can't buy him."

"The dragon knew," Thor said softly. "*Two hundred hosts, and the summit is where I buried them.* Two hundred climbers, and at last the beast is bonded to a boy who builds staircases. No wonder the old one sounds like a father. He has finally been *surprised*."

Up in the Eternals' block, Phastos was leaning forward with his fingers steepled and his eyes bright. "Architecturally, it's the elegant solution, and nobody on that mountain knows it yet," he said. "Exponential generation is trivial to design and impossible to *contain* — every version of it fails at the container, we just watched his shins prove that. But exponential *distribution*—" he opened his hands, "—the load balances itself across every willing container on the field. The system stops being a bomb and becomes a grid. Somebody should tell that child he just solved, by instinct, in a meadow, the problem that cracks every god-engine I've ever audited."

"He solved it because of *why*," Ajak said. "Mark the sequence. He did not find that power reaching for victory. He found it in the moment he gave up on being the hero of the story." She was quiet a moment. "Ten thousand years I have waited to see what humans do with real power. Nearly all of them answer *keep it*. The machine has gone to a great deal of trouble tonight to show us one who answers *share*." Her eyes went up, past the screen, to the dark above the dark. "I begin to suspect that is precisely what we are being prepared to need."

"Told you," Sersi said, to all of her family at once, insufferably, and even Sprite didn't argue.

The screen flickered.

The gacha wheel folded itself away, and in its place the screen assembled, piece by heraldic piece, an image that pulled the whole theatre forward in its seats: two crests, facing off across a chessboard rendered in fire and shadow. A crimson rose-and-sigil the room had learned to know. And opposite it, vast and burning, a bird wreathed in flame — wings spread, eternally dying, eternally *unbothered* — beneath a single line of specification text:

**HOUSE PHENEX. TRAIT: RESURRECTION FLAME. INJURY REJECTION: TOTAL. DEFEAT CONDITION: UNKNOWN.**

"Total," repeated Sam Wilson. "Injury rejection: *total*. It heals everything. How does a sixteen-times Rook punch beat a guy who heals *everything*?"

"It doesn't," said Logan, from the dark of the back rows. He hadn't spoken all chapter. He sounded like a man reading from his own file. "Take it from the guy who heals everything. Body ain't where you beat us." He tipped his head at the screen, at the burning, arrogant bird. "Flesh grows back. Ask that rich boy what happens when something makes him *quit*."

The screen, as if it had been waiting all night for exactly that, printed one last line and went dark:

**RATING GAME: GREMORY VERSUS PHENEX — PENDING.**

**TEN DAYS HAVE BEEN PAID. NEXT: WHAT THEY PURCHASED.**
